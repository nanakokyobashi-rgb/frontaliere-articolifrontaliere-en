Porlezza: fake Carabinieri officer asks for gold, son stops scam

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 8 October 2026

## TL;DR - At Porlezza, attempted scam by a fake carabiniere - The phone call dates back to October 7, 2026 - Gold requested to secure the son's release

TL;DR

  • In Porlezza, attempted scam by a fake Carabinieri officer
  • The phone call dates back to October 7, 2026
  • Gold requested to secure the son's release
  • The son's return foiled the plan

Key facts

  • Location → Porlezza
  • When → Wednesday, October 7, 2026

  • Request → gold and valuables kept at home
  • Emergency → son supposedly arrested

On the morning of Wednesday, October 7, 2026, an elderly woman living in Porlezza received a phone call designed to cause fear and obtain the immediate handover of money or valuables. The man on the other end introduced himself as a Carabinieri officer.

The criminal reported a false emergency: according to the story told over the phone, the woman's son had been arrested. To secure his release, the victim would have to urgently hand over all the gold in the home. The request therefore concerned the valuables kept in the house and played on the need to act quickly.

The plan did not succeed, however. While the woman was still engaged in the conversation, her son returned home and immediately noticed his mother's agitated state. He realized that something was wrong and intervened directly in the phone call.

The moment of discovery

At that point, the scammer realized he had been found out and abruptly ended the call. The son's intervention therefore stopped the request before the gold or other valuables could be handed over.

The entire incident revolved around the false news of the arrest and the promise of securing the family member's release in exchange for valuable goods. The elderly woman did not have a chance to hand over what was requested, because her son's return made it possible to immediately recognize the anomaly in the phone call and stop the attempt.

Operational details

The request for gold is a warning sign

Fear as leverage

The case fits a scam pattern that primarily targets older people: exploiting anxiety about what will happen to a family member, the scammer pretends to be a law enforcement officer and creates such a sense of urgency that it prompts the victim to act before being able to verify what they are told.

In the case of Porlezza, the false emergency was linked to the alleged detention of the son. The requested handover directly concerned the gold and valuables kept at home, on the grounds that they were needed to secure the family member's release. The request for valuables thus becomes the central step in the scam: fear is used to make a decision urgent when it should instead be verified.

The Carabinieri once again urge people to treat requests made by phone or in person for money, jewelry, or other valuables as suspicious. As law enforcement agencies remind us, no one asks for payments, bail, or valuables to avoid an arrest or secure a family member's release.

This warning therefore also applies to requests made with an apparent official justification. The fact that the caller presents themselves as a military officer does not change the rule indicated by the Carabinieri: money or valuables must not be handed over based on a request received in a situation designed to cause alarm.

If you receive a similar call, hang up immediately and call 112. Do not hand over money or valuables or follow the supposed officer's instructions. In the case of Porlezza, it was precisely the son's intervention that prevented the valuables kept in the home from being handed over.

Key points

The investigations following the report

The call to 112

Once the call had ended, the son informed the Single Emergency Number 112 of what had happened. Shortly afterwards, the officers of the Compagnia di Menaggio reached the home in a patrol car and began investigating the case.

The subsequent operational developments indicated for the incident therefore concern the report made to the emergency control room and the checks initiated by the officers who intervened in Porlezza. The call to 112 came after the criminal had abruptly ended the conversation, realizing that he had been exposed by the son's intervention.

Parallel to the investigations, the Carabinieri asked the family to report without delay any other suspicious phone calls and any possible presence of unknown people near the home. The request therefore concerns both new telephone contacts and people noticed near the house.

The family was therefore asked to promptly communicate any further element attributable to the same type of situation. The officers' recommendation is directly connected to the attempt to obtain gold and valuables through a false emergency and a request made in the name of the Arma dei Carabinieri.

In Porlezza, the woman did not hand over her valuables. The failure to hand over the goods was made possible by the son's return while she was still on the phone and by his prompt reaction: he realized that something was not right, intervened in the conversation, and then reported what had happened to 112. The patrol of the Compagnia di Menaggio was thus able to begin investigating the attempted scam.
