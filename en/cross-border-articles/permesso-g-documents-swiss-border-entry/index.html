On January 1, 2024, the New Cross-Border Commuters Agreement changed the rules on taxation, permits and entry into Switzerland for those who work across the border. To cross the border crossings legally (Brogeda, Chiasso, Gaggiolo and others), the cross-border commuter must have specific documents and comply with precise procedures: this is not a secondary formality, but an essential requirement for working legally and meeting social security and tax obligations toward the Swiss AVS and the Italian INPS. Although Switzerland is not an EU member, it requires strict documentation and customs checks that vary according to the worker's status and the type of goods being transported. Understanding the procedure avoids delays, administrative penalties and complications that could jeopardize continuity of employment.

Mandatory documents: what the cross-border commuter must bring to the border crossings

The passport or Italian identity card is the basic document. Switzerland accepts both if they are valid. The identity card must have at least three months' remaining validity at the time of entry. The passport has no additional limitations compared with any other destination.

The G permit is the document that distinguishes the cross-border commuter from the tourist. It is a residence permit in Italy for employees or self-employed workers who carry out activities in Switzerland. It is not a visa (Switzerland does not require visas for Italian citizens), but an administrative authorization obtained from the local Swiss authorities (SECO/SEM) and registered by the Italian municipality of residence in the Canton of Ticino. The G permit does not authorize entry into Switzerland: it authorizes the holder to work and reside legally in Italy while carrying out professional activities in Switzerland. Without the G permit, the cross-border commuter operates in violation of Swiss migration law.

New cross-border commuters vs. already-active cross-border commuters: differences in the tax allowance

The New Cross-Border Commuters Agreement (applied from January 1, 2024, ratified by Italy with Law 83 of June 13, 2023) introduces a crucial distinction for taxation. The transitional regime under Art. 9 of the Agreement applies only to those who meet the treaty definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between December 31, 2018 and July 17, 2023; the covered employment income is taxed exclusively in Switzerland. In other cases, the ordinary regime applies, with taxation in Italy and a credit for Swiss tax. The 10.000 euro under Art. 4 of Law 83/2023 is merely the limit for the income referred to in Art. 1, paragraph 175, Law 147/2013, not a general tax allowance. This difference affects the payslip, the tax rebate declaration and overall tax management, but NOT the entry procedure at the border crossings: both profiles require a G permit and the same documents to cross the border.

Operational details

How the New 2024 Agreement Rules Taxation and Entry

The border crossing into Switzerland does not only face customs controls: its tax status is governed by the Italian-Swiss Convention (signed on 9 March 1976) and the New Frontier Agreement (signed on 23 December 2020, applied since 1 January 2024). Switzerland applies tax at source (Swiss tax withholding) directly on the paycheck, while Italy avoids double taxation by granting a tax credit in quadro CE della dichiarazione 730. This means that the border worker pays taxes in Switzerland, and Italy recognises what has already been paid north of the border. The procedure is coordinated: the Swiss employer pays the tax at the source, the border worker declares in Italy, the Revenue Agency accredits the refund.

Daily entry to the crossings does not require a specific customs declaration for income only: customs checks physical goods (cars, goods, contraband, money over €10,000) and not the compensation already received. However, the registration of the G permit with the Italian municipality of residence is a formal prerequisite that Swiss customs can verify in the event of a random check. A border crossing without a registered G permit is not legal and risks administrative sanctions, revocation of the permit itself and blocking of future renewal. SECO and SEM operate in synergy: registration deficiencies are tracked and communicated between offices.

Step-by-step Procedure: How to Obtain a G Permit and Cross the Border

Step 1: Registration with Your Italian Municipality of Residence Before starting work in Switzerland, the cross-border worker must go to their municipality of residence (Lugano, Bellinzona, Mendrisio, Locarno, Chiasso, etc.) in the Canton Ticino and formally notify the intention to work across the border. The municipality issues a residence certificate with mention of cross-border work and communicates personal data to SECO (Swiss State Secretariat for Economic Affairs). This step is preliminary: without municipal registration, the G permit application cannot proceed.

Step 2: Request for G Permit from Local Swiss Authorities The Swiss employer or the cross-border worker themselves requests the G permit from the SEM office (State Secretariat for Migration) of the canton where work will take place (Ticino, Valais, etc.). The permit is granted after verification of the availability of jobs for cross-border workers (annual quota set by SECO) and the economic situation of the company. SECO sets a maximum quota of cross-border workers hired each year: if the quota is exhausted, the application can be rejected even if the company is solid. This is one of Switzerland's main controls on new entries.

Step 3: Registration with the Swiss Municipality of Work (Optional but Recommended) Not mandatory, but many Swiss municipalities register cross-border workers for statistical and administrative purposes (USTAT collects data). It does not create residency in Switzerland and does not involve additional local social obligations.

Step 4: First Entry and Crossing the Border Once in possession of a G permit, passport/identity card, the cross-border worker can cross the border posts (Brogeda, Chiasso, Gaggiolo, Ponte Tresa, etc.). Swiss customs conduct random checks; a thorough check requires presentation of the G permit, Swiss income tax return (by end of February each year), and any goods subject to customs declaration. An identity card or passport is always mandatory. Customs also check alignment between the registered permit and data from the Italian municipality of residence.

📊 Document Checklist at the Border:

  • Italian passport or identity card (valid with at least 3 months remaining validity)
  • G permit (paper or digital depending on the canton)
  • Tax return 730 (not mandatory at the border, but recommended to demonstrate fiscal compliance)
  • Residence certificate from the Italian municipality (optional, to support the permit)

Deadlines and Renewals: What to Do Each Year

The G permit has limited validity (generally 1-5 years depending on the canton) and requires renewal before expiration. Deadlines vary by canton (Ticino, Valais, Graubünden). Renewal must be requested well in advance (at least 2-3 months before expiration), otherwise the cross-border worker loses legal status as a cross-border worker and risks more severe checks at border posts, up to being blocked from entering Switzerland.

The tax return with model 730 must be submitted by April 30 each year to the Italian Revenue Agency. This document is essential to recover the Swiss tax credit and demonstrate bilateral fiscal compliance. Those who do not submit the 730 do not access the refund and face double taxation.

What Changes in 2026: New Bilateral Agreement Fully in Force

In 2026, the New Bilateral Agreement for Cross-Border Workers is fully in effect (no longer in transition phase). The €10,000 threshold in Article 4 of Italian Law No. 83/2023 applies exclusively to employment income earned continuously and exclusively by Italian residents in a foreign border area or neighbouring country, as referred to in Article 1(175) of Law No. 147/2013; it is neither a general allowance nor an exemption from Swiss withholding tax. The entry procedure DOES NOT change: documents, G permit, border posts, customs checks remain identical. However, tax planning must account for the permanent exemption and the absence of further exemption increases. Old cross-border workers remain taxed only in Switzerland (Art. 9 of the 2020 Agreement).

Frequently Asked Questions
What document is required to cross the border as a border crossing?
Valid Italian passport or identity card. For the identity card, at least 3 months of residual validity are needed. The G permit (residence for border workers) is mandatory to work legally in Switzerland, but its possession is verifiable in case of random customs control. The permit is not a visa: Switzerland does not require visas for Italian citizens. Without a G permit registered with the Italian municipality, the border crossing is not legal.
What is the G permit and how do I get it?
It is a residence permit in Italy for those who work in Switzerland, it is not a visa. It is requested at the SEM (State Secretariat for Migration) office of the canton where you will have your place of work (Ticino, Valais, etc.), usually with the support of the Swiss employer. The Italian municipality of residence in Ticino must be informed and record the permit in its archives. Switzerland applies a maximum annual quota of hired border workers set by SECO: if the quota is exhausted, the request may be rejected.
Who qualifies for the transitional regime in Article 9?
Article 9’s transitional regime applies only to workers who meet the Agreement’s definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between 31 December 2018 and 17 July 2023; covered employment income is taxable exclusively in Switzerland. The ordinary regime applies in other cases, with Italian taxation and credit for Swiss tax where applicable. The €10,000 in Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is only the limit for income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law 147/2013, not a general allowance. How does source tax and double taxation work?
Switzerland applies the tax at source (withholding tax) directly on the paycheck, up to the indicated deductible (€10,000). Italy avoids double taxation by granting tax credit in the EC framework of model 730. You do not pay twice if you submit the declaration: you pay in Switzerland, Italy recognises what has already been paid. If you do not file the 730, you do not recover the benefit and unfortunately suffer double taxation.
What are the main Ticino border crossings?
The most used crossings are Brogeda (Ticino-Graubünden), Chiasso (Ticino-Como), Gaggiolo (border Como), Ponte Tresa (Ticino-Varese). Each crossing has customs opening hours and specific controls. In case of heavy traffic or extraordinary events (protests, bad weather, accidents), transit can slow down significantly. A border agent should plan travel times and keep the G permit on hand at all times during transfers.
