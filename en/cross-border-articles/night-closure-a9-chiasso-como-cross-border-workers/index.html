A9 Chiasso-Como: Nights of Closures and Construction Sites

The A9 motorway between Como and Chiasso will experience overnight closures for exceptional transports and the return of daytime construction sites, significantly impacting cross-border commuters.

The cross-border road network between Italy and the Canton of Ticino is preparing for new challenges. The A9 Lainate-Como-Chiasso motorway, a vital artery for thousands of cross-border workers who go to work in Ticino every day, will be the scene of interruptions and slowdowns starting from the first days of March 2026. Autostrade per l'Italia has announced night closures and the resumption of important maintenance works which will inevitably affect travel times and travel planning.

Specifically, to allow the transit of exceptional transports, the stretch between Como Centro and Chiasso, heading north (towards Switzerland), will be closed from 10pm on Tuesday 3 March to 5am on Wednesday 4 March. This night-time interruption will require drivers, in particular cross-border commuters returning to Ticino or preparing for morning shifts, to exit at the Como Centro junction. From there, the recommended route involves continuing on the ordinary road towards Chiasso, until reaching the city customs. A detour which, although manageable during times of lower traffic, can always reserve surprises in terms of additional time and stress for those with rigid schedules. It is a picture that is often repeated at the Brogeda border, where traffic pressure is a constant. The source Ticinonline (tio.ch) highlighted how such interventions are now a habit, defining the situation as the return of the "endless construction site" along the Italian-Swiss route, an expression that well sums up the perception of many commuters.

The return of the daytime construction site on the Fati Viaduct

Not just busy nights: from Monday 1 March, daytime construction sites will resume activities. The interventions will focus on the Fati viaduct, strategically located before the Monte Quarcino tunnel and the Lake Como exit. These works, preparatory to a broader rehabilitation of the pavement and the creation of a new platform water collection system, will involve a deviation of the roadway with transit on only one lane in each direction. Traffic management will be dynamic: Autostrade per l'Italia foresees a remodulation of the construction site based on flows and estimated travel times. During rush hours, between 5:00 and 10:00 in the morning towards Switzerland and from 16:00 to 19:00 towards Lainate/Milan, transit will be guaranteed on two lanes. An attempt to mitigate the impact on commuting which, however, will not completely eliminate the inconveniences. This alternation between night-time closures and daytime construction sites adds a further layer of complexity for border workers, who will have to constantly monitor the situation to avoid significant delays in accessing the Canton of Ticino.

Operational details

The implications of these changes to traffic on the A9 are multiple and directly affect the routine of cross-border commuters. Overnight closures, while temporary, require careful planning for those who work shifts or need to travel during those time slots. The alternative on ordinary roads, despite being a consolidated route, often means longer travel times due to crossing inhabited centers and local congestion, especially near the Chiasso customs. This translates not only into precious minutes lost, but also into an increase in fuel consumption and, consequently, daily travel costs.

The pavement rehabilitation works and the creation of the new water collection system on the Fati viaduct are necessary interventions for the safety and durability of the infrastructure. However, managing traffic with only one lane in each direction, although mitigated by two lanes during rush hour, represents a significant bottleneck. Cross-border workers who cross the border every day to reach Lugano, Mendrisio or Bellinzona will have to take into account delays and queues, especially on the days of greatest influx. The flexibility guaranteed by the two lanes during rush hour is an appreciable measure, but it remains essential for commuters to be informed in real time about the traffic situation.
