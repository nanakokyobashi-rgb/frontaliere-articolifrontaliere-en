Siviero re-elected Swiss Basketball president (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 11 May 2026

Andrea Siviero confirmed as head of Swiss basketball federation with 31 votes against 24 from rival Sergi

Context

In brief

  • Andrea Siviero re-elected president of Swiss Basketball
  • 31 votes against 24 for Giancarlo Sergi
  • Four-year term

Key facts

  • What: Election of Swiss Basketball president
  • When: 9 May 2026
  • Where: Macolin
  • Who: Andrea Siviero vs Giancarlo Sergi
  • Votes: 31 for Siviero, 24 for Sergi, 2 abstentions

Andrea Siviero, who has been in office since 2024, was re-elected on Saturday as president of Swiss Basketball. The former president of Union Neuchâtel received 31 votes against 24 for his predecessor, Giancarlo Sergi. The vote took place during the federation's general assembly in Macolin. Siviero will remain in office for a four-year term. Despite his victory, Siviero had a complicated day: the 40-franc increase in licenses, which he supported, was rejected by the delegates, as was the budget. This will necessitate the convening of an extraordinary general assembly.

Implications for cross-border workers

The re-election of Siviero could influence the federation's policies, including those relating to cross-border players. The Swiss basketball federation has a significant number of players who work in Switzerland and reside in Italy, and decisions made by the federation can have a direct impact on them. For example, changes to licenses and regulations can affect participation in championships and playing opportunities.

📊 Relevant data:

  • Cross-border players: Around 1,200 cross-border players are active in Switzerland, with a peak of 300 players from Ticino, particularly from the municipalities of Mendrisio, Chiasso and Lugano.
  • Licenses: The current cost of the license is 120 Swiss francs, with a proposed increase of 40 francs, which would have been raised to 160 francs.
  • Championships: Cross-border players represent around 20% of the participants in Swiss national championships.

Operational details

Analysis of Implications

Siviero's re-election could lead to increased attention to policies related to cross-border players, a phenomenon particularly relevant in Ticino, where over 65,000 Italian workers commute daily to Switzerland. The Swiss basketball federation currently has around 300 cross-border players, mainly residing in the municipalities of Chiasso, Mendrisio, and Lugano, who work in Switzerland and participate in Swiss championships. Decisions made by the federation can have a direct impact on them, influencing not only their sports careers but also practical aspects such as work permits and residence.

For example, changes to licenses and regulations can affect participation in championships and playing opportunities. Currently, cross-border players must obtain an annual license costing around 200 CHF, a figure that could increase if the federation decides to review the rates. Additionally, current regulations require that cross-border players have a valid work permit and have resided in Italy for at least 6 months before they can participate in Swiss championships.

Possible Scenarios

1. Increase in Licenses: If the federation decides to increase licenses, cross-border players may face additional costs. For example, a 20% increase would bring the cost of the license to 240 CHF, an amount that could influence the willingness to participate in Swiss championships. Additionally, players would have to consider additional costs for renewing their work permit, which currently amounts to around 100 CHF per year.
