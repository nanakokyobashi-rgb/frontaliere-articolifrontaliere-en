Living in Santa Maria Maggiore and working in Ticino as a frontier worker is an economically advantageous choice for those seeking a salary in Swiss francs with an Italian cost of living. The municipality in the province of Verbano-Cusio-Ossola has in recent years become the preferred base for cross-border workers who wish to reconcile high income with limited expenses.

What is the G permit and who is entitled to it

A frontier worker is a worker who resides in Italy and is employed in Switzerland. The residence and work permit, known as the "G permit", is the document that allows this activity. According to the Italian-Swiss Convention signed on March 9, 1976, the frontier worker is subject to taxation in Switzerland: there is no double taxation because Italy recognizes Swiss income through the tax credit declared on Form 730 (section CE of the annual tax return).

The new tax agreement in force from 2024

As of January 1st, 2024, the new Frontier Workers Agreement came into force, signed on December 23, 2020 and ratified by Italy through Law 83 of June 13, 2023. The agreement introduces two tax exemption regimes:

  • Legacy frontier workers continue to be taxed only in Switzerland (Art. 9 of the 2020 Agreement).
  • The transitional regime under Art. 9 of the Agreement applies only to those who satisfy the treaty definition and have worked in the Graubünden, Ticino, or Valais between December 31, 2018 and July 17, 2023; the covered employment income is taxed exclusively in Switzerland. In all other cases, the ordinary regime applies, with taxation in Italy and a credit for Swiss tax. The 10.000 euros under Art. 4 of Law 83/2023 are merely the limit for the income referred to in Art. 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013, not a general tax allowance. This exemption reduces the taxable base subject to Swiss withholding tax, generating significant tax savings for the worker.

Santa Maria Maggiore: location and access to the border crossings

Santa Maria Maggiore is an Italian municipality strategically located for access to the Brogeda and Gaggiolo border crossings toward the Canton of Ticino. Travel times to the main employment hubs in Ticino (Lugano, Bellinzona, Mendrisio, Locarno) typically range from 45 minutes to 1.5 hours, depending on congestion at the border crossings and the final destination. The A2 motorway connects Verbania to Chiasso, while the A9 serves the areas of the Mendrisiotto.

How Swiss withholding tax works

Tax is withheld directly from the payslip by the Swiss employer. The total rate combines AVS (old-age and survivors' insurance) 5.3%, AI (disability insurance) 5.3%, IPG (salary tax) 1.1%, LAINF accident insurance 0.7–1.5%, and LPP (second pillar of occupational pension provision) 7–18% depending on the age bracket. Italy does not apply any further withholding thanks to the tax-credit mechanism in Form 730.

Operational details

Costs of living: the economic advantage

The economic engine of the cross-border worker is the spread between the Swiss salary and the Italian cost of living. A gross salary of CHF 5,500–6,000 per month is common in Ticino for roles in industry, healthcare, ICT or construction. After all taxes and contributions (AVS 5.3%, AI 5.3%, IPG 1.1%, insurance 0.7–1.5%, LPP 7–18% per age group), the net drops to CHF 4,100–4,300 per month.

In Italy, a family can live in Santa Maria Maggiore with around €1,800–2,000 per month: rent €600–700, groceries €400, car €300, utilities €200, healthcare and education €200. Converted at the CHF/EUR exchange rate, the cost is CHF 1,950–2,150. The savings margin reaches CHF 2,000–2,200 per month, equivalent to over CHF 24,000 per year.

Disadvantages and hidden costs

Commuting and traffic: CHF 300–500 per month in fuel, car maintenance, Swiss vignette and Lugano/Bellinzona parking. The Brogeda and Gaggiolo crossings are congested from 06:30 to 09:00 and from 16:30 to 19:30, generating delays of 30–60 minutes. Over 200 days/year on the road means loss of 200–300 hours of free time per year.

Administrative complexity: Italian declaration + Swiss affairs (currency exchange, bank account management, G permit, INPS registration). Many cross-border workers hire accountants to manage the two administrations.

Instability of the CHF/EUR exchange rate: A change of 5% means CHF 1,000 monthly loss of purchasing power. In the long term, the franc appreciates against the euro, but short to medium term fluctuations have an impact.

Impact on the family: Children's school in Italy (diplomas not always recognized in CH), healthcare divided between two countries, bureaucratic complexity for family permits (spouse, children).

Swiss pension: AVS, BVG, KVG

AVS and AI

The cross-border worker contributes to the AVS (old age-survivors insurance) and AI (disability) at the rate of 5.3% + 5.3% (employee + employer), accumulating annuity credits at 65 years (men) or 64 years (women). Years of contributions under 44 (men) or 43 (women) reduce the final pension.

LPP (Second pillar)

LPP is mandatory if the gross annual income exceeds CHF 23,155. The contribution rates vary by age group: young people (25–34 years) 7%, intermediate (35–44) 10%, seniors (45–54) 15%, close to retirement (55–65) 18%. Many cross-border workers underestimate the 2nd pillar: failure to pay in the younger years means a 20–30% lower pension at 65.

KVG and healthcare option

The cross-border worker has the right to choose health insurance (KVG): he can opt for the Swiss KVG (adult deductible CHF 300–2,500, high premiums) or remain in the Italian insurance (cheaper but lower coverage for dentist, physiotherapy, specialist care). Many cross-border commuters choose the Italian option, saving CHF 100–200 per month, but lose Swiss coverage.

Key points

Before Transfer: Checklist and Documents

Moving to Santa Maria Maggiore as a border worker requires meticulous administrative preparation. Here are the essential steps.

Step 1: Requesting the G Permit

The G permit is requested at the cantonal migration division (SEM) in Ticino, usually online or at regional counters. The necessary documents include:

  • G permit application (cantonally issued form)
  • Copy of Swiss employment contract or job offer letter
  • Certificate of liability insurance (civil responsibility)
  • Declaration of resources (bank statement and pay slip)
  • Certificate of good conduct from Italy (required by Prefecture or Questura)

Approval times vary from 2 to 4 weeks after receiving complete documentation. The G permit is valid for five years with a contract of more than one year; with a shorter contract it lasts as long as the contract.

Step 2: Anagraphic and Fiscal Registration in Italy

In Italy, register with the Municipality of Santa Maria Maggiore for anagraphic registration and obtain a tax code from the Revenue Agency. The border worker must declare Swiss income in the Italian 730 model, filling in the CE section (foreign tax credits) to avoid double taxation. The first declaration is critical: errors in the CE section generate payment notices.

Step 3: Italo-Swiss Bank Account

Opening an Italian bank account facilitates local payments (rent, utilities, groceries). Meanwhile, maintain a Swiss account with the employer or a Swiss bank. Some Italo-Swiss institutions offer convenient exchange services (CHF→EUR) reducing costs from 1–2% to 0.3–0.5%.

Step 4: Health Insurance

If you opt for Swiss LAMal, contact a recognized insurance company (CSS, Helsana, SWICA) and subscribe to a policy with an adult deductible of CHF 300–2,500. If you prefer to remain in Italy, register with the INPS as a foreign worker (the Swiss employer often handles this directly).

Fiscal Deadlines: The Border Worker's Calendar

January–February: Receipt of Swiss income certification (CertRed) from the pay slip and preparation of the Italian 730 model.

30 April: Deadline for submitting the 730 model to the Italian Revenue Agency (possible extension to 31 May with a delay).

June–July: Any Swiss tax rectifications and communications from the cantonal administration (DFE/DSS).

November–December: Annual AVS/AI/LPP reporting to the employer and verification of the taxable base for the following year.

Frequently Asked Questions
Can I live in Santa Maria Maggiore and work in Ticino without a G permit?
No. The G permit (residence permit and cross-border work) is mandatory for border workers working in Switzerland. Working without permission is illegal and exposes you to penalties (fine, expulsion). The application for the G permit must accompany the job offer and is managed by the Migration Division (SEM) of the Canton of Ticino. Approval times are generally 2–4 weeks.
Does the Swiss source tax also cover Italian taxes?
The Swiss source tax (20–25% total between AVS, AI, IPG, insurance, LPP) is a withholding on the Swiss paycheck. Italy does not apply additional taxes because it recognizes Swiss income through the tax credit in Form 730 (EC framework). The Italian-Swiss Convention of 9 March 1976 provides that there is no double taxation: the border worker only pays in Switzerland.
With the € 10,000 deductible, how much do you save me in taxes?
The deductible reduces the tax base in Switzerland. If you earn CHF 60,000 gross per year, without excess you pay taxes at source on CHF 60,000; with € 10,000 (about CHF 10,900), you pay on CHF 49,100. The savings depend on the Swiss marginal rate (about 20%), so the advantage is about CHF 2,180 per year. The benefit increases for higher wages.
What are the exact waiting times for permit G?
Generally 2–4 weeks from the date of receipt of the complete documentation at the Migration Division (SEM) of the Canton of Ticino. The time can be extended if there are documentary deficiencies or if the verification of credibility requires communications with Italy (Prefecture, registry offices). It is advisable to submit the application with all the attached documents to avoid delays.
If I change employer, does the G permit remain valid?
The G permit remains valid as long as you have an active employment contract in Switzerland. If you change employer, you must report the change to the SEM within 30 days, providing the new contract. If you lose your job and do not find another one within 3–6 months, the G permit can be revoked and you will have to leave the Swiss territory.
