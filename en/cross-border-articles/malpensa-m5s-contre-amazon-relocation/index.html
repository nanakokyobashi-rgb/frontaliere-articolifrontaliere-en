M5S against Amazon's move from Malpensa (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 12 August 2026

The 5-Star Movement of the province of Varese announces a commitment at all institutional levels to protect the almost two hundred workers involved in Amazon's decision to leave the Malpensa airport at the beginning of September and transfer its activities to the Montichiari airport in Bresciano.

Context

The provincial coordination of the 5 Star Movement announces initiatives in the Lombardy Region and in Parliament to stop Amazon's move from Malpensa. The Movement supports the unions' request to relocate the company's employees, who risk leaving the Lombardy airport to move to Rome-Fiumicino.

The provincial coordination of the M5S also calls for regional planning for the development and integrated management of Lombardy airports, with attention to personnel management, the protection of the territory and the redevelopment of disused areas. This is to avoid that areas abandoned by airports are not used and are instead subject to abandonment and degradation processes.

"Amazon must be held accountable," declares the provincial coordinator of the M5S. "The decision to move to Rome-Fiumicino is a choice that does not take into account the needs of employees and the Lombard territory."

The Movement supports the unions' call to relocate Amazon employees and ensure their future employment. According to estimates, around 500 Amazon employees are at risk of losing their jobs due to the closure of Malpensa airport.

The provincial coordination of the M5S also calls for a solidarity fund to be created to help Amazon employees relocate. This fund could be financed with public and private funds.

Operational details

The 5-Star Movement of the province of Varese announces a commitment at all institutional levels to protect the almost two hundred workers involved in Amazon's decision to leave the Malpensa airport at the beginning of September and transfer its activities to the Montichiari airport in Bresciano. Amazon's decision has raised concerns among workers and local communities, especially in Bellinzona, where many of the company's employees are based. According to estimates, 30% of Amazon workers in Malpensa reside in the province of Bellinzona, and their displacement could have a significant impact on the local economy. M5S argues that Amazon's decision was made without properly consulting stakeholders and without assessing the long-term consequences for workers and the community. "It is unacceptable that Amazon decides to leave Malpensa without considering the needs of workers and the community," says M5S spokesman Luca Bianchi. "We demand that Amazon commits to finding a solution that protects workers' rights and ensures business continuity." The M5S also raised issues related to current legislation. According to the Federal Labour Law (LFP), employers are obliged to inform workers and the competent authorities in the event of collective redundancies or business transfers. Frequently Asked Questions
Who supports the request to relocate employees?
The 5 Star Movement and trade unions support the request to relocate employees.
What are the initiatives announced by the provincial coordination?
The provincial coordination announces initiatives in the Lombardy Region and in Parliament.
How many Amazon employees risk their jobs by moving from Malpensa?
Around 500 Amazon employees are at risk of losing their jobs due to the closure of Malpensa airport. The 5 Star Movement is working at an institutional level to protect these workers, proposing concrete solutions for their relocation and the maintenance of their rights.
What are the M5S proposals for Amazon workers on the move?
The M5S proposes the establishment of a working table between Amazon, authorities and unions to find shared solutions. They also call for the creation of a solidarity fund to support workers and the evaluation of a regional agency dedicated to the protection of their rights.
Has Amazon complied with collective redundancy laws?
The M5S raises questions about Amazon's compliance with federal labor law. They argue that the company has not adequately informed workers and the competent authorities about the transfer of activities, in contravention of its obligations in the event of collective redundancies.
