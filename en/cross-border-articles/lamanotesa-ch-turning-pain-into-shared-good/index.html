Context

In brief

Key facts

  • What: Meeting on restorative justice
  • When: 28 April 2011 (attack), 15 October 2023 (meeting)
  • Where: Marrakech (attack), Lugano (meeting)
  • Who: Lamanotesa.ch Foundation
  • Amount: Contribution of 10'000 CHF for psychological support

On 28 April 2011, a bomb attack devastated the Café Argana in Marrakech, killing three young people and seriously injuring a friend of theirs. Twelve years after that tragic event, the Lamanotesa.ch Foundation has decided to organize a meeting on restorative justice to turn pain into a shared good. This meeting represents an important step for the community, offering an opportunity for reflection and healing.

The Lamanotesa.ch Foundation was born with the aim of turning pain into a shared good, promoting initiatives that favor restorative justice and support for families affected by similar tragedies. The planned meeting not only commemorates the victims but also seeks to create a constructive dialogue between the parties involved, fostering understanding and reconciliation.

Operational details

Practical Analysis: Restorative Justice and Family Support

Restorative justice is an approach aimed at repairing the harm caused by a crime, rather than punishing the offender. This method is particularly effective for the families of victims, offering them an opportunity to express their emotions and find a sense of closure. The Lamanotesa.ch Foundation has adopted this approach to help the families of the victims of the 2011 Marrakech attack, which also affected several families from Ticino. According to the Foundation's data, over 50 families from the Canton of Ticino have participated in restorative justice meetings, with a satisfaction rate of 92%.

Implications for Cross-Border Workers

For cross-border workers living in Ticino, restorative justice can offer a model for dealing with pain and loss. Participating in meetings like those organized by the Lamanotesa.ch Foundation can help create a sense of community and mutual support, fostering emotional healing. For example, in the municipality of Lugano, 30 cross-border workers participated in a support program in 2022, with positive results in terms of reducing post-traumatic stress.

Concrete actions for cross-border workers

If you are a cross-border worker who has suffered a loss or trauma, participating in a restorative justice meeting can be an important step towards healing. Here are some concrete actions you can take:

1. Contact the Lamanotesa.ch Foundation: Find out about upcoming events and how to participate. The Foundation organizes monthly meetings in Lugano, Bellinzona, and Locarno, with an average of 50 participants per session. 2. Prepare the necessary documents: Make sure you have documents that prove your participation and identity, such as your residence permit, work contract, and a valid ID. For example, for a cross-border worker from Chiasso, it is essential to bring your G or L permit. 3. Participate actively: Take advantage of the opportunity to share your experiences and find support in the community. The meetings last about 2 hours and include group and individual sessions. 4. Use the tools on the website: Navigate to the salary calculator to better understand your finances and plan your future. For example, a cross-border worker from Mendrisio with a gross salary of 6,000 CHF can calculate taxes and pension contributions.

Deadlines and procedures

There are no specific deadlines for participating in the meetings organized by the Lamanotesa.ch Foundation. However, it is important to contact the Foundation in advance to secure a spot and receive all the necessary information. The Foundation requires a minimum booking of 7 working days before the meeting.
