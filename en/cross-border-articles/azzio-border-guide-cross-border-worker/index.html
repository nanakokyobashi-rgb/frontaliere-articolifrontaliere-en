Living in Azzio and working in Ticino is an option that concerns thousands of cross-border workers. The municipality's geographical location in the province of Varese, close to the Ticino border, and the Italian cost of living make this scenario attractive compared with living directly in Switzerland. However, anyone deciding to move must be aware of the new cross-border workers agreement that entered into force on January 1, 2024, which significantly changes the taxation of employment income in Switzerland.

The agreement, signed on December 23, 2020 and ratified by Italy through Law 83 of June 13, 2023, introduces an exemption of €10,000 for new cross-border workers and a transitional partial-exemption regime for those who are already cross-border workers (€7,500 from 2024 to 2033). These changes directly affect net take-home pay and the Italian tax return. It is therefore not possible to ignore the current legal framework before deciding to move.

Azzio as a starting point for a cross-border worker

Azzio is not the only possible choice among the border municipalities, but its proximity to the main crossings (Chiasso, Brogeda, Ponte Tresa) makes it a practical base for those working in Canton Ticino. The G permit, which allows holders to reside in Italy and work in Switzerland, is the legal instrument that legitimizes this cross-border residence arrangement. The permit is valid for one year and is renewable, provided that the employment relationship with a Ticino employer is maintained without prolonged interruptions.

The choice to live in Azzio entails assessing the cost of living compared with Ticino, travel times to the main economic centers (Lugano, Bellinzona, Locarno), and proximity to Italian public services (schools, healthcare, local administration). Not all these aspects have the same impact on every cross-border worker: the variables depend on the employment sector, family composition, personal needs, and the network of relationships already established in Italy.

Geographical proximity is only part of the assessment. The decisive element remains the economic sustainability of the move, which requires understanding cross-border taxation, Swiss social security contributions (AVS and LPP), and health insurance (LAMal). Only a clear understanding of these factors enables a potential cross-border worker to make an informed decision.

Operational details

How taxation works for a cross-border worker from Azzio

Taxation on employment income in Switzerland is covered by withholding tax, a deduction made only in Switzerland by the employer. Italy does not apply parallel withholdings to cross-border workers, thanks to the Italian-Swiss Convention signed on March 9, 1976. This means that there is no 'double taxation': the income is taxed only once by Switzerland.

The effective rate varies depending on the income bracket, at cantonal and federal levels. Switzerland withholds these amounts directly from the payslip. Italy grants a tax credit in Form 730 (section CE) to offset taxes already paid in Switzerland. Those with modest employment income in Ticino can receive significant refunds through this mechanism.

The new 2024 agreement: refunds and tax-free allowance

The new cross-border worker agreement that entered into force on January 1, 2024, changes the calculation of withholding tax. New cross-border workers (those who began working after July 17, 2023) benefit from an annual tax-free allowance of €10,000 on which no tax is calculated. Those who were already cross-border workers enter a transitional regime with an exemption of €7,500 from 2024 to 2033. This means a reduction in the amount withheld directly from the payslip compared with the previous regime.

Swiss contributions: AVS, LPP and LAMal

Those who work in Ticino pay AVS (basic pension) at 5.3% of gross remuneration, borne by the employee. AI (disability insurance) and IPG (earnings) are included in this rate. LPP (pension fund, second pillar) varies between 7% and 18% depending on age and the company plan, and is mandatory if the annual salary exceeds CHF 22,620.

For LAMal (mandatory Swiss health insurance), cross-border workers have the right of option: they can remain in the Italian healthcare system or subscribe to Swiss LAMal with annual deductibles between CHF 300 and CHF 2,500. This choice has consequences for medical coverage and the overall cost of healthcare protection.

Refunds and Italian tax return

The cross-border worker from Azzio files the annual Italian tax return (730) with Swiss data in section CE. If the tax paid in Switzerland exceeds the Italian tax calculated on the same basis, Italy grants a tax credit that reduces the amount due. Refunds are not automatic: they require the correct tax return and, sometimes, a communication to the Italian Agenzia delle Entrate.

Key points

Azzio pre-relocation checklist

Anyone deciding to move to Azzio to work in Ticino must follow some administrative steps. First of all, it is necessary to find and sign an employment contract with a Ticino employer before proceeding: permit G is conditional on the job offer. In parallel, it is necessary to:

1. Secure a residence in Azzio (rented or owned), with documentation (lease agreement or deed). 2. Submit the permit G application to the SEM (Migration Secretariat) of Canton Ticino. 3. Register with Azzio's Italian registry office (registration in the Population Register). 4. Enroll in Swiss social security (AVS/AI/LPP) through the employer. 5. Take out health insurance (LAMal in Switzerland or remain in the Italian healthcare system, at your choice). 6. Inform the Italian Revenue Agency of the change of residence and frontier-worker status.

Some of these procedures can take place in parallel; others must precede the actual move. Permit G is generally not granted until residence in Italy has actually been established.

Timing and deadlines

The actual move to Azzio is a process that typically takes 4-6 weeks from signing the employment contract to the delivery of the permit and registration in the population register. However, the timing varies depending on the speed of the local Italian administrations and Swiss cantonal services.

Once relocated, the frontier worker must file the Italian income tax return each year by May 31 (the standard deadline for Form 730) or June 30 (Unico). This deadline is crucial for receiving the tax rebates and for notifying the Italian Revenue Agency of their status as a resident of Azzio working in Switzerland. Failure to meet this deadline makes it impossible to recover the tax credit in subsequent years.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the requirements of the G permit to live in Azzio and work in Ticino?
The G permit requires an employment contract with a Ticino employer, a proven residence in Azzio (rent or property), and an application submitted to the SEM of the Canton of Ticino. The permit is valid for one year and is renewable. From 17 July 2023, new frontier workers will benefit from an exemption of €10,000 per year on the Swiss source tax. It is not necessary to be an EU citizen: the G permit is also open to third-country nationals.
How does taxation work for those who live in Azzio and work in Ticino?
The tax at source is calculated in Switzerland by the employer and withheld directly in the paycheck. There is no double taxation: Italy does not apply additional taxes on income from employment in Switzerland. Thanks to the 1976 Italian-Swiss Convention, income is taxed only once by Switzerland. In Italy, the tax return (730) is presented indicating the Swiss income in the EC framework to obtain the tax credit, which reduces the Italian due.
Do I need to sign up for Swiss Health Insurance (LAMal) or can I stay in the Italian health service?
Border workers with a G permit have the right of option: they can choose to remain enrolled in the Italian health service or sign a Swiss LAMal. If you choose the LAMal, you will have to pay an annual excess between CHF 300 and CHF 2,500 depending on the model you choose. The choice also affects where to receive medical care. Many border workers maintain the Italian health service for continuity of care and lower initial costs.
When do social security contributions (AVS/LPP) become valid if I live in Azzio and work in Ticino?
AVS/AI/IPG contributions start from the day you start working in Ticino at 5.3% as an employee. For the LPP (second pillar), enrollment takes place if the annual salary exceeds CHF 22,620. Payments are made by the Ticino employer and registered with the Swiss social security institutions. Swiss social security is managed by bodies other than INPS: AVS for the base, pension funds for the second pillar.
If I change my residence from Azzio to another Italian municipality, what happens to the G permit?
If you change your residence from Azzio to another Italian municipality, you must notify the SEM and the local Migration Office of the transfer. The G permit remains valid if you continue to work in Ticino, but the Italian residence must always be proven and consistent with your personal data. If you move to Switzerland, the G permit expires and you must apply for a B permit. Also notify the Italian Revenue Agency of the change.
