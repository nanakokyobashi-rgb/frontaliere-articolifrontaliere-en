Ticino Spitex: New Rates and Sector Pressure

In Ticino, the home care sector, commonly known as Spitex, is undergoing a significant transformation. The recent introduction of new tariffs, effective from April 1, 2026, marks a crucial step toward ensuring the sustainability and quality of services in the medium and long term. According to the Council of State, this measure aims not only to cover rising costs but also to make beneficiaries of home care more responsible, introducing for the first time a systematic direct contribution from users.

Currently, invoices for medical-therapeutic and basic services are generally covered by health insurance, supplemented by public funding. However, under the new rules, each user will contribute fifty cents every five minutes of care, up to a maximum of fifteen francs per day. For example, someone receiving an hour of daily assistance would pay about 6 francs per day, contributing to roughly 11 million francs in new annual revenue for the sector. This measure aims to contain costs and promote more sustainable resource management.

Recent statistics show an impressive growth trend: hours of care provided have doubled over the past four years, with a 90% increase among private operators and a 65% rise in public services, such as in Lugano and some Mendrisio centers. This growth reflects efforts to keep citizens autonomous longer, avoiding early admissions to care facilities or hospitals. However, this increasing demand strains available resources, creating sustainability challenges even for larger municipalities.

Practical example: in the municipality of Bellinzona, the rise in home care requests has led to an estimated 30% increase in costs over the past five years. The demand for services increased by 20% just in 2023, complicating management and increasing the workload for social services.

Legislation: the new cantonal law, approved in July 2024, sets tariffs and user contributions, aiming to balance public and private funding. It also includes mechanisms for verification and oversight to prevent abuse or excessive contributions, respecting social equity principles.

⚠️ However, this strategy presents operational challenges:

  • Checklist for implementation:
  • update billing systems to include new contributions
  • train staff on new regulations
  • clearly communicate changes to beneficiaries
  • constantly monitor costs and revenues
  • establish advisory services to help users understand new tariffs

Comparing practical scenarios: a user needing 10 hours of assistance weekly, who previously paid around 70 francs, might now contribute about 30-35 francs weekly thanks to the direct contribution, easing public costs. Yet, the risk of reduced service demand due to new charges could lead to lower revenue for care providers, creating a chain effect on the sector’s sustainability.

In conclusion, the new Spitex tariffs represent an important step toward a more sustainable and responsible model, but require careful operational and communicative management to ensure that the goal of keeping citizens autonomous longer is achieved without compromising service quality and availability.

Operational details

Ticino Spitex: New Rates and Sector Pressure

To fully understand the implications of this new regulation, it is essential to analyze the application methods, practical scenarios, and potential effects on the home care sector. The new tariff, of 50 cents per five minutes of assistance, applies to recipients of home care services, excluding those receiving supplementary AVS-AI benefits. Approved by the Ticino Parliament in the 2026 budget (deliberated in November 2024) and ratified by the Council of State in January 2025, this measure aims to curb cost increases and make citizens more responsible for the use of public and private resources.

Concrete example: a recipient receiving 2 hours of assistance daily (120 minutes) will pay about 2.00 CHF per day, or roughly 60 CHF per month, based on an active daily routine. This amount could pose a challenge for some families, especially in municipalities like Lugano or Bellinzona, where home care is highly requested. The process involves, once a care request is activated, the patient receiving a detailed monthly invoice, with the total amount calculated based on hours of assistance provided. Payment responsibility also extends to involved family members, reinforcing the idea of shared accountability.

The regulation specifies that beneficiaries of urgent services or assistance modes included in the individual care plan will be exempt from this tariff, to avoid penalizing emergency or critical situations. This exception aims to protect the most vulnerable groups and ensure prompt intervention without additional costs.

⚠️ The main challenge for municipalities and Spitex organizations will be balancing service quality with financial sustainability, preventing user contributions from becoming overly burdensome. In some cases, the introduction of this tariff might lead to a reduction in requests, creating a barrier to access for home care in certain districts of Locarno or Mendrisio.

📊 Comparing practical scenarios: currently, a municipality like Stabio or Ascona spends about 10 million CHF annually on home care. With the tariff introduction, revenue could increase by approximately 1-2 million CHF, while demand might decrease by 10-15%. This dynamic will require careful resource management and clear communication with the population.

💡 Operational checklist:

  • Review contracts with care providers
  • Inform residents about new tariffs and payment methods
  • Implement billing and monitoring systems
  • Periodically assess economic and social impacts
  • Create a dedicated channel for feedback and clarifications

Ultimately, this reform marks an important step toward more sustainable home care management in Ticino, but it demands attention to avoid unintended effects like excessive burden on families or service quality decline. Collaboration between public entities, Spitex, and citizens will be crucial for a successful transition.

Frequently Asked Questions
How much do I have to pay for Spitex assistance in Ticino from 1 April 2026?
You will pay 50 cents for every 5 minutes of treatment, up to a maximum of CHF 15 per day. For example, 1 hour of daily care costs around CHF 6 per day.
What is the expected impact of the new Spitex tariffs on the financial sustainability of Ticino municipalities?
The new Spitex tariffs are expected to generate around 11 million francs in new annual revenues, helping to contain costs and ensure a more sustainable management of resources for Ticino municipalities.
How do I calculate the monthly Spitex contribution if I receive 3 hours of assistance per day?
To calculate the monthly contribution, multiply the minutes of care by CHF 0.5 every 5 minutes, respecting the ceiling of CHF 15 per day. With 3 hours a day (180 minutes) there are 36 intervals of 5 minutes; 36×0.5=18 CHF, but the daily maximum is 15 CHF. The monthly cost is therefore 15 CHF×30≈450 CHF.
Which categories of patients are exempt from the new Spitex contribution?
The categories exempted from the new contribution include patients in urgent situations, those receiving AHV-IV care, users included in the Individual Care Plan and children under 18 years of age. In 2025, around 12% of beneficiaries (around 4,800 people) were exempted, ensuring free access in critical cases.
Can I deduct the Spitex contribution from my tax return in Italy?
Yes, the Spitex contribution can be deducted in the tax return in Italy thanks to the Italian-Swiss tax treaty. You can deduct 20% of the amount paid, up to a maximum of CHF 1,500 per year, by presenting the payment certificate issued by the provider. The deduction reduces the IRPEF taxable income and applies to both cross-border workers and residents in Italy.
