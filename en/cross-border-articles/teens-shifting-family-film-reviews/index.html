The Locarno Film Festival brings stories to the big screen that often escape the mainstream circuit. Thisyear, among the proposals reported by the editorial staff of La Regione, two titles stand out that address the world of youth from very different angles: 'Los días libres' by director Lucila Mariani and 'Ego Reach We All' by Ghanaian Amartei Armar.

The first, 'Los días libres', has been called "interesting but a bit disappointing". The film seems to want to tell something profound related to adolescence, but something is not completely convincing. The second, 'Ego Reach We All', is instead described as "successful", an adjective that suggests a more complete surrender.

Two looks at adolescence

Both films focus on teenagers struggling with complex family dynamics. On the one hand, the search for identity and spaces of their own — the so-called 'shifting', a term that refers to a youthful phenomenon linked to reality

Operational details

Beyond the review: what to look for in these films

For those who live on the border between Italy and Switzerland, the Locarno Film Festival is a unique opportunity to approach a different cinema, far from multiplex cinemas. But beyond the judgment of merit, these two titles offer interesting insights into issues that also affect the families of border workers.

The theme of adolescence and the relationship with parents is universal. For those who work across borders, with long hours and daily commutes through crossings such as Brogeda or Gaggiolo, managing family time becomes a daily challenge. Films like 'Los días libres' and 'Ego Reach We Alla' can be a starting point to reflect on how children experience distance and the presence of parents.

An opportunity for cultural comparison

The Locarno festival is not only cinema: it is also a moment of encounter between different cultures. The presence of a Ghanaian director next to an Argentine director testifies to the international vocation of the event. For border crossers who cross the border every day, participating in events like this can be a way to integrate working life with cultural life, discovering a Ticino that goes beyond offices and factories.

Key points

How to experience the Locarno Film Festival from the border

Participating in the festival does not require special formalities for those residing in Italy and working in Switzerland. Here are some practical tips for planning a smooth movie night.

1. Check the screening times : the program is busy and the evening screenings may end late. Check the schedules of trains returning to Italy, especially on weekdays. 2. Parking: If you arrive by car, consider interchange car parks and less-trafficked crossings such as Gaggiolo or Ponte Tresa, especially on weekends. 3. Tickets: Buy in advance online to avoid files, especially for the most anticipated movies.

For those coming from afar, a good solution is to combine the day at the festival with a visit to the city. Locarno offers much more than the cinema: the lakefront, the castle and the surroundings are worth a walk.
