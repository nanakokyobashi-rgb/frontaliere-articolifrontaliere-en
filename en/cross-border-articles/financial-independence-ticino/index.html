LUGANO – The FIRE (Financial Independence, Retire Early) movement has attracted attention in the Canton of Ticino, especially among those who work as cross-border workers and try to optimize savings and investments to anticipate retirement. Jeffrey Hochegger, investment strategist at Raiffeisen Switzerland, offered a banking perspective on this financial philosophy.

According to Hochegger, determination to remain calm during market swings and consistency in investing are key to long-term success. The FIRE movement values ​​the time it takes for money to grow through compound interest. However, he stresses that the choice to adopt this lifestyle is very personal.

The current context is characterized by uncertainties: the war in Iran has fueled inflation and interest rates are rising, factors that represent risks for stock markets. The United States maintains an uncertain trade policy with tariffs further impacting the economic environment. Despite this, some technology sectors are recording record values, driven by enthusiasm for artificial intelligence. This has led to many markets becoming expensive.

Advice for investors

Hochegger suggests that those starting now spread out their investments over time, avoiding investing everything immediately. Those who are already invested should consider reducing positions slightly to maintain liquidity, useful in the event of market corrections. However, exiting the market completely is not recommended, as history shows that it is more important to stay invested, even during crises such as the tech bubble, financial crisis, pandemic or war in Ukraine.

As for saving, the FIRE theory proposes high savings, sometimes exceeding 50% of income. Hochegger observes that it is not necessary to set a precise percentage, but to understand that the amount of savings depends on the willingness to give up certain expenses. Analyzing personal expenses in detail allows you to identify possible savings.

In the Ticino and cross-border context, savings can be considered on living expenses, such as housing, transport (car vs public transport), KVG health insurance and holidays.

Raiffeisen Bank offers interested clients suitable investment products, such as investment funds, asset management mandates and fund savings plans, which enable the regular and automated purchase of units to reduce the impact of price fluctuations.

Implications for cross-border workers

The context of double taxation and the recent signing of the New Cross-Border Agreement between Italy and Switzerland make it important to plan pension savings well, considering AVS and LPP, in addition to KVG health insurance. The high savings suggested by the FIRE theory may be more difficult to achieve for cross-border commuters, but the strategy of investing calmly and consistently is also well suited to those who have to manage currency exchanges between the euro and the Swiss franc.

The comparison between living in the city or in the Ticino countryside, and the cost associated with transport or insurance, can significantly influence the ability to save. For example, those who live near Brogeda or Gaggiolo could consider using public transport to reduce costs related to cars and insurance.
