A smaller deficit

The Municipal Executive has given the green light to the 2027 Budget of the City of Lugano, which now goes to the City Council for review and approval. The accounts remain in the red, but the deficit is lower than in previous projections. The forecast points to a loss of 14,7 million francs, 4,8 million less than the 19,5 million included for the same year in the 2026-2033 Financial Plan.

The comparison with the previous multi-year projection makes the adjustment more evident: at that time, the estimated deficit was 30,1 million, thus 15,4 million higher than now forecast. For the Municipal Executive, this figure confirms the financial recovery path undertaken. It does not, however, close the matter: the Executive points out that further measures will be needed to bring the accounts back toward a lasting balance.

Budget items

As regards ordinary operations, the document sets out expenses of 510,8 million francs and revenue of 224,1 million. The amount to be covered through taxation is given as 286,7 million. Estimated tax revenue amounts to 221 million from individuals and 51 million from legal entities.

The Municipal Executive is keeping the two tax multipliers unchanged: 80% for individuals and 85% for legal entities. The choice is linked to the desire to continue rebalancing without increasing the tax burden through the multipliers and to preserve the city's attractiveness for residents and businesses.

The plan also provides for 97 million in net investment in 2027. The sum includes the deferred payment of part of the balance due for the purchase of PalaRaiffeisen. Self-financing is estimated at 16,6 million, while the overall result comes to negative 80,4 million. The same presentation therefore highlights the need to monitor the evolution of debt and the sustainability of investments.

The 2026 update

The Municipal Executive also announced that the projected closing of financial year 2026 is expected to result in a surplus in current operations. The signal is positive, but it does not amount to a structural rebalancing of the city's finances, which will still require time and further measures. The Budget incorporates the recovery measures already decided by the Executive and requested by the City Council. It does not, however, include the measures needed to offset from 2028 the financial consequences of the popular votes held in 2025, according to current financial planning.
