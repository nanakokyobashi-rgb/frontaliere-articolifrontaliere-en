The Ticino construction sector is preparing to experience the summer holiday period, a time traditionally dedicated to the planning and closure of construction sites, but not without concerns. Nicola Bagnovini, director of the Swiss Company Impresari Costruttori (SSIC), has drawn up a balance sheet of the current situation involving the entire sector. Topics of focus include procurement management, difficulties in sourcing materials from the Middle East, and the urgent need to manage work during heat spikes. Continuity of investment remains a crucial node to ensure the stability of a sector that represents a fundamental pillar of the cantonal economy. Companies must reconcile contractual deadlines with the protection of the health of workers exposed to high temperatures, a challenge that is repeated every year with the arrival of the hot season. Operational details

The current economic situation requires a deep reflection on how to manage orders. For companies operating in Canton Ticino, the issue of material procurement is particularly sensitive. Supplies from distant geographical areas, such as the Middle East, expose the sector to risks of delay due to geopolitical or logistical tensions, which can directly affect the progress of work. It is in this context that the border worker, an essential component of the Ticino workforce, is operating. The management of workers during the summer season requires special attention to current regulations on health and safety in the workplace. Companies must ensure that breaks, hydration and protection from extreme heat are guaranteed, complying with the directives governing the employment of personnel. Compared to the past, investment planning needs to be even more accurate. Construction companies are called to a constant balancing exercise between compliance with fixed costs, including AVS social security contributions and labour costs, and the fluctuation of raw material prices. Construction entrepreneurs point out that maintaining investments is the key to not losing ground in a market, the Swiss one, which continues to demand high-quality infrastructure.

Key points

For those working in the construction sector in Ticino, the planning of summer activities must follow established procedures. The contractors, coordinated through the SSIC, invite companies to strictly adhere to the summer work safety plans. It is essential that every worker is informed about the emergency procedures in case of heat-related illness. The safety documentation must always be up-to-date and available on the site. Regarding the fiscal and pension management, companies must ensure the correct application of the payroll tax for employees, ensuring that payments to the competent authorities are regular. Transparency in the management of employment contracts remains an element of protection for the entire supply chain. The tools offered by the portal can help workers verify their position in terms of pay slip and contributions, facilitating the understanding of a complex system that requires precision. Collaboration with the management is the fundamental step to avoid disputes related to delays in deliveries; clear communication on the delivery times of materials is an integral part of professional site management.

Control and Planning Procedures.

The technical offices of the construction companies must constantly monitor the contractual deadlines, relating to suppliers to anticipate any criticalities in international supply chains. The long-term planning of investments, discussed by Nicola Bagnovini, must also find its reflection in the daily financial management of the companies. Frequently Asked Questions
What are the main concerns of the Ticino construction industry for the summer?
The main concerns relate to the management of construction sites during heatwaves, investment planning and difficulties in sourcing materials from the Middle East, which can affect delivery times.
Who does SSIC represent in the context of the construction industry?
SSIC is the Swiss Company Impresari Costruttori, which acts as a reference point for companies in the sector, dealing with the analysis of operational criticalities, such as procurement and risk management related to climatic and market conditions.
What does a construction company have to do to handle the work during the heatwave?
Companies must implement specific safety measures for workers, such as the management of working hours, hydration and heat protection, ensuring compliance with occupational safety directives.
