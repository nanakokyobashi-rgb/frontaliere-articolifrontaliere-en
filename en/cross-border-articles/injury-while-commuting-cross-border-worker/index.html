Travelling accident: border protection and reporting

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 22 August 2026

Find out how the LAINF (0.7-1.5%) covers accidents during the journey to work for border workers with a G permit between Ticino and Italy: complaint procedure, guarantees and rights.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Travelling accident covered by LAINF (0.7-1.5% employee contribution)
  • Border worker with G permit has coverage as a resident Swiss worker
  • Report within 48 hours to the employer: prerequisite for recognized protection
  • Ordinary route covered; travel is assessed on a case-by-case basis

Key facts

  • What: Accident during the usual commute
  • Who: Employee border worker (G permit, mandatory LAINF contributions)
  • Where: Journey between Italian residence and Swiss workplace
  • Coverage: 0.7-1.5% LAINF (to be paid by the employer)
  • Complaint: At the employer, sent to LAINF
  • Rights: Medical expenses, loss of earnings, disability annuity, survivors' allowance

A border worker who crosses the border between Ticino and Italy every morning to go to work in Switzerland is covered by the LAINF from the moment he gets into the car. The commuting accident — the accident that happened during the ordinary commute from home to work — is a daily reality that affects thousands of cross-border workers every year. The protection exists and is called the Federal Law on Accident Insurance, but knowing how it works and what procedure to follow is essential to assert rights before the insurer.

The LAINF does not distinguish between the worker who resides in Zurich and the one who resides in Como, as long as he owns a permesso G and takes that road every morning to reach the employer

Operational details

Ordinary journey and transfer: a distinction that counts

Most border crossers do not know that there is a line — thin but important — between the ordinary route (automatically covered) and the transfer (whose status is similar, but the assessment may vary). An ordinary journey is the usual route from home to work every day: from Como to Lugano, from Menaggio to Bellinzona, from Varese to Chiasso. A trip, on the other hand, is an extraordinary move: the employer sends the worker to a meeting in another Swiss city, or the worker stops at work later to attend a company event.

What changes? In general, both situations remain covered by the LAINF, but the implications may differ in the case of a complex claim. For example: a border worker who returns home at midnight after a business dinner is always entitled to cover (it is a trip recognised by the employer). However, if thatinjury happens while the worker is making a detour — they stop to run a personal errand before returning — the chain of causation may be harder to prove. It does not mean that the coverage falls, but the evaluation phase of the LAINF body could be more in-depth.

Key points

Claims Procedure: The Essential Steps

Filing a claim is not complicated, but it must follow a precise order. The cross-border worker will not handle most of this alone: it is the employer who manages communication with the insurance provider. However, knowing the steps helps prevent delays and ensures that nothing is lost.

Step 1 — Immediate notification (within 24–48 hours) The cross-border worker informs the employer (human resources manager, personnel administration, or company reporting line). Communicate basic details: time and location of the incident, type (road accident, workplace injury, other), witness names if present, insurance policy number if a vehicle is involved.

Step 2 — Medical examination The cross-border worker visits their trusted doctor or the emergency room if the injury is serious. The doctor issues a medical certificate containing clinical diagnosis, recommendations (rest, physiotherapy, follow-up) and expected duration of work absence. This document is crucial: the LAINF provider always requires it as the first attachment.

Step 3 — Transmission by the employer The employer sends the medical certificate and the incident report (LAINF standard form provided by the employer or downloadable) to the LAINF insurer within the legal timeframe. This step is the employer's sole responsibility; however, the cross-border worker may request written confirmation that the communication has been sent.

Step 4 — Evaluation by LAINF The insurer evaluates the notification and medical certificate. If everything is in order, the file is opened and coverage is recognized. If information is missing, the provider contacts the cross-border worker or employer for clarification. This phase typically lasts 5–15 days.

Step 5 — Payments and reporting Once recognized, the injury is covered. The LAINF provider will pay: medical expenses (hospital, specialists, medication, physiotherapy), lost wages if the worker is on leave, any disability or annuity benefits if the damage is permanent.

Essential documents to keep:

  • Original medical certificate
  • Emergency report (if available)
  • Written communication to the employer
  • Confirmation of receipt from the LAINF provider
  • Medical prognoses and follow-up reports

Frequently Asked Questions
If a border worker is injured on the way, does the LAINF cover medical expenses immediately?
The LAINF covers all authorized medical expenses (hospital, doctor, drugs, physiotherapy) up to the maximum provided. However, the recognition of coverage depends on the rapid transmission of the communication to the employer and the medical certificate to the LAINF body. Once the file is opened (usually within 5–15 days), refunds begin retroactively from the date of the incident.
Does the G permit allow me to receive the same treatment as a Swiss resident worker for the LAINF?
Yes. A border worker with a G permit is obliged to contribute to the LAINF (0.7-1.5% of the gross salary, borne by the employer) and has an identical right to coverage in the event of an accident, both on the road and in the workplace. There is no discrimination or exclusion based on Italian permit or residence.
How many days do I have to report the injury to the employer without risking coverage?
The notification must be made within 48 hours (best practice) or at most within a few days from the date of the accident. LAINF entities accept reports even slightly later if justified (e.g. hospital admission), but the delay could complicate the evaluation. Do not wait: report it to the employer immediately.
If I get injured while running a personal errand on the way, do I stay covered?
The LAINF cover protects the injury during the direct and usual journey. If the border worker stops for a significant personal commission, the causality with the work path can be evaluated more carefully. If the deviation is minor (a coffee, a short purchase), coverage usually remains recognised; the LAINF entity assesses it on a case-by-case basis.
If LAINF denies my coverage for an accident while on the road, can I appeal?
Yes. The border worker has the right to appeal the decision before the competent cantonal authorities (in Ticino: Department of Health). The appeal is free for employees. You can be represented by a lawyer or union. Many appeals against unjustified denials are upheld.
