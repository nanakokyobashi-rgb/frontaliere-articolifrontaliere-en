On 19 May 2019, the population of Ticino rejected, with 73% of votes against, a cantonal project which provided for traffic lights along the route between Cadenazzo and Quartino, instead of roundabouts. Seven years later, on August 29th, the first systems were installed in Cadenazzo.

The program concerns the Magadino Plan. Further installations are planned: by the end of 2027, the roundabouts between Cadenazzo and Quartino will be gradually replaced by traffic lights. The initiative was born from a study and a decision by FEDRO, the Federal Roads Office. In 2020, the road became federal property; until then it was cantonal.

Investment and objectives

The total expected expenditure amounts to approximately 40 million francs: eight million will be allocated to traffic lights and the remaining 32 million to the improvement of the entire road network. For the FEDRO, the objectives are to make traffic more fluid and increase safety on a road traveled by almost 30,000 vehicles a day, which has always been among the busiest in the Canton of Ticino.

The first phase, however, produced negative reactions. In September, in the weeks following the installation in Cadenazzo, numerous motorists reported queues and longer journey times. In some cases, they reported, the times doubled or tripled.

The traffic lights located in front of the Cadenazzo roundabout, the only one destined not to be eliminated, also remain at the center of the debate, as well as the requirement to turn right leaving via Stradonino, which connects Gudo to Cadenazzo. Patti Chiari's crew documented the complex maneuvers required by buses and trucks, which obstruct the flow of traffic.

The protests led Claudio Isabella, grand councilor of the Centre, and seven other parliamentarians from different parties to question the cantonal government. Simone Gianini, liberal national councilor, instead asked Bern for explanations. The Federal Council responded that it is premature to draw conclusions and that the effectiveness will be assessed after the conclusion of the work.

Operational details

What to measure before judging

For a cross-border commuter traveling the Cadenazzo-Quartino route, the practical question is whether the time lost in the first weeks represents the outcome of the project or a settling-in phase. The source describes both situations, but at different times. Confusing the September protest with the forecast for 2028 would make the picture simpler than it is.

The response of the Federal Council points in the same direction: it is still too early to assess the actual effectiveness of the entire project. Key points

How to follow the transition phase

Those who cross the Magadino Plain for work can read the timeline operationally, without anticipating the final outcome. The soundest procedure is to separate the section travelled, the date of the experience and the data used for comparison.

Four steps

1. Identify the route segment. Check whether your route includes Cadenazzo-Quartino, the Cadenazzo area or the via Stradonino exit, connecting Gudo and Cadenazzo. These are the points mentioned in Patti chiari's reconstruction.

2. Place the episode on the timeline. On August 29 last year, the first traffic lights were installed in Cadenazzo; in September, reports of queues and longer travel times emerged; at the beginning of October, USTRA reported stabilization. Separating these phases avoids improper comparisons.

3. Compare the time with the benchmark used by USTRA. The comparison published by the Federal Roads Office concerns the travel time for the route during the same period of the previous year. The forecast of around 30% instead concerns peak hours in 2028, when the project will be completed.

4. Keep the expected result distinct from the verified result. The replacement of the roundabouts will continue progressively through the end of 2027. Only after the works are completed, according to the Federal Council's response, will it be possible to assess the actual effectiveness of the project as a whole.

For those travelling by car, the observation must also include the configuration indicated in front of the Cadenazzo roundabout, the only one that will not be dismantled. If the route involves via Stradonino, the obligation to turn right must be taken into account; the program documented that buses and trucks may be forced into laborious maneuvers. However, this data does not justify automatically extending that pattern to every vehicle or every day.

This framework enables the cross-border commuter to read the project in phases: concrete experience, stabilization communicated by USTRA and final forecast. There is no need to choose today between promise and protest; one needs to know which data is being observed and what deadline it refers to. The 30% estimate remains tied to 2028, while assessments of effectiveness await the end of the works.
