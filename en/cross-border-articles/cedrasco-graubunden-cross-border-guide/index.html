Moving to Cedrasco and working in the Graubünden represents a concrete opportunity for those looking for a Swiss salary without abandoning their Italian residence. But the path is not automatic: it requires mastery of the rules governing cross-border work between Italy and Switzerland. The foundation is the G Permit, the authorization that allows an Italian citizen residing in Cedrasco to work in the Graubünden while maintaining residence in Italy. This is not a Swiss residence permit, but rather a specific authorization for work activity.

The legal basis is the Convention on double taxation between Italy and Switzerland, signed on 9 March 1976, integrated by the New Cross-Border Agreement applied from 1 January 2024 (Law 83/2023 of Italian ratification). The threshold of 10,000 euros is not a general exemption: art. 4 of Law 83/2023 limits it to the income referred to in the art. 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013. Old cross-border commuters remain taxed only in Switzerland (art. 9 of the 2020 Agreement).

G Permission: rights and features

The G permit is valid for five years with a contract lasting more than one year; with a shorter contract it lasts as long as the contract. Switzerland does not impose numerical limits on cross-border workers from EU countries, as long as you are an Italian citizen regularly employed by a company in Graubünden. The Swiss employer presents the application to SECO (State Secretariat for Economics), starting a simplified administrative process.

Registration with INPS (Italy) and with the competent AVS compensation fund (Switzerland) is mandatory to activate recognition and the right to social benefits in both countries. Once hired, you will receive an AVS membership number which the employer will use to withhold social security contributions from your pay slip.

Taxation: tax at source and refunds

Taxation follows the principle of territoriality: the withholding tax is withheld in Switzerland by the employer. There are no "double withholdings" (neither in Switzerland nor in Italy at the same time). On salaries up to CHF 148,200 per year, the withholding tax for a basic cross-border worker is estimated at approximately 4.5% (a percentage which may vary based on the canton of Graubünden and the municipality of residence in Cedrasco).

Italy avoids double taxation through the tax credit in the EC framework of the 730 declaration. This mechanism allows you to recover the refunds due - tax credits paid in Switzerland which the Revenue Agency recognizes as a refund. The annual declaration is fundamental: a CAF or accountant expert in cross-border workers guarantees the correct compilation and maximization of recoverable credits.

Operational details

AVS and Swiss social security contributions

Unlike pure Swiss employees, cross-border workers registered with the AVS pay contributions based on their gross Swiss salary. The AVS/AI/IPG contribution is equal to 5.3% paid by the employee, plus 1.1% to 1.5% for accident insurance (LAINF) depending on the sector of employment. These contributions are paid directly from your paycheck, together with withholding tax.

A crucial aspect: the cross-border worker who works in Graubünden is not obliged to subscribe to the second Swiss pillar (LPP, professional pension insurance) if he resides in Italy. The LPP covers an age range between 25 and 64 with rates ranging from 7% to 18% based on age and income level. If your contract in Graubünden includes LPP, evaluate together with your employer whether it is worth maintaining it or obtaining an exemption as a non-Swiss resident. The Italian INPS continues to manage a minimum fraction of the contribution for "different incomes" or roundings not covered by AVS.

KVG choice and right of option

As a cross-border commuter with G Permit, you have the right to opt for health insurance: you remain covered by the Italian system (CMI, Carta Nazionale Servizi) or you switch to LAMal svizzero. KVG offers franchises between CHF 300 and CHF 2,500 per year for adults. The choice depends on personal needs: if you work in Graubünden, it may be convenient for KVG for convenience at Swiss healthcare facilities, but it remains an optional decision as long as you reside in Italy.

Registered cross-border workers retain full freedom of choice between the two systems. If you opt for KVG, the communication must take place within the established timeframe (typically 30 days from the first employment in Switzerland). If you remain in CMI, you continue to enjoy Italian coverage for both outpatient and hospital services, albeit in Italian facilities.

Costs of living: Cedrasco vs Graubünden

Living in Cedrasco means benefiting from rents and property prices that are generally low compared to Swiss border municipalities. Border Lombardy (Valchiavenna, where Cedrasco is located) offers housing costs compatible with a less expensive lifestyle than the Ticino or Graubünden area. However, daily travel to Graubünden impacts fuel, car maintenance and commuting logistics. Evaluating travel times and transport costs is fundamental in the overall economic evaluation of the choice.

Key points

Step-by-step process for moving

If you decide to live in Cedrasco and work in Graubünden, follow this administrative procedure:

Step 1 — Check the job posting. Make sure you have a concrete job offer from a company registered with the Graubünden cantonal authorities. The employer will manage the G Permit request at SECO and provide the necessary documentation (contract, duties, duration).

Step 2 — Request for the G Permit. The Swiss employer submits the application to the competent authorities (SECO, Secretariat of State for Economic Affairs). This step is a prerequisite for starting work. Average approval times are 2-4 weeks. Once approved, the permit is permanent as long as you maintain employment with the same employer.

Step 3 — INPS Italy registration. Register with INPS as a cross-border worker. Provide documentation: copy of Swiss employment contract, certificate of Italian residence in Cedrasco, personal data declaration. The threshold of 10,000 euros is not a general exemption: art. 4 of Law 83/2023 limits it to the income referred to in the art. 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013.

Step 4 — AVS membership number. Once employed in Graubünden, you will receive an AVS membership number from the relevant Swiss compensation office. Communicate this number to your employer so that they can correctly manage social security withholdings (5.3% AVS/AI/IPG LAINF) from your pay slip.

Step 5 — Choice KVG or CMI remaining. Decide whether to remain covered by Italian healthcare (CMI) or switch to KVG. Communicate your choice to the insurer within the required timeframe (30 days from first employment, usually). If you don't communicate, you automatically stay in CMI.

Step 6 — Annual tax return. At the end of the calendar year, fill out the Italian 730 including the CE table (tax credits for taxes paid in Switzerland). A CAF or accountant expert in cross-border transport will guide you in correctly completing and claiming refunds.

Frequently Asked Questions
What permission do I need to work in the Graubünden from Cedrasco?
The G Permit, a permanent authorisation for border workers that allows cross-border work while maintaining Italian residence. It is not a Swiss residence permit. The Swiss employer requests it from SECO and it is free of charge. Once approved, it does not require annual renewals as long as it remains in the same employment.
How does the taxation of the Swiss salary received by Cedrasco work?
The tax at source is withheld in Switzerland by the employer (about 4.5% on salaries up to CHF 148'200). Italy avoids double taxation by means of a tax credit in the EC framework of 730. The €10,000 limit is not a general allowance: Article 4 of Italian Law No. 83/2023 applies it only to the income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law No. 147/2013.
I am a new border crossing registered after 17 July 2023. What tax benefits am I entitled to?
The €10,000 limit is not a general allowance: Article 4 of Italian Law No. 83/2023 applies it only to the income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law No. 147/2013. This facility is permanent and recognised by declaration 730 (EC framework).
Do I need to join the Swiss AVS if I work in Graubünden at Cedrasco?
Yes, automatic enrollment in the AVS (Old Age and Disability Insurance) is mandatory. You pay contributions at 5.3% on the basis of the Swiss gross salary. If the employer offers LPP (second pillar), consider whether to accept it or obtain exemption as a non-resident. INPS Italia integrates a minimum fraction; consult an accountant for the correct distribution.
Can I stay covered by Italian healthcare (CMI) or do I have to choose LAMal?
G border workers have the right of option: stay in Italian CMI or switch to Swiss LAMal. LAMal offers CHF 300-2,500 annual adult franchises. The choice remains optional if you reside in Italy; communicate the preference to the insurer within 30 days of starting work. If you don't communicate, you automatically stay in CMI.
