How to calculate Ticino source tax

By Marco Ferrari (editorial byline of the newsroom, AI-generated text) · Published on 22 August 2026 · Updated on 31 October 2026

Practical guide to the calculation of the tax at source for border workers Ticino-Italy: rates, 2024 deductibles, tax credit in 730 and interactive simulator.

  • When: Effective from 1 January 2024 (New Cross-border Workers Agreement)
  • Where: Switzerland (payroll deduction); Italy (credit in 730)
  • Who: Cross-border workers with G Permit (classified as old or new from 17 July 2023)

  • Basic agreement: Italy–Switzerland of 9 March 1976

The New Cross-Border Agreement, signed on 23 December 2020 and applied from 1 January 2024, has redesigned the withholding tax rules for those who cross the borders between Ticino and Italy on a daily basis. For cross-border workers who work in Switzerland and reside in Italy, the withholding tax is withheld ONLY by the Swiss employer. It does not happen, contrary to what is sometimes believed, in both countries.

The Convention between Italy and Switzerland, signed on 9 March 1976, provides that Switzerland has the exclusive right to tax the income from employment of those who reside in Italy but work on Swiss territory. Italy, in turn, avoids double taxation by granting a tax credit in the 730 form (CE framework), where the cross-border worker recovers the amount already paid to the Swiss source.

From 1 January 2024, the new regime introduced differentiated deductibles. Old cross-border commuters remain taxed only in Switzerland (art. 9 of the 2020 Agreement). The threshold of 10,000 euros is not a general exemption: art. 4 of Law 83/2023 limits it to the income referred to in the art. 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013. This distinction reflects the transitional methods desired by Italy through Law 83 of 13 June 2023, the act of ratification of the Agreement, to protect those who were already operating under this tax regime.

The Swiss withholding tax is not a real tax in the traditional sense, but rather an advance withholding tax on the gross salary. The Swiss employer carries out the withholding according to the federal (AVS/AI/IPG), cantonal and municipal rates in force, which vary by canton. Ticino has its own cantonal rates. The cross-border worker thus receives his net salary in Switzerland and, at the end of the year, declares his income to the Italian Revenue Agency, benefiting from the tax credit for the amount already paid.

The role of the Italian tax credit

When the cross-border worker fills out the Italian form 730, he declares the foreign gross income (that from Swiss work) in the EC framework. The Revenue Agency recognizes the credit for Swiss taxes already paid, calculated on the basis of the Italian IRPEF (23% up to €28,000, 33% between €28,001 and €50,000, 43% over €50,000). If the Swiss withholding tax is greater than the IRPEF due to Italy, the cross-border worker obtains a refund; if it is less, pay the difference.

Operational details

How to calculate: Swiss rates and contributions

Withholding tax in Switzerland is not a single tax, but rather an aggregate of contributions and taxes paid by the employee on the pay slip. The current federal rates are:

  • AVS/AI/IPG (social security, disability insurance): 5.3% of gross salary
  • AD/AC (unemployment insurance and skills accumulation): 1.1% up to a maximum income of CHF 148'200
  • LAINF (accident insurance): 0.7–1.5% depending on the sector of work
  • LPP (company pension fund): 7–18% depending on age group (25 years and over)

All of these payments constitute the withholding tax that appears on the cross-border worker's Swiss pay slip. The calculation is crude: the gross salary is reduced by these contributions before any cantonal or municipal tax deduction. Switzerland, in fact, uses an advance withholding system which extracts the contributions directly and leaves the subject with the net of any further cantonal tax debts.

Comparison scenario: before vs after the 2024 New Agreement

The threshold of 10,000 euros of the art. 4 of Law 83/2023 concerns exclusively income from employment carried out abroad, in a border area or in a neighboring country, continuously and exclusively, by people resident in Italy, referred to in the art. 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013; it is not a general exemption nor an exemption from Swiss withholding tax. The regime of the new Agreement applies from 1 January 2024. A cross-border worker paid withholding tax on his entire income, with very few exceptions.

The threshold of 10,000 euros is not a general exemption: art. 4 of Law 83/2023 limits it to the income referred to in the art. 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013. Theoretically, this change reduces the immediate tax burden on your paycheck. However, the Italian Revenue Agency still calculates the full gross income (without exemption), and the tax credit is calculated on the part actually withheld in Switzerland. The difference between the old and new regime therefore manifests itself during the annual declaration in 730, where the cross-border worker can benefit from a more generous reimbursement if the Swiss exemptions have limited the withholding compared to the Italian IRPEF.

The issue of double taxation

Although the 1976 Convention grants the right of taxation to Switzerland, Italy does not completely renounce it. If the Swiss income exceeds the Italian tax levels (IRPEF), Italy applies the full local rate and the cross-border worker recovers via 730 the lower of the two amounts (actual Swiss tax versus IRPEF due). This mechanism does not completely resolve double taxation in all income brackets, particularly for high incomes where the IRPEF may exceed the Swiss withholding tax and the credit is not sufficient to break even.

Key points

Declaration procedure and deadlines

Every fiscal year, the cross-border commuter is obliged to declare Swiss earned income to the Italian Revenue Agency. The steps are simple:

1. Request the salary certificate (Jahreslohnausweis or equivalent documentation) from the Swiss employer which shows gross, withholdings, contributions and any applicable deductible. 2. Fill in the Italian form 730 in the section dedicated to foreign income (CE framework). Report your annual gross income and the amount of foreign taxes withheld. 3. Communicate the data when submitting the 730 at the CAF/Italian patronage or via an online agency. 4. The Revenue Agency recognizes the tax credit and calculates the refund or payment due.

The deadline for filing form 730 is April 30 of the year following the income period. For example, 2025 Swiss earned income must be declared by 30 April 2026. The Swiss salary certificate is the main supporting documentation; keep it in case of checks by the Agency.

Consultations and support

If the calculation in the 730 is complex or the reimbursement does not correspond to expectations, the cross-border worker has several options:

  • Contact the relevant Italian CAF/patronage specialized in foreign income;
  • Check with the Swiss employer the correctness of the withholdings applied on the pay slip;
  • Contact an accountant specialized in CH–IT cross-border taxation;
  • Consult the help desk of the Italian Revenue Agency for doubts about the tax credit.

Don't underestimate the importance of keeping all Swiss documentation (salary certificate, pay slips) for at least five years: in the event of an Italian tax audit, these documents are proof of the Swiss withholding tax and your right to the tax credit.

Frequently Asked Questions
Which income does the €10,000 limit apply to?
Article 9’s transitional regime applies only to workers who meet the Agreement’s definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between 31 December 2018 and 17 July 2023; covered employment income is taxable exclusively in Switzerland. The ordinary regime applies in other cases, with Italian taxation and credit for Swiss tax where applicable. The €10,000 in Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is only the limit for income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law 147/2013, not a general allowance. FTA factsheet · Agreement, Article 9 · Normattiva, Article 4.
How do I recover the source tax paid in Switzerland?
Using the Italian form 730, filling in the CE (Income from foreign sources) table with the Swiss gross income and the amount of the withholding tax actually paid. The Revenue Agency compares the Swiss withholding tax with the personal income tax due (23–43% in stages) and grants credit for the lower amount. If the withholding tax is higher, you receive a refund; if lower, you pay the difference.
What source tax rates apply in 2024-2025?
The current rates are: AVS/AI/IPG 5.3% (employee contribution), AD/AC 1.1% up to CHF 148'200, LAINF 0.7-1.5% (by sector), LPP 7–18% by age group (from 25 years). These federal and cantonal rates apply on the paycheck. Check with the Swiss employer the total withheld, as Ticino cantonal rates may vary.
When do I have to file the 730 declaration as a border worker?
By 30 April of the year following the income period. For example, 2025 income must be declared by April 30, 2026. Use the Swiss salary certificate (Jahreslohnausweis) as supporting documentation. Keep the documents for at least five years in case of control.
What is the difference between Swiss withholding tax and Italian personal income tax?
Swiss withholding tax is paid ONLY to Switzerland and is an aggregate of social security contributions (AVS, AD, LPP, etc.). Italian IRPEF is a progressive tax (23% up to €28,000, 33% up to €50,000, 43% more) applied by Italy. In 730, the tax credit recognizes the smaller of the two amounts. If Switzerland withholds more than the IRPEF, you receive reimbursement; otherwise you pay the difference.
