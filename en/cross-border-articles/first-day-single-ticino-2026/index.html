The first day of work in Ticino as a single border worker involves a series of administrative obligations that must be managed before the actual start of the tasks. Unlike an employee in Italy, the border worker operates according to Swiss employment, social security and tax laws — a system that requires specific document preparation to avoid delays or administrative errors that could compromise salary accreditation or social security coverage.

Operational details

Withholding tax: Swiss treatment and Italian tax credit

Unlike an Italian employee who pays the national progressive tax through the employer or the Revenue Agency, the cross-border worker in Switzerland is subject to the withholding of the Ticino federal and cantonal tax directly on the gross salary every month. This does not involve double taxation: the bilateral Italy-Switzerland system, based on the Convention of 9 March 1976, provides that income from work is taxed in the Canton of work (Ticino), but the cross-border worker receives a tax credit from the Italian tax authorities in the annual tax return (CE framework of the 730 form). Your net salary in Switzerland is therefore the basis for the calculation; in Italy, the declaration will not generate an additional tax, but rather a possible refund if the cantonal rate is lower than the Italian marginal rate.

Mandatory social security: AVS, LPP and additional contributions

From the first day of work, the employer and the cross-border worker jointly pay the following contributions:

  • AVS/AI/IPG (Old age insurance, disability and survivors' benefits): 5.3% of the gross amount paid by the employee. This begins to build the right to an old age pension (currently at 65 for men, with adjustments underway) and also covers disability and survivors' pensions.
  • AD/AC contributions (unemployment/unemployment assistance): 1.1% gross, with a maximum cap of CHF 148,200 of annual income. There is no need to do job searches like in Italy; it is an automatic contribution from the first month.
  • LAINF (Accident insurance): 0.7–1.5% of the gross depending on the sector (construction, offices, manufacturing). It covers accidents while commuting (journey) and at work, without a deductible.
  • LPP (Pension Insurance, second pillar): percentage between 7–18% depending on the age of the worker (from 25 years onwards). It is not a fixed national rate like in Italy; the rate varies by age group and company pension fund (if any). This fund is complementary to the AVS and is very important for your future pension.

Comparison of scenarios: new border crossings vs transitional regime (from 2024)

The situation changes depending on when you became a cross-border worker. The New Cross-border Workers Agreement, signed on 23 December 2020 and applied from 1 January 2024 (with Italian ratification through Law 83 of 13 June 2023), introduces differentiated tax refunds:

Old cross-border commuters remain taxed only in Switzerland (art. 9 of the 2020 Agreement). This means that out of 36,000 euros gross per year from work in Ticino, in Italy you declare 36,000 − 7,500 = 28,500 euros, with an IRPEF rate only on this figure (23% up to €28,000). The procedure: enter the amount in the EC part of the 730 declaration and communicate the foreign income via the Revenue Agency.

The threshold of 10,000 euros is not a general exemption: art. 4 of Law 83/2023 limits it to the income referred to in the art. 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013. In practice, if you earn 36,000 euros gross in Ticino, you declare 36,000 − 10,000 = 26,000 in Italy, applying the IRPEF rate only on this basis.

In both cases, the federal and cantonal Ticino tax has already been withheld at source; the Italian tax credit compensates for any difference if the Swiss rate is lower than the marginal Italian rate.

Key points

Checklist operativa: step-by-step from the first day

Step 1 — Verify permit and entry documents Present yourself to the employer with a valid passport or identity card. If the G permit has not yet been issued, bring the receipt of the SEM request. If it is the first day and you do not have the receipt, contact the SEM or the competent cantonal office (Lugano, Bellinzona, Mendrisio) the day before.

Step 2 — Fill out the anagraphic and fiscal forms The employer will provide: personal data form (complete address in Italy, phone number, beneficiary credited with salary), AVS form (for the assignment of the number if you do not have it), declaration for the calculation of the withholding tax (degree of recipient, affiliated church if applicable, personal deductions). Fill out and sign all the forms.

Step 3 — Authorization for salary crediting Provide the IBAN of your Italian (or Swiss if you maintain a Swiss account) bank account. The crediting usually occurs every 25–30 days from the beginning of the work month. Check with the HR office the exact date of the first salary.

Step 4 — Registration with LAMal by the end of the month The employer will notify the cantonal insurance agency (Lugano) of your hiring. By the end of the hiring month, you will receive the notification of registration with LAMal. Frontiersmen with a G permit have the right to opt: you can remain in the Italian health insurance system (CMI of your region) instead of switching to LAMal. If you choose to stay in Italy, notify LAMal in writing (within the deadline indicated in the notification) and show the employer the CMI coverage certificate. The LAMal adult franchisee ranges from CHF 300 to CHF 2500 (varying annually and by insurer); if you stay in CMI, you depend on the Italian franchises and coverage.

Step 5 — First payroll and verification of deductions Upon receipt of the first salary (usually within 30–45 days), check that the payroll includes: gross salary, AVS/AI/IPG 5.3%, AD/AC 1.1% (if applicable), LAINF 0.7–1.5%, LPP (7–18% depending on age), federal and cantonal withholding tax, net bank account. If you see unknown items or incorrect amounts, contact the payroll office on the same day.

Frequently Asked Questions
What documents should I bring on the first day of work in Ticino?
Valid passport or identity card, original G permit (or SEM request receipt if not yet issued), Italian tax code number, IBAN for salary accreditation, AVS number if available, LAMal certificate during registration or CMI documentation if you remain in the Italian system. Inform the employer if it requires other industry-specific documents (professional patent, medical certificate, etc.).
Who pays the tax at source on my frontier salary?
Tax at source (federal + cantonal Ticino) is withheld directly by the Swiss employer every month on your paycheck. There is no double taxation: the Italy-Switzerland system provides for an Italian tax credit. In the annual 730 return, the Revenue Agency compensates or returns the difference between the Swiss rate applied and the Italian IRPEF rate.
Do I need to sign up for LAMal or can I stay in the Italian healthcare system?
As a border crossing with a G permit, you have the right of option. If you do not notify LAMal by the end of the month of employment, you are automatically enrolled in Swiss health insurance. If you wish to remain in the Italian system (CMI), you waive LAMal in writing and show the employer the valid CMI coverage certificate. LAMal deductibles vary between CHF 300 and CHF 2500 per year depending on the insurer and the year.
What social security contributions are withheld from my paycheck?
AVS/AI/IPG: 5.3% gross. AD/AC: 1.1% gross (up to CHF 148,200 per annum). LAINF: 0.7–1.5% gross according to the sector. LPP: 7–18% gross according to age (from 25 years onwards). These payments start from day one and build pension, disability, unemployment and accident entitlements.
Which income does the €10,000 limit apply to?
Article 9’s transitional regime applies only to workers who meet the Agreement’s definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between 31 December 2018 and 17 July 2023; covered employment income is taxable exclusively in Switzerland. The ordinary regime applies in other cases, with Italian taxation and credit for Swiss tax where applicable. The €10,000 in Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is only the limit for income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law 147/2013, not a general allowance.
