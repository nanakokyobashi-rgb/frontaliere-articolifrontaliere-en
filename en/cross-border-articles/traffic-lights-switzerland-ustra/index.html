In 2025, Swiss motorways recorded 68.040 hours of congestion, about 20% more than the previous year. The figure accompanies USTRA's strategy to install more than 120 new traffic lights at motorway interchanges. The aim is to make traffic on the main arteries more regular, while accepting that the slowdown may shift to the cantonal and municipal road network.

For those arriving from the local network, the expected benefit on the main carriageway may therefore not coincide with a faster entry. This is the compromise factored into the strategy: preserving the main routes, even by shifting part of the waiting time.

The measure after the 2024 vote

The popular rejection in 2024 of the motorway expansion project prompted USTRA to seek alternatives that do not require new lanes. The central measure is «ramp metering»: traffic lights at access points regulate vehicles entering at intervals, with the aim of preventing sudden slowdowns and reducing the risk of accidents on the main carriageway.

In Switzerland, 15 installations of this type are already operational. The new traffic lights under consideration would be concentrated mainly in the large urban agglomerations of Zurich and Basel and in French-speaking Switzerland. According to USTRA's estimates, ramp metering would make it possible to increase interchange capacity by up to 5%. However, this is not yet a decision ready for implementation: the actual timelines for the 120 installations are not yet certain.

Where the congestion can shift

The practical issue for the cross-border commuter is not just the number of traffic lights, but the transfer of congestion. A more fluid main road can coexist with a longer queue before the entrance. This is the strategy's paradox: improving access capacity and, at the same time, slowing down the local network.

The Basel precedent

The discussion north of the Gotthard shows why the local impact is the most sensitive issue. In the Basel region alone, 19 installations are under review. Basel-Stadt and Schaffhausen claim they were excluded from the initial analyses. The Union of Swiss Cities, however, fears that traffic could spill into residential neighborhoods, posing risks to pedestrians and cyclists.

USTRA maintains that Cantons and Municipalities will be involved in defining the individual projects. This assurance does not yet amount to a timetable, but it indicates that the assessment must also look at the local network. The second measure examined by USTRA remains separate: on 24 motorway sections, the temporary conversion of the emergency lane into an additional driving lane during peak hours is being assessed. This too is an option under study, not an already widespread change.
