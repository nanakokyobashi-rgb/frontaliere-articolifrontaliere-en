Beko summit: Astuti warns of deep crisis

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 29 September 2026

The provincial table met on Monday, September 28 at Villa Recalcati on the Beko dispute in Cassinetta; Astuti (Pd) warns: "the commitments have not been respected, it is workers and territory that pay".

From the industrial plan to the provincial roundtable

The territorial political coordination roundtable on corporate crises in the province of Varese met on Monday 28 September at Villa Recalcati. The situation at the Beko plant in Cassinetta was once again at the center of the meeting, while the discussion focused on determining the status of the implementation of the agreement reached months ago at the ministerial roundtable.

The starting point is the industrial plan signed by the Turkish multinational to carry out a series of investments in Italy. According to the source's account, those investments are still nowhere to be seen. In recent days, the metalworkers' unions, Fiom, Fim and Uilm, have raised the issue of timing. The provincial meeting therefore served to take stock of the implementation of the agreement and the gap between the commitments made and what has been delivered.

Samuele Astuti, a regional councillor for the Pd who took part in the roundtable, argued that the commitments had not been honored and that the cost continues to fall on workers and the local area.

«What has emerged clearly is that the commitments made have not been honored and that, once again, workers and the local area are paying the price».

Astuti rejected explanations linked to the complexity of the situation, demand trends and sales. In his view, a multinational may get its sales forecasts or production plan wrong, but the price of these choices must not be borne by workers.

The issue does not end within the Cassinetta plant. The crisis concerns employees, the numerous companies in the supply chain and, more generally, the province's manufacturing system. Also involved are the social and business fabric of the Varese area, the families of the female and male workers, and the territory's productive and employment capacity.

Hence the appeal for a strong and united response, with institutions, trade unions and businesses at the same table. Astuti also called for a change of pace from Regione Lombardia, starting with reemployment programs, arguing for the need for a preventive approach to industrial crises. The indicated risk is that the loss of productive and employment capacity will push the province into a deep crisis.

Operational details

The crisis beyond the plant

For a cross-border worker, the connection remains territorial and employment-related: the text provides no references to permit G, AVS, LPP, LAMal, withholding tax, rebates, double taxation or Canton Ticino. The documented scope is the province of Varese, where the dispute is described as an issue capable of affecting the social and entrepreneurial fabric.

A second element concerns the distribution of responsibilities. Astuti does not consider explanations related to the complexity of the situation, demand and sales sufficient. Key points

From the news to monitoring

The operational path that emerges from the meeting is not an individual procedure, but a sequence of public checks. To follow the dispute without confusing announcements with results, the reader can focus on four key points.

Four key points to follow

1. Industrial plan. The first reference is the Turkish multinational's commitment to make investments in Italy. The verification concerns their actual visibility, because the source notes that they still cannot be seen.

2. Ministerial agreement. Fiom, Fim and Uilm have raised the issue of timing. The provincial roundtable has placed at the center the implementation of the agreement reached months ago at the ministerial roundtable: this is the step to follow to understand whether the commitments are moving forward.

3. Territorial response. The request does not concern a single party. Institutions, trade unions and companies are called upon to build a strong and united response, because the crisis affects the plant, the supply chain and the provincial manufacturing system.

4. Preventive redeployment. Astuti has asked Regione Lombardia for a change of pace, starting with redeployment paths capable of anticipating industrial crises. The indicated criterion is to intervene before the problems have already erupted.

This order makes it possible to keep distinct the elements that have already emerged: the promised investments, the ministerial agreement, the role of the trade unions and the response of the regional institutions. It also makes it possible to keep connected the parties indicated by the source: female and male workers, families, companies in the supply chain and the province's production system.
