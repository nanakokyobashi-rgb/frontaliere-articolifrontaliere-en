Swiss bank account for cross-border workers: fees and documents

Di Laura Bianchi · Published on 25 February 2026 · Updated on 11 October 2026

How to compare accounts, cards, CHF/EUR exchange and variable requirements for people working in Ticino and living in Italy, with official sources.

Context

At a glance

  • A CHF account with a Swiss bank can simplify salary payments and spending in Switzerland, but it is not a universal requirement for every cross-border worker.
  • There is no reliable average fee: the total depends on residence, the banking package, cards, paper documents, cash withdrawals and currency conversion.
  • Credit Suisse is no longer a separate bank: the company was removed from the register in 2024 and Swiss former Credit Suisse clients were migrated to UBS infrastructure in March 2026.

Key facts

  • Purpose: receive and use CHF, not replace tax or work-permit checks.
  • Area: Ticino and Italy, with different rules for each bank.
  • Published costs: UBS lists CHF 5 per month for a current account with electronic documents and CHF 9 with paper documents; UBS key4 FX lists subscriptions of CHF 2, CHF 5 or CHF 10 per month.
  • Other examples: PostFinance publishes CHF 25 per month per account for customers domiciled abroad; BancaStato describes the PRIMA account as an account for salary payments and everyday banking, available as part of a package.
  • Documents to prepare: valid identification, an employment contract or confirmation, address and tax residence; a bank may ask for a G permit, but that is not a universal banking rule.
  • Verification date: 11 October 2026.

Opening a Swiss account can be useful for someone working in Ticino and paid in Swiss francs. It keeps the CHF balance separate, makes local expenses easier to track and helps you decide when to transfer euros to Italy. It should not be presented as compulsory for everyone: whether an Italian account is accepted, what fees apply and which data are requested depend on the employer, the bank and the contract. Before signing, ask how the salary will be credited and who pays the cost of any international transfer.

There has been an important change concerning Credit Suisse. UBS states that Credit Suisse AG ceased to exist as a separate legal entity on 31 May 2024 and that UBS succeeded to its rights and obligations. On 18 March 2026 UBS also announced completion of the migration of Swiss-booked clients to its infrastructure. An up-to-date guide can therefore discuss UBS and other active institutions, but it must not list Credit Suisse as a separate bank available for new accounts.

Operational details

Which bank should you compare in Ticino?

The right question is not which bank is “the best” in every case, but which combination of account, card, exchange service and support fits your use. Official pages change, and a price for a Swiss resident does not automatically apply to someone living in Italy.

UBS

The UBS current-account page lists CHF 5 per month with electronic documents and CHF 9 with paper documents. These are prices for the product shown on that page, not a promise that a cross-border worker can open it online: UBS says that digital opening of a private account requires residence and taxation in Switzerland and directs people living abroad to a dedicated contact. The price sheet also says that the former UBS me banking package for cross-border commuters has not been actively offered since 31 March 2025. Do not rely on older guides that quote a fixed cross-border package price.

For currency transfers, UBS key4 FX lists three subscriptions: Basic at CHF 2 per month, Standard at CHF 5 and Premium at CHF 10. The service also gives a CHF 25,000 monthly limit and says that people who work in Switzerland but live abroad can use the cross-border offer. These are currency-service costs, not account fees: compare the exchange markup, euro SEPA transfers and any OUR charges.

BancaStato

The official PRIMA factsheet describes the account as a transaction account for salary payments and everyday operations. It lists cash withdrawals, cards and payments through InLinea, and states that the account can be opened in CHF and EUR as part of a package. The separate price list gives the relationship fees and exemptions. Do not turn an old advertisement into an “average cost” for every cross-border worker. Ask for the current package and card price for your Italian domicile.

PostFinance and other banks

PostFinance publishes a CHF 25 monthly fee for each private account when the customer is domiciled abroad. The same documentation says that the standard private CHF account requires adulthood and Swiss residence. These two points show why a price alone is not enough: first check whether the product is available to an Italian resident and under which conditions. Raiffeisen banks and other regional institutions may have their own procedures and price lists; ask the branch or customer service for a written answer.

Key points

Documents and procedure

1. Clarify the salary payment: ask the employer whether an Italian IBAN is accepted, whether a Swiss IBAN is preferred and whether the employer has special rules for salary payments. 2. Compare the exact product: separate account or package fee, debit or credit card, out-of-network withdrawals, euro transfers, CHF/EUR conversion, paper mail and the foreign-domicile fee. 3. Prepare the documents: valid identification, an employment contract or proof of work in Switzerland, domicile address, tax residence and, when available, the G permit. The bank may request more documents for identification, anti-money-laundering checks or source of funds. 4. Check the G permit separately: Ticino requires the application before work starts. For an employee whose employer is based in Ticino, the current procedure includes an identity-document check at a gendarmerie post and the certified copy must be attached to the application. For adult EU/EFTA citizens, the canton publishes CHF 75 for issue and renewal. These are immigration requirements, not a bank-account fee. 5. Check the exchange route: for euro payments, ask whether the transfer can use SEPA and which exchange rate applies. For frequent transfers, compare the total cost of account, conversion and credit to the Italian account instead of looking only at the monthly fee.

Frequently asked questions

Is a Swiss account mandatory? No. It is often convenient for CHF and Swiss expenses, but being a cross-border worker does not automatically create a general obligation. Check the employer instructions and the bank conditions.

Is the G permit enough to open an account? No. The G permit establishes a work and cross-border status, while the bank decides whether to accept the relationship and may request identity, contract, domicile, tax residence and further checks.

What cost should be budgeted? Do not use old price ranges as a market average. Use the product price sheet: the official examples above show that the cost can be an account fee, a package, a currency service or a foreign-domicile charge.

Frequently Asked Questions
Is a Swiss account mandatory for someone working in Ticino?
No. A Swiss account can simplify salary payments in CHF and local expenses, but being a cross-border worker does not by itself create a general obligation. Check the employer instructions and the bank conditions.
Is the G permit enough to open an account?
No. The G permit concerns work and cross-border residence status. The bank may also request identification, a contract, domicile, tax residence and source-of-funds checks under its own procedure.
How should I compare the costs?
Compare the account or package fee, cards, withdrawals, euro transfers, CHF/EUR conversion, paper documents and foreign-domicile charges. UBS and PostFinance publish examples for different products, not a market average.
