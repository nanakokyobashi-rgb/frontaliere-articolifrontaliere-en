The Ticino government is considering the possibility of blocking reimbursements to Italy, a move that could have significant repercussions for cross-border workers and local communities. The so-called 'health tax', introduced for cross-border workers who opt for the Italian health system instead of the Swiss one, is at the center of the debate. President Claudio Zali announced that the discussion will take place soon, by June, but the hypothesis of blocking reimbursements is seen as a threat, especially by those who oppose the contribution for older cross-border workers.

📊 Details and Impacts

The so-called health tax is the contribution to the Italian National Health Service set by Law 213/2023 (Art. 1, paragraphs 237-239): from 3% to 6% of the net salary earned in Switzerland, with a minimum of 30 and a maximum of 200 euros for each month worked, set each year by the region of residence. However, the measure has not been well received by politics across the border, with criticism from various political figures.

Regional councilor Silvana Snider of the Lega supports the contribution, stating that the tax has nothing to do with the tax agreement. On the contrary, the Lombard regional councilor of the Democratic Party, Samuele Astuti, criticizes the move, defining it as a violation of an international agreement. Astuti presented a motion that was rejected, asking the Lombardy Region to urge the Italian Government to repeal the health tax.
