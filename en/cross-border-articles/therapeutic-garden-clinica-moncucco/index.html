Operational details

Therapeutic integration and care environment

The introduction of the Intensive Care Garden at the Moncucco Clinic represents a significant evolution in the management of healthcare spaces. Unlike conventional facilities, this environment has been designed to promote sensory stimulation and relaxation, factors considered crucial for the recovery of critically ill patients. For healthcare professionals operating in the Canton of Ticino, the presence of such infrastructure changes the approach to care, allowing for the diversification of rehabilitation activities. The management of a patient in intensive care, which usually requires constant surveillance and complex technological monitoring, finds in this space an operational extension that does not compromise clinical safety. The planning of movements and the organization of healthcare staff shifts must harmonize with the use of this garden, ensuring that each outing is planned according to the medical needs of the patient. This approach requires specific training for the staff, so that the outdoor environment is used with the same precision and attention given to medical procedures within the clinic. The uniqueness of the project in the Swiss context places the Moncucco Clinic in a prominent position regarding the humanization of care, a theme increasingly debated among healthcare institutions in the Canton of Ticino. The availability of a therapeutic garden is not just an architectural element but a resource that requires careful logistical management, including the maintenance of spaces and the protection of patients during exposure to the open air. Procedures and management of therapeutic access

Access to the new Intensive Care Garden at Moncucco Clinic follows rigorous procedures, dictated by the medical protocols in force for the safety of patients. It is not a freely accessible space, but rather an area integrated into the care pathway, whose access is subject to the clinical evaluation of the responsible physician. To understand how healthcare facilities in the Canton of Ticino, such as Moncucco Clinic, manage the integration of such spaces, it is necessary to refer to the internal regulations that govern patient care. The healthcare personnel, who operate according to the directives of the Department of Health and Social Affairs, must ensure that each patient authorized to use the garden is constantly under monitoring. The planning of rehabilitation sessions in the garden is included in the individual care plan, taking into account the clinical conditions, the degree of mobility, and the risk of infection. Frequently Asked Questions
What does the Garden of Intense Care represent for Clinica Moncucco?
The Garden of Intense Care is a new therapeutic and rehabilitation space inaugurated by the Moncucco Clinic, unique in the Swiss healthcare landscape. This garden has been specifically designed to promote the care of patients, integrating natural elements into the rehabilitation process.
What are the benefits of the Intense Care Garden for patients?
The garden is designed to improve the psychophysical well-being and functional recovery of patients in need of prolonged and assisted care. It offers an environment that deviates from traditional hospital inpatient rooms, favouring sensory stimulation and relaxation.
How is access to the Intense Care Garden managed?
Access to the garden follows strict procedures, dictated by the medical protocols in force for the safety of patients. It is not a space for free use, but an area integrated into the care path, whose access is subject to the clinical evaluation of the doctor in charge.
