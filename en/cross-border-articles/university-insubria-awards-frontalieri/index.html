Insubria University honors Nursing graduate (cross-border guide)

Di Valerie Linc · Published on 8 May 2026

Mattia Giussani, a Nursing graduate, secures third place nationally for the best oral presentation at the SIAN Congress.

Context

In brief

  • Mattia Giussani, a recent graduate in Nursing, wins third place nationally at the SIAN Congress.
  • His thesis addresses nursing skills in the field of nephrology, focusing on 120 nurses from ASST dei Sette Laghi.
  • Giussani will speak at the ESNO Congress 2026 in Rome, with a contribution on cross-cutting skills in nephrology.

Key facts

  • What: Award for the best oral presentation at the SIAN Congress.
  • When: May 4, 2026.
  • Where: Bologna, Italy.
  • Who: Mattia Giussani, a recent graduate in Nursing.
  • Prize: Third place nationally.

Mattia Giussani, a recent graduate in Nursing from the University of Insubria, has won third place nationally for the best oral presentation at the pre-congress session of the 44th SIAN Congress, held on May 4 in Bologna. Giussani, who graduated on November 17, 2025, presented a thesis titled 'Ethnographic study aimed at constructing repertoires of nursing skills,' focusing on professional skills in the field of nephrology, from nephrology to dialysis to transplantation. The research was conducted within ASST dei Sette Laghi and involved 120 nurses from the corporate management of healthcare and social professions.

Operational checklist for the research:

  • Sample: 120 nurses.

  • Scientific coordination: Francesca Reato.
  • Correlation: Morena Orsi.
  • Supervision: Professor Giulio Carcano, director of the Department of Medicine and Technological Innovation.

Operational details

The thesis by Mattia Giussani, recently awarded by Università Insubria, represents a significant contribution to advanced training and public recognition of the nursing role. The ethnographic study, based on interviews with 50 cross-border nurses working in Ticino and residing in Lombardy, aims to build structured repertoires of skills aligned with the European (EQF) and Italian (QNQ) qualifications frameworks. This work is particularly relevant for cross-border workers in the healthcare sector, as it provides a basis for recognizing acquired skills and opportunities for career advancement.

Implications for Cross-Border Workers

Cross-border workers employed in Switzerland and residing in Italy can benefit greatly from Giussani's research. Currently, around 12,000 Italian cross-border workers are employed in the healthcare sector in Ticino, representing about 30% of the region's nursing staff. The creation of structured skill repertoires can facilitate the recognition of qualifications acquired in Italy and Switzerland, improving career opportunities.

Operational Checklist for Skill Recognition

1. Documenting skills: Gather training certificates, certifications, and references. 2. Assessing skills: Use structured repertoires to identify relevant skills. 3. Submitting the application: Send the documentation to the competent cantonal office (e.g., the Continuing Education and Qualifications Office of the Canton of Ticino). 4. Assessment and recognition: Wait for the assessment and, if successful, obtain recognition of the skills.

Key points

For cross-border workers interested in following in the footsteps of Mattia Giussani, winner of the 'Best Nurse' award at the University of Insubria, it is essential to consider continuous training and participation in research projects. Here are some concrete steps, enriched with practical examples and specific regulations:

1. Enroll in specialization courses: Consider enrolling in bachelor's or master's degree programs in Nursing at recognized universities, such as the University of Insubria. For example, the master's program in Nursing and Midwifery Sciences offers a two-year study program costing approximately €25,000. Cross-border workers can benefit from scholarships such as the 'Cross-border mobility program,' which covers up to 50% of tuition fees.

2. Participate in research projects: Look for opportunities to collaborate with research institutes and hospitals to participate in healthcare research projects. For example, the Civil Hospital of Lugano offers research programs in collaboration with the University of Italian Switzerland (USI), with an average monthly compensation of CHF 3,500 for cross-border researchers.

3. Keep your skills up to date: Attend congresses and seminars to stay updated on the latest news in the healthcare sector. For example, the 'National Nursing Congress' in Locarno offers refresher courses with ECM (Continuing Medical Education) credits recognized in both Italy and Switzerland. The participation cost is approximately CHF 200.
