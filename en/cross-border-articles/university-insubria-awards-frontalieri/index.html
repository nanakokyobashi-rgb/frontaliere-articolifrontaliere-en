Insubria University honors Nursing graduate (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 8 May 2026

Mattia Giussani, a Nursing graduate, secures third place nationally for the best oral presentation at the SIAN Congress.

In brief

Key facts

  • What: Award for the best oral presentation at the SIAN Congress.
  • When: May 4, 2026.
  • Where: Bologna, Italy.
  • Who: Mattia Giussani, a recent graduate in Nursing.
  • Prize: Third place nationally.

Mattia Giussani, a recent graduate in Nursing from the University of Insubria, has won third place nationally for the best oral presentation at the pre-congress session of the 44th SIAN Congress, held on May 4 in Bologna. Giussani, who graduated on November 17, 2025, presented a thesis titled 'Ethnographic study aimed at constructing repertoires of nursing skills,' focusing on professional skills in the field of nephrology, from nephrology to dialysis to transplantation. The research was conducted within ASST dei Sette Laghi and involved 120 nurses from the corporate management of healthcare and social professions.

Operational checklist for the research:

  • Sample: 120 nurses.
  • Methodology: Ethnographic study.
  • Field: Nephrology, dialysis, transplantation.
  • Scientific coordination: Francesca Reato.
  • Correlation: Morena Orsi.
  • Supervision: Professor Giulio Carcano, director of the Department of Medicine and Technological Innovation.

Practical comparisons:

  • Scenario 1: Nurses with experience in nephrology vs. those with experience in dialysis.
  • Scenario 2: Technical skills vs. relational skills.
  • Scenario 3: Theoretical training vs. practical training.

Giussani is already expected at the ESNO Congress 2026 — European Specialist Nurses Organisation — scheduled in Rome from June 4 to 6, where he will give an oral presentation to an international audience. Currently, Giussani works in a neurorehabilitation department for severe acquired brain injuries at the Mendrisio Hospital in Ticino, where he has developed specific skills in neurocognitive rehabilitation.

Relevant regulations:

  • Legislative Decree 42/2019: Defines nursing skills in Italy.

"A result that enhances the quality of nursing training at the University of Insubria and the contribution of its students to scientific research in the healthcare field" — Fabio Angeli, president of the degree course.

Operational details

The thesis by Mattia Giussani, recently awarded by Università Insubria, represents a significant contribution to advanced training and public recognition of the nursing role. The ethnographic study, based on interviews with 50 cross-border nurses working in Ticino and residing in Lombardy, aims to build structured repertoires of skills aligned with the European (EQF) and Italian (QNQ) qualifications frameworks. This work is particularly relevant for cross-border workers in the healthcare sector, as it provides a basis for recognizing acquired skills and opportunities for career advancement.

Implications for Cross-Border Workers

Cross-border workers employed in Switzerland and residing in Italy can benefit greatly from Giussani's research. Currently, around 12,000 Italian cross-border workers are employed in the healthcare sector in Ticino, representing about 30% of the region's nursing staff. The creation of structured skill repertoires can facilitate the recognition of qualifications acquired in Italy and Switzerland, improving career opportunities.

Regulations and Costs

According to the 2009 Lugano Convention, cross-border workers in Switzerland must have their qualifications recognized to access positions of greater responsibility. The recognition process can take up to 6 months and involve administrative costs of up to 500 CHF. However, with the adoption of structured repertoires, time and costs could be reduced by 30-40%.
