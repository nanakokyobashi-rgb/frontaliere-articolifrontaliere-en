Context

Your Complete Toolkit — Calculators and Comparators for Cross-Border Workers

Managing finances as a frontaliere means navigating two tax systems, two currencies, two healthcare frameworks, and two pension architectures — simultaneously. Making good decisions requires accurate data, not guesswork. This guide walks you through every tool available on Frontaliere Ticino, explains what each one does, and helps you use them in the right order for maximum impact.

Net Salary Calculator

The cornerstone of your financial planning. The salary calculator takes your gross Swiss salary and computes your net take-home pay after all mandatory deductions:

  • AVS (1st pillar): 5.3 % of gross salary — Switzerland's universal state pension contribution
  • AC (unemployment insurance): 1.1 % on salaries up to 148,200 CHF
  • LAINF (accident insurance): approximately 0.7 % — covers non-professional accidents
  • IJM (daily sickness allowance): approximately 0.8 % — provides 80 % salary during illness
  • LPP (2nd pillar): age-bracketed occupational pension contributions (7–18 % depending on age)
  • Source tax (imposta alla fonte): Ticino withholding tax calculated from official A/B/C/H tax tables based on your marital status, number of children, and salary bracket

For frontalieri under the 2026 New Agreement (start date after 17 July 2023), the calculator also models Italian IRPEF taxation with the 10,000 EUR exemption (franchigia) and the Swiss tax credit. For old-regime frontalieri (pre-July 2023), the calculator shows the tax-exempt scenario. This distinction is critical: the difference in net pay between old and new regimes can be several thousand euros per year at the same gross salary.

CHF-EUR Exchange Rate Comparator

Your Swiss salary arrives in francs, but most of your expenses are in euros. The exchange comparator shows live rates across 16 operators — banks, fintechs, and specialized FX services. The spread difference between the cheapest and most expensive provider is typically 1.5 % on any given day. On a 5,500 CHF monthly conversion, that 1.5 % means roughly 80 EUR per month — nearly 1,000 EUR per year — saved simply by choosing the right service.

LAMal Health Insurance Comparator

The LAMal comparator lets you compare premiums from 14 Swiss health insurers authorized to cover cross-border workers. Filter by insurance model (standard, family doctor, HMO, telmed), franchise level (300–2,500 CHF), and age group (child, young adult, adult). The premium difference between the cheapest and most expensive insurer for identical coverage can exceed 150 CHF per month — making this comparison one of the highest-ROI activities you can undertake as a frontaliere.

Financial Planning Tools — Cost of Living, Pensions, and Taxes

Cost of Living Comparator — Permit B vs Permit G

The most important strategic decision for any frontaliere is whether to live in Switzerland (Permit B) or commute from Italy (Permit G). The cost of living comparator models both scenarios side by side, incorporating housing costs, transport, health insurance, groceries, childcare, and taxes for your specific profile. It shows you the net disposable income under each option, helping you make this life-changing decision with data rather than assumptions.

Pension Planner — AVS, LPP, 3a, and INPS

Retirement planning for cross-border workers spans two countries and three Swiss pillars plus the Italian INPS system. The pension simulator projects your monthly retirement income from each source, factoring in contribution years, salary progression, and the bilateral Italy-Switzerland totalization agreement. It shows you whether you are on track for a comfortable retirement or whether you need to increase your voluntary contributions.

Pillar 3a Simulator

Switzerland's tax-advantaged third pillar is the most powerful voluntary savings tool available to frontalieri. The 3a calculator models your potential accumulation based on the 2026 maximum deductible of 7,258 CHF, your chosen investment strategy (savings account, balanced fund, equity fund), and your time horizon. It also calculates the annual tax savings from the deduction — often exceeding 1,000 CHF per year at marginal rates of 15 % or higher.

Tax Return Simulator

For new-regime frontalieri subject to Italian IRPEF, understanding your Italian tax return is essential. The tax return simulator walks you through the calculation: gross Swiss income converted at the official Agenzia delle Entrate exchange rate, minus the 10,000 EUR franchigia, through the IRPEF brackets, minus the tax credit for Swiss withholding tax already paid. The result shows your net Italian tax liability — often a surprise (positive or negative) for first-time filers.

Withholding Tax Rate Tables

Ticino's source tax rates depend on your tariff code (A for single, B for married single-earner, C for married dual-earner, H for single parent), salary, and number of children. The withholding rate lookup tool shows you the exact percentage applied to your salary bracket, so you can verify that your employer is withholding the correct amount. Errors are more common than you might think — especially when family status changes (marriage, new child) and the employer does not update the tariff code promptly.

Tax Calendar

Cross-border workers must track deadlines in both Switzerland and Italy. The tax calendar consolidates all critical dates: Italian tax return filing (modello 730 and Redditi PF), Swiss withholding tax correction requests, pillar 3a contribution deadlines, AVS statement requests, and more. Missing a deadline can mean penalties, lost deductions, or delayed refunds — the calendar ensures nothing slips through the cracks.

Career, Permits, and Border Tools

Job Board

The job board aggregates positions from 89 companies actively hiring in Ticino and the greater Swiss-Italian border region. Filter by sector (IT, finance, pharma, construction, hospitality, logistics), location (Lugano, Mendrisio, Bellinzona, Chiasso), and salary range. Each listing includes key details about the employer, contract type, and whether the position is suitable for cross-border workers. Whether you are entering the Swiss job market for the first time or considering a career change after years as a frontaliere, this is your starting point.

Permit B vs Permit G Comparison

The permit comparison tool provides a structured, side-by-side analysis of the two main residence/work permits available to Italian citizens working in Switzerland. It covers taxation, health insurance options, housing requirements, renewal conditions, family reunification rules, and practical implications for daily life. This is not a generic government brochure — it is tailored specifically to the frontaliere context with real-world examples and financial scenarios.

Border Crossing Wait Times

Nothing ruins a frontaliere's morning like an unexpected 45-minute queue at Brogeda or Stabio. The border crossing times tool shows current and historical wait times at major Ticino border crossings, helping you choose the fastest route and optimal departure time. It also flags planned roadworks, customs operations, and seasonal patterns (August holidays, Christmas shopping period) that affect crossing times.

Frequently Asked Questions
How much net income do I earn per month working in Switzerland as a cross-border commuter?
Use the net salary calculator on Frontaliere Ticino, entering gross salary in CHF, marital status, children, canton and municipality of Italian residence. The tool calculates the monthly net considering AHV (5.3%), AC (1.1%), UVG (0.7%), IJM (0.8%), BVG, Ticino withholding tax and Italian IRPEF (with a deductible of EUR 10,000 for new cross-border commuters).
What is the cost differential between Permit B and Permit G for a cross-border commuter with a family?
The B Permit (residence in Switzerland) can result in a higher cost for healthcare (LAMal with lower deductibles but variable premiums by age group) and taxation (higher withholding tax for medium-high incomes), while the G Permit (residence in Italy) imposes the Italian IRPEF with a deductible of 10,000 EUR. The difference can be around EUR 3,000-5,000 per year for an average household.
What are the most important tax deadlines to remember for Switzerland-Italy cross-border commuters in 2026?
Key deadlines include April 30 for the Italian tax return (Form 730 or Redditi PF), June 30 for the payment of the first IRPEF advance, September 30 for the second advance and balance, and July 31 for the payment of IMU if you own a property in Italy. For Switzerland, the AHV deadline is 31 December.
How does the €10,000 exemption for new cross-border commuters in Italy work in 2026?
The deductible of EUR 10,000 applies to income generated in Switzerland for new cross-border commuters (activity started after 17.07.2023). Only the part of income above this threshold is taxed in Italy, with progressive IRPEF rates. For example, out of 50,000 EUR gross, only 40,000 EUR will be subject to Italian taxation.
What is the maximum annual savings that can be achieved with third pillar 3a for a cross-border commuter in 2026?
In 2026, the maximum deductible contribution into third pillar 3a is CHF 7,258. If you pay in the maximum amount each year for 20 years with an annual return of 2%, you would accumulate around CHF 180,000. The annual tax savings would be around CHF 1,500 to CHF 2,000, depending on the marginal tax rate.
