Decrease in Italian border crossers in Ticino: UST DATA (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 8 August 2026

UST data show a decrease of 0.6% for border crossers in Ticino and 1.1% for Italians between April and June compared to 2025.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Frontier workers in Ticino: decrease of 0.6% on an annual basis
  • Italian workers: decrease of 1.1% between April and June
  • Significant increase in border crossers from France
  • Causes: new tax agreement and better conditions in Italy

Key facts

  • What: Change in frontier numbers
  • When: Second quarter 2026
  • Where: Switzerland and Canton Ticino
  • Who: Federal Statistical Office (FSO)
  • Amount: 414,262 total border crossers in Switzerland

The Federal Statistical Office (FSO) published data for the second quarter of 2026, highlighting a mixed picture for cross-border work. Domestically, 414,262 border crossers arrived in Switzerland for work, marking a slight increase of 0.5% compared to the previous quarter and 1.3% year-on-year. However, this positive trend does not affect the Canton of Ticino, where there has been a decrease.

Territorial analysis and national flows

The figures show that in the last three years the only significant growth has been recorded among workers from France. In contrast, flows from Italy, Germany and Austria remained broadly stable. Specifically, between April and June, the number of people who crossed the border from Italy fell by 1.1% compared to the same period in 2025.

The geographical distribution of the increases on an annual basis sees the highest values in the Geneva region

Operational details

Implications for the Ticino labour market

The drop in numbers recorded by the UST is not an isolated figure, but is combined with the observations of GastroTicino. At the beginning of the year, the Federation of Merchants and Hoteliers reported a sharp decrease in applications from Northern Italy. A critical aspect concerns the most qualified profiles, which today would show less interest in employment in the Canton of Ticino.

# Bending factors

To understand why Italian workers are less likely to move, it is necessary to analyse two main drivers cited by the source:

1. The tax impact: the new tax agreement between Switzerland and Italy, which came into force in July 2023, is indicated by the Ticino unions as a cause of greater economic burden for those who work on the border. This directly affects the net profitability of the permesso G. 2. Italian competitiveness: trade unions in Italy have achieved an improvement in working conditions, especially in the catering sector, making the option of staying in their country more attractive than in the past.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the trend of border crossers in Ticino according to UST data?
In Ticino, the number of border crossers decreased by 0.6% on an annual basis. Specifically, between April and June, people from Italy who crossed the border for work decreased by 1.1% compared to the same period in 2025.
What are the causes of the decline in Italian workers in Ticino?
The main causes include the new tax agreement between Switzerland and Italy (which entered into force in July 2023), which according to the Ticino unions entails a greater burden for border workers, and the improvement in working conditions obtained by the Italian unions, especially in the catering sector.
In which Swiss regions has the number of border crossers increased?
The most marked year-on-year increases were recorded in the Geneva region (+2.8%), the Plateau (+1.8%), northwestern Switzerland (+1.0%) and central Switzerland (+2.7%).
