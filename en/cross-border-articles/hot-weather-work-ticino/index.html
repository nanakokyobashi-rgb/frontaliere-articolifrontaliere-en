The proposals that emerged from the meeting between trade unions and the Council of State have several practical implications for workers in Ticino, including border workers working in sectors particularly exposed to heat. The proposed model, inspired by the Canton of Valais, involves the use of scientific indices to determine specific thresholds beyond which intervention obligations are triggered. These thresholds are not unique but vary depending on the professional reality: what is considered risky for a construction worker may not be so for an office worker, and vice versa for those who work in closed but hot environments such as kitchens or laundries.

For workers in Ticino, including border workers working in sectors particularly exposed to heat, there are several concrete steps that can be taken right now. The first is the active reporting of situations that are considered to be at risk. As pointed out by Evaristo Roncelli of OCST, many workers do not know how to independently assess whether the conditions they are experiencing constitute a real health hazard. Reporting these situations to trade unions (UNIA, OCST, USS or VPOD) allows to build a detailed picture of the problems on the territory, essential to justify targeted interventions.
