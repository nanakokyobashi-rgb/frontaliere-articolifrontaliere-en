Common border funds: MEF decree signed (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 18 September 2026

Minister Giorgetti signed the decree for the allocation of funds to the Municipalities within 20 km of the border with Ticino, Graubünden and Valais.

Context

In brief

  • MEF decree signed for funding of border municipalities
  • Resources allocated to municipalities within 20 km of the border
  • Territories bordering Ticino, Graubünden, and Valais involved

Key facts

Who: Minister of Economy and Finance Giancarlo Giorgetti What: Decree for the allocation of the state contribution Territorial radius: 20 kilometers from the border line Cantons involved: Ticino, Graubünden, and Valais Funding source: Fund for the economic development of border territories

The Minister of Economy and Finance, Giancarlo Giorgetti, has officially signed the decree defining and allocating the state contribution intended for border municipalities. The act, made known through a direct note from the MEF, represents the definitive turning point for the funding of Italian territories located adjacent to Switzerland. This measure is not an isolated intervention, but the concrete implementation of commitments previously undertaken between the two States.

The regulatory origin of the intervention

The measure is based on agreements signed between Italy and Switzerland within the framework of the historic Rome and Bern Agreement of 2020. The objective is to provide economic resources to those populated centers that, due to their geographical position, manage the complexities related to the constant flow of cross-border workers and residents. The funds necessary for the disbursement are drawn from the Fund for the economic development of border territories, an institution created specifically to mitigate disparities and strengthen marginal areas.

The allocation of resources follows precise geographical criteria. To be considered beneficiaries, Municipalities must be located within a radius of 20 kilometers from the border line. The scope is further defined based on the bordering Swiss territories: the benefit indeed concerns Italian municipalities bordering the Cantons of Ticino, Graubünden, and Valais. This perimeter ensures that the funds reach exactly where the impact of commuting is most pronounced, ensuring that local administrations have the means to manage the infrastructural friction generated by daily flows towards Switzerland.

Operational details

Implicazioni per l'area di frontiera

L'erogazione di questi fondi ha un impatto diretto sulla qualità della vita di chi attraversa ogni giorno il confine. I lavoratori che possiedono un permesso G e risiedono nei comuni beneficiari vedranno un potenziamento dei servizi che utilizzano quotidianamente. Il decreto specifica infatti che le risorse saranno destinate a tre pilastri principali: la crescita economica locale, il potenziamento delle infrastrutture territoriali e il rafforzamento dei servizi pubblici.

Analisi dei benefici concreti

Il potenziamento delle infrastrutture è l'aspetto più critico per il frontaliere. Un incremento dei fondi a disposizione dei Comuni può tradursi in una migliore gestione della viabilità urbana e dei collegamenti verso i valichi. Sebbene il decreto non specifichi singoli progetti, la destinazione d'uso verso le infrastrutture suggerisce un'attenzione verso la mobilità e la manutenzione stradale, elementi essenziali per chi deve raggiungere il posto di lavoro in Canton Ticino nei tempi previsti.

Per quanto riguarda i servizi pubblici, l'attenzione è rivolta specificamente alle comunità di confine e ai lavoratori frontalieri. Questo significa che le amministrazioni comunali potranno investire in servizi di supporto che rendano più efficiente la gestione amministrativa e sociale di una popolazione che vive in un regime di transnazionalità. Il rafforzamento dei servizi pubblici è essenziale per bilanciare il carico che i frontalieri apportano ai servizi locali (come trasporti, gestione rifiuti e manutenzione spazi pubblici) pur versando le imposte alla fonte in Svizzera.

Il sostegno alla crescita economica locale mira a creare un ecosistema più attrattivo anche sul lato italiano. L'idea è che i Comuni di frontiera non siano semplici "dormitori" per chi lavora in Svizzera, ma centri economici vitali. Questo potrebbe portare a un miglioramento dell'offerta commerciale e professionale locale, riducendo la dipendenza esclusiva dal mercato del lavoro elvetico e migliorando la vivibilità complessiva dei centri situati entro i 20 km dal confine.

Key points

Procedure and next steps

The disbursement of funds follows the administrative procedure established by the MEF. Since the decree has been signed, the next phase concerns the actual distribution of resources from the Fund for the Economic Development of Border Areas to individual municipal budgets. Municipalities that meet the criteria (within 20 km of the border with Ticino, Grisons, or Valais) will need to coordinate with state authorities for the crediting of contributions.

What to do for residents and workers

For the individual cross-border worker, no direct action to request funds is required, since the beneficiary is the municipal authority. However, it is advisable to monitor the resolutions of your municipality of residence to check how these investments will be allocated. If you reside in a border municipality, you may notice the start of new roadworks or the expansion of public services dedicated specifically to those who work across the border.

For those planning their annual budget or wishing to understand how the economic impact of the border area affects their purchasing power, it is useful to constantly monitor the costs of managing commuting. In particular, investment in local infrastructure can indirectly affect travel times and therefore transport costs.

Frequently Asked Questions
Which municipalities are entitled to the funds of the MEF decree?
All Italian municipalities located within a radius of 20 kilometers from the border line with Switzerland, specifically in the territories bordering the Cantons of Ticino, Graubünden and Valais, are entitled to the funds.
What will these resources actually be used for?
The resources will be allocated to support local economic growth, the strengthening of territorial infrastructures and the strengthening of public services dedicated to border communities and frontier workers.
Where does the money come from?
The funds are drawn from the Fund for the economic development of the border territories, as provided for by the agreements between Italy and Switzerland within the framework of the Rome and Bern Agreement of 2020.
