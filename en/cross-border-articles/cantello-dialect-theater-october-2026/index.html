The Teatro Pax di Cantello will host the amateur company Tutti in Scena on Saturday, October 10 at 9 p.m. and Sunday, October 18 at 3 p.m. The troupe opens the autumn season with a lively comedy in dialect, built around family misunderstandings, Japanese culture and relationship dynamics.

The comedy is «Those Japanese Foreigners», adapted into dialect by Alessandro Campi from «I Know a Lot About the Levant» by Andrea Oldani. The work brings to the stage the contrast between an interest in Japanese culture and the difficulty faced by those who do not know or appreciate the customs and traditions of the Land of the Rising Sun.

Two performances and a story of misunderstandings

At the center of the story is Lucia, who is very passionate about Japanese culture. Her husband Ettore, however, must face a more complicated problem: confessing his ignorance and fundamental lack of interest in that world to her. The family situation thus becomes fertile ground for misunderstandings and comedy, while couple and family dynamics are brought to the stage through the language of dialect theatre.

The choice to schedule two performances places the comedy’s presentation in October, with one performance at 9 p.m. and another at 3 p.m. In both cases, the venue is Teatro Pax and the company is Tutti in Scena.

Alessandro Campi plays Ettore, while Nicoletta Macchi plays Lucia. The cast also includes Giovanni Valli as Iango, Master Okuno’s assistant; Roberto Andriolo as Master Okuno; Teresa Baj Rossi as Elisa, Lucia’s friend; Massimo Rasi as Aldo, Ettore’s friend; Gino Casali as Dario, Lucia’s brother; and Giovanna Marino as a police officer.

The production is directed by Angelo Alfieri. Set design is by Giorgio Gandolfi, while Carlo Casartelli handles lighting and sound. The performance takes place under the patronage of the Municipality of Cantello, and admission is free.

Operational details

The contrast that fuels the comedy

The story described by the company lies in the distance between what Lucia considers important and what Ettore neither knows nor shows any interest in knowing. It is family life, with its differences in sensitivity, that becomes the ground on which misunderstandings develop.

Lucia's passion for Japanese culture defines the wife's point of view. Ettore's ignorance instead creates the problem he has to confess. The relationship between the two is not told merely as a difference in tastes: the source presents it as a couple dynamic capable of involving the family environment as well. Comedy therefore arises from the way the characters deal with the customs and traditions of the Land of the Rising Sun without sharing the same level of interest.

A network of relationships on stage

The characters around the couple broaden the scope of the situation. Iango is Master Okuno's assistant, while the master is played by Roberto Andriolo. Elisa is Lucia's friend and Aldo is Ettore's friend; Dario is Lucia's brother. Finally, Giovanna Marino plays a police officer. The presence of these figures brings the comedy into a family and relational dimension, one that the presentation describes as being made up of misunderstandings and comedy.

The choice of dialect is also part of the proposal. «Quei giargianes da giappunes» is not presented as a story separate from Andrea Oldani's text, but as the dialect version created by Alessandro Campi. The shift to the language of dialect theater thus becomes the way in which the company stages the contrast between Lucia's passion and Ettore's lack of interest.

The production then brings together distinct artistic and technical roles: Angelo Alfieri handles the direction, Giorgio Gandolfi the sets, and Carlo Casartelli the lights and sound. These are the elements indicated to accompany the performance at Teatro Pax.
