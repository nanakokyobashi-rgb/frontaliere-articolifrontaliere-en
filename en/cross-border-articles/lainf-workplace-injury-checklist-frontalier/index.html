LAINF accident: report checklist for border workers (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 23 August 2026

How to report an accident to LAINF if you are a border worker. Correct procedure, differences in occupational disease, operational checklist with deadlines.

Context

In a nutshell

  • LAINF covers accidents at work for border workers in Switzerland with G permit
  • Rate 0.7–1.5% of salary, paid entirely by the employer
  • Complaint within 3 days of injury, mandatory procedure
  • Net difference between acute injury and occupational disease (slow development)

Key facts

  • What : Compulsory Swiss occupational accident insurance
  • Who: G-licensed border workers working in Ticino and Switzerland
  • Rate: 0.7–1.5% of gross employee salary (varies by risk sector)
  • Manager: SUVA or authorised private insurers (second employer)
  • Reporting deadline: Within 3 calendar days of injury
  • Coverage: Accidental accidents, including commuting accidents (home-work)
  • Exclusions: Occupational diseases have separate insurance schemes

Protection against the risks of accidents at work is a fundamental pillar of Swiss social security. Those who work in Ticino or Switzerland with border status (G permit) remain covered by LAINF, the insurance against occupational accidents. This coverage is mandatory and its cost — between 0.7% and 1.5% of gross salary depending on the sector and risk profile — falls entirely on the employer.

Many border crossers do not know that the LAINF is activated automatically from primo giorno di lavoro svizzero. It is not necessary to register in person: the employer has the obligation to guarantee coverage within the beginning

Operational details

The accident report: procedures and timing

When a border worker suffers an accident at work, the employer must immediately inform the LAINF insurer (SUVA or other manager). This does not eliminate the worker's right and duty to report the event: if the employer does not act, the border worker can contact the insurer or the cantonal labour office directly.

The written complaint must contain:

  • Name, surname, date of birth of the worker
  • Permit number G (if available)
  • The precise date and time of the accident
  • Detailed description of the dynamics
  • Location of the accident (factory, construction site, road, etc.)
  • Name of the employer and company registration number with the insurer
  • Any witnesses

If the border worker does not know which insurance he is enrolled in, he can contact the Ticino Canton Employment Office or the supervisory body (SECO at the federal level). In a few days, the insurer will communicate the provisional recognition of coverage and initiate the medical investigation.

Key points

Checklist operational: what to do after an injury

When an injury occurs at work, the border worker must act quickly:

In the minutes/hours following the injury: 1. Immediately notify the head/responsible person of the company 2. Get visited by the company doctor (if present) or go to the ER 3. Ask the doctor for a certificate with date, time, type of injury, initial prognosis 4. Collect contacts of any witnesses (colleagues) 5. Take a photo of the injury location if it is safe to do so

Within 3 days of the injury: 6. Contact the LAINF insurer (the employer should have already contacted them) 7. If you do not know the insurer, ask the employer or contact SECO (Federal Office) 8. Send the ER doctor's medical certificate to the insurer 9. Complete the injury report form (provided by the insurer or downloadable online) 10. Keep copies of all medical and administrative documents

In the days/weeks following: 11. Follow the recommended therapies and medical visits 12. Inform the insurer of any changes in prognosis or change of doctor 13. If the return to work is slow, check with LAINF the possibility of advance payments on pension 14. Document all uncovered medical expenses (possible tax deductions)

Tools and support for border workers

Border workers have the right to free consultation at the trade union or Ticino patronage. Many trade unions have specific counters for border workers and can support in negotiations with the insurer if the injury recognition is contested.

Frequently Asked Questions
Who pays the LAINF contribution for a border worker?
The Swiss employer pays the entire LAINF contribution (0.7–1.5% of the gross salary) in favour of the border employee. It is not withheld from the worker's paycheck, unlike AVS/AI or LPP, which are paid jointly by employer and employee. LAINF is full business cost.
Is a commuting accident covered by LAINF?
Yes, completely. If the border worker suffers an accident during the authorised journey from home to work to home (including the usual routes connecting to public transport), LAINF covers medical care and allowances as if the accident had occurred at the employer's premises. The important thing is to show that the path was direct and reasonable.
What is the difference between an accident and an occupational disease for LAINF purposes?
An injury is an acute and sudden event (fall, cut, bruise) fully covered by LAINF. An occupational disease is a disease that develops slowly as a result of work (chronic tendonitis, substance dermatitis, noise hearing loss). The occupational disease requires medical assessment of the causal link and has longer recognition times.
How long do I have to report an injury to LAINF?
The accident report must be received by the insurer within 3 calendar days from the date of the event. The employer has an obligation to do so, but the frontier worker may do so directly if the employer delays. After 3 days, the payment of benefits may be delayed, although the right to benefit generally remains valid.
G leave and LAINF coverage: is the border crossing always insured?
Yes. The G permit holder working in Switzerland is compulsorily covered by LAINF from the first day of the employment contract. If the employer does not register the employee with the insurer, the cantonal employment office automatically manages a security cover. The border worker does not have to pay anything and does not run the risk of insurance overdraft.
