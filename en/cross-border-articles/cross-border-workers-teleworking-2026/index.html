Cross-border workers and remote work: 2026 changes (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 7 May 2026

New rules for cross-border workers who work from home in Italy. Find out what changes and how to adapt

Context

In brief

  • New rules for cross-border workers telecommuting from 2026
  • Up to 50 days of working from home in Italy
  • Impact on AHV, BVG, and LAMal

Key facts

  • What: New rules for cross-border workers telecommuting
  • When: From January 1, 2026
  • Where: Canton Ticino and Italy
  • Who: Cross-border workers with a G permit
  • Amount: Up to 50 days of working from home per year

The Canton of Ticino has announced new rules for cross-border workers who wish to work from home in Italy. From January 1, 2026, cross-border workers with a G permit will be able to work up to 50 days a year from their home in Italy. This measure aims to improve the quality of life for cross-border workers, allowing them to save time and commuting costs.

Impact on social insurances

The new rules will have a significant impact on social insurances such as AHV, BVG, and LAMal. Cross-border workers will continue to pay contributions in Switzerland, but the rules for calculating contributions may change. It is important that cross-border workers inform themselves through their employer and the competent authorities to understand how these changes will affect their specific situation.

Procedures for cross-border workers

Cross-border workers who wish to take advantage of telecommuting will have to follow a specific procedure. First, they will need to obtain approval from their employer. Subsequently, they will need to inform the competent Swiss authorities, such as the Cantonal Employment Office, and the Italian authorities, such as the INPS. It is fundamental to respect all deadlines and submit the required documentation to avoid sanctions.

Practical Analysis

The new rules on teleworking for cross-border workers represent an important opportunity to improve the quality of life and reduce commuting costs. However, it is essential to understand the practical implications and the procedures to follow to avoid problems.

Tax Impact

One of the most important aspects to consider is the tax impact. Cross-border workers who work from home in Italy will continue to pay taxes in Switzerland but may be subject to different rules regarding tax deductions and tax benefits. It is advisable to consult an accountant or tax expert to understand how these changes will affect your situation.

Comparison with Other Regions

The new rules for teleworking for cross-border workers in Ticino are more favorable compared to other neighboring regions. For example, in Lombardy, cross-border workers can only work from home in exceptional cases and for a limited period. The new rules of the Canton of Ticino offer greater flexibility and opportunities, making Ticino a more attractive destination for cross-border workers.

In the second scenario, a cross-border worker works in Chiasso and wants to work from home in Como for 50 days a year. In both cases, cross-border workers will have to follow the established procedures and respect the teleworking day limits.

For cross-border workers who wish to work from home in Italy, it is important to follow a specific procedure. Here are the steps to follow:

1. Obtain approval from the employer 2. Inform the competent Swiss authorities, such as the Cantonal Employment Office 3. Inform the Italian authorities, such as INPS 4. Keep track of teleworking days 5. Respect the limits of teleworking days set

Useful tools

To help cross-border workers manage teleworking, Canton Ticino has made some useful tools available. For example, you can use the salary calculator to calculate your net salary and better understand the impact of the new rules. Additionally, you can consult the practical guides available on the Canton Ticino website for further information and tips.

Deadlines and documentation

It is important to respect deadlines and submit the required documentation to avoid penalties. Cross-border workers will have to submit the required documentation within the established deadlines and ensure that they respect all the rules and limits of teleworking days. In case of doubts or questions, you can contact the competent authorities to obtain further information and assistance.
