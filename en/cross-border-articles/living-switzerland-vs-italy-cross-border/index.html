Living in Switzerland vs Italy: Real Comparison for Cross-Border Workers

Di Laura Bianchi · Published on 15 March 2026

Permit B or Permit G? Detailed comparison of costs, taxes, healthcare, quality of life and economic convenience with 2026 practical examples.

Context

At a glance

Compare the annual amount left over, not gross pay. Working in Switzerland while living in Italy can be worthwhile when net income after applicable taxes and contributions covers housing, healthcare and commuting and leaves more than the realistic alternative. A G permit applies to someone working in Switzerland while keeping their residence abroad; a B permit applies to residence in Switzerland. These are residence categories and are not enough, by themselves, to calculate tax or disposable income.

For a high-level price comparison, Eurostat’s 2025 household-consumption price-level index (EU average=100) places Switzerland at about 171 and Italy at about 100. This is a national comparison based on purchasing power parities. It does not compare rents in Lugano and Como, describe every spending category, or represent a specific household budget.

Source and methodology: Eurostat price-level indices. A personal decision still requires comparing the actual municipalities, housing, household and spending patterns.

Operational details

Tax and health coverage

The Italy–Switzerland agreement on cross-border workers was signed in 2020 and entered into force on 17 July 2023. The rules distinguish new cross-border workers from people who may qualify for the transitional regime. Eligibility depends on the agreement’s requirements and work history, not only on the permit label. For new cross-border workers covered by the agreement, Switzerland withholds 80% of the ordinary source-tax rate, while Italy taxes under its own rules and grants a credit for Swiss tax within the applicable limits. Check your circumstances in the official Swiss tax authority FAQs.

Health coverage also needs an individual check. Italy-resident workers who meet the requirements may have a right to choose between Swiss insurance and the Italian national health service; eligibility, affected family members and deadlines depend on the case. Ticino generally indicates a three-month period to exercise the option. See the official cantonal information and our health coverage comparison.

Key points

Is it worth working in Switzerland and living in Italy?

It can be worthwhile if, for the same job and household, the income left after applicable taxes and contributions covers Italian living costs and commuting better than the Swiss-residence option. Gross pay in CHF alone cannot answer the question. Compare the annual amount left in each scenario: net income actually available minus housing, utilities, groceries, health coverage, commuting, parking and childcare. Use the same 12-month period and currency, and include a 13th salary or allowances only when the offer provides them. Then assess travel hours and commuting frequency separately, since time and costs can change the result.

How to compare the two options

Before moving or starting a cross-border commute, prepare two annual budgets using the same categories:

1. Use current listings and ancillary charges for homes actually available in the municipalities you are considering. 2. Add groceries, utilities, childcare and other household expenses without applying generic percentages. 3. Estimate taxes and social contributions based on tax residence, income and household circumstances; confirm the result with a qualified adviser. 4. Include health coverage, commuting, parking and how often you travel. 5. Consider travel time and the services you would use day to day.

The cost-of-living comparator and net salary calculator help organize estimates; results depend on your inputs and are not tax advice. Check border crossing times with the border service. There is no salary threshold or saving percentage that makes a G or B permit better for everyone.
