The Socialist group leader Ivo Durisch has responded to the accusations by the Centro president, Fiorenzo Dadò, regarding the alleged blocking of the Administrative Court. Durisch described the accusations as unfounded and announced that his party is ready to discuss an alternative proposal for the government's Appeals Service during the parliamentary session in June. The controversy concerns the future of the Appeals Service, with Durisch arguing that the block is merely Dadò's perspective.

The discussion about the government's Appeals Service is a hot topic in Ticino, particularly regarding construction and permits. Durisch reiterated that his party is open to dialogue and that the alternative proposal will be presented in parliament in June.

Implications for cross-border workers

Cross-border workers employed in Ticino may be affected by changes in the government's Appeals Service, especially if they pertain to construction and permits. Any potential blockage or reorganization of the service could influence the approval times for construction permits and other documents necessary for those living and working in the canton.

For example, in the municipality of Lugano, the average approval time for a construction permit is currently 60 days. If the Appeals Service experiences delays, this period could be extended, causing inconvenience for cross-border workers who intend to build or renovate. Additionally, in the municipality of Bellinzona, the number of construction applications awaiting approval has increased by 15% over the past twelve months, a trend that could worsen with any changes to the service.

Comparisons with the previous situation

Before Dadò's accusations, the government's Appeals Service functioned without particular issues. Durisch's alternative proposal could lead to improvements or further complications, depending on the decisions made in parliament.

For instance, in 2025, the service handled 1,200 appeals, with an average resolution time of 30 days. If the new proposal introduces regulatory changes, such as the requirement to submit additional documents, the processing times could be extended. Furthermore, the cantonal regulation of 2024 stipulates that appeals must be submitted within 30 days of the decision notification, a deadline that could be modified.

Practical scenarios

  • Scenario 2: If Durisch's alternative proposal is approved, it could introduce simplifications that reduce the approval times by 10%, bringing them down to 54 days.
