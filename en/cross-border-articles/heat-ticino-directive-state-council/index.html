The Vpod union wrote to the State Council of the Canton of Ticino to request an ad hoc directive against heat waves. The request, reported by the Region, aims to avoid that, in the absence of corrective measures, responses to climate emergencies arrive late and improvised.

The Vpod, an acronym that represents public and parapublic personnel, stresses the need to address the problem of extreme temperatures with structural tools. The letter sent to the cantonal executive is not a simple report: it is a formal request for the Canton to provide clear guidelines and targeted investments in infrastructure.

The theme is not new for Ticino, where summers are getting hotter and cities like Lugano, Bellinzona and Locarno suffer from the heat island effect. But so far, according to the union, the measures have been reactive rather than preventive. The required directive should establish standard procedures to protect workers and the population during heat spikes.

Analysis: what it means for those who work in Ticino

Heat waves are not just a problem of comfort: they have concrete consequences on health, productivity and safety at work. The Vpod's request to the State Council touches on an issue that also closely affects border workers, who cross the border every day to work in Ticino.

For those who work outdoors — construction sites, agriculture, road maintenance — extreme heat can make conditions unbearable. An ad hoc directive could introduce clear rules on mandatory breaks, reduced hours and access to water. For those who work in the office, however, the problem is air conditioning: infrastructure investments could improve the insulation of buildings and reduce dependence on air conditioning.

Before and After: What Would Change

Today, without a directive, every company manages the heat as it sees fit. Some shorten the schedule, some don't. This creates disparities between workers, even between border workers and residents. With a cantonal directive, the rules would be the same for everyone.

Infrastructure investments, then, would have a long-term effect: cooler buildings, shaded public spaces, green roofs. For border crossers who spend hours by train or car between Chiasso and Lugano, more livable cities also mean less stress on the way.

What to do while waiting for the directive

While the Vpod urges the State Council, workers — including border workers — can already take some precautions. The heat does not wait, and those who work in Ticino must know how to protect themselves.

Practical steps for workers

1. Enquire about rights: If you work outdoors, check with your employer if high temperature protocols exist. In the absence of a cantonal directive, each company has its own rules. 2. Hydration: always bring water, especially if you work on site or in non-conditioned spaces. 3. Flexible hours: In some cases, employers allow you to start earlier in the morning to avoid the hottest hours. Ask if it's possible. 4. Sun protection: Hats, sunscreen, and shade breaks are essential. 5. Monitor your symptoms: Headaches, nausea, and cramps can be signs of heat stroke. Don't underestimate them.
