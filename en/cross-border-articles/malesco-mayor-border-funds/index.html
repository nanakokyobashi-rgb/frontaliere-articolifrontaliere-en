Frontiersmen, Malesco writes to the Government for funds (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 19 September 2026

Enrico Barbazza, mayor of Malesco, requests the Government to fully transfer the resources allocated to border municipalities to support local services.

Context

In brief

  • The mayor of Malesco demands the full transfer of cross-border resources.
  • Enrico Barbazza writes to the Government to defend funds allocated to the territories.
  • An immediate discussion between local institutions and the national Government has been requested.
  • These resources are essential for services, road networks, and municipal security.

Key facts

  • Promoter: Enrico Barbazza, Mayor of Malesco
  • Recipients: Prime Minister, Ministers of Economy/Interior, Parliamentarians
  • Objective: Full transfer of resources to border municipalities
  • Motivation: Support for local services, road networks, and infrastructure

Enrico Barbazza, mayor of Malesco, has formalized an urgent request addressed to the highest offices of the Italian State, including the President of the Council of Ministers, the Minister of Economy and Finance, and the Minister of the Interior, as well as parliamentarians from border regions. The letter aims to raise the issue regarding the defense of financial resources derived from cross-border labor, which are allocated to border municipalities according to current regulations. The mayor of Malesco emphasizes that these funds do not represent a discretionary concession, but rather a resource linked to an economic and social phenomenon that daily impacts thousands of workers and their families. The central point raised is the need to guarantee the full transfer of the sums due to local administrations, which are on the front line in managing the effects of international mobility.

…

Operational details

The issue raised by Enrico Barbazza highlights the financial fragility of border municipalities, where the presence of a large number of workers employed in Switzerland places constant pressure on public services. A practical analysis of this situation shows how territory management in cross-border areas requires higher investments compared to inland zones, due to the need to manage intense commuter flows passing through crossings such as Brogeda or Gaggiolo. The concern of mayors, as expressed by the representative of Malesco, does not only concern the present, but the long-term sustainability of local infrastructure. If the resources allocated to municipalities were reduced, we would witness a deterioration in the quality of life for resident citizens and for the cross-border workers themselves who experience the territory on a daily basis.

Key points

The current situation requires constant monitoring by local administrators and active participation from cross-border commuters in supporting the requests of their municipalities. The demand for an immediate discussion with national institutions represents a necessary step to clarify the future of financial transfers. For mayors, the duty to defend the interests of their communities outweighs institutional disagreements, imposing a shared responsibility to protect the revenues that allow for the maintenance of local services. Mayor Barbazza has reiterated his willingness to engage in a serious and constructive dialogue, hoping that the Government will intervene promptly to prevent local public finances from suffering further cuts or unjustified withholdings.

Why are these resources essential for municipalities?
The resources derived from cross-border work are fundamental to guarantee essential services such as land maintenance, roads, security, transport and social services. Local administrations in the front line face specific burdens related to commuter flows to crossings such as Brogeda or Gaggiolo. Without this support, small municipalities risk suffering a deterioration in the quality of life, being unable to cope with the management responsibilities deriving from their geographical position.
What are the next steps required of local authorities?
Mayor Barbazza calls for an immediate confrontation with national institutions to prevent resources from being centrally withheld. Territories and citizens are invited to monitor developments and support the demand for transparency. The ultimate goal is to protect the revenues necessary for the functionality of territorial services, avoiding that the weight of local public finance is further penalized by cuts or failure to allocate the funds due.
