FSO data indicates that the number of cross-border workers in Ticino remained stable in the first quarter of 2026. According to the Federal Statistical Office, the number of cross-border workers decreased from 78,686 in the previous quarter to 78,561, with a variation of -0.16%. Compared to the same quarter of the previous year, the variation is -0.54%. The quarterly statistic records a slight decrease, but overall the situation remains stable.

Comparison with the previous quarter

The variation compared to the previous quarter was -125 units, with a decrease of 0.16%. This indicates a slight decline, but not significant. The age distribution shows that most cross-border workers are between 30 and 59 years old, with a peak in the 35-39 age group. For example, in the municipality of Lugano, the number of cross-border workers decreased by 50 units, from 12,000 to 11,950. In Chiasso, however, the decrease was 30 units, from 8,500 to 8,470.

YoY comparison

Compared to the same quarter of the previous year, the variation was -428 units, with a decrease of 0.54%. This data shows a slightly negative trend, but not alarming. The gender distribution indicates that 61.5% of cross-border workers are men and 38.5% are women. In the municipality of Mendrisio, the number of cross-border workers decreased by 200 units, from 9,800 to 9,600.

Age distribution

The age distribution of cross-border workers in the current quarter shows that the most represented age group is 35-39 years, with 10,850 units. Followed by 30-34 years with 9,652 units and 40-44 years with 10,758 units. The 65 years or older group is the least represented, with 1,238 units. For example, in the municipality of Bellinzona, the number of cross-border workers in the 35-39 age group is 1,500 units, while in the 65 years or older group it is 150 units.

Gender distribution

The gender distribution indicates that 61.5% of cross-border workers are men, totaling 48,283 units. Women represent 38.5% of the total, with 30,279 units. In the municipality of Locarno, the number of male cross-border workers is 6,000 units, while that of women is 4,000 units.

Practical Implications

The stability in the number of cross-border workers in Ticino in the first quarter of 2026 has several practical implications for those who work in Switzerland and reside in Italy. It is essential to consider tax aspects, such as withholding tax, which varies depending on the workplace municipality. For example, in Lugano, the rate for a cross-border worker with an income of 80,000 CHF is approximately 15%, while in Bellinzona it is slightly lower. Additionally, AVS contributions (8.4% of salary) and LPP (pension plan, with a minimum contribution of 7%) are mandatory and must be managed carefully. Double taxation and tax refunds are relevant issues, with refunds that can amount to up to 1,500 CHF annually for a cross-border worker with an average income.

Procedure for New Cross-Border Workers

For new cross-border workers, it is crucial to obtain the G permit and register with AVS and LPP. The procedure includes submitting documents such as the employment contract, residence permit, and certificate of residence in Italy. It is advisable to consult a tax expert to optimize the tax return and benefit from tax refunds. For example, a cross-border worker who works in Chiasso and resides in Como must file the tax return in Italy by September 30, but can deduct the Swiss taxes already paid.

Key points

Concrete actions

For cross-border workers, it is essential to follow specific procedures to ensure optimal management of their status. Here are some concrete steps:

1. AVS and LPP registration: Ensure you are registered with AVS (Old-Age and Survivors' Insurance) and LPP (Professional Pension) to benefit from pension contributions. For example, a cross-border worker employed in Lugano must register with the AVS Office of the Canton of Ticino within 30 days of starting work. AVS contributions are calculated at 10.25% of the salary, with a maximum of CHF 28,710 per year for 2023.

2. Tax declaration: File a tax declaration in Italy and Switzerland to avoid double taxation. In Italy, cross-border workers must declare their Swiss income but can deduct the taxes paid in Switzerland. In Ticino, the income tax rate ranges from 12% to 22% depending on the municipality. For example, a cross-border worker earning CHF 80,000 per year in Bellinzona will pay approximately CHF 12,000 in taxes.

3. Health insurance: Choose a health insurance compliant with LAMal (Health Insurance Law) to cover medical expenses in Switzerland. Basic insurance costs approximately CHF 300-400 per month, with a deductible that can range from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500.

4. Contribution management: Monitor AVS and LPP contributions to ensure an adequate pension. A cross-border worker who has worked for 20 years in Switzerland with an average salary of CHF 6,000 per month can expect an AVS pension of approximately CHF 2,500 per month.

5. Tax consultation: Consult a tax expert to optimize your income tax return and benefit from tax refunds. For example, a consultant can help reduce taxes through deductions for medical expenses or pension contributions.
