The New Cross-Border Workers Agreement was signed on December 23, 2020, and has been in force since January 1, 2024. Italy ratified it through Law 83 of June 13, 2023. Therefore, the phrase «new 2026 tax agreement» should be corrected: the agreement has been in force since 2024, not since 2026.

For a cross-border welder working in Canton Ticino, the central tax issue is withholding on employment income. The source states that tax at source is withheld in Switzerland. Italy avoids double taxation through the tax credit indicated in section CE of Form 730.

The dates do not serve the same purpose: December 23, 2020 concerns the signing, June 13, 2023 the Italian ratification, January 1, 2024 the entry into force, and July 17, 2023 the threshold used to distinguish old cross-border workers. Separating these steps avoids assigning to 2026 an effective date that the source places in 2024.

The distinction between old and new cross-border workers

The regime changes depending on the category identified by the source. New cross-border workers, by contrast, have a tax-free threshold of €10'000.

Permit G forms part of the administrative framework to be considered for cross-border work. Switzerland is not a member of the European Union or the European Economic Area. Here, the tax framework is defined by the dates of the agreement, the tax-free thresholds and the withholding rule.

Operational details

Withholding tax and comparison between periods

Swiss withholding and Italian IRPEF belong to areas that the source keeps distinct. For the cross-border worker's employment income, the withholding is in Switzerland; the credit in section CE of the 730 is the Italian mechanism cited to avoid double taxation. The Italy-Switzerland Convention against double taxation was signed on March 9, 1976.

Comparing the Italian brackets

The Italian figure changes depending on the period considered. For 2024–2025 income, the indicated rates are 23%, 35% and 43%. From 2026, the source reports 23% up to €28'000, 33% from €28'001 to €50'000 and 43% above €50'000. For 2026, the stated reference is Law 199/2025, Art. 1 para. 3. The change to the middle bracket does not justify turning these percentages into an estimate of the welder's net income: there is no individual income to which to apply them.

The table serves to avoid confusing an IRPEF bracket with Swiss withholding. Tax rates, including federal and cantonal ones, are set by federal and cantonal laws. At federal level, they are administered by AFC/ESTV, the Federal Tax Administration; cantonal tax administrations are also involved. They are not set by UFAS, which concerns social security, nor by BFS, which collects statistical data.

For the cross-border worker in the Canton of Ticino, the correct comparison therefore involves three separate elements: classification as an old or new cross-border worker, tax period and type of levy. Automatically adding an Italian percentage to the Swiss one would produce an interpretation incompatible with the rule of a single withholding in Switzerland and the Italian tax credit.

Key points

Swiss contributions, LAMal and limitations of the available data

The percentages indicated do not in themselves define a net salary. The source lists some Swiss rates and contributions: AVS/AI/IPG equal to 5.3% for the employee; AD/AC at 1.1%, with the cap of CHF 148'200; LAINF between 0.7% and 1.5%; LPP between 7% and 18% depending on age bracket, starting at age 25. These are withholding and pension data, not a specific salary range for the profession of welder.

LAMal and pension provision are not withholding tax

LAMal is health insurance. Cross-border workers with a G permit have the right of option and, for adults, the deductibles indicated range from CHF 300 to 2500. LAMal must therefore be kept separate both from withholding tax and from the AVS, LPP, AD/AC and LAINF items. The material does not characterize LAMal as “health tax.”
