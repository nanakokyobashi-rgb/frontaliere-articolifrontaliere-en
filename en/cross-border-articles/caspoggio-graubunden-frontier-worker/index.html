The Caspoggio-Graubünden border crossing

Living in Caspoggio and working in Graubünden means being a cross-border worker in the strict sense: residence in Italy, salary from Switzerland, tax on both sides. Starting from 1 January 2024, the New Cross-border Workers Agreement (signed on 23 December 2020, ratified by Italy with Law 83 of 13 June 2023) has reorganized the tax regime for this category. The tax withholding takes place in Switzerland (no double withholding), while Italy recognizes a tax credit in the EC framework of the 730 declaration to avoid overtaxation. The Italian-Swiss Convention of 9 March 1976 establishes the coordination principles; the cross-border worker pays in the country where he works (Switzerland) and receives compensation in Italy.

The cross-border worker classification is rigid: the G permit is specific for those who reside in Italy and work in Switzerland on a daily basis. Old cross-border commuters remain taxed only in Switzerland (art. 9 of the 2020 Agreement). those who start later are "new cross-border workers" with a higher INPS deductible.

Taxation in Switzerland: withholding tax and mandatory contributions

The Swiss employer withholds withholding tax directly from the salary, which varies between 8% and 22% depending on the canton of Graubünden and the Italian province of residence (it is a cantonal/municipal rate, not a federal one). This is the only direct tax that the worker sees on his Swiss pay slip. At the same time, the cross-border worker pays 5.3% of AVS (Old Age and Survivors Insurance) and AI (Disability Insurance) plus IPG (Daily Sickness Allowance); the giver adds an equivalent contribution that is not visible. Furthermore, contributory AD (Unemployment Insurance) and AC (Layoff Fund) at 1.1% up to CHF 148,200 of annual salary, LAINF (Accident Insurance) between 0.7% and 1.5%, and LPP (Professional Pension) from 7% to 18% depending on the age group (from 25 years). KVG (health insurance) is separate: each cross-border commuter chooses a Swiss company (Helsana, Sanitas, Groupe Mutuel, CSS, etc.) and pays a private monthly premium with franchise (uncovered) varying from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 per year.

Double taxation and refunds

In Italy, Swiss income is subject to progressive IRPEF: 23% up to €28,000, 33% from €28,001 to €50,000, 43% over €50,000. If applied directly, it would involve payment in both Switzerland and Italy on the same income. To avoid this, the cross-border worker requests the tax credit in the EC part of the annual declaration (by 31 May), indicating the gross Swiss income and the Swiss tax paid. The Revenue Agency applies the Italian IRPEF rate and subtracts (credit) the Swiss tax already paid. If the Swiss tax is higher, the cross-border worker receives a refund from the declaration; if less, pay the difference. The balance depends on the Swiss cantonal rate and the Italian IRPEF rate: usually, for medium-low incomes, the credit almost entirely offsets the double burden.

Operational details

INPS contribution regime and exemption thresholds

The key distinction for a cross-border worker is the annual INPS exemption threshold, namely the amount below which Italian social security contributions are not triggered. Old cross-border workers continue to be taxed only in Switzerland (Art. 9 of the 2020 Agreement). For the purposes of Art. 9 of the Agreement, “old cross-border workers” are people who met the tax requirements and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais on July 17, 2023, or during the period from December 31, 2018 to July 17, 2023. For employment income covered by Art. 9, taxation remains exclusively at source in Switzerland; for other workers, the ordinary regime applies, with taxation in Italy, a credit for Swiss tax and Swiss withholding at 80% of the ordinary rates. The 10.000 euros in Art. 4 of Law 83/2023 are merely the income limit referred to in Art. 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013, and not a general exemption for new cross-border workers. Sources: AFC fact sheet · Agreement, Art. 9 · Law 83/2023, Art. 4. The threshold of 10.000 euros is not a general exemption: Art. 4 of Law 83/2023 limits it to the income referred to in Art. 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013. At an average INPS contribution rate of 10–12%, the annual difference is between €250 and €300.

Above the exemption threshold, the cross-border worker must register with INPS and report Swiss income in the annual Italian tax return. Switzerland does not pay INPS contributions (Swiss AVS/AI/LPP contributions are coordinated with the Italian system, not cumulative). A cross-border worker who does not want to lose contribution years may voluntarily pay into INPS to cover periods below the threshold, but this is not mandatory if the income remains below the exemption threshold.

LAMal: Swiss medical coverage

LAMal is mandatory private health insurance in Switzerland, very different from the Italian National Health Service. Every cross-border worker must take out a policy within three months of starting work. The companies (Helsana, Sanitas, Groupe Mutuel, CSS, Assura, Swica and others) offer plans with deductibles chosen by the cross-border worker. A deductible of CHF 300 means an annual deductible of CHF 300, after which the insurer covers 90%; a deductible of CHF 2'500 reduces the premium but increases the initial out-of-pocket amount. For a cross-border worker from Caspoggio, the average monthly premium is around CHF 150 to CHF 250 (depending on age, gender, chosen deductible and canton of residence). The right of option allows the cross-border worker to choose the insurer freely; switching is always possible with 30 days' notice. LAMal coverage also applies during leave, illness and accidents; it does not cover situations expressly excluded from the contract (e.g. unreported accidents, dental treatment above the limits).

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the difference between permit G and permit B for a border crossing from Caspoggio?
The G permit is specific for those who permanently reside in Italy (Caspoggio) and work in Switzerland on a daily basis (pure border worker). The B permit is for those who intend to reside in Switzerland. Given that the border crossing from Caspoggio maintains Italian residence, the G permit is the most appropriate. The permit is issued by the SEM (State Secretariat for Migration) and registered with the canton of Graubünden. Validity: 5 years, renewable.
Which income does the €10,000 limit apply to?
Article 9’s transitional regime applies only to workers who meet the Agreement’s definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between 31 December 2018 and 17 July 2023; covered employment income is taxable exclusively in Switzerland. The ordinary regime applies in other cases, with Italian taxation and credit for Swiss tax where applicable. The €10,000 in Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is only the limit for income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law 147/2013, not a general allowance. FTA factsheet · Agreement, Article 9 · Normattiva, Article 4.
How does the tax credit work in the EC framework of declaration 730?
In the EC framework of form 730 (Italian annual declaration), the border worker reports: (1) Swiss gross income, (2) Swiss source tax paid (from AFC certificate). The Revenue Agency applies the Italian personal income tax rate and recognizes a credit equal to the Swiss tax. If the Swiss tax is higher than the Italian IRPEF debt, the frontier worker receives a refund; if lower, he pays the difference. This mechanism avoids double taxation on the same source of income.
What happens if I change jobs in Switzerland during the year?
If the border crossing company changes Swiss employer during the year (e.g. in June), it continues to pay AVS/AI/LPP contributions and tax at source at the new employer. The AFC is automatically updated if the employer changes membership. For the Italian annual return, the border worker reports the total income from both employers and the cumulative Swiss tax paid. If the address also changes in Italy, communicate the new data to INPS and the Revenue Agency within 30 days for administrative continuity.
Does the Swiss LAMal also cover my family members in Italy?
LAMal covers the border worker and the family members who live with him in Switzerland. If the family stays in Italy, Italian medical coverage (National Health Service) is automatic for them. The border worker can choose whether to extend a LAMal policy also to family members in Italy (optional additional coverage, with higher costs), but it is not mandatory. The choice depends on personalised needs and insurance costs.
