The Association of Ticino Municipalities has decided to appeal to the Federal Court against the government's changes to the fire protection regulation, which came into force on 1 January 2025. The decision was made following an in-depth analysis of the new provisions, which, according to the association, introduce tasks and burdens not provided for by the cantonal law 550.1 of 2018. Among the critical points are the new regulations that impose additional responsibilities on local authorities without adequate financial or technical support, such as the obligation to equip themselves with advanced fire detection systems by 2027, at an estimated cost of around 500,000 francs per municipality.

The changes to the regulation were introduced with the aim of improving fire safety throughout the Canton of Ticino, in line with European regulations. However, the Association of Ticino Municipalities argues that these changes were implemented without adequate consultation with local authorities. Moreover, they look to Lausanne as an example of best practice, where fire protection regulations were introduced with greater involvement of stakeholders and state funding of 70% of the costs.

Operational details

Analysis of Changes

The amendments to the fire protection regulation, which came into force on January 1, 2024, introduce a series of new provisions that could have a significant impact on Ticino municipalities. Among the main novelties are the introduction of new training obligations for municipal staff and the obligation to equip themselves with more advanced fire protection equipment. For example, municipalities will now have to train at least two members of staff each year on advanced fire extinguishing techniques, with an estimated cost of around 5,000 CHF per course. Furthermore, it will be mandatory to equip themselves with state-of-the-art fire extinguishers, with an average investment of 2,000 CHF per municipal building. These changes could entail additional costs for the municipalities, which already have to cope with tight budgets.

Comparisons with the Previous Situation

Before the amendments, Ticino municipalities were required to comply with less stringent regulations in terms of fire protection. The new provisions introduce higher standards, which could require significant investments in terms of human and technical resources. For example, the municipality of Lugano has estimated a 30% increase in operating costs for fire protection management. Furthermore, the lack of specific clarifications on the implementation of the new regulations has created further uncertainties among local administrators. The municipality of Bellinzona, for example, has requested clarifications from the Department of Institutions of the Canton of Ticino, but has not yet received definitive answers.

Key points

What Municipalities Should Do

Municipalities in Ticino should start preparing for the implementation of the new fire protection regulations, which will come into effect on January 1, 2025. In particular, it is crucial to assess the additional costs and resources needed to comply with the new provisions. For example, the municipality of Lugano estimates a 15% increase in operational costs for updating fire protection systems, while Bellinzona anticipates a 20% rise in maintenance expenses.

"Planning is essential to tackle the new regulatory challenges."

Municipalities should closely monitor the situation's evolution and actively participate in consultations with cantonal authorities. It is advisable to create a dedicated internal task force, involving experts in safety and financial management. Additionally, it is useful to exchange experiences with other municipalities that have already started the adaptation process, such as Locarno, which reduced implementation times by 30% thanks to careful planning.
