Italian frontiersmen down: −0.6% in Ticino in Q2 2026 (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 21 August 2026

In the second quarter of 2026, entries from Italy fell by 1.1%. In Ticino the drop is 0.6%. New tax agreement and better working conditions in Italy among the factors.

Context

Brief Overview

  • Italian cross-border workers in decline in Q2 2026: -1.1% at national level
  • In Ticino, the decline is moderate: -0.6% on an annual basis
  • 414,262 cross-border workers employed in Switzerland in the second quarter

Key Facts

  • What: Decrease in Italian cross-border workers employed in Switzerland
  • When: Second quarter 2026 (April-June)
  • Where: Canton Ticino and entire Swiss territory
  • Who: Federal Statistical Office
  • Amount: -1.1% entries from Italy, -0.6% Ticino

In the second quarter of 2026, the flow of Italian cross-border workers to Switzerland has significantly slowed down. Entries from Italy have contracted by 1.1% compared to the same period last year. Against a 0.5% overall increase in the number of cross-border workers employed in Switzerland in the quarter compared to the previous quarter, and 1.3% compared to the second quarter of 2025, the situation in Canton Ticino was more critical: the total number of cross-border workers decreased by 0.6% on an annual basis.

The data from the Federal Statistical Office documents 414,262 cross-border workers employed in Switzerland between April and June 2026. This decline particularly affects the main area of Italian labor attraction, Canton Ticino, which hosts the vast majority of Italian cross-border workers in Switzerland.

Why the decline?

The slowdown in flows is attributed to multiple factors. Firstly, the new tax agreement between Italy and Switzerland, which came into effect in July 2023, has modified the taxation regime for new cross-border workers, making the transfer of residence less convenient for some workers compared to the past. The Swiss withholding tax and the new exemption regime have a direct impact on the monthly net take-home pay and the overall economic advantage.

Operational details

The uneven trend of the Swiss regions

The phenomenon is not uniform throughout the Confederation. On an annual basis, the largest increases were recorded in the Geneva region with 2.8%, in the Plateau with 1.8%, and in northwestern Switzerland with 1%. Central Switzerland saw an increase of 2.7%, albeit on lower absolute numbers. This geographical variability reflects different dynamics in the different Swiss regional labour markets.

On the contrary, in addition to Ticino, two other strategic areas recorded a decrease: Zurich with −1.5%, the main financial hub of Switzerland, and eastern Switzerland with −1.2%. This means that the decline is not limited to the southern border, but also affects some northern and eastern regions, suggesting a more general slowdown in the Swiss cross-border market in the sectors and areas where flows from Italy were most significant.

Variability by country of origin

A further analysis emerges from the aggregate data by country of origin. In the last three years, the most significant increase has affected workers from France, while the number of those from Italy, Germany and Austria has remained substantially stable. This means that the Italian trend is not part of a general recession of border flows, but a specific dynamic of flows with the southern Italian border.

Key points

What it means for the Italian frontier worker

If you are an Italian frontier worker or considering transferring your residence in Switzerland to work in Ticino, these data have concrete implications for taxation and salary. The 2023 fiscal agreement introduced different thresholds between "older" frontier workers (with transition rights until 2033) and new frontier workers, directly impacting the source tax paid to the canton and the double taxation regime you faced before the reform.

Practical procedure: check your situation

If you are already a frontier worker, no immediate action is required. However, if you are evaluating whether to accept a position in Ticino or move your residence, it is advisable to analyze your fiscal situation in detail:

Frequently Asked Questions
In 2026, did the number of Italian border crossers in Switzerland decrease?
Yes, in the second quarter of 2026, entries from Italy fell by 1.1% year-on-year. In the Canton of Ticino, the decline was 0.6% per year. According to the Federal Statistical Office, 414,262 border workers were employed in Switzerland between April and June 2026, with a growth of 0.5% compared to the previous quarter and 1.3% compared to Q2 2025.
Why the decline in Italian border workers in 2026?
Two main factors explain the decline. The new tax agreement between Italy and Switzerland, which entered into force in July 2023, has modified the tax regime for new frontier workers, impacting tax at source and deductibles. Secondly, the improvement in working conditions in Italy, especially in the catering sector, has reduced the incentive to move to Switzerland.
Is Ticino the only Swiss region to decline in Q2 2026?
No. In addition to Ticino (−0.6%), Zurich (−1.5%) and eastern Switzerland (−1.2%) also recorded decreases on an annual basis. In contrast, the Leman region scored +2.8%, the Plateau +1.8%, northwestern Switzerland +1% and central Switzerland +2.7%.
How do I calculate my net salary as an Italian border worker in Ticino in 2026?
You need to consider Swiss source tax, AVS/LPP contributions and LAMal coverage. The new agreement of 2023 modified the allowances between "elderly" frontier workers (with transition until 2033) and new frontier workers. What professional profiles were affected by the 2026 decline?
According to GastroTicino, the decrease particularly affected the most qualified workers from Northern Italy, especially in the catering sector, where working conditions in Italy have improved significantly in recent years.
