Living in Fenegrò and working in Ticino as a border worker

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 21 September 2026

Practical guide on taxation, connections and costs for those who choose Fenegrò as a residence working in the Canton of Ticino.

Context

In brief

  • Withholding tax applied only in Switzerland
  • New Cross‑border Agreement in force from 1° gennaio 2024
  • Exemption of 10.000 € for new cross‑border workers
  • Exemption of 7.500 € for old cross‑border workers (until 2033)

Key facts

Cross‑border Agreement: signed 23/12/2020, effective 01/01/2024 Italy ratification: Law 83 of 13/06/2023 Double‑tax convention: 09/03/1976 Exemption for new cross‑border workers: 10.000 € Exemption for old cross‑border workers: 7.500 € Tax withholding: Only in Switzerland

The choice to reside in Fenegrò to work in the Canton of Ticino fits into a precise regulatory framework, mainly governed by the New Cross-Border Workers Agreement signed on 23 December 2020 and in force since 1 January 2024. This agreement, ratified in Italy by Law 83 of 13 June 2023, defines the tax parameters for those who cross the border daily. Switzerland, although not a member of the EU/EEA, maintains stable relations with Italy also based on the Convention to avoid double taxation signed on 9 March 1976.

The applicable tax regime

For those who work in Ticino and reside in Fenegrò, the withholding tax on employment income is withheld exclusively in Switzerland. Italy avoids double taxation through the application of a tax credit, which the taxpayer must indicate in the CE section of the income tax return (model 730). This mechanism ensures that the tax burden is correctly managed between the two states, without the worker paying the full tax in both countries.

The management of tax rates, both at federal and cantonal level, is the responsibility of the Federal Tax Administration (AFC/ESTV) and the respective cantonal administrations. It is important to note that entities such as the Federal Statistical Office (BFS) or UFAS are respectively responsible for data collection and social security, and they have no authority in setting tax rates. Operational details

The analysis of the convenience of living in Fenegrò involves evaluating different tax scenarios depending on the start date of the cross-border activity. The current system clearly distinguishes between those who were already cross-border workers before 17 July 2023 and those who started afterwards.

Differences between new and old cross-border workers

The so-called old cross-border workers benefit from a transitional regime covering the period 2024–2033, maintaining a tax exemption of 7.500 euros. Conversely, new cross-border workers, namely those who acquired the status after the cutoff date, can benefit from an allowance of 10.000 euros. This distinction is fundamental for calculating taxable income in Italy and for the possible determination of the tax credit.

From the perspective of social contributions, the Swiss system provides precise deductions that affect the payslip. The AVS/AI/IPG contributions are set at 5,3% of the employee's salary, while the unemployment insurance (AD/AC) absorbs 1,1%, with a maximum ceiling of 148.200 CHF. To these is added the LAINF, which ranges from 0,7% to 1,5%, and the occupational pension scheme LPP, with rates varying between 7% and 18% depending on the age bracket, starting from age 25.

The cost of living and health insurance

Living in Italy and working in Switzerland allows you to optimize the cost of living, but introduces the obligation to manage health insurance (LAMal). Cross-border workers with a G permit have an option right that allows them to choose between the Swiss system and that of their country of residence. In the case where LAMal is chosen, deductibles for adults range from 300 to 2,500 CHF. To compare the impact of these expenses relative to the Swiss salary, it is recommended to consult the data on cost of living between Ticino and Italy.

Key points

For those who decide to move to Fenegrò and start working in Ticino, it is necessary to follow a strict administrative procedure to regularize their tax and social security position.

Operational steps for the cross-border worker

The first step consists of obtaining the G permit, the document that attests to the status of a cross-border worker. Once employment begins, the employer will proceed with the withholding of tax at source, which will be paid to the Swiss tax authorities. In parallel, the worker must monitor the contributions paid to AVS and LPP for the future pension.

In Italy, the Revenue Agency and the MEF manage the receipt of income produced abroad. It is essential that the cross-border worker correctly files the tax return, using the CE section of the 730 form to request the tax credit and neutralize double taxation, as provided for by the 1976 Convention. For those who have doubts about completion or amounts, it is possible to use a simulator for the tax return.

Management of deadlines and entities

It is essential to keep track of communications coming from AFC/ESTV regarding taxes and from INPS for possible pension coordination. If one opts for Swiss health insurance, it is necessary to define the LAMAL deductible within the prescribed deadlines to avoid penalties or limited coverage.

Frequently Asked Questions
Where is the source tax paid for those who live in Fenegrò and work in Ticino?
Income tax at source is only withheld in Switzerland. To avoid double taxation, Italy grants a tax credit that the border worker must declare in the EC framework of the model 730.
What is the tax difference between new and old frontier workers?
Old border workers (active before 17/07/2023) enjoy a transitional regime 2024-2033 with an exemption of 7,500 euros. New frontier workers, on the other hand, are entitled to a deductible of 10,000 euros.
What are the mandatory social security contributions in Switzerland?
The main contributions include AVS/AI/IPG (5.3% paid by the employee), AD/AC (1.1% up to a cap of CHF 148,200), LAINF (0.7-1.5%) and LPP (7% to 18% based on age, starting at age 25).
