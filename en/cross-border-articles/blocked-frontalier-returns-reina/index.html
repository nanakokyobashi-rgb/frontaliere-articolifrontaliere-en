Cross-border rebates blocked: Reina (Iv Varese) warns of state credibility risk (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 2 July 2026

Salvino Reina, provincial secretary of Italia Viva, criticizes the blocking of cross-border rebates and warns of the risks to the credibility of the state.

Context

In brief - Salvino Reina criticizes the block on cross-border rebates. - Reina warns of the risks to the credibility of the State. - Rebates are a symbol of collaboration between territories. - What: Blocking of cross-border rebates. - When: June 30, 2024. - Where: Varese. - Who: Salvino Reina, provincial secretary of Italia Viva. - Amount: Not specified. The political earthquake produced by the freezing of cross-border rebates for Lombardy announced on June 30 by the Swiss Council of State continues to be discussed. After Alessandro Alfieri and Furio Artoni, Salvino Reina, provincial secretary of Italia Viva and councillor for the budget and cross-border workers of Saltrio, also expressed his opinion on the issue. "For too long now, the centre-right government, both national and regional, has been dealing with an issue with lightness and superficiality which, instead, represents a test for the credibility of the institutions", commented Reina. A message addressed both to Minister Giancarlo Giorgetti and to the President of the Lombardy Region, Attilio Fontana. "Perhaps it is good to remind him that the border territories are not peripheries of the state but European laboratories where every day the ability of institutions to collaborate beyond national affiliations is measured. They sprinkle their heads with ashes because, however embarrassing, the time has come to take a step back on a "health tax" that has caused nothing but problems in Italian-Swiss relations and that in any case has already amply demonstrated to be unconstitutional, difficult to apply, unfair and useless for the objective it sets itself". ### Implications for cross-border commuters For Italia Viva, putting the rebate mechanism at risk would not only be "useless", but even "very dangerous". "Rebates to municipalities are not a privilege or a political concession. They are the symbol of a pact between territories that share the economy, infrastructure and people. The Meloni government and the region led by the Northern League member Fontana are cracking this pact in an absurd and inexplicable way". Letting this happen "would mean compromising a heritage of collaboration built over decades of cross-border relations. The real stakes are not only the transfer of a few tens of millions of euros but also the quality of relations between Italy and Switzerland and the trust of border communities in the institutions that should represent them. And that trust, once lost, is much more difficult to repay than any rebate."

Operational details

Analysis of the implications The blocking of cross-border rebates has significant implications for cross-border commuters working in Switzerland and residing in Italy. Rebates are a form of financial compensation that Italian municipalities receive from Switzerland for public services offered to cross-border commuters. This mechanism is crucial to maintain an economic balance between the two countries and to ensure that cross-border commuters can continue to benefit from the necessary public services. ### Impact on cross-border collaboration The decision to freeze rebates could jeopardise the collaboration between Italy and Switzerland, which has been built over decades. This cooperation pact is essential for the operation of border crossing points, such as Brogeda and Chiasso, and for the management of cross-border traffic. A deterioration in relations could lead to delays and complications for cross-border commuters who cross the border on a daily basis. ### Risks to the credibility of the state Salvino Reina stressed that the credibility of the state is at stake. The ability of institutions to comply with international agreements is crucial to maintaining the trust of border communities. A step back on the issue of rebates could be seen as a sign of weakness and inability to keep the commitments made. ### Possible scenarios If the block on rebates continues, Italian municipalities could find themselves in financial difficulties, with possible cuts to public services. This could have a direct impact on the quality of life of cross-border commuters, who could be deprived of essential services such as health and education. In addition, the situation could lead to social tensions and an increase in discontent among cross-border workers. ### Comparison with the previous situation Before the rebate freeze, Italian municipalities benefited from financial compensation that allowed them to offer adequate public services to cross-border commuters. This system has worked for decades, helping to maintain an economic balance between the two countries. The decision to freeze rebates represents a disruption of this balance and could have long-term consequences. ### Concrete procedures Cross-border commuters must be aware of the implications of this decision and the possible actions they can take. It is important to monitor developments and prepare for any changes in administrative procedures. Consulting with a tax expert can be helpful in better understanding your personal implications and planning for any changes.
