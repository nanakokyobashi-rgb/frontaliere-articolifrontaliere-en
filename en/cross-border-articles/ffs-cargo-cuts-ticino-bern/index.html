FFS cargo cuts: Bern responds to Ticino (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 16 September 2026

The federal government rejects the resolution of the Canton of Ticino on the cuts of FFS Cargo, confirming the management autonomy of the company and the centrality of the north-south axis.

Context

In brief

  • Bern does not intervene on SBB Cargo cuts
  • The canton's parliamentary resolution rejected
  • No room for strategic change
  • Centrality of the north-south corridor confirmed

Key facts

  • Questioned: Albert Rösti
  • Recipient: Daria Lepori
  • Period of cuts: 2025 and this year
  • Destination of the letter: President of the Grand Council

The freight traffic landscape along the north-south axis records a clear stance by the federal government regarding the parliamentary resolution submitted to counter the dismantling of SBB Cargo's activities in the Canton of Ticino. Arriving during the summer on the desk of Grand Council President Daria Lepori, the official response signed by the head of the Federal Department of the Environment, Transport, Energy and Communications, Albert Rösti, formally closes the door to any corrective intervention by Bern. The federal executive reiterates its firm intention to strictly apply the legal provisions currently in force, maintaining a line of consistency with respect to the framework conditions established at the national level for the transport market. The stance comes following the resolution submitted to the Federal Palace in the month of May, a political act born with the objective of mobilizing central institutions in the face of operational reductions implemented by the Swiss Federal Railways between 2025 and the current year, which first hit the combined transport sector and subsequently that of single wagonload freight. On decisions of an occupational and internal nature to the company, the Federal Council's note is unequivocal, specifying that such choices fall entirely within the management autonomy of SBB, without the government exercising direct influence. The political direction of the Federal Executive toward the transport company materializes solely through the definition of strategic objectives assigned to the owner, while operational management remains entrusted to market dynamics within the general regulatory framework. Despite concerns raised at the cantonal level for the economic and logistical future of the region, the Federal Council expressed satisfaction that no outright layoffs occurred in Ticino, highlighting that the affected staff were able to benefit from internal relocation options, with over half of the workers involved continuing to operate in the rail freight traffic sector.

The centrality of the transalpine railway line

In the official document transmitted to the Ticino authorities, the central government strongly reiterates that the strategic centrality of the Canton of Ticino along the north-south transit axis remains a fixed point of federal transport policy. However, this territorial confirmation does not translate into a brake on corporate decisions adopted by the railway management. According to what was clarified by the head of the competent federal department, there are no regulatory or political prerequisites for the transport company to back down on the decisions undertaken, nor are there margins for action for the federal government to impose a change in the strategic course undertaken by the railway company. The planning and execution of the operational cuts reflect the structural setup defined by current regulations, leaving unchanged the perimeter of action within which SBB manages its commercial traffic flows and heavy rail transport logistics.

Operational details

The in-depth analysis of the response received from Bern highlights complex dynamics that characterize the relationship between the business choices of large public service companies and the institutional expectations of peripheral or border regions. The stance expressed by the Federal Council highlights a gap between the requests for intervention formulated by local political bodies and the constitutional and legal limits that regulate the action of the central administration regarding former federal monopolies. For economic operators and for those who live and work in the cross-border fabric, these business decisions reflect a profound transformation of the logistics and transport sector, where competitive pressure and the pursuit of economic efficiency end up prevailing over traditional functions of capillary service to the territory. The decision to abandon a binding modal shift objective for domestic freight traffic, decided by parliament during the recent total revision of the freight transport law, demonstrates that the reference legislative framework does not provide specific coercive instruments to force the movement of goods by rail outside of the major trans-Alpine routes. This legislative approach consolidates a market regime in which SBB's business choices enjoy a wide margin of maneuver, aimed at restoring financial health and optimizing operational processes, even when this entails a contraction of regional services linked to single-wagonload freight or proximity combined transport.

Future scenarios for freight transport and regional logistics

The repercussions of this political and economic orientation extend well beyond the immediate trade union or employment dispute, directly touching the overall logistical architecture of the Ticino territory. The confirmation that current instruments for the promotion of rail freight traffic are considered adequate by the federal government excludes the introduction of new mandates or targeted subsidies to support unprofitable domestic traffic. For Ticino companies that depend on rail freight transport, the panorama outlined by Bern's response imposes a strategic reflection on the supply chain and the need to integrate with the main logistical corridors, accepting a model in which proximity services undergo progressive dismantling in favor of centralized hubs. The transition of personnel, while having avoided mass layoffs thanks to internal relocation measures, still marks a contraction of direct operational presence in the cantonal territory, reducing the capillarity of railway logistics skills available locally. The current situation confirms that the Swiss freight transport market is moving towards greater corporate concentration, leaving cantonal authorities with limited room for maneuver to influence the industrial strategies of a federal company that responds primarily to budget and efficiency constraints established by the owner and by federal public transport laws.
