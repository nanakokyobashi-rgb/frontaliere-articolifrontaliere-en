ECB and SNB: What's Next for Italy-Switzerland Border Residents (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 3 August 2026

The decisions of the European and Swiss central banks affect the CHF/EUR exchange rate, which has implications for cross-border commuters.

Context

In brief

  • ECB and SNB left interest rates unchanged.
  • The interest rate differential affects the CHF/EUR exchange rate.
  • The SNB is ready to intervene in the foreign exchange market.

Key Facts

  • What: ECB and SNB left interest rates unchanged.
  • When: ECB on 23 July, SNB on 18 June.
  • Where: Decisions taken in Frankfurt and Zurich.
  • Who: ECB and SNB.
  • Amount: ECB rates: 2.25%, 2.40%, 2.65%. SNB rate: 0%.

At six in the morning, at the customs of Chiasso or Ponte Chiasso, the queue of cars is already long. Those who travel it every day to go to work have learned to check the exchange rate of the day even before traffic: it is now an automatic gesture, because the salary arrives in francs and the groceries, rent, mortgage remain in euros. On July 23, the European Central Bank left its key interest rates unchanged, confirming the pause after the June hike. The Swiss National Bank, for its part, had already been stuck at zero since June 18.

For those who live on this double border, the simultaneous shutdown of the two central banks is not a detail for a news agency: it defines the terrain on which the exchange rate will move in the coming weeks.

Operational details

This is where the two decisions talk to each other. A wider rate differential tends to make the franc relatively more attractive to those seeking a haven from volatility, supporting its value against the euro. An SNB that is ready to intervene is pushing in the opposite direction, to contain precisely that strengthening. Which of the two forces will prevail is not decided at the table: it depends on how inflation, energy prices and capital flows evolve in the coming weeks, not on a statement published today. It is worth saying clearly: this article describes monetary policy decisions that have already been made public, not exchange rate forecasts or investment advice. For significant amounts, the reference remains a qualified consultant.

Between the end of summer and mid-September, two events focus attention. The first is the ECB meeting on 9-10 September in Berlin: the central bank has hinted that a new hike remains possible if the energy shock worsens. The second is the SNB's quarterly valuation, which is expected around the same time — some market calendars indicate the end of September, but at the time of writing, the SNB had not yet published an official date. However, it has already shown with its announcement in June that it considers the level of the franc a priority.

Key points

The decisions of the European and Swiss central banks directly affect the CHF/EUR exchange rate, with significant implications for border crossers living between Italy and Switzerland. The stability of interest rates and the SNB's willingness to intervene in the foreign exchange market create an environment of uncertainty that requires attention and planning. Following ECB and SNB meetings closely, reading official press releases and using transparent currency exchange tools are essential steps to better manage your finances in this context.

Frequently Asked Questions
What does the contemporary standstill of central banks mean for frontier workers?
The simultaneous standstill of central banks means that the CHF/EUR exchange rate could remain stable in the coming weeks. However, it is important to monitor ECB and SNB meetings for any changes.
How can I prepare for the upcoming ECB and SNB meetings?
Mark the dates of the meetings in the calendar and read the official communiqués carefully. Use transparent currency exchange tools to check the conditions applied to your exchanges.
What are the practical implications of the rate differential for frontier workers?
A wider rate differential tends to make the franc relatively more attractive, supporting its value against the euro. This can affect the purchasing power of frontier workers who receive a salary in francs and have expenses in euros.
