Operational details

The aggregation of Basso Mendrisiotto is an ambitious project aiming to create a larger and stronger municipality, but it is not without challenges. Among the main issues raised are financial tensions between the involved municipalities, with Chiasso emerging as the main point of discussion. Vacallo Mayor Marco Rizza pointed the finger at Chiasso, accusing it of a lack of self-criticism on the financial situation. 'Some self-criticism would be necessary,' Rizza declared. 'Financial humility is needed. This attitude could slow down the process.' This criticism is particularly significant given that Chiasso is the largest municipality in the area and has a current tax multiplier of 88%, higher than that of other municipalities in the region, such as Mendrisio (85%) and Riva San Vitale (80%). This means Chiasso contributes more significantly to the balance of the future aggregated municipality. At the center of the discussion remains the tax multiplier of the future municipality. The aggregation study assumes an average of around 95%, a percentage higher than that of several municipalities in the region. This could have significant repercussions on the budget of the new municipality, which will need to find a balance between the different financial needs of the involved municipalities. For example, if the tax multiplier of the new municipality were 95%, the municipality would have to manage a higher share of local taxes, which could influence budgets for public services such as schools, hospitals, and infrastructure. Vacallo Mayor Marco Rizza also confirmed that the project is proceeding, but with some challenges that need to be addressed with great attention. 'We are proceeding, but there are some challenges that we need to address with great attention,' he explained. 'The project is not at risk of being abandoned. The path continues, but it is clear that there are challenges that need to be resolved before reaching the population.' One of the main challenges concerns the current regulations. The aggregation of municipalities is regulated by the 2009 cantonal law, which states that the tax multiplier of the new municipality cannot exceed 100%. However, the Basso Mendrisiotto aggregation study assumes an average of around 95%, meaning the new municipality would need to request an exception to the cantonal regulation. This process can be long and complex and may require an in-depth analysis by cantonal authorities. Another crucial aspect is the management of human resources. Aggregation involves the need to integrate the administrations of the involved municipalities, which requires significant reorganization. This process can be made easier with accurate planning and transparent communication between municipalities. For example, the municipality of Balerna, which recently completed an aggregation process with Riva San Vitale, created an operational checklist for human resource management, which includes steps such as identifying key competencies, training staff, and defining roles and responsibilities. The popular vote, initially scheduled for June 2027, might be postponed to allow for further in-depth studies and revisions of the report. This could extend the project timeline and create further uncertainties for the involved citizens. For example, in the case of the aggregation of Balerna and Riva San Vitale, the vote was postponed by one year due to technical issues and necessary in-depth studies. This led to a period of uncertainty for the citizens, who had to wait for further clarifications before expressing their vote. In summary, the aggregation of Basso Mendrisiotto is a project with the potential to create a stronger and more efficient municipality, but it requires work on self-criticism and collaboration between the involved municipalities to overcome financial and political challenges. The tensions between Chiasso and the other involved municipalities must be addressed with humility and transparency, and the project must proceed with accurate planning and open communication with the population.
