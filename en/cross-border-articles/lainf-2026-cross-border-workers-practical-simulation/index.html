The LAINF for Ticino border guards: protection guaranteed

LAINF is Switzerland's compulsory occupational accident insurance. For Ticino border crossers who daily change the crossings of Brogeda, Chiasso or Gaggiolo, it represents one of the pillars of social protection. The cost is minimal for the worker: the employer pays the LAINF contribution equal to 0.7–1.5% of the wage bill. In return, the border worker receives full coverage in the event of an accident at work, an accident on the way home from work, and recognised occupational diseases.

Operational details

How LAINF Coverage Works: Practical Scenarios for Border Workers

The LAINF provides three distinct levels of protection. The first is the accident during working hours in the company: any accident that occurred within the company structure is covered. A border worker who works in a Ticino factory and suffers a bruise, a fracture, a burn is immediately protected. SUVA intervenes to cover medical expenses, days of absence and, if permanent, the corresponding compensation.

The second level concerns the accident on the commute, during the home-work journey. For border workers who daily travel the road to Chiasso, Brogeda or Mendrisio, LAINF covers accidents that occurred along the direct and reasonable route between the Italian residence and the Swiss workplace. If you get injured at the crossing during the usual commute (fall while crossing the bridge, road accident at the border), the coverage exists. However, if the journey is extraordinary or unjustified (voluntary deviation for personal reasons), coverage may be denied. SUVA assesses the case on the basis of the reasonableness of the course and consistency with usual practice.

The third level is occupational disease. Unlike an accident, which is punctual, an occupational disease progressively sets in. Common examples: herniated disc from prolonged manual work, respiratory allergies from exposure to substances

Operational checklist: what to do after a workplace accident

Immediately after the accident, the cross-border commuter must follow a precise sequence of actions. The first steps determine the quality and speed of the compensation claim.

Within the next few hours (same day of the accident):

  • Inform the employer verbally and, if possible, also in writing (email, WhatsApp with timestamp)
  • Request immediate medical assistance if necessary (hospital, emergency room, primary care physician)
  • Keep the initial medical receipt or report
  • Photograph the accident scene if possible (workplace, border crossing, road) and the sustained damage (abrasions, swelling, visible fractures)

Within the first working day:

  • Formally request the UVG/LAINF accident report form from the employer or the name of the designated insurer
  • Collect the names of any witnesses (colleagues present at the time)
  • Obtain a medical certificate of unfitness for work (Arbeitsunfähigkeitszeugnis) with the start date and expected duration

Within 3 working days (absolute deadline):

  • The employer must file an official report to SUVA using the 'Accident Report' form
  • Personally verify with the employer that the report has been submitted (ask for written confirmation)
  • If the employer fails to act, the cross-border commuter independently submits the report to SUVA with medical documentation

Subsequent days (during convalescence):

  • Stay in contact with the SUVA office to monitor the status of the claim
  • Deliver all medical reports (specialist visits, physiotherapy, diagnostic exams) to SUVA
  • Verify the continuity of payments during the absence: UVG/LAINF covers 80% of the salary up to the 90th day
  • Keep a copy of all sent documents for your personal records

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the difference between LAINF and occupational disease?
LAINF is the general insurance against accidents at work. It covers two categories: acute injuries (sudden traumatic event such as a fall) and occupational diseases (injuries that develop over time due to work, such as a herniated disc). The occupational disease requires more robust medical documentation to prove the causal link with the work activity. Both are managed by SUVA, but with slightly different procedures for the acceptance of the practice.
How much time do I have to report an accident to my employer and SUVA?
You border worker must inform the employer IMMEDIATELY (same day, if possible). The employer is obliged to file a formal complaint with SUVA within 3 working days of the accident. If the employer does not comply, you can file the complaint directly with SUVA. Exceeding the 3-day deadline risks reducing the recognized performance and lengthening the acceptance time of the practice.
If I get injured while traveling home from work, am I covered?
Yes. LAINF covers injuries during the direct and reasonable commute between your residence and the workplace. For border crossers Ticino-Italy who cross the crossings of Brogeda, Chiasso, Gaggiolo or Ponte Tresa, an injury during the usual commuting is covered. However, if the route is extraordinary or unjustified (voluntary deviation for personal reasons), coverage may be denied by SUVA.
During injury leave, do I still receive my full salary?
No. LAINF covers 80% of your salary for the first 90 days of absence. The allowance is paid by SUVA to the employer or directly to you, according to the company's procedures. After 90 days, if the disability persists, SUVA determines whether to recognize a temporary or permanent annuity. In the meantime, your AVS, AI, and LPP contributions continue regularly without interruption.
How does an accident affect my tax return as a border worker?
If you are a G Permit border worker residing in Italy, the reduced income due to absence from accident is still declared both in Switzerland and in Italy. The LAINF allowance (80% of the salary) is considered income from work and subject to taxation in Switzerland with withholding tax.
