KOF Barometer: modest recovery for Swiss economy

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 30 April 2026

The KOF barometer signals a slight improvement in April, but prospects remain modest below the multi-year average.

Context

In brief

  • KOF Barometer at 97.9 points in April
  • Improvement of 2.3 points compared to March
  • Economic prospects remain modest

Key facts

  • What: KOF Barometer
  • When: April 2026
  • Where: Switzerland
  • Who: KOF Institute of the Swiss Federal Institute of Technology Zurich
  • Amount: 97.9 points

The KOF barometer, the business climate indicator of the KOF Institute of the Swiss Federal Institute of Technology Zurich, recorded a slight improvement in April, standing at 97.9 points. This value represents an increase of 2.3 points compared to the March data, revised from 96.1 to 95.6, which was the lowest since mid-2025. Despite the improvement, the parameter remains below the multi-year average of 100 points, indicating modest economic prospects for the Swiss economy.

Sectors in recovery

Indicators from the manufacturing industry, services, and consumer demand contributed to the increase in the index. For example, in the manufacturing sector, the export of machinery and equipment recorded a 3.2% increase compared to March, with a significant increase in exports to the European Union. The services sector also showed signs of recovery, with a 1.8% increase in consulting and IT activities.

However, negative signals come from the hotel and catering sector, with a 2.5% drop in hotel bookings in the Canton of Ticino, particularly in tourist destinations such as Lugano and Locarno. The indices relating to foreign demand, financial and insurance services, as well as construction are virtually unchanged.

Implications for cross-border workers

For cross-border workers employed in Switzerland, this modest economic situation could influence job opportunities and salaries. It is important to monitor the evolution of the labor market and fiscal policies, such as withholding tax and AVS contributions, which could be influenced by these economic prospects.

📊 Operational checklist for cross-border workers:

  • Check the latest tax regulations for cross-border workers, such as withholding tax, which in 2026 is set at 35% for residents in Italy.
  • Check AVS contributions, which for cross-border workers are 10.25% of gross salary.
  • Monitor job offers in recovering sectors, such as manufacturing and IT services.
  • Consider the possibility of training in sectors with higher demand, such as engineering and consulting.

Comparison with the previous situation

Compared to previous months, the KOF barometer shows a timid sign of recovery, but not robust enough to indicate a complete recovery. This could mean that Swiss companies are still facing significant challenges, indirectly affecting cross-border workers who depend on the Swiss economy for their livelihood.

💡 Practical example: A cross-border worker from Chiasso, employed in the manufacturing sector, could benefit from the increase in exports to the EU, with possible salary increases or performance-related bonuses. However, a worker in the hotel sector in Lugano may face greater difficulties, with possible reductions in hours or layoffs.

⚠️ Warning: Modest economic prospects could lead to greater competition for jobs, especially in struggling sectors. It is essential for cross-border workers to stay updated on job opportunities and tax policies to quickly adapt to market changes.

Tax Policies and Contributions

Modest economic prospects could also influence tax policies and mandatory contributions such as AHV (Old-Age and Survivors' Insurance) and BVG (Professional Pension). For example, the Swiss Federal Council has proposed an increase in AHV contributions from 2024, with a 0.1% increase for employers and workers.

📊 Relevant Data:

  • AHV Contributions: Currently, AHV contributions are 8.4% of gross salary, with an expected increase to 8.5% in 2024.
  • BVG: The minimum mandatory contributions for professional pension are 7% of gross salary, with a variable part depending on the employer.

💡 Operational Checklist for Cross-Border Workers:

  • Financial Planning: Consult a financial advisor to optimize AHV and BVG contributions.
  • Investments: Consider long-term investment options, such as private pension funds or savings accounts.
  • Documentation: Keep tax and pension documents up to date to avoid penalties.

Given the current economic context, it is essential that cross-border workers take concrete measures to protect their financial and professional well-being. Here are some steps they can follow:

2. Diversify skills: Invest in continuous training and acquiring new skills to increase competitiveness in the job market. This can include professional update courses or certifications in growing sectors. This can include creating an emergency fund, investing in pension plans like the LPP, and managing AVS contributions. For example, an emergency fund should cover at least 3-6 months of living expenses. For a cross-border worker who spends CHF 3,000 per month, this means saving between CHF 9,000 and CHF 18,000.

4. Explore cross-border opportunities: Consider job opportunities both in Switzerland and Italy, taking advantage of tax benefits such as tax refunds and withholding tax. Use tools like the permit comparator to evaluate available options. 5. Stay informed: Follow economic news and tax policies that could affect their work and financial situation. Subscribe to newsletters and participate in webinars to stay updated on the latest news.
