32-year-old Swiss woman seriously injured in wingsuit accident in Austria (cross-border guide)

A 32-year-old Swiss citizen was seriously injured in a wingsuit accident in Hohenems, Austria, during a flight with 15 jumpers.

Key facts

  • What: Wingsuit accident
  • When: 9 May 2026
  • Where: Hohenems, Austria
  • Who: Austrian police
  • Amount: 15 jumpers involved

A wingsuit accident that occurred today in Hohenems, Austria, involved a Swiss citizen who was seriously injured, according to the Austrian police. The 32-year-old Swiss woman collided with a 48-year-old German woman during a flight involving a total of 15 jumpers. According to initial reports, the young woman lost consciousness while turning to the right. Her parachute opened automatically, and after touching a high-voltage line, she fell into the Rhine, from where she was brought ashore by two bathers.

The German citizen managed to open her parachute but also hit an electrical line and fell from a height of 10 meters. The group jumped from an airplane at about 4,000 meters altitude. The parachutes, used for landing, were seized.

Concrete actions for cross-border workers

For cross-border workers who practice extreme sports, it's important to follow some concrete actions to ensure their safety. Here's a step-by-step guide on what to do:

1. Check your health insurance

Make sure you have adequate health insurance that covers any accidents abroad. Contact INPS or a private insurance company to verify your coverage.

2. Memorize emergency numbers

Memorize the general emergency number of the country where you practice the extreme sport. In Austria, the number is 112.

3. Attend safety courses

Sign up for extreme sports safety courses. These courses can provide valuable information on how to prevent accidents and what to do in case of an emergency.

4. Buy adequate equipment

Buy high-quality equipment and make sure it's always in good condition. Perform regular checks and replace the equipment when necessary.

5. Learn about local regulations

Learn about local regulations regarding extreme sports. Each country has its own laws and regulations, so it's important to know those of the place where you practice the sport.

6. Create a safety plan

Create a safety plan with your sports partners. The plan should include emergency procedures, meeting points, and emergency contacts.

7. Practice with an experienced partner

Always practice with an experienced partner. Having someone with you who knows the sport and safety procedures well can make a difference in case of an emergency.

8. Always carry an emergency kit

Always carry an emergency kit that includes a phone, a flashlight, a first aid kit, and other emergency tools.

9. Share your plan with someone

Share your safety plan with someone who is not participating in the activity. This person should know where you are and what to do in case of an emergency.
