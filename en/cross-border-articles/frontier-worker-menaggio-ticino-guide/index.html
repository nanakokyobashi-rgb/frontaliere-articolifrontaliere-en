Menaggio, a town on Lake Como, is a popular choice among cross-border commuters working in Ticino. The lakeside quality of life, access to Italian services, and a generally lower cost of living than in Ticino attract young people, families, and professionals in the healthcare, construction, services, and industrial sectors. But moving to Menaggio and starting cross-border employment requires an understanding of three pillars: the work permit, CH-IT taxation, and the Swiss social security system.

The G Permit: authorization and restrictions

To work legally in Ticino while maintaining residence in Italy, you need the G Permit (cross-border commuter). It is the main authorization for CH-IT cross-border employment, tied to a specific employment contract and subject to federal administrative issuance rules. The validity of the G Permit and the renewal conditions depend on Swiss regulations and bilateral CH-IT agreements, including the New Cross-Border Commuter Agreement applicable from January 1, 2024. Issuance requires the approval of SEM (State Secretariat for Migration) and the canton of Ticino; processing times usually range from 2 to 8 weeks.

Double taxation and tax rebates: how it works

A critical issue for cross-border commuters concerns withholding tax. Switzerland withholds it directly from the payslip (there is no parallel withholding in Italy on the same income source). Italy recognizes a tax credit in the CE section of the 730 tax return to avoid double taxation, under the Italian-Swiss Convention signed on March 9, 1976.

The New Cross-Border Commuter Agreement (applied since January 1, 2024) introduced tax exemptions for cross-border employment income. Legacy cross-border commuters continue to be taxed only in Switzerland (Article 9 of the 2020 Agreement). For the purposes of Article 9 of the Agreement, “legacy cross-border commuters” are people who met the tax requirements and worked in Graubünden, Ticino, or Valais on July 17, 2023, or during the period from December 31, 2018 to July 17, 2023. For employment income covered by Article 9, taxation remains exclusively at source in Switzerland; for other workers, the ordinary regime applies, with taxation in Italy, a credit for Swiss tax, and Swiss withholding at 80% of the ordinary rates. The 10.000 euro amount specified in Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is merely the income threshold referred to in Article 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013 and not a general exemption for new cross-border commuters. Operational details

Advantages of living in Menaggio and working in Ticino

The choice to reside in Menaggio offers concrete advantages for the cross-border worker. The cost of properties (purchase or rent) is generally lower than in Ticino, reducing the economic pressure on the family unit. Access to Italian cultural, health and administrative services remains manageable, just as the community is often stable and aware of the border reality. The natural landscape of Lake Como offers a recognized quality of life.

On an economic level, the Swiss salary - even after faithful tax withholdings - remains competitive compared to equivalent Italian salaries for the same professional profile. Furthermore, the G Permit allows the right of option on KVG (Swiss health insurance). Many cross-border commuters choose to maintain Italian coverage (National Health System) or enroll in a Swiss KVG depending on family needs, costs and the preferred network of healthcare facilities. The KVG deductibles for adults vary between CHF 300 and CHF 2,500. This flexibility is an added value.

Disadvantages: Swiss social contributions and commuting

The downside is substantial. Swiss social contributions are mandatory and affect your paycheck. AVS (old age/disability insurance) costs 5.3% to be paid by the employee. The LAI (occupational accident insurance) varies from 0.7% to 1.5%. The LPP (professional pension, second pillar) varies from 7% to 18% based on the age group of the worker (starting from 25 years). Added up, these withdrawals amount to approximately 14-25% of your gross paycheck, depending on contract setup and age.

In addition to social security contributions, the daily or weekly Menaggio-Ticino commute involves physical wear and tear (long journey by car or public transport), transport costs (petrol, car maintenance, or public passes), and significant time away from home and family. For those with school-age children, managing the logistics of commuting can be complex.

Key points

Transfer procedure: the key steps

If you decide to move your residence to Menaggio and start work in Ticino, here are the essential steps in order:

1. Finding the employment contract in Ticino: Searching for a Swiss employer is the first step. The contract must be regular and for an indefinite period (or for a prolonged period). Limited casual work may require Permit L instead of G.

2. Registration with the Swiss authorities: The employer (or his HR intermediary) submits the request for the G Permit to the SEM (State Secretariat for Migration) in Switzerland. The procedure includes checks of compliance with the Swiss labor market to verify that the salary and conditions comply with cantonal practice.

3. Change of residence in Italy: Parallel to the Swiss request, you will have to register with the Municipality of Menaggio (change of Italian residence). At the same time, register with the Ticino cantonal administration (Population, Migration and Civil Rights Office or equivalent) to formalize the border situation.

4. Opening a Swiss bank account: Almost all Swiss employers pay their salaries into a CH bank account. It is useful to open an account with a Ticino or Swiss bank; some Italian banks offer accounts for cross-border workers with cross-border management.

5. INPS registration as a cross-border worker: Even if you work in Switzerland, you must regularize your Italian social security position with the INPS, declaring your status as a cross-border worker. This allows you to receive the annual tax credit.

6. Communication to tax services: Register with the Italian Revenue Agency for the annual declaration 730 (CE framework), essential for the tax credit on Swiss payments.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the G Leave and who can have it?
The G Permit is the work authorization for border workers who reside in Italy and work in Switzerland. It can be obtained by an Italian citizen with a regular employment contract in Ticino (indefinite or long-term). It is issued by the Swiss SEM (Secretariat of State for Migration) at the request of the employer and has a renewable annual validity. The release takes about 2-8 weeks and the consent of the canton of Ticino.
How does double taxation between Italy and Switzerland work?
New cross-border workers also declare their income in Italy and recover the Swiss tax through the tax credit, while old cross-border workers remain taxed only in Switzerland (Art. 9 of the 2020 Agreement). Italy recognises a tax credit in the EC framework of declaration 730, on the basis of the Italian-Swiss Convention of 9 March 1976. In practice, the employee pays taxes in Switzerland, but must declare income in Italy to recover the tax credit and avoid double penalties.
How much do Swiss social contributions cost and how do they affect the paycheck?
Swiss social contributions are mandatory: AVS (old age/disability) 5.3%, LAINF (accidents) 0.7-1.5%, LPP (occupational pension) 7-18% based on age (from 25 years). Added together, they amount to about 14-25% of the gross paycheck. Article 9 keeps covered employment income taxable exclusively at source in Switzerland; other workers follow the ordinary regime, with ordinary taxation in Italy, a credit for Swiss tax and Swiss withholding at 80% of the ordinary rates. Do I have the right to choose Italian or Swiss health insurance?
