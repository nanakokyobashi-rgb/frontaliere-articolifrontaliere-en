Sound-absorbing barriers: noise on the Varese connector road (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 26 September 2026

A reader's letter reports an echo or vibration every time a car drives on state road 707, audible in three areas of Varese, and asks ANAS when it will be resolved.

Context

In brief

  • Barriers installed on state road 707
  • Noise every time cars pass
  • Sounds audible in three areas of Varese
  • Appeal to ANAS: when will it be resolved?

Key facts

  • Intervention → sound-absorbing barriers
  • Road → state road 707
  • Purpose → reduce the noise of passing cars
  • Locations indicated → via Goldoni, viale Borri and largo Flaiano
  • Reported effect → echo/vibration every time a car passes
  • Recipient of the appeal → ANAS

The installation of sound-absorbing barriers on state road 707 is at the center of a report addressed to ANAS. The intervention, carried out to reduce the noise caused by cars traveling along Varese's motorway link, is described by the reader as a paradoxical case: instead of decreasing, the noise would have become extremely bothersome.

The letter received and published also indicates where the phenomenon would be perceptible. The sound is heard in via Goldoni, viale Borri and largo Flaiano. The description therefore links the problem to a specific stretch of road and to three explicitly named points in the city, without extending the report to other areas.

The account contained in the letter

The passage described is always the same: a car passes, noise is produced, and the noise can be heard in the streets mentioned. The reader is not speaking only of an occasional disturbance, but of an effect that recurs every time cars pass. In the reader's account, the barrier installed to absorb the sound would therefore be associated with an acoustic response opposite to the purpose of the intervention.

“I suspect an error in the installation of the sound-absorbing barriers, which produces an annoying echo/vibration whenever a car passes.”

The hypothesis concerns an installation error. It is the reader who links that error to the echo or vibration; the sentence is presented as an assumption within an appeal, not as the result of a technical assessment reported in the text.

The final point of the letter is a request to ANAS, made through the newspaper: when will the problem be resolved? The question reflects the practical nature of the report. It does not ask for a general explanation about the barriers, but for an answer regarding the disturbance produced by their effect, as perceived when cars pass.

The news therefore remains anchored to three elements: the intervention on state road 707, the reported acoustic effect and the request for a solution. The source presents the text as a reader's letter received and published. The heart of the report is the contrast between the stated purpose of the barriers and the experience described in the streets near the link road.

The contrast between purpose and effect

The term sound-absorbing identifies the intended function of the intervention: reducing noise. The reader instead describes the opposite effect, with an extremely annoying sound every time a vehicle passes. This opposition is the practical key to the case: the report does not challenge the idea of barriers in the abstract, but asks for a solution to what, according to the writer, occurred after their installation.

How to follow up on the report

The published letter offers a concrete outline for describing a similar problem without turning a hypothesis into a certainty. The first step is to indicate the work concerned: the sound-absorbing barriers installed by ANAS on state road 707, on the Varese motorway link. In this way, the recipient immediately receives the reference used in the original report.

The second step consists of describing the phenomenon with the same precision: an extremely annoying noise every time cars pass. The letter adds the places where the sound would be audible, via Goldoni, viale Borri and largo Flaiano. These details are already present in the text and are enough to delimit the problem described.

Four elements to keep separate

  • Work: the sound-absorbing barriers on state road 707.
  • Perceived effect: an echo or vibration as each car passes.
  • Hypothesized cause: a possible installation error.
  • Request: to know from ANAS when the problem will be resolved.

The distinction between effect and cause is the most delicate point. In the source, the noise is described as the reader's experience; the installation error, on the other hand, is presented as a hypothesis. A faithful report must preserve this difference and not present the hypothesis as a technical verification. The request must also be kept in its original form: it is a question addressed to ANAS through the newspaper.

Frequently Asked Questions
What does the reader report about sound-absorbing barriers?
According to the letter published, the barriers installed by ANAS on state road 707 to reduce car noise would have produced the opposite effect. An extremely annoying noise, described as echo or vibration, would be heard at every car pass. The reader links the problem to a possible installation error.
In which places would the noise be audible?
The noise is indicated as audible in Via Goldoni, Viale Borri and Largo Flaiano. The source then places the signalling in the area of the Varese motorway link and reports that the phenomenon occurs when cars pass by. These are the points named in the received and published text.
Who is the request addressed to and what does it ask?
The appeal is addressed to ANAS through the newspaper. The reader asks when the problem of the noise that, according to his report, occurs with each passage of the cars will be solved. The source presents the request as a question on the solution of the disorder, without indicating an already fixed deadline.
Were the barriers supposed to reduce noise?
Yes. The letter says that ANAS installed them on state road 707 with the purpose of reducing the noise caused by cars in transit. However, the reader argues that the opposite effect has occurred and assumes that the installation produces an echo or an annoying vibration when the cars pass.
