Injury on routes: LAINF coverage for border workers (cross-border guide)

When a border crosser suffers an accident on the way home from work: how LAINF coverage works, legal differences and the reporting procedure

Context

In a nutshell

  • LAINF automatically covers accidents that occurred during the ordinary commute for all employees, including border workers
  • An accident on itineraries is distinct from work trips with different rules and timing
  • The complaint must be communicated immediately to the employer, who is obliged to transmit it to the insurer

Key facts

  • What: Accident during the ordinary journey between home and place of work (on routes)
  • Where: Switzerland, on the way to work
  • Who: Employees with Swiss contract, including border workers with G Permit
  • Coverage: LAINF (Federal Accident Insurance Act)
  • Procedure: Immediate notification to the employer of the → complaint to the insurer
  • Protection: Medical care, temporary allowance and disability pension if necessary

What is an itinerary injury for the border worker

A border worker who suffers an accident on the way to work in Switzerland benefits from the full coverage of LAINF, the federal accident insurance. This right is guaranteed to all employees, regardless of where they reside: a worker who lives in Italy and crosses the border every morning to the Canton of Ticino receives the same protection as those who reside in Switzerland. Injury on Routes Versus Travel: Crucial Distinctions

One of the most sensitive issues for the border worker concerns the difference between an "accident on routes" (during the ordinary home-work journey) and an accident that occurs during a work trip . Although both can generate compensatory rights, the legal framework is significantly different and the border guard must understand this in order to protect themselves.

Injury on routes:

  • It is necessary during the ordinary and direct route between home and office
  • It is an automatic part of the working day
  • It is covered by LAINF without the need for an explicit order from the employer
  • The protection starts from the moment of departure from home until arrival at work

Job transfer:

  • The accident occurs in a temporary workplace, different from the usual one
  • It is preceded by an explicit mandate or request from the employer to go to another location
  • Coverage remains LAINF, but the protected period is longer: from departure from home to return, even if multi-day or overnight
  • Travel documentation (service order, travel tickets, vouchers) is important

Injury Reporting Procedure: Step-by-Step Guide

When an accident occurs while commuting, the cross-border worker must follow a precise procedure so that the claim is processed correctly and rights are protected without delays.

Step 1 — Immediate Notification to the Employer Immediately after the accident, the cross-border worker must inform the employer (or the responsible company representative) of what happened. This oral communication is sufficient at that moment, but it is also advisable to put it in writing — an email, a WhatsApp message, or a dated note. This documentation must include: precise date, time, location of the accident, how it occurred, any witnesses, and the name and contact details of these witnesses. Do not wait days: immediacy is crucial for legal documentation.

Step 2 — Employer Initiates Official Report The employer has a legal obligation to report the accident to LAINF insurance within legal deadlines (generally a few days). It is not the employee's responsibility to make this communication directly. The company has a contact person, usually the human resources department, who completes the official accident report form and transmits it to the insurer. The cross-border worker can ask the employer for written confirmation that the report has been submitted.

Step 3 — Collection of Supporting Documentation The cross-border worker must collect and keep:

  • Medical report from the emergency room or healthcare facility (if necessary)
  • Digital photographs of the accident scene (e.g. vehicle damage, road conditions at the time)
  • Full names and telephone numbers of witnesses, if present
  • Copy of any written communication sent to the employer
  • Receipts for medical expenses paid out of pocket (if applicable)

Frequently Asked Questions
What exactly is an itinerary injury?
It is an accident that occurs during the ordinary and direct route between home and work. It is considered part of the working day and is automatically covered by LAINF (Federal Accident Insurance Act) without the need for explicit orders from the employer.
If I change my usual route for traffic, do I stay covered?
Yes, small detours (for a traffic light, alternate parking, or to avoid clogging) remain covered. They are considered part of the ordinary journey. However, significant deviations for personal commissions may exclude LAINF coverage.
Should I contact the accident insurance directly?
No. The employer has a legal obligation to notify LAINF of the accident within the statutory time limits. You only need to inform the employer immediately of what happened, providing precise details about the place, time, circumstances and witnesses.
What care is covered after an injury on itineraries?
All necessary medical care: emergency room, specialist visits, physiotherapy, prescribed drugs, surgeries. The healthcare provider invoices LAINF directly. If you pay out of your own pocket, keep receipts and request reimbursement from the insurer.
If I stay off work for weeks, do I get an allowance?
Yes. LAINF provides a daily allowance for the period of inability to work (temporary disability). If the consequences are permanent, the insurance provides a disability pension to compensate for the loss of earning capacity.
