The Ticino job market is experiencing growth in several key sectors. According to updated data, professions such as IT, engineering, and healthcare are among the most in-demand. This trend offers new opportunities for cross-border workers seeking employment in Canton Ticino.

These sectors are continually expanding and offer stable and well-paid career prospects. Cross-border workers with skills in these areas can easily find employment in Canton Ticino.

Operational details

Practical Analysis

Job opportunities in the Canton of Ticino represent a great chance for cross-border commuters to improve their economic and professional situation. However, it is important to consider the tax and pension aspects to maximize the benefits.

Tax Implications

Cross-border commuters must take into account the withholding tax on income from work, which is only deducted in Switzerland. Italy avoids double taxation with the tax credit, which is calculated in the CE section of the 730 form. This system allows cross-border commuters to avoid paying double taxes on their income.

New Cross-Border Agreement

The new Cross-Border Agreement, signed on December 23, 2020, and in effect from January 1, 2024, introduces new rules for cross-border commuters. Old cross-border commuters, already such before July 17, 2023, benefit from an exemption of €7,500 with a transitional regime until 2033. New cross-border commuters, on the other hand, have a franchise of €10,000.

Rates and Contributions

Swiss rates and contributions include AVS/AI/IPG at 5.3%, AD/AC at 1.1% (with a limit of CHF 148,200), LAINF from 0.7% to 1.5%, and LPP from 7% to 18% for age groups starting from 25 years. In Italy, IRPEF varies from 23% up to €28,000, 35% from €28,001 to €50,000, and 43% over €50,000.

Health Insurance

Cross-border commuters have the right to choose health insurance (LAMal), with deductibles ranging from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500. It is important to choose the deductible that best suits your needs to optimize healthcare costs.
