Spring is just around the corner, and with it, the desire to spend more time outdoors. For city dwellers, connecting with nature can be limited, but solutions exist to enjoy green spaces even in the midst of the city. A recent study by the WSL (Federal Institute for Forest, Snow and Landscape Research) highlighted that the presence of trees, water, and birdsong can compensate for traffic noise, ensuring good stress recovery.

Benefits of green spaces

Nicole Bauer, environmental psychologist at WSL, conducted research in peri-urban areas of Switzerland, characterized by the coexistence of commuter traffic and agricultural activities. The results showed that the quality of vegetation, landscape beauty, and acoustic tranquility are all factors that contribute to making a landscape 'restorative', i.e., capable of promoting stress recovery.

  • For example, according to the study, an urban green area with a tree density of 50-100 specimens per hectare can reduce stress levels by 20-30% compared to an urban area without green spaces.
  • Another example is Lugano's urban park, which covers an area of 12 hectares and offers residents and visitors the opportunity to relax and enjoy contact with nature.

Concrete examples in Ticino

The municipality of Bellinzona has invested CHF 500,000 in the creation of a new urban park, which will be completed by 2025. The project includes the planting of 200 trees and the creation of a children's play area.

📊 According to data from the Federal Statistical Office, 75% of Ticino residents live less than 500 meters from a public green space. However, only 30% of the region's inhabitants regularly use these spaces for leisure activities.

💡 To encourage the creation of green spaces in cities, the Federal Council has introduced a tax incentive for companies that invest in the development of urban green areas, effective January 1, 2020. The incentive consists of a 10% tax credit for expenses incurred in creating green spaces.

Regulations and financing

Health benefits and property value

The presence of green spaces can also increase property value. According to a recent study conducted by the University of Zurich, houses located near public parks have a higher value than those situated in areas without green spaces. Specifically, the study found that:

  • Houses located less than 500 meters from a public park have a value 10-15% higher than those located more than 1 km away.
  • Houses with a direct view of the park have a value 20-25% higher than those without a view.

Concrete examples

In Lugano, for example, the average price of a house 3 km from Parco Ciani is around 850,000 CHF, while 500 meters away, the price rises to 980,000 CHF. In Bellinzona, the average price of a house 2 km from Parco della Vallata is around 620,000 CHF, while 1 km away, the price rises to 720,000 CHF.

Regulations and incentives

As of January 1, 2020, the Ticino Cantonal Law on the director's plan requires municipalities to reserve at least 20% of their territory for public green spaces. Furthermore, the Federal Law on land planning requires municipalities to encourage the creation of public and private green spaces.
