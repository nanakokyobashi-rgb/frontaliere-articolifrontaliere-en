Saturday afternoon, during the passage of the Giro di Lombardia, the Como Local Police Operations Center received an anxious call after it had been transferred by 112. Elisa’s water had just broken, and she was about to give birth; dad Alejandro was at home with her. Reaching the hospital, however, seemed almost impossible: the city streets were blocked for the race, amid the crowd’s excitement and traffic closures.

The Local Police responded immediately: the two officers assigned to the Giro motorcycle escort detail, Deputy Commander Sergio D’Angelo and Commissioner Francesco Polito, reached the couple’s home in Albate on their motorcycles within just a few minutes.

The race to Valduce

From Albate, Local Police motorcycles escorted the parents’ car toward Valduce hospital, clearing a path through the paralyzed traffic and protected checkpoints. The two officers’ prompt response enabled the couple to reach the hospital just in time.

Baby Rio was born at 16.57, less than 40 minutes after the parents’ first phone call; the baby weighs 4 kilograms and measures 54 centimeters. The source does not provide the exact time of the call, but specifies that less than 40 minutes elapsed between that call and the birth.

At home, three-year-old little sister Selva was waiting for her mother, father and newborn baby brother. The incident brings together the presence in the city, duty, efficiency and great emotion described in the account. The officers were already engaged in escorting the Giro, while the family was awaiting immediate help to overcome a situation made almost impossible by traffic. This convergence is what makes the incident extraordinary for the family and for the entire Como Command.
