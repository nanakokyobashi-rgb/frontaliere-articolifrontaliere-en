Key facts

New Cross-Border Worker Agreement: January 1, 2024 Ratification Italy: Law 83 of 13/6/2023 Double Taxation Convention: March 9, 1976 Old Cross-Border Worker Exemption: CHF 7,500 (until 2033) New Cross-Border Worker Exemption: CHF 10,000 Swiss Withholding Tax: Deducted in Switzerland Italian Tax Credit: Section CE of the 730 form

Transferring residency to an Italian border municipality like Brenta, with the aim of working in Ticino, requires careful consideration of several factors, chief among them the impact of the new Cross-Border Worker Agreement, which came into effect on January 1, 2024. This agreement, signed on December 23, 2020, and ratified by Italy with Law 83 of June 13, 2023, introduces significant changes compared to the previous regulations. The Double Taxation Convention between Italy and Switzerland, dated March 9, 1976, continues to represent the reference regulatory framework, but the new agreement modifies some practical aspects for cross-border workers. One of the most relevant changes concerns the tax exemption: for the so-called «new» cross-border workers, meaning those who were not already active in Ticino before July 17, 2023, the threshold for exemption from Ticino cantonal tax has been raised to CHF 10,000. For «old» cross-border workers, who retain the right to the CHF 7,500 exemption, a transitional regime is foreseen which will extend until 2033. It is fundamental to understand that the withholding tax on employment income is deducted exclusively in Switzerland. Italy, to avoid double taxation, grants a tax credit, which cross-border workers can claim by completing section CE of the 730 form. This mechanism ensures that income earned in Switzerland is not taxed twice. The choice to reside in Brenta, as in other neighboring municipalities, is often dictated by the search for a potentially lower cost of living compared to Ticino, while maintaining geographical proximity that allows for manageable commute times. However, it is essential to analyze in detail transport costs, infrastructure, and tax specifics to make an informed decision. The goal is to balance the economic benefits derived from working in a high-income market like Ticino with daily needs and tax obligations in the country of residence.

Operational details

Implicazioni Fiscali e Previdenziali: Dettagli per il Frontaliere

La gestione fiscale per chi vive a Brenta e lavora in Ticino è un aspetto cruciale, influenzato dal nuovo Accordo sui frontalieri. L'imposta alla fonte, prelevata direttamente dalla busta paga svizzera, si basa su aliquote che variano in funzione del reddito e dello stato civile. Per i contribuenti con un reddito annuo lordo fino a CHF 148'200, è applicata un'aliquota che tiene conto anche della deduzione per la franchigia (CHF 10'000 per i nuovi frontalieri, CHF 7'500 per i vecchi). Oltre a queste imposte dirette, i lavoratori dipendenti devono considerare i contributi sociali svizzeri. L'AVS/AI/IPG ammonta al 5.3% del salario lordo, a cui si aggiunge l'1.1% per AD/AC (fino al cap di CHF 148'200). La LAINF (assicurazione contro gli infortuni non professionali) varia tra lo 0.7% e l'1.5%, mentre la LPP (Cassa Pensione) ha aliquote progressive, a partire dal 7% per la fascia d'età 25-34 anni, fino a superare il 18% per le fasce più anziane, applicata sulla parte di salario eccedente il minimo LPP. Sul versante italiano, l'IRPEF prevede un'aliquota del 23% per scaglioni di reddito fino a €28'000, che sale al 33% per redditi tra €28'001 e €50'000, e al 43% per quelli superiori a €50'000. La convenzione contro le doppie imposizioni, tramite il credito d'imposta, evita che il reddito venga tassato due volte, ma è essenziale una corretta dichiarazione dei redditi in entrambi i paesi per ottimizzare il carico fiscale complessivo. Per quanto riguarda l'assicurazione malattia (LAMal), i frontalieri con permesso G hanno diritto di optare per l'assicurazione svizzera o quella italiana. Se si sceglie la LAMal, è possibile beneficiare di franchigie adulte che vanno da CHF 300 a CHF 2'500, con un impatto diretto sul premio mensile. La scelta tra le due opzioni assicurative dipenderà dalle esigenze personali, dalla composizione familiare e dai costi associati.

Frequently Asked Questions
When does the new Border Arrangement come into force?
The new Borders Agreement entered into force on 1 January 2024. This agreement, signed on 23 December 2020, was ratified by Italy with Law 83 of 13 June 2023.
What is the new tax exemption for frontier workers?
For new frontier workers, i.e. those who were not active in Ticino before 17 July 2023, the tax exemption was raised to CHF 10,000. The old frontier workers maintain the exemption of CHF7,500, with a transitional regime until 2033.
Where do I pay taxes if I work in Ticino and live in Brenta?
Income tax at source is only withheld in Switzerland. Italy applies a tax credit to avoid double taxation, which can be managed through the CE framework of the model 730.
What are the Swiss social contributions to consider?
The main Swiss social contributions include AVS/AI/IPG (5.3%), AD/AC (1.1%), LAINF (0.7-1.5%) and LPP (Pension Fund, progressive rates from 7% upwards for the age group 25-34 years).
