Lugano: Underused Park and Ride Facilities, Subsidized Buses on the Rise

Di Laura Bianchi · Published on 7 March 2026

In 2024 Lugano spent 1.2 million on bus subscription subsidies, while park and ride lots see low usage and high costs.

Context

TL;DR

  • Lugano spends 1.21M CHF on public transport subsidies in 2024.
  • Park and Ride facilities in Lugano are underutilized.
  • Bus subscriptions are popular among young people and students.
  • Lugano plans to increase subsidies to 1.26M CHF in 2025.

Key facts

  • Total expenditure: 1.21 million Swiss francs in 2024.
  • Subsidies distributed: 5,349 discounted subscriptions in 2024.
  • Zone 10 subscriptions: 2,512 subscriptions for young people, students, and pensioners.
  • Zone 10+11 subscriptions: 2,348 subscriptions in 2024.
  • Park and Ride revenue: 226,000 francs in fees collected.
  • Park and Ride costs: 825,000 francs spent on management.
  • Projected expenditure: 1.26 million Swiss francs in 2025.
  • Projected subsidies: 5,596 subsidies in 2025.

In 2024, the City of Lugano continued to focus on public transport by encouraging the purchase of subscriptions, but issues have emerged regarding the use of Park and Ride facilities, which are perceived as unattractive by users. According to data provided by the Municipality in response to a query submitted by Elena Rezzonico and six other left-wing councilors, the total expenditure on public transport subsidies amounts to 1.21 million Swiss francs, with 5,349 discounted subscriptions distributed across various city areas.

The incentive system has seen growing participation, with 2,512 subscriptions just for zone 10, which includes young people, students, and pensioners, and an additional 2,348 subscriptions for zones 10+11. Growth is also confirmed for 2025, with a projected expenditure of 1.26 million and 5,596 subsidies.

Alongside this financial commitment, Lugano’s two Park and Ride lots recorded disappointing usage: the City collected 226,000 francs in fees but spent 825,000 on their management. This imbalance highlights how the Park and Ride model, often considered strategic for reducing urban traffic, struggles to take off in Lugano’s reality.

Lugano, with its important hub position for cross-border commuters coming from Lombardy and other neighboring provinces, suffers from complex mobility that requires effective solutions. The low use of Park and Ride facilities also impacts public transport policies integrated with border crossings such as Brogeda and Gaggiolo. Daily travel by cross-border workers to centers like Mendrisio and Bellinzona demands an efficient and affordable transport system, which currently relies more on bus subscription subsidies than on intermodal parking infrastructures.

A Municipality spokesperson emphasized that buses are particularly appreciated by young people and students, confirming the usefulness of subsidies in promoting sustainable mobility among the younger segment of the population. However, the issue remains of the low appeal of Park and Ride lots, which could instead ease city traffic if better promoted and integrated with public transport infrastructure and border crossings.

Operational details

The detailed analysis provided by the Municipality highlights how Lugano’s public transport incentive policy is based on a complex regulation that provides differentiated subsidies according to age groups and subscription zones. In 2024, 975 subscriptions were subsidized for young people and 136 for students in zone 10 alone, while for the combined zones 10+11 there were 1,020 young people and 198 students, along with smaller numbers of pensioners and adults.

This territorial and demographic breakdown reflects the Municipality’s attempt to address the mobility needs of different social categories and cover the most frequented urban and suburban areas. The expenditure of around 1.2 million francs for 2024 and the expected increase to 1.26 million in 2025 indicate a strong political will to support public transport as a tool to contain road congestion and reduce environmental pollution.

However, the report highlights how the Park and Ride lots, conceived as hubs for transferring from private vehicles to public transport, are underutilized, generating a clear management deficit. Operating costs of 825,000 francs far exceed revenue from fees, which amount to 226,000 francs.

This situation calls for reflection on the planning and promotion of these facilities, which in Lugano seem not to effectively meet the travel habits of cross-border commuters and residents. Integration with bus lines, especially those used by young people and students benefiting from Arcobaleno subsidies, needs to be improved to increase their attractiveness. Furthermore, no concrete plans have been specified to revise the management model or to enhance information campaigns aimed at users.

Cantonal and federal sustainable mobility regulations push for greater use of public transport, but Lugano’s reality reveals a gap between intentions and user behavior. Improving the offer, perhaps with more frequent services, discounted fares for cross-border commuters, and optimized connections with border crossings such as Chiasso and Mendrisio, could reverse this trend. SUPSI and USI could be involved in feasibility studies to design innovative solutions, while the cantonal DFE and SECO offices could coordinate interventions to facilitate cross-border commuting more sustainably.

Key points

For cross-border commuters and Ticino residents who travel daily between Lombardy and Canton Ticino, efficient mobility management represents an economic and social priority. The bus subscription subsidies offered by the City of Lugano are a concrete opportunity to reduce transport costs and encourage the use of public transport, especially among young people and students, categories that are more likely to adopt sustainable habits.

However, the low use of Park and Ride facilities suggests that more incisive and targeted measures are needed. One strategy could include expanding promotions and revising fees, as well as improving communication toward cross-border commuters passing through the Brogeda and Gaggiolo border crossings. Additionally, integrating bus lines with regional rail services could make public transport more competitive compared to private cars.

For interested users, it is useful to know that the Arcobaleno system offers subsidized subscriptions in various zone combinations, with costs and benefits that vary depending on age and social category. Incentives are also available for pensioners, a significant segment of the Ticino population.

Current figures—over 5,000 subsidized subscriptions and more than one million francs invested annually—demonstrate a significant commitment to sustainable mobility, but the challenge is to increase participation and optimize existing infrastructure such as Park and Ride lots.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the subsidies offered by the Municipality of Lugano for public transport?
The Municipality of Lugano spent 1.21 million francs on subsidies for public transport, distributing 5,349 subsidized passes in various city areas.
What is the impact of little-used Park and Ride parking spaces on traffic management in Lugano?
The limited use of Park and Ride in Lugano reduces the effectiveness in reducing urban traffic, despite their intended strategic role. This generates a management deficit (825,000 CHF outgoing vs 226,000 CHF incoming in 2024) and undermines the sustainability of the model.
What are the possibilities to encourage the use of Park and Ride car parks in Lugano?
To increase the attractiveness of Park and Ride, fares could be reduced, communication improved and public transport services could be better integrated with facilities, encouraging their use as a transition point between cars and public transport.
