Living in Civate and working in Ticino: the guide (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 21 August 2026

Permits, taxation, AVS and everything you need to know to move to the border area and start working as a border worker in Ticino.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Civate (Como) is a short distance from the Ticino border, a potential base for border workers
  • Tax at source withheld in Switzerland for border workers with G Permit
  • New Frontier Agreement in force from 1 January 2024: exemption €7,500- €10,000 per year

Key facts

  • What: Transfer and cross-border work between Como and Ticino
  • When: From 1 January 2024 (New Frontier Agreement in force)
  • Where: Civate (Italy) → passes Brogeda, Gaggiolo (Ticino)
  • Who: Border workers with G Permit (employee work)
  • Exemption amount: €7,500 (frontier workers prior to 17/7/2023) or €10,000 (new)
  • Regulations: New CH-IT Agreement signed on 23 December 2020, in force from 1 January 2024

Living in the Civate area and working in Ticino is an increasingly frequent choice for those looking for a balance between housing costs and quality of life. The geographical location, a short distance from the Ticino crossings of Brogeda and Gaggiolo, transforms this area of Lombardy into an attractive logistics base for dependent border workers. But what are the concrete effects on the paycheck and what are the tax implications when income is earned in Switzerland?

Operational details

G Leave and Swiss Social Contributions

Those who live in Civate and work in Ticino have access to the G Permit (border worker), which allows employees to work in Switzerland while maintaining their Italian residence. The permit is requested by the Ticino employer from the Swiss migration authority and is usually granted within 30–60 days.

Social security contributions are paid in Switzerland according to the Swiss compulsory system:

  • AVS/AI/IPG: 5.3% to be paid by the employee (old-age insurance, disability, daily sickness benefit)
  • AD/AC (unemployment and supplementary pension): 1.1% borne by the employee, with contribution limit CHF 148'200
  • LAINF: 0.7–1.5% (professional accident insurance)
  • LPP (second pillar): 7–18% depending on age group (from 25th year of age), shared between employee and employer

Italy does not directly manage these contributions while the border worker works in Switzerland. It is Switzerland that provides the AVS (Old Age and Survivors Insurance) pension starting from retirement age — 65 years for men, 64 years for women — calculated on contributions paid during border activity.

Key points

Checklist practice before transfer

Before choosing to live in Civate and searching for a job in Ticino, a checklist helps to prevent organizational and bureaucratic surprises:

1. Job search in Ticino: Identify the Ticinese employer. Check if they offer contracts to Italian residents and if they support the request for a Permesso G to the migration authority.

2. Documents: Collect Italian documents (valid ID card, tax code, anagraphic extract) and prepare Swiss documents (the employer will then coordinate the permit request with the authorities).

3. Legal residence: Establish a legal residence in Civate or in the chosen municipality. Italian anagraphic registration is essential for tax declaration and identification at the Revenue Agency.

4. Bank account: Open a bank account with an international bank (preferably Swiss or with international connections) to receive the salary in CHF without excessive exchange fees.

5. Health insurance: Once the contract with the employer is signed, activate the Swiss LAMal coverage. The employer usually handles the registration, but verify the effective dates (usually 5–7 working days from the signature).

Step-by-step: from first steps to the first day of work

Months 1–2 (Research and negotiation): Active search for Ticinese job announcements and interviews with companies. Once the written offer is received from the employer, they will submit the Permesso G request to the migration authority and the competent cantonal authorities.

Months 2–3 (Authorization): The migration authority examines the permit request (30–60 days on average). Meanwhile, proceed with the search for accommodation in Civate, sign a rental or purchase contract, and register with the Italian municipality's anagraphic office.

Frequently Asked Questions
Can I live in Italy and work as an employee in Switzerland as a border worker?
Yes, with the G Permit (border crossing). The Ticino employer requests permission from the Swiss migration authority. The source tax is withheld in Switzerland; in Italy you declare the income through the EC framework of the model 730 and request the tax credit for the taxes paid in Switzerland.
How much tax do I pay if I live in Civate and work in Ticino?
It depends on your annual income. From 1 January 2024, the New Frontier Agreement provides for an exemption of €7,500 per year (if frontier before 17/7/2023) or €10,000 (if new frontier). Beyond this threshold, income is subject to Italian progressive personal income tax (23–43%). However, the Swiss tax credit reduces the final burden.
Is health insurance covered in Switzerland or Italy?
You must enroll in LAMal (Swiss health insurance) at an institution operating in Ticino. Italy does not cover the border for work carried out in Switzerland. Deductibles range from CHF 300 to CHF 2500 for adults.
How much does Permit G cost?
The G Permit has no direct cost to the border worker. It is the employer who submits the application to the migration authority. Check with the employer if it covers any administrative costs associated with the request.
If I work a few years as a border worker, can I then move to Switzerland?
Yes, you can apply for Permit B (Swiss residence) after years of border crossing, if your employer supports it. The change from Permit G to Permit B changes the tax, contributory and social security regime, and may have implications for future AVS income.
