Living in the Civate area and working in Ticino is an increasingly common choice for those seeking a balance between housing costs and quality of life. Its geographical location, a short distance from the Ticino border crossings of Brogeda and Gaggiolo, turns this area of Lombardy into an attractive logistical base for employed cross-border commuters. Civate as a cross-border commuter base

Civate is located on Lake Como, a short distance from the Ticino border. Choosing to live in this area entails practical advantages compared with moving to Switzerland: the cost of living in Italy—in particular, rent and housing—generally remains more moderate than prices in Ticino, while still being in an area with strong scenic appeal and good public and private services.

Commuting is manageable for those working in the main urban centers of Ticino (Lugano, Bellinzona, Locarno). We do not have exact travel times from Civate to individual offices in Ticino, but the geographical proximity suggests that daily travel via the available border crossings is sustainable.

The tax regime for cross-border commuters: the New Agreement of 2024

The New Agreement on Cross-Border Commuters between Italy and Switzerland, signed on December 23, 2020 and in force since January 1, 2024, redefined the rules for people residing in Italy and working in Switzerland. The most significant element is withholding at source: employment income tax is withheld in Switzerland for cross-border commuters. There is no double taxation, because Italy recognizes a tax credit through section CE of the tax return (Form 730).

Legacy cross-border commuters continue to be taxed only in Switzerland (Article 9 of the 2020 Agreement). The transitional regime under Article 9 of the Agreement applies only to those who meet the treaty definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between December 31, 2018 and July 17, 2023; the covered employment income is taxed exclusively in Switzerland. In other cases, the ordinary regime applies, with Italian taxation and a credit for Swiss tax. The 10.000 euro under Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is only the limit for the income referred to in Article 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013, not a general tax-free allowance. Operational details

G permit and Swiss social contributions

Those who live in Civate and work in Ticino access the G Permit (border workers), which allows subordinate work in Switzerland while maintaining Italian residency. The permit is requested by the Ticino employer from the Swiss migration authority and is usually granted within 30–60 days.

Social security contributions are paid in Switzerland according to the mandatory Swiss system:

  • AVS/AI/IPG: 5.3% paid by the employee (old age insurance, disability, daily sickness allowance)
  • AD/AC (unemployment and supplementary pensions): 1.1% paid by the employee, with a co-payment limit of CHF 148'200
  • UVG: 0.7–1.5% (professional accident insurance)
  • LPP (second pillar): 7–18% depending on age group (from 25 years of age), shared between employee and employer

Italy does not directly manage these contributions while the cross-border worker works in Switzerland. It is Switzerland that provides the AVS (Old Age and Survivors Insurance) pension starting from the retirement age - 65 years for men, 64 years for women - calculated on the contributions paid during cross-border activity.

KVG: compulsory Swiss health insurance

As an Italian resident working in Switzerland, the cross-border worker acquires the right (and obligation) to insure with the KVG (Swiss compulsory health insurance). There is no alternative: the coverage must be activated with an insurance institution that operates in the Canton of Ticino (where he works).

Deductibles vary from CHF 300 to CHF 2500 for adults, depending on the choice of coverage. The KVG covers medical treatment, hospitalization, drugs and rehabilitation according to Swiss tariffs. This represents a significant change compared to Italian healthcare: services are managed by the Swiss system, not by the Italian one, even if the cross-border worker resides in Italy.

Italian tax declaration and tax credit

The cross-border worker declares his income from work in Switzerland in the Italian form 730, EC framework section, and requests the tax credit for the Swiss tax already paid through the monthly withholdings.

The Revenue Agency calculates the tax credit by comparing the tax paid in Switzerland with the Italian IRPEF applicable on the same income. If the Swiss tax is lower than the theoretical Italian IRPEF, the Italian State reimburses the differential. The Italian IRPEF follows progressive rates: 23% up to €28,000, 33% from €28,001 to €50,000, 43% over €50,000. The threshold of 10,000 euros of the art. 4 of Law 83/2023 concerns exclusively income from employment carried out abroad, in a border area or in a neighboring country, continuously and exclusively, by people resident in Italy, referred to in the art. 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013; it is not a general exemption nor an exemption from Swiss withholding tax.

Key points

Practical pre-transfer checklist

Before choosing to live in Civate and look for work in Ticino, a checklist helps prevent organizational and bureaucratic surprises:

1. Job search in Ticino: Identify the Ticino employer. Check whether it offers contracts to cross-border workers resident in Italy and whether it supports the request for the G Permit from the migration authority.

2. Documentation: Collect the Italian documentation (valid identity card, tax code, personal data extract) and prepare the Swiss documentation (the employer will then coordinate the permit request with the authorities).

3. Legal domicile: Establish a legal residence in Civate or in the chosen municipality. The Italian registry registration is essential for the tax declaration and for identification at the Revenue Agency.

4. Bank account: Open a bank account at an international institution (possibly Swiss or with an international connection) to receive your salary in CHF without excessive exchange commissions.

5. Health insurance: Once the contract with the employer has been signed, the Swiss KVG coverage is activated. The employer usually manages the registration, but checks the effective times (usually 5–7 working days from signature).

Step-by-step: from the first steps to the first day of work

Months 1–2 (Research and negotiation): Active research on Ticino job advertisements and interviews with companies. Once the employer has received the written offer, he will forward the G Permit request to the migration authority and the competent cantonal authorities.

Months 2–3 (Authorization): The migration authority reviews the permit request (30–60 days on average). At the same time, proceed with the search for a home in Civate, stipulate the rental or purchase contract, and register in the registry office of the Italian municipality.

Month 3–4 (Formalization): Receive Permit G from the migration authority. Officially sign the contract with the Ticino employer. Open Swiss bank account.

First day of work: Present documentation to the employer (passport/ID, G permit, bank details for salary). Wait for confirmation of your KVG registration.

Frequently Asked Questions
Can I live in Italy and work as an employee in Switzerland as a border worker?
Yes, with the G Permit (border crossing). The Ticino employer requests permission from the Swiss migration authority. The source tax is withheld in Switzerland; in Italy you declare the income through the EC framework of the model 730 and request the tax credit for the taxes paid in Switzerland.
Who qualifies for the transitional regime in Article 9?
Article 9’s transitional regime applies only to workers who meet the Agreement’s definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between 31 December 2018 and 17 July 2023; covered employment income is taxable exclusively in Switzerland. The ordinary regime applies in other cases, with Italian taxation and credit for Swiss tax where applicable. The €10,000 in Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is only the limit for income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law 147/2013, not a general allowance. FTA factsheet · Agreement, Article 9 · Normattiva, Article 4.
Is health insurance covered in Switzerland or Italy?
You must enroll in LAMal (Swiss health insurance) at an institution operating in Ticino. Italy does not cover the border for work carried out in Switzerland. Deductibles range from CHF 300 to CHF 2500 for adults.
How much does Permit G cost?
The G Permit has no direct cost to the border worker. It is the employer who submits the application to the migration authority. Check with the employer if it covers any administrative costs associated with the request.
If I work a few years as a border worker, can I then move to Switzerland?
Yes, you can apply for Permit B (Swiss residence) after years of border crossing, if your employer supports it. The change from Permit G to Permit B changes the tax, contributory and social security regime, and may have implications for future AVS income.
