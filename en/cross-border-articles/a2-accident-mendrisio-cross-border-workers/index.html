A2 accident in Mendrisio: queues for cross-border commuters

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 2 October 2026

Accident in Coldrerio on the A2: a 68-year-old woman slightly injured, a 73-year-old man in life-threatening condition, and delays of up to an hour and a half toward Chiasso for cross-border commuters.

Context

In brief

  • Accident shortly before 15.30 in Coldrerio
  • 73-year-old in life-threatening condition after the rear-end collision
  • A2 closed between Mendrisio and Chiasso
  • Delays of up to an hour and a half on the Rivera-Chiasso route

Key facts

  • Location: Coldrerio
  • Time: shortly before 15.30
  • Circumstances: car collided with an articulated lorry stopped in the emergency lay-by
  • Injured: 68-year-old with minor injuries; 73-year-old in life-threatening condition

The sequence of the emergency

Shortly before 15.30, in Coldrerio, a car driven by a 68-year-old Swiss woman collided with an articulated lorry stopped in the emergency lay-by. The driver sustained minor injuries. The passenger, a 73-year-old man, was instead airlifted to hospital in life-threatening condition.

The accident required the intervention of emergency services and the landing of Rega. To allow these operations, the A2 was temporarily closed between Mendrisio and Chiasso. The closure therefore affected a section also used by cross-border workers from the province of Varese on their journey home from Switzerland.

The reopening of the motorway came after the phase required for the emergency response, but it did not immediately restore normal conditions. Traffic continued to suffer heavy disruption, extending the problems beyond the section directly affected by the accident.

TCS estimated delays of up to an hour and a half on the Rivera-Chiasso route. The estimate concerns the direction indicated in the report and conveys the extent of the slowdown recorded in the afternoon, without providing separate times for each individual point on the A2.

Meanwhile, traffic diverted onto ordinary roads brought Mendrisio and the roads toward Genestrerio, Balerna and Chiasso to a standstill. The problem therefore did not remain confined to the motorway: the local road network also absorbed vehicles leaving the main route.

For cross-border workers from Varese, the most delicate moment coincided with the time of the return journey to Italy. The long queues affected workers using the A2 to return from Switzerland, while the Mendrisiotto remained paralysed by the impact on traffic.

The situation therefore followed a precise progression: the accident in Coldrerio, the temporary suspension of motorway traffic between Mendrisio and Chiasso, the reopening and finally the diversion of traffic onto ordinary roads. These stages, all indicated in the reconstruction, explain why the disruption affected not only the site of the rear-end collision, but also the return journey through the Mendrisiotto. The report explicitly links these repercussions to the time when workers were returning to the province of Varese.

Operational details

For the cross-border commuter returning to the province of Varese, the criticality described by the source does not end at the time of closure. The decisive step is the sequence between the motorway and ordinary roads: even after the reopening of the A2, the repercussions continued to weigh on the Mendrisiotto and on the roads towards Genestrerio, Balerna and Chiasso.

A cross-border commuter who is faced with similar news can read it with an operational sequence built only on the available data. It is not a diversion map, because the source does not indicate alternative routes; it serves to separate what is confirmed from what is not indicated.

1. Locate the critical point. The event took place in Coldrerio, on the A2. This identifies the stretch of the news and does not allow the conditions of the rest of the route to be established, because the source does not provide that data.

2. Distinguish between the two phases. The A2 was first closed between Mendrisio and Chiasso for rescue and landing of Rega, then reopened. The reopening, on its own, does not coincide with the end of the disruptions described.

3. Use the estimate without turning it into a promise. The TCS has indicated delays of up to an hour and a half on the Rivera-Chiasso route. It is the quantitative reference available; the source does not allocate that time to each trip, each municipality or each lane.

4. It treats the ordinary road system as an affected area, not as a guaranteed alternative. Mendrisio and the roads towards Genestrerio, Balerna and Chiasso were affected by the poured traffic. The news does not say which road was free nor does it provide a replacement route.

5. Separate the news from other practices. The episode concerns the return journey of Varese cross-border workers. To check other queue episodes, consult the tempi di attesa ai valichi and use the storico del traffico e delle code as an archive, without attributing further data to this source.

Frequently Asked Questions
Where did the accident on the A2 occur?
The accident occurred shortly before 15.30 in Coldrerio. A car driven by a 68-year-old Swiss woman rear-ended a tractor-trailer stopped in the emergency lay-by. The driver sustained minor injuries; the passenger, a 73-year-old man, was airlifted to hospital in life-threatening condition.
How long was the A2 closed?
The A2 was temporarily closed between Mendrisio and Chiasso to allow emergency services to respond and the Rega helicopter to land, and was then reopened. The source does not indicate the exact duration of the closure. After reopening, however, the TCS estimated delays of up to an hour and a half on the Rivera-Chiasso route.
What difficulties did Varese cross-border commuters face?
The A2 is one of the routes used by cross-border commuters from the province of Varese to return from Switzerland. During the evening rush hour, disruptions included long queues, delays of up to an hour and a half on the Rivera-Chiasso route, and traffic at a standstill on ordinary roads in Mendrisio, Genestrerio, Balerna and Chiasso.
