Swiss franc hits 2025 summer low: impact on commuters (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 2 September 2026

The Swiss franc has reached its lowest since summer 2025, with the euro at 0.9435 francs on 2 September 2026. Discover the implications for frontier workers.

Context

In brief

  • The Swiss franc has reached lows since summer 2025.
  • The euro touched 0,9435 francs on September 2, 2026.
  • The interest rate gap between Switzerland and the Eurozone pushes towards the euro.
  • The ECB expects a deposit rate increase to 2.50%.

Key facts

  • What: Swiss franc drop to lows since summer 2025.
  • When: September 2, 2026.
  • Where: Switzerland.
  • Who: Swiss National Bank (SNB), European Central Bank (ECB).
  • Amount: Euro at 0,9435 francs.

The Swiss franc has touched lows since summer 2025, with the euro reaching 0,9435 francs on September 2, 2026. This decline is mainly due to the interest rate gap between Switzerland and the Eurozone. The Swiss National Bank's (SNB) key rate is at 0.00%, while the European Central Bank's (ECB) rate is at 2.25%. The prospect of higher yields directs capital flows towards the euro. The recent Eurozone inflation data, rising to 3.3% in August, strengthens expectations of a more decisive monetary tightening by the Frankfurt institution. Markets now take for granted a deposit rate increase to 2.50%, questioning the duration of this strict line.

Impact on cross-border workers

The decline of the Swiss franc has direct implications for Italian cross-border workers in Switzerland. With the euro strengthening, the purchasing power of cross-border workers in Italy could increase, making the purchase of goods and services in their country of residence more convenient. However, it is important to consider that currency fluctuations can also affect the cost of living in Switzerland, where many cross-border workers reside. Additionally, interest rate changes can influence investment returns and credit conditions, with potential impacts on the personal finances of cross-border workers.

Operational details

Practical Analysis

The depreciation of the Swiss franc and the strengthening of the euro have significant implications for Italian cross-border workers. With the euro strengthening, cross-border workers who receive their salary in Swiss francs may see an increase in their purchasing power in Italy. This could make the purchase of goods and services in their country of residence more convenient, improving their quality of life.

However, it is important to consider that currency fluctuations can also affect the cost of living in Switzerland. Many cross-border workers reside in Switzerland, and exchange rate fluctuations can influence rental costs, food, and other consumer goods. Additionally, interest rate changes can affect investment returns and credit conditions, with potential impacts on the personal finances of cross-border workers.

Financial Management Strategies

In this context, cross-border workers should adopt financial management strategies to mitigate the risks associated with market fluctuations. One of the most effective strategies is investment diversification. Cross-border workers might consider investing a portion of their salary in financial instruments denominated in euros to benefit from the strengthening of the European currency. Additionally, it is important to closely monitor the decisions of central banks and adjust investment strategies based on market forecasts.

Another important strategy is tax planning. Cross-border workers should be aware of the tax implications of currency fluctuations and interest rates. For example, exchange rate changes can affect the calculation of withholding tax and tax refunds. It is therefore essential to consult a tax advisor to optimize one's tax situation and benefit from any available tax breaks.

Key points

Action: what to do concretely

Italian cross-border workers must take concrete measures to manage the financial implications of currency fluctuations and interest rates. Here are some practical actions that can be taken:

Monitor central bank decisions

Cross-border workers should closely monitor the decisions of central banks, particularly those of the European Central Bank (ECB) and the Swiss National Bank (SNB). Changes in interest rates can affect investment returns and credit conditions, with potential impacts on the personal finances of cross-border workers. It is therefore essential to be informed about central bank decisions and to adjust investment strategies based on market forecasts.

Diversify investments

One of the most effective strategies to mitigate the risks associated with market fluctuations is to diversify investments. Cross-border workers might consider investing part of their salary in financial instruments denominated in euros to benefit from the strengthening of the European currency. Additionally, it is important to diversify investments across different asset classes, such as stocks, bonds, and real estate, to reduce the risk of losses.

Plan taxation

Cross-border workers should be aware of the tax implications of currency fluctuations and interest rates. Changes in the exchange rate can affect the calculation of withholding tax and tax refunds. It is therefore crucial to consult a tax advisor to optimize one's tax situation and benefit from any available tax breaks. Additionally, cross-border workers should be informed about the tax regulations in force in Switzerland and Italy to avoid any penalties and benefit from any tax breaks.
