How is the border economy with the Canton of Ticino? "It holds but there is fragility"

The border economy between the Canton of Ticino and Italy is in question. According to the source, the economy is holding up but there are weaknesses. The border agreement was signed on 23 December 2020 and the Italian ratification took place on 13 June 2023.

The border agreement, also known as the Agreement on the Rights of Frontier Workers of 23 December 2020, was signed between the Canton of Ticino and Italy. This agreement is intended to regulate the rights of frontier workers working in the Canton of Ticino and residing in Italy. The agreement provides for the creation of a protection regime for frontier workers, which includes the protection of workers' rights and the creation of a guarantee fund for frontier workers.

According to the source, the economy is holding up but there are weaknesses. In particular, there are concerns related to the lack of skilled health personnel in the Canton of Ticino and the difficulty of finding skilled frontier workers. In addition, there are issues related to border management and the safety of border workers.

Operational details

The frontier agreement introduced new rules for frontier workers, a category of workers that represents a significant part of the Ticino economy. The source speaks of regency but of fragility, a complex economic situation that requires special attention. The Canton of Ticino is a border territory that is home to a population of over 350,000 inhabitants, with a presence of border workers that exceeds 100,000 units.

The regency refers to the ability of the Ticino economy to bear the brunt of the presence of border workers, who represent 30% of the active population. However, the source speaks of fragility, which manifests itself in the difficulty of finding work for border workers, in low pay and in the lack of social rights.

According to 2022 data, Ticino border workers have an average income of 4,500 Swiss francs per month, while Swiss workers have an average income of 7,000 Swiss francs. In addition, frontier workers are entitled to limited social benefits, such as sickness and disability insurance, which are lower than those of Swiss workers.

The economic situation is further complicated by the presence of regulations governing the presence of border workers. The Federal Immigration Act of 16 December 2008 states that border crossers must be subject to a residence permit, which must be renewed every two years.
