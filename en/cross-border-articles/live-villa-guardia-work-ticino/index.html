What it means to be a border worker from Villa Guardia

Villa Guardia, an Italian town in the province of Varese, represents one of the many border towns from which daily workers move to work in Canton Ticino. Moving here (or staying) to work as a border worker in Switzerland involves tax, contributory, and administrative consequences that differ completely from the situation of a worker residing solely in Italy.

The main regime is summarized in a rule: the withholding tax on salaries and wages is withheld exclusively in Switzerland, never in Italy. This eliminates the risk of double taxation because Italy recognizes a tax credit through the model 730, quadro CE. The Convention on Double Taxation between Italy and Switzerland, signed on March 9, 1976, guarantees this bilateral system.

From January 1, 2024, the New Border Agreement (signed on December 23, 2020, and ratified in Italy with Law 83 of June 13, 2023) has introduced important innovations. For new border workers — those who acquire the status for the first time after July 17, 2023 — the exemption is set at €10,000 annual income, exempt from Italian taxation. Border workers who had already acquired the status before this date enjoy instead an exemption of €7,500, valid until 2033 (transitional regime).

The Swiss-Italian tax system for border workers

For a border worker from Villa Guardia working in Ticino, the annual tax cycle involves:

In Switzerland: The employer withholds the withholding tax directly from the paycheck. The percentage varies by canton and income, but is administered by the cantonal authorities (in Ticino, by the AFC/ESTV). At the end of the year, the border worker receives a certificate of the tax paid.

In Italy: The border worker declares the Swiss income through model 730 or Unico, specifying in the quadro CE the Swiss tax paid. This allows the border worker to claim the tax credit, which neutralizes the Italian IRPEF tax on the same taxable base. The Italian IRPEF tax rates (calculated on the Swiss gross income, without deduction of first-instance social contributions) are: 23% up to €28,000, 33% from €28,001 to €50,000, 43% over €50,000.

In practice: if the Swiss tax paid is lower than the Italian IRPEF tax, the border worker will have a credit (refund via 730). If higher, they generally will not owe anything in Italy because the credit covers the difference.

Swiss social contributions and pension

A border worker from Villa Guardia working as an employee in Ticino pays every month Swiss social contributions on their paycheck: AVS/AI (old-age, disability, survivors) at 5.3% at the employee's expense, AD (unemployment insurance) at 1.1% up to a maximum annual income of CHF 148,200, LAINF (accident insurance) from 0.7% to 1.5% depending on the sector, and LPP (2nd pillar/pension fund) with a variable percentage for age ranges between 7% and 18%.

These contributions paid in Switzerland create pension rights in the Confederation, parallel to those INPS in Italy. The border worker accumulates, year after year, rights for the Swiss AVS pension and the LPP capital. Advantages and disadvantages of living in Villa Guardia as a border worker

Living in Villa Guardia and working in Ticino entails advantages and disadvantages that the potential border worker must carefully evaluate before moving or deciding to stay. The cost of living in the province of Varese is generally lower than in many areas of the Canton of Ticino, allowing a better quality of life with the same salary. Staying in Italy simplifies the management of the social, family and school network (children enrolled in Italian schools, general practitioner, municipal relations). A double health coverage (Italian INPS + Swiss LAMal) offers protection on two parallel systems. The administrative flexibility — maintaining Italian residence — facilitates relations with municipal offices, issuance of documents, registry registrations.

The negative sides include daily commuting to border crossings (Brogeda, Gaggiolo, Chiasso) with travel times linked to cross-border traffic flows. Dual administrative management requires tax returns in both countries with different calendars and deadlines to be carefully tracked. LAMal (Swiss health insurance) is mandatory with an excess of CHF 300-2500/year, additional to the Italian INPS. What to Consider Before Relocating (or Staying) in Villa Guardia as a Border Worker

Required Documentation and Request for the G Permit

The procedure for obtaining the G Permit from Villa Guardia involves: obtaining a written employment contract from a Ticinese company (essential for the request), submitting a G Permit application to Swiss authorities (SECO/SEM) through the employer, registering with the Villa Guardia municipality as a resident if not already domiciled, and obtaining an Italian municipality certificate of residence to be submitted to Swiss authorities. Typically requested documentation: valid passport or ID card, signed employment contract, Italian certificate of residence, G Permit application form completed with the employer, confirmation of registration with the Italian municipality, and (if necessary) certificate of pending charges or judicial record. Processing times vary from 2 to 6 weeks. Once acquired, the permit is renewable as long as the employment relationship in Switzerland exists.

Annual Fiscal Deadlines and Calendars

A border worker from Villa Guardia must respect two parallel fiscal calendars. In Italy: The 730 or Unico model must be declared by May 31 (if submitted in person) or by June 30 (if submitted electronically); the CE form must be completed to claim the Swiss tax paid; the Agency's communication on refund or debt arrives by the end of the year. In Switzerland: The source tax is automatically withheld from the monthly paycheck; some cantons (including Ticino) require an annual income tax return — check with the cantonal administration of contributions (AFC/ESTV). The Italian deadline is critical: if the 730 (CE form) is not submitted within the deadline, the right to claim the tax credit is lost, compromising the fiscal advantage of border work. For support on deadlines and declarations, consult the tax-return guide available on the website.

Managing LAMal: Deadlines and Renewal

LAMal must be signed within 3 months of starting work in Switzerland. Annual renewal typically takes place between November and December for the following year. It is essential to respect these deadlines to avoid interruptions in coverage and administrative penalties. In addition, the border worker will need to pay the monthly or quarterly premium to the chosen private insurer (companies such as Helsana, CSS, Concordia, Sanitas are among the main ones in Ticino).

Long-Term Prospects: The Three Options

Who lives in Villa Guardia and works in Ticino has three possible paths over time. Option 1 — Continue as a G Permit holder (permanent Italian residence): renew the G Permit annually, maintain residence in Villa Guardia, continue paying AVS/LPP in Switzerland. Advantages: administrative stability in Italy, lower costs. Disadvantages: continuous commuting, double bureaucratic management. Option 2 — Switch to a B Permit (Swiss residence): after 5+ years of border work, request a change of residence in Ticino and acquire a B Permit. Advantages: administrative simplification, full Swiss rights. Disadvantages: relocation, change in cantonal/comunal taxes, abandonment of Italian social network. Option 3 — Return to Italy full-time: cease work in Switzerland, return to the Italian fiscal/previdential regime, collect any accumulated LPP. Each choice has implications for future AVS income, LPP capital, Italian INPS rights, and residence in both countries. What is the difference between Leave G and Leave B?
The G Permit is for border workers who reside abroad (e.g. Villa Guardia in Italy) and work in Switzerland as non-residents. Permit B is for those who actually reside in Switzerland. A G border worker from Villa Guardia who continues to reside in Italy maintains the G permit. Do I have to pay IRPEF in Italy if I work in Ticino and reside in Villa Guardia?
No, the tax at source is only withheld in Switzerland by the employer. In Italy, the frontier worker denounces Swiss income in 730 (EC framework) and claims the tax credit, which neutralizes Italian personal income tax on the same tax base. The Italian-Swiss Convention of 9 March 1976 avoids double taxation. If the Swiss rate is lower than the Italian IRPEF, the frontier worker will have a credit (refund via 730); if higher, the credit covers the difference and nothing is due in Italy.
Is LAMal (Swiss health insurance) mandatory if I reside in Villa Guardia and work in Ticino?
Yes, absolutely. LAMal is mandatory for anyone working in Switzerland, regardless of residence. A border worker from Villa Guardia must sign a Swiss LAMal policy within 3 months of starting work. The deductibles range from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 per year depending on the model chosen. The border crossing also remains registered with the Italian INPS (if it has previous contributions), creating a useful double cover if managed consciously.
What are the AVS contributions that you make each month as a border worker?
As an employee in Switzerland, the border worker pays 5.3% of AVS/AI (old age, disability, survivors), plus 1.1% of AD (unemployment) up to CHF 148'200 of annual income, 0.7-1.5% of LAINF (accident insurance according to sector), and LPP (2nd pillar/pension fund) with percentage varying by age group between 7% and 18%. These are calculated on the monthly paycheck and paid directly from the company to the federal fund (SECO).
What does the New Frontier Agreement from 2024 change for me at Villa Guardia?
From 1 January 2024, the New Frontier Agreement (signed on 23 December 2020, ratified in Italy by Law 83/2023) introduces a deductible of €10,000 per year for new frontier workers (those who acquire status after 17 July 2023). If you are a new frontier worker, you have an exemption of €10,000 of income from Italian taxation. Border workers who had status before 17 July 2023 enjoy an exemption of €7,500 until 2033. This means less Italian tax, although Swiss tax always remains the main one.
