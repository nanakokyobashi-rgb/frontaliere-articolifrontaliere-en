For a first orientation, use the canton’s Find a job page and Job-Room. Prepare your CV, availability, languages, distance to the workplace and G-permit situation before applying.

Operational details

How to read the Ticino labour market

The USTAT labour-market page records 79,121 cross-border workers in Q2 2026. The STAF series is quarterly and results from Q1 2024 onward are marked as provisional: the figure describes the statistical population for that period, not the number of available vacancies or the probability of being hired.

For September 2026, SECO reports 4,803 registered unemployed people in Ticino and a rate of 2.9%; the Swiss rate was 3.0%. The SECO measure is administrative and covers people registered with regional placement offices. USTAT reports an ILO rate of 6.1% for Q2 2026, based on another definition. The two percentages should not be placed in the same ranking.

Demand varies by occupation, language, experience and the economic cycle. Avoid lists of sectors that supposedly hire the most unless they come from a dated official table: use the wording of the advert, note recurring skills and check the publication date. Vacancy counts also change every day.

Key points

Where to look for a job

The public starting point is the Ticino canton Find a job page, which brings together cantonal services and links to public-administration vacancies. Job-Room is the national platform for matching vacancies and candidates and for regional placement-office services. Search both, filter by occupation and location, and save the advert URL with the date you read it.

Complete the search with employer websites, professional bodies and agencies authorised for your occupation. An aggregator can help you discover a position, but verify the application on the original source. Do not assume an advert is still open because it appears in search results: check the closing date, workplace, employment percentage and target applicants.

Use our job board as a search aid; do not treat the displayed number of vacancies as permanent. A speculative application works only when it is addressed to the right employer and profile: explain why you know the activity, what problem you can solve and at which location you could work.

A CV for an application in Switzerland

There is no single mandatory Swiss CV. The document should be readable, current and consistent with the advert. Start with the most recent experience and describe verifiable results: tools used, responsibilities, project size and your own contribution. Avoid percentages or figures that you cannot explain in an interview.

Include contact details, place of residence, availability, languages with an understandable level, digital skills and your current or expected authorisation to work in Switzerland. Attach diplomas, employment certificates and references only when requested or genuinely useful. A photograph does not replace skills and is not a guarantee of selection.

Prepare an Italian version for Ticino positions and an English or German version when the advert requests it. Give the file a recognisable name, for example «Surname_Name_CV_2026.pdf». Check that dates, employment percentages and contact details match your online profile and cover letter.

A practical application strategy

Before sending your CV, extract five elements from the advert: duties, essential skills, language, location and contract type. Answer in the same order with short examples. If the position requires presence in Lugano, Bellinzona or Mendrisio, explain how you will reach the workplace and when you are available; do not leave the recruiter to guess about commuting.

Keep a log with employer, role, date, source, CV version and application status. This lets you update a document when an employer changes an advert and prevents you from sending the same text to different positions. A targeted application is more useful than an undifferentiated list of submissions.

After applying, monitor the address listed in the advert and prepare a concrete answer about motivation, notice period, availability and salary expectations. Do not present an amount found in a blog as a market fact: for comparisons use the USTAT salary page and ask the employer how its proposed range is constructed.

Salary data: median, G permit and a real offer

The latest USTAT salary-structure data available concern 2024. In the Ticino private sector, the standardized gross monthly median wage was CHF 5,393; for cross-border workers with a G permit, the median was CHF 4,800. Standardization converts wages to a full-time equivalent of 40 hours over 4 1/3 weeks per month. It is not net pay, a legal minimum or a promise for every occupation.

The same table shows CHF 3,710 at the tenth percentile and CHF 9,620 at the ninetieth percentile for the Ticino private sector: the spread is wide. Position, experience, responsibility, working time, collective agreement and sector explain why one figure is not enough for negotiation.

When you receive an offer, ask for annual gross pay, number of payments, employment percentage, any bonus, working arrangement and start date. Compare like with like and keep salary, expense reimbursement and benefits separate. For the full reference use USTAT, 2024 wages and check applicable minimums with the Ticino Labour Section.

Offer and contract: what to check

Before accepting, request a written offer and read it with the contract. Check employer and workplace, role, start and end dates, employment percentage, gross pay and number of payments, probation, notice periods, holidays, working hours, remote work and insurance cover. If the role is covered by a collective or standard employment agreement, ask which text applies and where its minimums are stated.

Do not confuse a sentence in an advert with a contractual clause. Bonuses, thirteenth salary, travel reimbursement, training and remote-work arrangements should have clear conditions. Keep the version of the offer and ask questions before signing, especially when the contract refers to company rules or a collective agreement.

Rules can depend on the sector and personal situation. For cantonal references, consult the Ticino Labour Section; for a legal or union question, obtain qualified advice. This guide does not replace the contract or professional advice.

G permit and recognition of qualifications

For an EU/EFTA citizen who lives in an EU/EFTA state and works for a Swiss employer, the G permit covers cross-border employment. SEM states that the worker normally returns to the foreign home every day or at least once a week and that border zones have been abolished: an EU/EFTA cross-border worker can work anywhere in Switzerland and live in any EU/EFTA region. For an open-ended contract or one longer than a year, the stated validity is five years; for a shorter contract it follows the contract term.

Before starting, ask the employer and canton which procedure applies to your case. Activities of up to three months per calendar year follow the notification procedure described by SEM; do not automatically extend that rule to every employment relationship.

Check the qualification too. SERI explains that recognition may be required for a regulated profession; for a non-regulated profession it is generally not required, although an employer may request documents or an assessment. Check the exact occupation, not only the diploma name.

Interview and first steps after an offer

For the interview, prepare three concrete examples of your work, an explanation of your professional change and an answer about the journey to the workplace. Ask what the priorities are in the first months, how the role is measured, who you will work with and which languages you will actually use. If pay is discussed, clarify annual gross pay, number of payments, employment percentage and variable components.

After the offer, keep the contract, identity document, diplomas, employment certificates and permit communications in one folder. Do not assume that permits, taxes, health insurance or pension arrangements are identical for every cross-border worker: they depend on the personal situation and applicable rules. Start with the information from SEM and the Ticino canton, then confirm with the competent authority.

If the search does not succeed, update your CV and keywords using the feedback received. Do not change official data to fit one application.

Frequently Asked Questions
How many cross-border workers are recorded in Ticino?
USTAT records 79,121 cross-border workers in Q2 2026 in the STAF series; the figure is quarterly and recent periods are marked as provisional.
What is the median wage in Ticino?
In the Ticino private sector, the 2024 standardized gross monthly median was CHF 5,393; for cross-border workers with a G permit it was CHF 4,800. It is a median, not net pay or a guaranteed offer.
Where can I find reliable vacancies?
Start with the Ticino canton Find a job page, Job-Room and the employer’s website. You can also use Frontaliere Ticino’s job board, checking the original advert and its date.
What conditions apply to the G permit?
For EU/EFTA citizens, the normal return to the foreign home is daily or at least weekly. SEM states that mobility covers all Switzerland and that an open-ended contract or one longer than a year has a five-year validity.
Does an Italian diploma need recognition?
It depends on the occupation. SERI says to check the list of regulated professions: recognition may be required there; for non-regulated professions it is generally not required.
