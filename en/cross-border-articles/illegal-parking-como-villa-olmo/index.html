On 2 May 2026, Como's local police carried out a targeted enforcement operation near Villa Olmo on the lakefront: more than fifty vehicles parked illegally — in the park's paths, on the pavement of Via Cantoni and in green areas — were fined. Among the vehicles identified by officers were several carrying Ticino Canton Swiss plates, a pattern that is already well known: the cross-border worker who is employed in Switzerland but lives or parks on the Italian side is not exempt from the Italian Highway Code.

This incident is a useful starting point for examining the daily mobility costs of frontalieri residing in the provinces of Como, Varese or Verbano-Cusio-Ossola. Every day tens of thousands of people cross the border into the Canton of Ticino for work. How many of them accurately calculate the true total cost of their commute — parking, fuel, tolls, train passes, ZTL fines — and compare it against their Swiss net salary?

Park-and-ride near Swiss border crossings: costs and capacity

Illegal parking often stems from a lack of information about alternatives. Here are the main park-and-ride options in the border zone:

  • Ponte Chiasso (CO): interchange parking near the Ponte Chiasso railway station, with direct train connection to Lugano. Typical municipal tariffs of €1–2 per day. Capacity is limited: arrive before 7:00 am on working days.
  • Gaggiolo/Stabio (VA): car park near the pedestrian crossing and bus route for Stabio and Mendrisio. A practical option for those working in the Mendrisiotto without needing to bring a car into Switzerland.
  • Luino – Ponte Tresa (VA): for those working in the Lugano area via the Ponte Tresa border crossing. Municipal car parks in Luino with access to the ferry or the cross-border bus.
  • Domodossola (VB): for frontalieri working in the Canton of Valais or reaching Switzerland via the Simplon Pass. Covered multi-storey car park near the station, affordable rates, direct rail connection to Brig.

1. Check the Como municipality's ZTL map before driving into the city centre: enforcement hours and authorised access points vary by zone. 2. Use the "Como Parking" app or the municipal website to find pay-and-display and hourly parking in the old town. 3. Como's ZTL zones: what frontalieri need to know

The Limited Traffic Zones (ZTL) of the municipality of Como are active during specific time windows (check the municipality's website as hours can change seasonally):

  • Historic centre / Piazza Vittoria: generally active on weekdays and public holidays during daytime hours
  • Lakefront: access restricted during peak hours and summer weekends
  • Automated access points: OCR camera system that reads number plates — a physical barrier is not required to receive a fine

ZTL fine amounts (Art. 7 Highway Code):

  • Unauthorised entry into ZTL: €83 to €332 (30% reduction if paid within 5 days)
  • Second infringement in the same year: doubled base penalty
  • Foreign vehicles (Swiss plates): notice is sent through the bilateral cooperation agreement or to the registered owner in Switzerland

Swiss-registered vehicles are not exempt. The bilateral cooperation agreement between Italy and Switzerland on road traffic allows fines to be transmitted across the border. Recipients of a ZTL fine with Swiss plates in Como can appeal to the Como Prefect within 30 days of notification, or to the Justice of the Peace within 30 days.

Bike and train: the most cost-effective option for Como-area frontalieri

For those living within 5–7 km of a Trenord station, the bike-plus-train combination is often the most economical: it eliminates parking costs in Italy, avoids ZTL zones entirely and reduces the total annual mobility cost. Trenord allows bicycles on regional trains outside rush hours with a reduced supplementary ticket. On the Swiss side, SBB trains accept bicycles with a booking.

Key points

Practical guide: parking in Como and surroundings without penalties

This overview covers the most common situations and solutions for frontalieri who use a car.

Scenario 1 — Working in Switzerland, parking in Italy: The optimal solution is to use park-and-ride facilities near border crossings (see body1). If parking in Como city centre for personal reasons (errands, appointments), use only the signposted pay-and-display car parks, avoiding pavements, green areas and zones reserved for residents (yellow kerb markings). Disc-only blue zones require a parking disc and are limited to 1 or 2 hours.

Scenario 2 — Parking in Switzerland with an Italian-registered vehicle: Those bringing an Italian-registered car to Lugano or Mendrisio must comply with Swiss rules: pay-and-display machines, blue zones (parking disc), multi-storey car parks. Swiss fines for irregular parking range from CHF 20 (expired disc zone) to CHF 120 or more for more serious violations. Italian-registered vehicles are not exempt.

Scenario 3 — Receiving a ZTL fine with foreign plates: If a frontaliere with Swiss plates receives a ZTL fine, the options are: 1. Pay within 5 days with a 30% reduction (amount and IBAN given in the penalty notice) 2. File an appeal with the Como Prefect within 30 days of notification 3. Seek assistance from a CAF tax assistance centre or patronato in your Italian municipality of residence
