Dolores Poretti, 91: 'I find beauty in every moment' (cross-border guide)

Dolores Poretti, 91, resident at the Polis elderly home in Pregassona, has a life story worth telling. In view of Coop's Good Deed Day, we spent some time with her to learn more about her experience. Dolores, affectionately called 'Dolly,' is a woman who has faced many challenges in her life but who today finds 'something beautiful' in every moment.

Widowed too soon, Dolores had to raise her daughter alone after her husband's death in a plane crash. Despite the difficulties, she found the strength to move forward and build a meaningful life. 'I was a seamstress, and ladies would come to us to have their clothes made to measure. They all dressed so chic,' Dolores recalls with a smile. 'They were a bit heavy... they were never satisfied,' she adds with a quick wit that reveals her indomitable spirit.

Today, Dolores lives at the Polis elderly home in Pregassona, where she feels pampered and appreciated. 'I feel good, I'm very pampered, and I lack nothing,' she says proudly. 'I am one of the favorites...' she adds with a mischievous smile. 'They don't say it, but...'

The company of a volunteer

A fundamental element in Dolores' life is the company of a Red Cross volunteer who spends time with her every Tuesday. 'She comes every Tuesday for several years. We talk and drink coffee. We get along very well together,' Dolores emphasizes. This relationship is a perfect example of what it means to do a good deed, a central theme of Coop's Good Deed Day.

Advice for the young

Dolores also has some advice for the young: 'You should always be present. There's always something beautiful around the corner.' This message of hope and positivity is a precious gift that Dolores shares with everyone around her.

Practical Implications for Cross-Border Workers

The story of Dolores Poretti offers many practical lessons for cross-border workers living and working in Ticino. First, it's important to recognize the importance of community and social support. For cross-border workers, who often find themselves living far from their families and friends, building a local support network can make a big difference in quality of life.

How to Build a Support Network

1. Join local groups: Participate in community events and join local interest groups. This can help you meet people with similar interests and build lasting friendships. 2. Volunteering: Offer your time as a volunteer. This not only allows you to do a good deed but also helps you meet new people and feel more connected to the community. 3. Recreational activities: Participate in recreational activities such as cooking classes, language lessons, or sports clubs. These activities can be a great way to meet new people and improve your skills.

The Importance of Mental Health

Dolores' story also underscores the importance of mental health. Coping with the loss of a spouse and raising a child alone can be extremely difficult. For cross-border workers, who may feel isolated, it is crucial to take care of their mental health. Seeking support from mental health professionals or participating in support groups can be very helpful.

Practical Tips for Mental Health

1. Talk to someone: Don't hesitate to seek support from friends, family, or mental health professionals. Talking about your feelings can help you manage stress and anxiety. 2. Practice mindfulness: Mindfulness techniques such as meditation and deep breathing can help you manage stress and improve your mental well-being. 3. Maintain a routine: A daily routine can provide a sense of stability and control, especially during stressful times.

Conclusion

Dolores Poretti's story is a powerful reminder of the importance of community, social support, and mental health. For cross-border workers living and working in Ticino, building a local support network and taking care of their mental health can make a big difference in their quality of life. By following the practical tips mentioned above, cross-border workers can improve their overall well-being and fully enjoy their life in Ticino.
