The Swiss and Italian governments have signed a new tax agreement for border workers, which provides for a deductible of €10,000 and a transitional regime until 2033. This agreement aims to simplify the lives of border workers and reduce taxes.

However, it is important to note that this agreement has not yet entered into force and will only apply from 2023. Until then, border workers will have to continue to follow the tax rules in force.

The new agreement provides that border workers working in Switzerland and residing in Italy can benefit from a deductible of €10,000. This means that they will not be required to pay taxes on their pay, up to a certain limit.

But how does it work in practice? Here is a concrete example: if a frontier worker works in Switzerland and earns €50,000 per year, he will benefit from the €10,000 deductible and will not be required to pay taxes on the excess part.

But not only that: the new agreement also provides for a transitional regime until 2033. This means that border crossers can continue to benefit from the €10,000 deductible even after 2023, but only until 2033.

Tax situation

The tax situation in Ticino is complex and can vary depending on the nationality and tax regime chosen. As a border crossing, you can choose to be subject to the “opt-out” or “opt-in” tax regime. The "opt-out" scheme allows you to be exempt from direct tax in Switzerland, while the "opt-in" scheme requires you to pay direct tax in Switzerland and to declare income also in Switzerland.
