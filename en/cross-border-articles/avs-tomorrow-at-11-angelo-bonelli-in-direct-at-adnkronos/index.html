The event represents an opportunity for the deputy of Avs, Angelo Bonelli, to present his ideas and positions on current issues, in particular those concerning cross-border work in Ticino. The 'Adn Talks' format is a new space for discussion and comparison on the Adnkronos website and YouTube channel, which offers the opportunity to delve into current issues with political and institutional figures. Cross-border work is a topic of great importance for the Canton of Ticino, as many residents work in Italy and vice versa. According to data from the Federal Statistical Office, in 2020 about 42,000 people residing in Ticino worked in Italy, while about 15,000 people residing in Italy worked in Ticino. This represents about 20% of the total workforce of the Canton of Ticino. Cross-border work is regulated by the Convention on Social Security between Switzerland and Italy, signed in 1983 and amended in 2002. The Convention provides for cross-border workers to be covered by the social insurance of the country in which they work, but they may choose to be insured in the country of residence. In Ticino, cross-border workers are required to pay the AVS (Old Age and Survivors Insurance) contribution at the time of the tax return, while in Italy they pay the INPS contributions. Cross-border work is an increasingly widespread reality in Ticino, with many residents travelling daily to Italy for work. However, this choice also entails some complications from a fiscal and contributory point of view. For example, frontier workers have to pay taxes in both Switzerland and Italy, unless they opt for single taxation in Switzerland. In this case, however, they must pay an additional 4.5% at source on the gross income received in Italy. In addition, they must pay social security contributions in both countries, unless they are exempted under bilateral agreements. In addition, border workers also have to deal with regulations on social security contributions, which vary depending on the sector to which they belong and the nationality of the worker. For example, Italian workers working in Switzerland must pay Swiss social security contributions, while Swiss workers working in Italy must pay Italian social security contributions.
