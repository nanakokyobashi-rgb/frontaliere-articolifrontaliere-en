Key facts

  • What: Littering

  • Who: Local police of Cantello

The fight against environmental degradation along the border strip between Italy and Switzerland has marked a new point in favor of legality. A Swiss citizen, a 'waste cross-border worker,' was identified and sanctioned by the local police of Cantello after being caught abandoning waste on the municipal territory. The man was notified of a 100 euro fine for improper disposal. The dynamics of the verification in Cantello The man would have crossed the border crossing with the specific aim of emptying some bags of waste into a public bin in the town. A quick action that did not, however, escape the vigilant eye of the authorities. Thanks to an accurate verification and investigation activity carried out by the agents of the local police, it was possible to precisely trace the identity of the offender and proceed with the notification of the administrative sanction.

Operational details

Analysis of Practical Implications

The abandonment of waste by Swiss cross-border workers in Italy raises several practical implications. Firstly, it is important to consider the normative differences between the two countries. In Switzerland, the waste collection system is well-structured, and disposal costs are included in local taxes. In Italy, however, regulations can vary significantly from municipality to municipality, and disposal costs can be higher.

This disparity can lead to situations where cross-border workers, to avoid additional costs, try to dispose of waste in Italy, where regulations are less stringent. However, as demonstrated by the case of Cantello, this practice can have negative consequences. Not only do cross-border workers risk fines, but they also contribute to environmental degradation.

Comparison of Swiss and Italian Regulations

For cross-border workers, comparing Swiss and Italian regulations can be complex. In Switzerland, waste must be placed in official bags and deposited at designated collection points. In Italy, regulations may vary, but generally, it is necessary to follow the instructions of the municipality of residence.

Another aspect to consider is planning. Cross-border workers should organize their movements so that they can dispose of waste at the appropriate collection points, avoiding excessive accumulation. Additionally, it is useful to know the collection points along their usual routes, so they can dispose of waste correctly even while traveling.
