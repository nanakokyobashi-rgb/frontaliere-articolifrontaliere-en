Living in Bellagio, working in Ticino: border guide (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 5 September 2026

Moving to Bellagio and working in Ticino as a border worker: discover the tax regime, AVS contributions, LAMal and how to plan the transfer.

Context

In brief

  • Withholding tax only deducted in Switzerland for cross-border workers
  • New Cross-Border Agreement effective from 1 January 2024
  • AVS/AI contributions: 5.3% for employees, LAINF 0.7–1.5%
  • LAMal: deductible CHF 300–2500, option right Permit G

Key facts

  • Withholding tax: Deducted only in Switzerland
  • New Agreement: Effective from 1 January 2024
  • AVS/AI: 5.3% for employees
  • LAINF: 0.7–1.5% depending on sector
  • LPP: 7–18% by age group (from 25 years)
  • Deductible: €7,500 for old cross-border workers (until 2033), €10,000 for new ones

Bellagio, overlooking Lake Como, is a popular residential choice for those working in Ticino. The province of Como is within manageable distances from major Ticino work centers: Lugano, Mendrisio, Chiasso, Bellinzona. For this move, understanding the tax, contribution, and insurance regime governing Swiss-Italian cross-border workers is essential, especially after the New Cross-Border Agreement came into effect on 1 January 2024.

The tax system is the central pillar. The withholding tax on employment income is deducted solely by Switzerland, avoiding double taxation. Italy subsequently applies the tax credit (CE box of the 730 tax return) to recognize what has been paid to the Swiss tax authorities. This mechanism, regulated by the Convention on Double Taxation between Italy and Switzerland signed on 9 December 1976, protects the cross-border worker from excessive tax pressure.

Operational details

What to Consider Before Moving to Bellagio

Moving to Bellagio to work in Ticino involves significant practical and financial considerations. The cost of living is the first evaluation: Bellagio is an expensive residential area on Lake Como. Rents and property prices reflect the scenic value and proximity to the Swiss border. A commuter must assess whether the net salary in Ticino (after AVS deductions of 5.3% and LAINF 0.7–1.5%) compensates for housing and commuting costs. A comparative table between monthly net income and rental costs is essential for making an informed decision.

Advantages and Disadvantages of the Choice

The advantages of living in Bellagio include quality of life, a lakeside landscape, an established community of commuters (offering support networks and practical knowledge), and geographical proximity to Ticino, ideal for daily commuting. The disadvantages include significant rental and property costs, daily commuting on mountain roads exposed to weather variability, and the obligation to subscribe to health insurance.

Regarding the choice of insurance: commuters with Permit G have the option to choose between Swiss LAMal and Italian SSN. If you opt for LAMal, the deductibles for adults range from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 annually, depending on the coverage. If you remain in the Italian SSN, you must notify the Swiss authorities of your renunciation of LAMal. This choice directly impacts health coverage and annual costs.

Registration Procedure and Permit G

Before moving, ensure you have the Permit G (commuters). This is mandatory for daily work in Switzerland. The documentation is submitted by the Ticino employer, who coordinates with the Swiss authorities (SEM — State Secretariat for Migration). Without Permit G, the move is legally prohibited.

Key points

Checklist for moving and starting as a cross-border commuter

Step 1: Calculate your net monthly income

Before any move, estimate your net income using a cross-border tax calculator. Subtract from the gross amount:

  • AVS/AI/IPG: 5.3%
  • LAINF: between 0.7% and 1.5% (depends on the sector)
  • LPP: between 7% and 18% (depends on age, from 25 onwards)
  • Federal and cantonal Ticino withholding tax

The result is your net monthly income, to be compared with the actual cost of rent, utilities, and living in Bellagio.

Step 2: Verify the G Permit with your employer

Ensure you obtain the G Permit before moving. The Ticino employer submits the documentation to the Swiss authorities (SEM). This permit is mandatory for daily work in Switzerland and is not a formality you can handle independently: your employer coordinates the process.

Step 3: Choose health insurance (LAMal or Italian SSN)

With the G Permit, you have the option to:

  • Join the Swiss LAMal (deductible CHF 300–2.500 for adults, depending on the chosen coverage)
  • Stay in the Italian SSN (with official notification to the Swiss authorities)

The choice affects premiums/deductibles and coverage. Consult a LAMal agent or local health authorities to evaluate which solution is best.

Step 4: Italian tax registration and communication to the Revenue Agency

Notify the Italian Revenue Agency of the change of residence. Your income will be subject to the new cross-border regime:

  • If you are an old cross-border commuter (already such before July 17, 2023): exemption €7.500 annually until 2033
  • If you are a new cross-border commuter: exemption €10.000 annually

These exemptions reduce the Italian taxable income and result in lower tax payments.

Frequently Asked Questions
Can I work in Ticino and live in Bellagio as a border worker?
Yes, with the G Permit (border crossing). The tax regime is governed by the New Frontier Agreement which entered into force on 1 January 2024. Income tax at source is withheld only from Switzerland, while Italy applies the tax credit to avoid double taxation. You will need to register with the Italian Revenue Agency and the Swiss SEM, and take out health insurance (Swiss LAMal or Italian SSN).
How much is the tax at source for border workers?
The tax at source is calculated according to the federal and cantonal rate of Ticino. It is not a fixed percentage for everyone: it depends on the canton where you work, your overall income, and the number of dependents. The Ticino employer withholds it from the paycheck. Use a tax calculator for a custom estimate.
What are the AVS and LAINF contributions that hold me back?
As an employee, you contribute 5.3% for AVS/AI/IPG and between 0.7% and 1.5% for LAINF (accident insurance). These amounts are deducted from gross pay each month. The employer contributes the same AVS/AI amount. These contributions finance Swiss social security and are mandatory.
Am I entitled to Swiss LAMal if I have G Permit?
Yes, you have a right of option. You can join LAMal with a deductible between CHF 300 and CHF 2,500 for adults (depending on the coverage chosen), or stay in the Italian SSN with notification to the Swiss authorities. The choice has an impact on premiums/deductibles and health coverage. Evaluate both options with an insurance agent.
Am I an 'old frontier' or 'new frontier'?
If you were a border worker before 17 July 2023, you are an 'old border worker' and will enjoy an exemption of €7,500 per year until 2033 (transitional regime). If you started after that date, you are a 'new frontier worker' with a deductible of €10,000 per year. This distinction is central in the calculation of taxable income in Italy.
