Serious accident in the mountain range: 27-year-old falls in Vallemaggia (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 14 August 2026

A 27-year-old Italian crashed for about ten meters in a rugged area on the Robiei Alps, in Vallemaggia (Ticino). His condition is serious and his life is in danger.

Key facts

  • What: Serious accident with fall
  • When: Tuesday evening, 11 August
  • Where: San Carlo, hamlet of Cevio, Alpe Robiei, Vallemaggia (Canton of Ticino)
  • Who: 27-year-old Italian resident in Italy
  • Details: Fall of about ten meters in the impervious zone

A serious accident shook the quiet of the Robiei pasture, located in the territory of San Carlo, a hamlet of Cevio in Vallemaggia, Canton Ticino. On the evening of Tuesday, August 11, just before 8 pm, a 27-year-old young man, of Italian nationality and resident in Italy, was the victim of a fall while engaged in pastoralism. The event occurred in a particularly rugged area of the mountain, making rescue operations more complex.

According to the first reconstructions provided by the cantonal police, whose investigations are still ongoing, the 27-year-old was in a stretch of terrain difficult to reach when, for reasons yet to be clarified, he lost his balance and fell for an estimated distance of about ten meters. The alarm was immediately given, triggering a coordinated response involving cantonal police officers and specialised personnel from Rega, the Swiss air rescue service.

Implications for mountain workers

The accident that occurred to the young shepherd on the Robiei Alps raises questions about safety in work activities carried out in isolated and potentially dangerous mountain environments. Although the source does not specify whether the 27-year-old was a Ticino border worker or if his activity was in any way directly linked to the Swiss labour market, this event draws attention to the inherent risks for those operating in rural or alpine contexts, often characterised by rough terrain and logistical difficulties for rescue. Vallemaggia, like many alpine areas, presents unique challenges for workers, ranging from pastoralism to infrastructure maintenance and summer tourism.

The rapidity of the intervention of Rega, the Swiss air rescue service, highlights the efficiency of emergency systems in Switzerland, a crucial aspect for anyone working in remote areas. However, the event also underlines the importance of adequate training and prevention measures to minimise risks. In a Canton like Ticino, where outdoor and rural activities have a certain relevance, safety at work in the mountains is an ever-present theme. The exact causes of the fall are not yet specified, but the cantonal police investigation will aim to clarify whether there was negligence or whether it was a tragic accident due to the nature of the terrain.

What to do in case of emergency in the mountains

In emergency situations in the mountains, especially in remote areas like the Alpe Robiei where the serious accident occurred, it is essential to act promptly and know the correct procedures for requesting assistance. The cantonal police and REGA were the first agencies to intervene, demonstrating the importance of an efficient and well‑organized rescue system, such as the one in Switzerland.

Rescue alert procedure:

1. Call for help: The first step is to contact the emergency numbers. In Switzerland, the general emergency number is 112. For mountain‑specific rescues, REGA can be contacted directly or via local emergency services. If you are in an area with phone coverage, it is essential to provide the most precise location possible, describing the surroundings, any landmarks, and the nature of the incident. 2. Assess the situation: If possible and safe, evaluate the condition of the injured person and provide first aid while waiting for rescuers, but without putting yourself in danger. 3. Wait for rescuers: Once the rescue has been alerted, stay in a visible and accessible area to facilitate identification by helicopters or ground teams.
