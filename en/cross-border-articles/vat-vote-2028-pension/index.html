The issue identified by the source

The original source presents the 13th AVS as a measure still awaiting financing. The funding is linked to the possibility that the standard VAT rate will rise from 8,1 to 8,5% starting in 2028.

The wording is cautious and does not amount to an announcement of a decision that has already entered into force. The reference to “or not?” indicates that the outcome remains linked to the vote on November 29. The material does not indicate the year of the vote, nor does it specify the expected revenue, the amount of the 13th AVS, or the share of funding that would derive from VAT.

For those following Swiss affairs from across the border, the verifiable information is therefore limited: the discussion concerns financing the 13th AVS and a possible change to the standard rate. No names of sponsoring authorities, statements by political parties, or implementation details are reported. Nor is it indicated whether the change would affect other VAT rates.

The news, as provided, does not make it possible to turn the hypothesis into an individual cost. A calculation basis and an individual amount are missing. The key figure to retain is the comparison between 8,1% and 8,5%, with effect indicated from 2028, while the decision is linked to the vote on November 29.

Operational details

The comparison between the two values

The change indicated by the source is 0,4 percentage points: 8,5% minus 8,1%. It is an arithmetic comparison between two rates, not an estimate of a final amount. In fact, the text provides neither a typical expenditure nor a base to which to apply the change.

The limits of the available information

The text provides a framework, not a complete implementation schedule. It says that the standard VAT rate should rise from 8,1 to 8,5% from 2028 and refers to the 29 November vote. It does not, however, indicate the full content of the question, the possible outcome, subsequent financing arrangements or the amount of the benefit. Without these elements, it is not possible to establish whether the increase will be approved or what the precise effect on spending would be.
