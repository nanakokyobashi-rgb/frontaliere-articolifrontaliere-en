The popular vote on the amendment to the Federal Law on Health Insurance (LAMal) is a topic of great interest for cross-border workers who work in Switzerland and reside in Italy. The amendment, proposed by the Federal Office of Public Health (BAG), concerns the uniform financing of healthcare services. This change could have a significant impact on the cost of health insurance for cross-border workers, who currently benefit from special conditions.

What the amendment provides

The proposed amendment to the LAMal aims to uniform the financing of healthcare services among the different Swiss cantons. Currently, cross-border workers who work in Switzerland and reside in Italy are subject to specific rules that could be revised. This could lead to changes in the rates and contributions paid by cross-border workers.

Impact on cross-border workers

Cross-border workers in Ticino could see an increase in insurance costs if the amendment to the LAMal is approved. Currently, cross-border workers benefit from advantageous conditions compared to Swiss residents, but the new legislation could reduce these benefits.

Concrete examples

  • Chiasso: A cross-border worker who currently pays around 300 CHF per month could see an increase to 345-375 CHF.
  • Lugano: A worker with a current premium of 400 CHF might face a cost of 440-480 CHF.
  • Bellinzona: A premium of 350 CHF could rise to 385-415 CHF.

Regulations and dates

The amendment to the LAMal is under discussion and the popular vote is scheduled for 2024. Current regulations provide that cross-border workers pay an insurance premium based on the income received in Switzerland, but the new law could introduce a system based on the actual cost of healthcare services.

The amendment to the LAMal could have various practical implications for cross-border workers in Ticino. For example, it might change how health insurance contributions and benefits are calculated. With the amendment, these costs could increase, especially for those residing in municipalities like Lugano, Mendrisio, or Chiasso, where premiums are already higher than the Swiss average.
