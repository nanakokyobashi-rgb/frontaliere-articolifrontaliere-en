The arrest of a Swiss federal police officer, suspected of leaking sensitive information to a drug trafficking organization, has reignited the debate on the fight against the mafia in Switzerland. Former federal prosecutor Rosa Maria Cappa has put forward a strong proposal for the Confederation: introducing the preventive seizure of assets, exactly as Italy did to hit mafia assets. The proposal is based on the fact that mafias are 'fundamentally profit-seeking associations with mafia methods' and that 'tools are needed to cut off the oxygen to the mafias.'

The case of the federal police officer

The case of the fedpol collaborator, which emerged during an operation involving France and Germany and led to the arrest of five other people in central Switzerland, has revealed what the director of the federal police, Eva Wildi-Cortés, described as an infiltration of institutions. An investigation has been opened to shed light on the case, which, if confirmed, would represent the most serious breach in the police system tasked with combating organized crime.

The proposal to seize mafia assets preventively in Switzerland could have significant implications for cross-border workers in Ticino. Comparison with the current situation

Currently, Switzerland does not have tools for the preventive seizure of mafia assets like those in Italy. This means that criminal organizations can accumulate wealth without the immediate risk of losing it. Rosa Maria Cappa's proposal aims to fill this gap, introducing a mechanism similar to Italy's, which has proven effective in hitting the assets of the mafias.

Possible scenarios

If the proposal is approved, cross-border workers could see an increase in asset seizure operations by Swiss authorities. This could lead to greater transparency and a reduction in illicit activities in the Canton of Ticino. However, it is important to note that the legislative process will be complex and could take several years to complete.

Comparison with other jurisdictions

In Italy, the preventive seizure of mafia assets was introduced for the first time in the 1990s and has proven to be an effective tool in the fight against organized crime. Switzerland, on the other hand, has a different legal system and may face unique challenges in implementing such a measure. However, the Italian experience could offer valuable lessons for Switzerland.
