Operational details

The tax classification is part of the analysis of the 1,519 companies considered by the territorial economic report, confirming the crucial role of corporate taxation in cross-border development between Lombardy and the Canton of Ticino. The first five companies thus reach, in total, over 231 million euros in taxes paid, highlighting a concentration of added value that also directly affects the flows of border workers residing in the provinces of Varese, Como and Sondrio.

This scenario is inevitably reflected in the Ticino border municipalities such as Mendrisio, Chiasso and Lugano, where many of the subsidiaries and companies owned by these industrial giants operate. The cross-border economic impact has to deal with the new tax legislation that entered into force on 17 July 2023, which redesigned the taxation of cross-border workers with the introduction of a tax at source in Switzerland up to a maximum of 80% and the return of a share in the Italian municipalities of residence, initially estimated based on geographical proximity bands within 20 kilometres of the border.

Key points

Here are the companies that pay the most taxes in Varese

The companies that pay the highest volume of taxes in the territory of Varese represent the economic engine of an area strongly interconnected with the Swiss market. At the top of this ranking are industrial and distribution giants such as Bticino, Sea Esercizi Aeroportuali, Novartis Farma, Irca and Tigros Real Estate. This tax dynamic has a direct impact on the cross-border financial flows and asset management of the thousands of workers residing in Ticino, in particular in border municipalities such as Mendrisio, Chiasso, Lugano and Stabio.

The Varese production fabric feeds an economic chain that directly involves the Swiss labour market.

To understand the impact of these tax balances on your personal situation, it is essential to consider the innovations introduced by the new tax agreement on frontier workers, ratified and entered into force on 17 July 2023, with effect from 1 January 2024. According to the new provisions, the so-called "new frontier workers" – that is, those who have started working in Ticino since that date – are subject to concurrent taxation between Switzerland and Italy, with the elimination of the historic deductible of 8,000 euros, replaced by a limited favourable treatment and a tax credit to avoid double taxation.
