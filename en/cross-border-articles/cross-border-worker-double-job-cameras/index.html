Double work and false stamping: jammed frontier (cross-border guide)

Di Valerie Linc · Published on 13 July 2026

A Varese municipal employee was reported for fraud and false attestations after working in Ticino during working hours.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Varese municipal employee caught working in Ticino
  • Video surveillance systems and telephone data revealed the deception

Key facts

  • What: Complaint for double work and false stamping
  • Who: Employee of a Municipality of Alto Varesotto
  • Where: Municipality of Varese and company based in Ticino
  • How: Crossroads between video surveillance and telephone data
  • Episodes: 18 unexcused absences
  • Crimes: Fraud against the State and false claims

The Guardia di Finanza of Varese, through the Compagnia di Luino, concluded an investigation that led to the identification of an employee of a Municipality of Alto Varesotto engaged in undeclared double work. The man, who holds the role of public green maintenance officer for the municipal administration, implemented a precise strategy to simulate his presence in the service.

The employee's modus operandi

According to the statement of the Guardia di Finanza, the employee systematically reached the town hall using a service vehicle of the entity. This step was essential to formally certify its presence in the workplace. Operational details

The analysis of this case highlights how the intersection of different data sources can make it almost impossible to conceal unauthorised cross-border movements. In the specific case, the determining element was not a single test, but the convergence of three different information flows that made it possible to reconstruct the movements of the subject with surgical precision.

# Surveillance tools

The authorities have used a cross-checking method that every border crossing should consider when assessing the management of their working hours: the analysis of images of video surveillance systems installed in neighbouring municipalities and the extraction of telephone data. This technical combination made it possible to map every single human movement during the time when it was formally in service for the Municipality.

The comparison between these digital evidence and the timestamps of the card led to the ascertainment of as many as 18 episodes of unexcused absence. This scenario highlights the legal risks to which those who try to combine a public job in Italy with a professional activity in Switzerland without the necessary authorisations are exposed.

Key points

To avoid legal and fiscal complications, it is essential that every cross-border worker properly manages their employment relationships, especially if one of the two is linked to the Public Administration. If you wish to undertake a second job in Ticino, you must follow a transparent procedure.

Procedure for combining activities

1. Contract verification: check if the current employment contract (especially if public) prohibits other paid activities or requires prior authorization. 2. Authorization request: submit a formal application to the employer to obtain permission to perform secondary activities, specifying the working hours and nature of the work. 3. Permits regularization: ensure that the Permesso G is correctly updated and that the Ticino company is aware of the employee's contractual situation. 4. Fiscal management: verify the impact of the second income on the tax declaration in Italy, remembering that the Swiss withholding tax is managed according to agreements between the two countries.
