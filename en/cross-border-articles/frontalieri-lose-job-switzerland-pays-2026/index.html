Frontier workers in crisis: Switzerland pays the price of revolution (cross-border guide)

• What: The reform of the management of unemployment benefits for cross-border workers. • When: April 2026. • Where: Switzerland. • Who: Cross-border commuters. • Amount: CHF 1 billion.

Switzerland is preparing for a possible financial earthquake. After more than ten years of complex negotiations in Brussels, the European Union has given the green light to a reform that could upset the Confederation's accounts. At the center of the debate is the management of unemployment benefits for cross-border workers, a change that closely affects the pockets of Bern and the lives of thousands of Italian, French and German workers.

With the new principle of Lex Locis Laboris, the music changes: the benefit will have to be paid by the country in which the contributions were paid. In simple terms, if a worker living in Italy loses his job in a Ticino company, Switzerland will have to pay for his unemployment.

The weight of the measure is closely linked to volumes. At the end of 2025, the number of active cross-border commuters in Switzerland reached 410,000, an absolute record in Europe. According to rumors collected by the Aargauer Zeitung relaunched by the Corriere del Ticino, the economic impact for the Swiss coffers would be enormous:

Minimum estimate: 500 million francs per year. Maximum estimate: almost 1 billion francs per year.

These figures vary according to the economic situation, but which nevertheless represent an unprecedented burden on the Swiss social system.

Operational details

Switzerland is preparing for a possible financial earthquake due to the reform of the management of unemployment benefits for cross-border commuters. The new law provides for Switzerland to pay unemployment benefits to cross-border commuters. The economic impact could be enormous, with estimates ranging from 500 million to almost 1 billion francs per year.

The French "hole": the frightening example

To understand the extent of the problem, just look at what happened in France. Between 2011 and 2023, the French unemployment fund (Unedic) accumulated a deficit of 9 billion euros precisely because of the current rules on cross-border commuters. In 2024 alone, the negative balance was 860 million.

The EU reform aims to remedy these distortions, but shifts the economic burden directly onto Switzerland's shoulders.

Bern's resistance and the political knot

Switzerland has not stood idly by. Together with Luxembourg, Bern has long tried to put the brakes on the reform, raising doubts about its ability to control beneficiaries residing abroad. While Luxembourg could obtain a transition period of up to seven years, for the Confederation the game remains open and very delicate.

In addition to the economic aspect, there is the political aspect. The dossier risks becoming fuel on the fire in the debate on relations between Switzerland and the EU and on free movement, fueling the campaigns of critics of bilateral agreements (such as the one linked to the "10 million" initiative).

It remains to be seen how the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (Seco) will handle the transposition of these regulations, which will in any case require explicit consent and the usual internal parliamentary steps.
