The Cantonal Hospital Authority (EOC) of Bellinzona has opened the search for a medical candidate for the internal medicine and intensive care departments. The announcement, posted on the hospital's recruitment portal, represents a concrete opportunity for Italian professionals who cross the border daily to work in Ticino.

At this time the operational details have not been disclosed: the deadline for submitting applications, the expected salary level, specific experience requirements or the selection procedure are still undefined. The EOC, however, is known for regularly hiring medical staff from Italy, thanks to geographic proximity and a shortage of specialists in certain areas.

For a cross-border doctor, joining a cantonal hospital means entering a structured work environment, with shifts regulated by the collective labour agreement of the Ticino public sector. The Bellinzona site, in particular, is a reference point for emergency medicine and intensive care in Italian-speaking Switzerland.

Operational details

Practical Analysis: Tax and Pension Framework

For an Italian doctor who crosses the border every day, working at EOC Bellinzona means fully entering the cross-border worker regime, but with some specificities related to the public sector. Withholding tax is calculated on gross salaries and paid monthly. Unlike cross-border workers in the private sector, those employed by a cantonal authority may be entitled to specific deductions for pension contributions and professional expenses, but only at the time of the annual declaration, if applicable.

From a pension perspective, the doctor will accumulate AVS (first pillar) and LPP (second pillar) contributions. The Canton Ticino pension fund offers supplementary coverage that, at retirement, is added to the state pension. For Italian cross-border workers, it is crucial to coordinate these contributions with their INPS position in Italy to avoid double taxation and to ensure the totalization of insurance periods for pension purposes.

Health insurance (LAMal) is mandatory and the premium is the employee's responsibility. Cross-border doctors can choose from various health insurance providers, but they must consider whether to maintain supplementary coverage in Italy for treatments received in their country of residence. This choice directly impacts the cost of living, which remains high in Ticino compared to the Italian border provinces.

Key points

How to apply and what to prepare

At the moment, the EOC has not published a deadline for this position, but it is likely that applications will be collected via the organization's online portal. For a cross-border doctor, preparing the application requires a few concrete steps.

Required documents (indicative list)

  • Curriculum vitae in European or Swiss format, with a professional photo.
  • Copy of the degree in medicine and any specialization.
  • Certificate of registration with the Italian professional register (and, if already obtained, registration with the MEBEKO register).
  • Certificates of language proficiency (Italian, preferably also German or French).
  • Cover letter explaining the interest in internal or intensive medicine in an intercantonal context.

If the candidate does not yet have recognition of their qualifications in Switzerland, they can still apply, but will need to initiate the equivalence procedure with MEBEKO. Waiting times vary from a few weeks to a few months, depending on the completeness of the documentation.

After hiring

Once the position is obtained, the doctor must apply for the cross-border worker G permit if they do not already have it. The procedure takes place at the Migration Office of the Canton of Ticino and requires the employment contract, a valid identity document, and proof of residence in Italy. The permit is issued within a few weeks and allows work exclusively in the specified canton.

To best manage tax aspects, it is advisable to use the net salary calculator to simulate the monthly payslip. To compare health insurance costs, one can consult the LAMal guide for cross-border workers. Finally, to stay updated on other job offers in Ticino, you can visit the job ads section of the site.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the requirements for an Italian doctor who wants to work at the EOC in Bellinzona?
The specific requirements for this position have not yet been disclosed. In general, to practice in Ticino an Italian doctor must hold a medical degree, have completed specialization (or be about to do so) and obtain recognition of the qualification from MEBEKO. Proficiency in Italian is essential and knowledge of German or French is often appreciated.
What type of permit is needed to work as a cross‑border doctor in Ticino?
For a doctor residing in Italy who works in Ticino, the G permit for cross‑border workers is the standard solution. It must be applied for after signing the contract, submitting the contract itself, the passport or identity card, and proof of residence in Italy. The permit allows work only in the canton of Ticino and must be renewed periodically.
How does withholding tax work for a doctor hired by the EOC?
The withholding tax is deducted monthly from the gross salary. The rate depends on the municipality of residence in Italy, marital status and number of children. For municipalities within 20 km of the border a tax exemption is provided. At the time of the annual tax return, the cross‑border worker can request a refund of any excess or deduct professional expenses.
Do AVS contributions paid in Switzerland also count toward the Italian pension?
Yes, thanks to the bilateral agreements between Switzerland and Italy, AVS contribution periods can be combined with INPS periods to accrue pension rights. Upon retirement, the doctor will receive a pension from the Swiss AVS and one from the Italian INPS, each calculated on the contributions actually paid in the two countries.
