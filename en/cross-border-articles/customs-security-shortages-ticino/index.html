Customs shortages: union asks for means and security (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 26 August 2026

The Garanto union denounces shortages of personnel and equipment in Swiss customs. What it entails for border workers at Ticino crossings.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Guarantee reporting shortages of personnel, training and equipment to Swiss customs
  • Episode of August 20 in Thônex: shooting with an injured person
  • Obsolete weapons in use: MP5 of almost 50 years, P30 pistols without warranty
  • Request: adequate staff, reinforced training, suitable infrastructures

Key facts

  • Who: Swiss Customs Staff Guarantee Union
  • What: Alert about critical shortages of personnel, training and equipment
  • When: Recent announcement; episode of 20 August in Thônex
  • Where: Swiss borders; specific episode in Thônex (Geneva)
  • Body: UDSC (Federal Office of Customs and Border Security)
  • Request: Adequate means, reinforced security and suitable infrastructure

The call of the union

The Garanto union today launched an official alert about the increase in cross-border crime, calling on the federal government to urgently upgrade personnel, specialised training and modern equipment for Swiss customs.

The alert follows the violent incident on 20 August in Thônex, where gunfire exploded near the border with one person injured. According to Garanto, this escalation of crime shows that the operational reality on the territory has changed rapidly, while customs remains a prisoner of frozen resources and obsolete equipment.

Operational details

Impact on commuting Ticino-Italy

The cross-border crime documented in the episode of 20 August in Thônex illustrates a concrete reality for those who transit daily at the Ticino-Italy crossings (Chiasso, Brogeda, Gaggiolo, Ponte Tresa). Garanto does not denounce abstract deficiencies, but operational conditions that directly affect border workers.

Reported shortfalls include: insufficient staff, reduced safety training, crumbling infrastructure, and outdated equipment. These problems are not isolated: when customs operate with limited resources, the effects have an impact on the quality of the customs service — transit processing times, detection of abnormal situations, reactivity to security emergencies.

The permessi frontalieri system represents the legal basis for daily commuting between Ticino and Italy, but this mobility also depends on the efficiency and security of customs gates. Organizational deficiencies and inadequate equipment directly compromise this experience.

The UDSC paradox

Garanto underlines a fundamental paradox: the UDSC contributes significantly to the revenue of the Confederation through customs duties and controls, yet remains subject to federal savings programs. This creates a disconnect between assigned responsibilities (border security, revenue) and allocated resources.

Key points

What we can expect in the coming months

The alarm raised by Garanto opens a political window. The federal government must respond with concrete resources, or the issue will remain an empty promise. Cross-border workers who daily transit through the Ticino border crossings should pay attention to developments, because waiting times and security levels at the checkpoints directly reflect the state of funding of Swiss customs.

Garanto has spelled out the requests: adequate staff, specialized security training, modern equipment (replacement of MP5 and obsolete pistols), and suitable infrastructures. If the federal government welcomes these demands, the improvement will first affect the efficiency of the border crossings.

Strategies for cross-border workers

Until concrete federal resources arrive, those who frequently transit the Ticino-Italy crossings can adopt some precautions. Checking in advance the waiting times at the main customs stations allows better planning during peak hours. Keeping documentation (G Permit, passport, customs documents) in order speeds up transits, especially if customs operates understaffed.

Reporting anomalies — lack of effective controls, systematic delays, suspicious situations — to local customs authorities helps document the real impact of shortcomings. These data can support future funding requests by Garanto and cross-border workers associations.

Frequently Asked Questions
What does' cross-border crime 'mean in the context of the Thônex episode?
The August 20 episode in Thônex (shooting with an injured person) illustrates how organized crime exploits border areas for illegal acts. Swiss customs is often the first contact with people who commit cross-border crimes. Garanto argues that insufficient equipment and personnel do not allow customs to effectively prevent these incidents.
What are the 'obsolete' weapons mentioned by Garanto?
The union cites MP5 machine guns with almost 50 years of use and P30 guns that are no longer guaranteed by the manufacturer. These weapons, if they require maintenance or replacement of parts, no longer receive official support, compromising the operational readiness of customs officers.
Does the USSC really contribute to the revenue of the Confederation?
Yes. The USDC collects duties on goods in transit, carries out customs checks and applies sanctions, generating significant revenue for the federal budget. Guaranto notes the paradox: while generating resources, the Office remains subject to federal budget constraints that do not allow for adequate reinvestment in personnel and equipment.
How do staffing shortages impact border crossers who transit on a daily basis?
With reduced headcount, fewer customs officers are available to process transits at crossings. During critical commute hours, queues lengthen and wait times increase. Border crossers who commute daily between Ticino and Italy already experience variability in crossing times; the deficiencies reported by Garanto could further aggravate the situation.
What request did the Garanto union make to the federal government?
Garanto asks for: adequate personnel for the tasks, reinforced training on security, modern equipment to replace obsolete weapons and devices, infrastructure suitable for crossings. The conclusion of the note is straightforward: 'Customs officers do not ask for privileges, but for the means to work and return home safely'.
