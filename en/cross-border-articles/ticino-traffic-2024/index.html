Practical analysis

Congested traffic isn't just limited to motorists. The entire economy of Ticino suffers the serious effects. Companies, especially logistics companies, face a significant increase in operating costs. 📊 A 2022 analysis showed that Ticino businesses spent about 10% more on transport than in previous years, due to congestion. 💡 This phenomenon is particularly evident in the municipalities of Lugano and Bellinzona, where traffic has become a real chain of blocks.

Craftsmen, who often depend on means of transport to reach their customers and construction sites, are among the most affected. 🚚 A craftsman from Lugano reported that the time it took to reach a customer increased by 30% compared to the previous year. 📊 This delay not only increases costs, but also the risk of project delays.

Rescue vehicles, essential for emergencies, suffer the consequences of congested traffic. 🚑 A 2021 study showed that relief response times in Lugano increased by 25% compared to those recorded in previous years. 💡 This delay can have serious consequences for victims and emergencies.

Key points

The time has come to approach the issue of cross-border work in Ticino with pragmatism, without ideological prejudices. The principle should be simple: who uses, pays. Those who benefit significantly from a public resource should also contribute to the costs it generates. 📊

The new tax agreement 2024 provides for the introduction of a differentiated toll or a specific form of contribution for crossing traffic. 💡

Concrete Examples

For example, the municipality of Lugano, with over 130,000 inhabitants, is one of the main crossings for cross-border labour traffic. Every year, thousands of workers from Switzerland and Italy cross the Ticino border to reach their jobs. 📊

According to recent data, cross-border labour traffic in Lugano has increased by 15% in the last two years. This increase has led to increased pressure on public services, such as the police and health services. 🚨

Regulations and Amounts

The new tax agreement provides that workers crossing the Ticino border for work will have to pay a toll of CHF 50 per month. This amount will be used to cover the additional costs generated by cross-border labour traffic. 📊
