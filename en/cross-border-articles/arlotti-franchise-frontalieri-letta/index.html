The President of San Marino, Arlotti, has written a letter to Enrico Letta, Fabrizio Saccomanni and Stefano Fassina, asking for the restoration of the tax exemption for cross-border workers in Ticino. This request comes at a time of great discussion regarding fiscal policies for transborder workers. The exemption, which was abolished in the past, could have a significant impact on withholding tax for cross-border workers.

Impact on taxation

Arlotti's request concerns the restoration of an exemption that would allow cross-border workers to benefit from a reduction in withholding tax. This could mean significant savings for many workers who cross the border daily to work in Switzerland. The exemption could be applied to workers residing in Italy as well as those residing in San Marino.

Procedure for cross-border workers

At the moment, it is not yet clear how and when this request might be accepted. However, it is important that cross-border workers are prepared for any changes. If the exemption is restored, workers will need to submit a specific application to the competent offices to benefit from the tax reduction. It is advisable to consult an accountant or tax expert to better understand how this change might affect your tax situation.

Practical Analysis

The restoration of the tax allowance for cross-border workers could have a significant impact on their tax situation. Currently, cross-border workers employed in Ticino are subject to withholding tax that can reach up to 35%. This means a significant portion of their salary is withheld to cover taxes. Restoring the tax allowance could reduce this rate, making it more advantageous for Italian and San Marino workers to work in Switzerland.

Comparison with the Previous Situation

Before the abolition of the tax allowance, cross-border workers could benefit from a reduction in withholding tax. This made it more advantageous for many Italian and San Marino workers to work in Switzerland. However, with the abolition of the tax allowance, many cross-border workers have seen an increase in the taxes they must pay. Restoring the tax allowance could therefore be an important step to improve the tax situation of cross-border workers.

Possible Scenarios
