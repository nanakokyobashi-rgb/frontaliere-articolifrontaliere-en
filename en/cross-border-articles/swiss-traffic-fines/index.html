How Swiss road fines work for Italian residents

Context

In a nutshell

  • Traffic fines in Switzerland are regulated by the Swiss Road Code (CSS) and vary according to the severity of the infringement.
  • Italian residents who circulate in Ticino, for example in Lugano or Bellinzona, are subject to the same rules and sanctions as Swiss residents.
  • Payment of fines must be made within 30 days of notification to avoid increases or enforcement proceedings.

Key facts

  • What: Swiss road fines, with amounts ranging from CHF 40 for minor offences (e.g. exceeding the speed limit of 1-5 km/h in zone 30) to over CHF 1,000 for serious offences (e.g. speeding of 26 km/h or more on the motorway).
  • When: Fines are issued immediately or notified within 6 months of the violation, as required by Article 28a of the Federal Law on Road Traffic (LCStr) of 19 December 1958.
  • Where: The most common infringements in Ticino occur on the A2 (Gotthard) and A13 motorways, as well as in urban centres such as Locarno and Chiasso, where checks are frequent.
  • Chi: Italian residents represent about 20% of foreign offenders fined in Ticino, according to 2023 data from the Federal Office of Roads (USTRA).
  • Amount: Concrete examples include CHF 120 for phone use while driving (Article 90.2 CSS) and CHF 250 for non-use of seat belts (Article 315.1 CSS).

Operational details

Implications for Italian residents

Swiss road fines can have significant implications for Italian residents, especially those who work or reside in Ticino. For example, a speeding fine in a municipality such as Lugano or Bellinzona can range from 100 to several thousand Swiss francs, depending on the severity of the infraction. According to the Swiss Highway Code (art. 27), exceeding the limit of 20 km/h in a 50 zone can result in a fine of at least 250 CHF. If not paid within the scheduled 30 days, the amount may increase by 20%, and in the event of non-payment, the Swiss authorities may initiate a compulsory collection procedure, with possible repercussions on the residence permit (Federal Law on Foreigners, art. 62).

In addition, from 1 January 2020, the Italian-Swiss agreement allows the exchange of data between the authorities, making it easier for Italian residents to identify and notify fines. For example, a parking ban fine in Mendrisio (40 CHF) can be sent directly to the Italian domicile, with a payment deadline of 30 days.

Key points

Action to be taken

If you receive a Swiss traffic ticket, it is essential to act promptly to avoid complications. First, read the report carefully to understand the violation, the amount, and the payment terms. For example, a fine for speeding at 70 km/h in an area limited to 50 km/h in Lugano can cost CHF 120 (according to the Order Fines Ordinance 2023). If you accept the penalty, pay by the indicated deadline (usually 30 days) via payment slip or online. In the event of a dispute, submit a written appeal within 10 days to the Department of Institutions of the Canton of Ticino, attaching evidence such as photos or testimonies.

⚠️ Warning: Failure to pay within the deadlines may result in a fine increase of up to 50% or the initiation of an enforcement procedure. For example, an unpaid fine of CHF 200 in Bellinzona can rise to CHF 300 with interest.

Operational checklist:

1. Check the amount and due date on the report. 2. Decide whether to pay or dispute within 10 days. 3. In the event of an appeal, send a reasoned letter with evidence to the Institutions Department. 4. Keep the payment receipt or copy of the appeal.
