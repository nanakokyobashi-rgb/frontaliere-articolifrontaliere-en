The Italian government finds itself today, Wednesday, May 6, 2026, at a crucial crossroads. At Palazzo Chigi, Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni presides over a decisive summit on appointments to independent authorities, a topic that has been stirring the majority for weeks. But it's not just a technical step: updates on the foreign policy line are also on the table, ahead of a delicate meeting with US Secretary of State Marco Rubio, scheduled for Friday at 11:30 AM.

The meeting with Rubio is politically significant. The visit by the US diplomacy chief aims to mend the rift between Rome and Washington, which opened after recent outbursts by Donald Trump against Italy, accused of lackluster cooperation on the Iran war front. Meloni has already reiterated her firm position, even on the sidelines of the latest European Political Community summit in Yerevan, distancing herself from any unilateral moves by Washington.

Appointments under discussion

In addition to Meloni, the two deputy leaders will be seated around the table at Palazzo Chigi: Forza Italia leader Antonio Tajani and Lega secretary Matteo Salvini. The goal is twofold: to close the open issues regarding independent authorities and to rally the coalition around a shared line, also in view of international commitments.

On the appointments front, the situation is complex. Blue sources reiterate that everything depends on Meloni, who is called upon to find a synthesis between the different sensitivities of the majority. In particular, Forza Italia would not back down on the name of the Leghista Federico Freni for Consob: the veto remains on the table and, at the moment, no openings are being registered. A different climate prevails at the Antitrust, where work continues to find a 'high-level' profile, capable of garnering broader consensus and defusing tensions among allies.

For the replacement of magistrate Roberto Rustichelli at the helm of the Authority for Competition and the Market, the name of the current Secretary General of the Authority, Guido Stazi, and that of the Secretary General of the Presidency of the Council, Carlo Deodato, continue to circulate, although it seems that Meloni does not want to deprive herself of her close collaborator.
