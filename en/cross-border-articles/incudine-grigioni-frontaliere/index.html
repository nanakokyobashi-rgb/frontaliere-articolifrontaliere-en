Switzerland is one of the countries with which Italy has entered into a border agreement, which allows Italian citizens to work in Switzerland and stay in the neighbouring country without having to obtain a residence permit. This agreement is particularly important for Italian citizens who live in municipalities such as Incudine, in the province of Bellinzona, and who work in Graubünden.

The Italy-Switzerland Double Taxation Convention provides for the reduction of double taxation for border workers. This means that Italian citizens working in Switzerland will not be subject to double taxation on income from work. In particular, the agreement provides that Switzerland does not impose income tax on wages and salaries received by Italian citizens working in Switzerland.

Operational details

Living in Anvil and working in Graubünden as a border worker

Living in Anvil and working in Graubünden as a frontier worker can be a beneficial choice for those looking to balance personal life and career. However, it is important to know the rules and taxes that apply in this scenario.

Tax at source

In Switzerland, tax at source is withheld only for frontier workers, so there is no need to pay contributions to the AVS/AI/IPG. However, it is important to note that the new Frontier Agreement, signed on 23 December 2020 and effective from 1 January 2024, introduces new rules for frontier workers.

Old and new frontier workers

Old frontier workers, that is, those who worked in Switzerland before 2024, can benefit from a transitional regime until 2033, with an exemption of €7,500. The new frontier workers, on the other hand, have a deductible of €10,000.

Double taxation agreement Italy-Switzerland

The Italy-Switzerland Double Taxation Convention was signed on December 9, 1976 and provides that taxes are paid only in one of the two countries. It is important to note that the Convention does not cover all types of income, so it is necessary to consult the legislation to know if it applies in case of specific situations.

Frequently Asked Questions
When did the New Agreement for frontier workers between Italy and Switzerland come into force?
The New Frontier Agreement was signed on 23 December 2020 and is officially in force from 1 January 2024. This agreement regulates cross-border employment relationships and introduces important tax innovations, including double taxation protections and specific tax management regimes in the two countries.
What are the deductibles for old and new frontier workers?
Old frontier workers, i.e. those who worked in Switzerland before 2024, benefit from a transitional regime until 2033 with an exemption of 7,500 euros. New frontier workers, on the other hand, can take advantage of a deductible of 10,000 euros on the income received from employment.
How does IRPEF taxation work in Italy for a border worker who earns 60,000 euros?
The IRPEF in Italy is structured on three rates: 23% up to 28,000 euros, 35% in the range from 28,001 to 50,000 euros and 43% for the part exceeding 50,000 euros. A worker who receives an annual income of 60,000 euros will be subject to progressive steps that reach the maximum rate of 43% on the excess portion.
What are the social contribution rates in Switzerland for employees?
In Switzerland, the AVS/AI/IPG contributions applied to employees are 5.3%. With regard to occupational pension (LPP), the rates vary from 7% to 18% based on the age group from 25 years onwards, while the LAINF for accidents ranges from 0.7% to 1.5%.
What does the 1976 Italy-Switzerland Double Taxation Convention provide for?
The Convention signed on 9 December 1976 establishes that employment income received by Italian citizens in Switzerland is not subject to double taxation in both States. The agreement ensures that taxes are paid in a way that avoids duplicate tax levy on wages and salaries.
