Figino Serenza: guide for border workers in Ticino (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 5 September 2026 · Updated on 25 September 2026

Permit G, taxation, LAMal and costs: everything that changes if you live in Figino Serenza and work in the Canton of Ticino as a border worker.

Context

In short

  • Permit G mandatory to work in Ticino from Lombardy
  • Withholding tax only in Switzerland, no double taxation
  • LAMal mandatory in Switzerland with CHF 300–2500 deductibles
  • AVS/LPP: contributions 5.3% on salary + employer payments

Key facts

  • What: Permit G + taxation for cross-border workers
  • When: From January 1, 2024 (new agreement in force)
  • Where: Cross-border workers Italy-Ticino from Lombardy
  • Who: SEM, SECO, Tax Agency, INPS
  • Tax rate: Single in Switzerland, tax credit in Italy
  • LAMal deductible: CHF 300–2500 annual for adults
  • Tax exemption threshold: €7,500 (historic from 17/7/2023), €10,000 (new cross-border workers)

Those who choose to live in Figino Serenza and work in the Canton of Ticino have access to an increasingly common opportunity among cross-border workers: staying in Lombardy, maintaining Italian residency, but transferring the Swiss salary. However, this model involves precise rules for taxation, social security, and insurance. The Permit G (authorization from the SEM) is the first mandatory document.

Permit G and Tax Treatment

The Permit G allows Italian residents to work legally in Switzerland. Those living in Figino Serenza must apply for it at the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) at least 6–8 weeks before the start date of work. Required documents: signed job offer, passport or ID card, residence certificate from the Italian municipality, proof of LAMal subscription.

A key point: the withholding tax is deducted exclusively in Switzerland. There is no double taxation CHF-EUR. The Convention between Italy and Switzerland of March 9, 1976 regulates this matter. Italy does not tax income from work carried out in Switzerland for cross-border workers; rather, they declare the income in the Italian model 730 and apply the tax credit (CE section) to avoid overlaps.

The New Cross-Border Workers Agreement, signed on December 23, 2020 and entered into force on January 1, 2024 (Law 83 of June 13, 2023 in Italy), introduces tax-free thresholds: historic cross-border workers (those already such before July 17, 2023) enjoy an exemption up to €7,500 annually in a transitional regime until 2033; new cross-border workers have a threshold of €10,000.

LAMal and Social Security

Swiss health insurance (LAMal) is mandatory for all workers. The cross-border worker must subscribe to it within 3 months of the start of the employment relationship. Deductibles for adults range from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 annually, depending on the policy and chosen fund. The employer typically covers a portion of the premium according to the collective agreement.

On the social security front, AVS/AI/IPG contributions (employee part) are 5.3% of gross salary. The company pays the same 5.3%. For unemployment (AD/AC), the rate is 1.1% with a cap of CHF 148,200 on annual salary. Accident insurance (LAINF) varies between 0.7% and 1.5% depending on the sector. The second pillar (LPP, supplementary pension) involves contributions between 7% and 18% depending on age, from the 25th year of age.

Operational details

Living in Lombardy, Working in Switzerland: The Economic Calculation

One of the main advantages of living in Figino Serenza and working in Ticino is the cost of living differential. Housing, food, and services in Lombardy are generally lower than in Ticino. This allows for a significant monthly savings potential. However, the commuter must balance this benefit with the recurring costs of commuting: fuel, highway A2 (for which the annual Swiss vignette CHF 40 is required), parking at the Ticino workplace, possible tolls.

The salary is paid in CHF (Swiss francs), while housing, food, and local expenses in Figino Serenza are in EUR (euros). The margin of convenience depends on a careful evaluation of individual items: rent, utilities, food, transportation, healthcare services. Many commuters use specialized salary calculators to simulate the actual net income and compare it with the cost of living.

Border Crossings and Commuter Routes

From Figino Serenza, the commuter has access to multiple border crossings into Ticino. Chiasso (via highway A2) is the most direct route to southern Ticino (Lugano, Mendrisio, south of Lugano border). Brogeda is a less congested alternative during peak hours. Gaggiolo is suitable for those working in the northern lake area (Luino, areas near Ticino). Ponte Tresa serves western Sopraceneri (Locarno, Ascona, Bellinzona).

Wait times at the border vary depending on the time of day and seasonal congestion. During morning and evening peak hours, Chiasso can experience delays. Some commuters adjust their departure times or use public transportation: Como–Chiasso regional trains, then Swiss rail connection to the destination (Lugano, Bellinzona, Locarno).

Consulting border crossing and historical traffic information before planning the route is a common practice.

Administrative Issues Before Moving

Before moving from Figino Serenza to Ticino, specific bureaucratic steps must be completed:

  • Notify the Figino Serenza municipality of the change of residence and obtain the residence certificate
  • Inform the Italian Revenue Agency of the new address and Swiss income source
  • Apply for a G permit with the competent SEM (State Secretariat for Migration)
  • Sign a LAMal contract with a Swiss insurance company (CSS, Helsana, Visana, SWICA, etc.)
  • Notify the Ticino company of the new address and start date
  • Consider opening a Swiss bank account for salary receipt (including CHF-EUR exchange fees in the evaluation)
  • In the first year, consult a tax advisor for correct income tax filing (CE box for tax credit)

Key points

Step-by-Step: Transfer and First Deadlines

Step 1 — Job Search and Written Offer. Identify the Ticino company and obtain a signed and dated job offer. This document is mandatory for the G Permit application.

Step 2 — G Permit Application. Contact the competent SEM (for Ticino: SEM Bellinzona) at least 6–8 weeks before the start date. Required: passport/identity card, job offer, Italian residence certificate, proof of LAMal subscription (even provisional).

Step 3 — Change Italian Residence. Go to the Figino Serenza Town Hall and register the transfer. Obtain the residence certificate for the G Permit application and for communications with the Tax Agency.

Step 4 — LAMal Subscription. Contact a Swiss insurer directly and activate the coverage. Major insurance companies operate online. Note: It is mandatory within 3 months from the start of work, but it is advisable to activate it earlier for the G Permit.

Step 5 — Bank Account. Consider opening a Swiss account with UBS, Credit Suisse, Raiffeisen, or others, or receiving the salary on an Italian account with subsequent CHF-EUR exchange. Evaluate fees and spreads.

Step 6 — First Year Tax Declaration. Consult a tax advisor for the Italian 730 form. Indicate the income declared in Switzerland and the tax credit in the CE section to avoid double taxation.

Recurring Costs to Budget

Once transferred to Figino Serenza, the cross-border worker incurs:

  • Rent/mortgage: Variable depending on the area and square meters in Figino Serenza
  • Highway and fuel: Estimated travel (e.g., 25 km × days/week × 52 weeks), considering gasoline and vignette CHF 40/year
  • LAMal: CHF 300–500 monthly depending on the deductible and policy
  • Bank Account: If Swiss, check monthly or annual fees (some accounts offer free management if the salary is domiciled)
  • Currency Exchange: If salary transfer in CHF → Italian account in EUR, consider bank spreads (0.5–2% depending on the bank)
  • Public Transport: Possible LC (Lugano–Como) or regional train subscription if choosing a combination of car/train

Frequently Asked Questions
Do I have to pay taxes in Italy and Switzerland if I live in Figino Serenza and work in Ticino?
No, there is no double taxation. Tax at source is withheld only in Switzerland. Italy does not tax labour income in Switzerland for frontier workers. In the Italian Form 730, declare the declared income to the Swiss tax authorities and apply the tax credit (CE framework) to avoid overlaps. The New Frontier Agreement (in force from 1 January 2024) provides for tax exemptions: €7,500 for historical frontier workers, €10,000 for new frontier workers.
What is G Leave and how do I request it?
Permit G is the authorization for Italian residents to work in Switzerland. Request from the SEM (Secretariat of State for Migration) at least 6–8 weeks before the start date of work. Required documents: signed job offer, passport/ID card, residence certificate from the Italian Municipality, LAMal subscription proof. Once approved, the permit allows Italy-Ticino to travel legally.
Is LAMal (health insurance) mandatory if I work in Switzerland?
Yes, it is mandatory for all workers in Switzerland. It must be signed within 3 months of the start of the employment relationship. Adult deductibles range from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 per year, depending on the fund and policy. The employer typically covers a share of the premium according to the applicable collective agreement.
How much do I pay for AVS/LPP if I work in Ticino?
On the gross salary, you pay 5.3% for AVS/AI/IPG (employee contribution), to which the company adds 5.3%. For the second pillar (LPP, supplementary pension), contributions range from 7% to 18% depending on your age, starting from the 25th year. Unemployment (AD/AC): 1.1% up to CHF 148,200 annual salary. Accidents (LAINF): 0.7-1.5% depending on the sector.
Which crossings should I use from Figino Serenza for Ticino?
It depends on the work destination. Chiasso (via A2) is the direct route to Lugano and south Ticino. Brogeda is less busy during peak hours. Gaggiolo serves those who work north of the lake (Luino, Varese areas). Ponte Tresa is indicated for Sopraceneri occidentale (Locarno, Bellinzona). Check the traffic forecast before leaving; alternatively, evaluate public transport (Como–Chiasso–Lugano trains).
