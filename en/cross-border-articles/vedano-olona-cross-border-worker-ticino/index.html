Operational details

Taxation and bilateral convention

The Convention between Italy and Switzerland for the avoidance of double taxation (9 March 1976) establishes that the income from employment of a cross-border worker is taxable in the country where the work is carried out, i.e. Switzerland. This means that the gross salary is subject to a tax withheld at source directly in Ticino, according to the federal and cantonal rates in force. The Ticino withholding tax is not an additional tax, but an advance on the tax due; the cross-border worker then recovers the excess using the EC form of the 730 declaration in Italy.

For the fiscal year 2024 and subsequent years, the New Agreement requires the cross-border worker to present the tax return in Italy (730 or Income if freelance/VAT number), reporting the Swiss income net of withholding tax. In the CE framework, it declares the tax paid in Switzerland; the Revenue Agency compares this with the Italian IRPEF due (23% up to €28,000, 33% between €28,001 and €50,000, 43% over €50,000). If the Swiss tax is lower, the cross-border worker integrates into Italy; if it is higher, he receives a credit (which is not converted into a direct refund for cross-border workers, but is compensated with future taxes according to Italian legislation).

Contributions and social security

An often overlooked aspect concerns social security contributions. The cross-border worker resident in Italy is registered with INPS for Italian social security, not with AVS (Swiss old-age and survivors' insurance). However, during the employment relationship in Switzerland, the employer pays contributions to the AVS/AI/IPG at the rate of 5.3% (employee share), together with other contributions (AD for unemployment and AC for acquisition of credit, 1.1% overall; LAINF for accident insurance, 0.7–1.5% depending on the sector). These Swiss contributions do not replace, but are added to, the Italian INPS payments, which the cross-border worker pays on the portion of income subject to Italian contributions.

The old-age pension for cross-border workers therefore combines the Swiss (AVS) and Italian (INPS) periods, with compensation algorithms defined by bilateral treaties. It is advisable to regularly check the payments at the INPS and at the federal statistics office (BFS) or directly at the AVS-INPS coordination centers in the border cantons.

Cost of living and housing strategy

The choice to live in Vedano Olona rather than Ticino is often motivated by the cost of living. Although official sources on Vedano Olona specifically are not available in this context, it is known that border Lombardy has generally lower housing costs (rents, mortgages, services) than the Ticino municipalities. A cross-border worker living in Vedano Olona therefore benefits from a salary in CHF (hard currency) and living expenses in EUR and IQD (lower costs), creating a margin for potential savings. However, this advantage must be weighed against the costs of daily transport to the crossings (fuel, A vignettes, A2/A9 tolls), car maintenance, and the time lost in cross-border commuting, especially in the event of congestion at the Chiasso, Brogeda or Gaggiolo crossings.

The cross-border worker must also evaluate whether health insurance (LAMal for those who opt for the Swiss regime, or Italian policy) is more convenient from an Italian residence or from a Ticino residence. If resident in Italy, the cross-border commuter with Permit G has the right to opt for insurance with a Swiss insurer (LAMal) with a modular deductible (from CHF 300 to CHF 2500 for adults); alternatively, they can remain covered by the Italian health service (SSN), while being able to take out a private Italian supplementary policy. This choice directly affects the net monthly budget.

Practical Procedure: Step-by-Step

For those already living in Vedano Olona or about to move, the concrete steps are as follows: 1. Verification of Border Crosser Qualification. Contact the SEM in the Ticino canton where the employer is based (e.g., SEM Ticino in Bellinzona). Verify if the employer is already registered and if the Permit G application can be submitted directly online or through the counter. 2. Document Collection. Valid passport or ID, Swiss employment contract, proof of residence in Vedano Olona (cadastral extract or municipal certificate), banking documentation, and previous year’s income statement (if available). 3. Application for Permit G. The Swiss employer, in coordination with the border crosser, submits the application to the competent cantonal authorities. In Ticino, the SPOP fee schedule for EU/EFTA citizens lists CHF 75 for an adult permit issue or renewal, CHF 60 for a job change and CHF 40 for a minor; for procedures marked with an asterisk, photo or signature data collection may add CHF 15. 4. Registration with INPS. Once the permit is obtained, the border crosser (or employer, depending on the procedure) registers the employment relationship with INPS using the SR163 form. This activates the Italian social security coverage and ensures protection against work-related accidents and diseases according to Italian regulations. 5. Coordination with AFC (Federal Tax Administration). The Swiss salary is communicated by the employer’s office to AFC, which applies the withholding tax according to the canton (Ticino) and tax class. No personal intervention is necessary, but it’s crucial to verify the correct residence address in the pay slip (indicated as “Wohnort: Italy” or similar). 6. Italian Tax Declaration (730). By April 30th of the following year (or May 31st for electronic declarations), the border crosser declares the Swiss gross income, tax paid in Switzerland, and applies the tax credit in the CE framework to the Italian Revenue Agency. If due, pay the Italian tax supplement; if overpaid in Switzerland, the credit carries over to subsequent years. 7. Annual Deadlines. The €10,000 threshold in Article 4 of Italian Law No. 83/2023 applies exclusively to employment income earned continuously and exclusively by Italian residents in a foreign border area or neighbouring country, as referred to in Article 1(175) of Law No. 147/2013; it is neither a general allowance nor an exemption from Swiss withholding tax. Promptly notify the employer and INPS of any changes.

Frequently Asked Questions
If I live in Vedano Olona and work in Ticino, am I a border worker?
Yes, subject to obtaining Permit G from SEM. The definition of a frontier worker is one who resides permanently in Italy and works as a wage earner in Switzerland for a Swiss registered employer. Vedano Olona, located in border Lombardy, falls within the permitted area of residence. The permit is formal, free and necessary to work regularly.
Do I pay taxes in Switzerland and Italy?
No, thanks to the Convention of 9 March 1976. The main tax is paid in Switzerland (treatment at source). In Italy, there is a 730 with the tax credit to avoid double taxation. If the Swiss tax is lower than the IRPEF due in Italy, integrates; if it is higher, the credit is offset in the following years.
Who qualifies for the transitional regime in Article 9?
Article 9’s transitional regime applies only to workers who meet the Agreement’s definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between 31 December 2018 and 17 July 2023; covered employment income is taxable exclusively in Switzerland. The ordinary regime applies in other cases, with Italian taxation and credit for Swiss tax where applicable. The €10,000 in Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is only the limit for income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law 147/2013, not a general allowance. Do I need to register for the Swiss AVS or the Italian INPS?
Italian INPS. The border worker resident in Italy is registered with INPS for social security, but the employer pays AVS/AI/IPG contributions (5.3%) during the Swiss relationship. At the time of retirement, Swiss working periods are coordinated with INPS for the calculation of the combined pension.
Should I take out Swiss (LAMal) or Italian health insurance?
It depends on your needs. Permit G allows the right of option: you can choose LAMal (deductible CHF 300-2500) with low premiums if young and healthy, or stay in the Italian SSN and integrate with a private policy. Compare premiums and coverage for your situation.
