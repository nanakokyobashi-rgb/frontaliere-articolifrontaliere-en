Fish die-off in Berneck: sewage in stream (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 5 May 2026

About twenty fish died due to sewage in a stream in Berneck, Canton St. Gallen. The responsible farmer will be reported.

Context

In brief

  • About twenty dead fish in a stream in Berneck
  • Effluent discharge from a farm
  • Farmer reported to the St. Gallen public prosecutor's office

Key facts

  • What: Fish kill caused by effluent
  • When: Saturday, May 3, 2026
  • Where: Berneck, Canton of St. Gallen
  • Who: St. Gallen Cantonal Police
  • Amount: Not yet specified

About twenty fish died on Saturday in a stream in Berneck, Canton of St. Gallen, due to the discharge of effluent from a farm. This was announced today by the cantonal police. The farmer, presumed responsible for the pollution, will be reported to the St. Gallen public prosecutor's office. The affected section of the stream has meanwhile been cleaned up by the fisheries inspectorate.

Environmental impact

The pollution of the stream had a significant impact on the local fauna, particularly the fish. The fish kill is an alarming signal that requires immediate attention from the competent authorities. The fisheries inspectorate has already begun cleaning up the affected section of the stream, but the long-term consequences for the local ecosystem are not yet clear.

Reporting procedure

The farmer responsible for the pollution will be reported to the St. Gallen public prosecutor's office. This step is fundamental to ensure that similar incidents do not happen again in the future. The report will also serve to establish responsibilities and determine possible sanctions or compensation for the damage caused.

Future prevention

To prevent similar incidents from happening again, it is essential to implement stricter preventive measures. This could include regular inspections of farms, more frequent checks of waste disposal infrastructure, and greater awareness of the importance of environmental protection.

The fish kill not only damages the local ecosystem but can also have repercussions on biodiversity. Fish are a fundamental part of the food chain, and their disappearance can have a cascading effect on other species. It is important to monitor the situation to assess the long-term impact.

Legal Responsibility

The farmer's complaint to the St. Gallen public prosecutor's office is a crucial step. This legal process will serve to establish responsibilities and determine any sanctions. It is important that the authorities act quickly to ensure that justice is served and that similar incidents do not happen again.

Key points

Concrete actions

Now that we know the implications of the Berneck incident, it's important to know what to do to prevent similar events in the future. Here are some concrete actions that can be taken:

Regular inspections

Local authorities should conduct regular inspections of agricultural companies to ensure they follow environmental regulations. These inspections should be conducted without notice to ensure that companies are always ready to comply with regulations.

Frequent infrastructure checks

Waste disposal infrastructures should be regularly checked to ensure they are functioning correctly. This includes regular maintenance and replacement of any defective components. Authorities should also monitor wastewater to ensure it does not contain pollutants.

Community awareness

Local authorities should organize public meetings and awareness campaigns to educate the community about the importance of environmental protection. These meetings should include information on how to report any issues and how to safely manage waste.

Collaboration with farmers

Local authorities should work with farmers to develop effective strategies for safely managing waste. This could include providing adequate equipment and training on how to safely manage waste. Farmers should also be encouraged to report any issues to the authorities.

Continuous monitoring

Authorities should continuously monitor the quality of water and the health of local wildlife to ensure there are no further incidents. This includes conducting regular studies and collecting water and wildlife samples for analysis. Authorities should also be ready to intervene quickly in case of an emergency.
