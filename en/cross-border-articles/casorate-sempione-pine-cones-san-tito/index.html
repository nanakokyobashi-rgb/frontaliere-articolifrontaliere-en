Operational details

Practical analysis<br><br>The Iris Gallarate CD project for the feast of San Tito in Casorate Sempione represents much more than just a creative activity: it is a concrete example of how social work can generate tangible value for the community. The boys and girls of the center have transformed raw materials collected in nature – such as the seven thousand pine cones collected in the woods and in the Bassetti Park of Gallarate – into over 150 decorations that will embellish Via Genova, a 550-meter street in the historic center.<br><br>This process required two years of constant commitment, with appointments organised two or three times a week, demonstrating how the regular programming of the CDD can be oriented towards specific and measurable objectives. As the educators pointed out, they could have achieved the same capacity building goals through other activities, but they chose to do so by doing something for the community, thus giving particular meaning to the work done at the center.<br><br>Meeting with the inhabitants of Via Genova was a key element of the project. Carla Zoia, coordinator of the CDD and a Casoratta citizen, explained how the idea was born from direct knowledge of the tradition of the feast of San Tito and the desire to create a meaningful collaboration. Key points

What can we learn from this project<br><br>The project of the Iris Gallarate Cdd for the San Tito festival in Casorate Sempione offers several practical applications that can be applied in similar contexts. Firstly, it demonstrates the importance of starting from the available raw material in the territory: in the specific case, the pinecones collected in the woods and at the Parco Bassetti in Gallarate became the basis for over 150 decorations. This approach reduces costs and values local resources.<br><br>Secondly, it shows how to transform a regular activity at the center into a project with a concrete and measurable objective. The educators explained that they could have achieved the same development of skills through other activities, but they chose to do it by creating something for the community. This change of perspective gave a particular meaning to the work done.<br><br>Thirdly, it highlights the value of the encounter with the territory. Carla Zoia, coordinator of the Cdd and Casoratese citizen, emphasized how the idea was born from direct knowledge of the tradition of the San Tito festival and the desire to create a significant collaboration with the inhabitants of via Genova. This positive exchange enriched both parties involved.<br><br>For those who would like to replicate a similar initiative, here are some practical steps:<br>1. Identify a concrete need in the territory (such as a street that needs to be decorated)<br>2. Involve the inhabitants from the early stages to create a sense of belonging<br>3. Start from the locally available raw material (pinecones, recyclable materials)<br>4. Organize regular meetings (two or three times a week) to maintain the work pace<br>5. Use different and accessible techniques (brush, spray cans, hot glue gun, silicone)<br>6. Provide moments of cultural deepening (such as the meeting with don Giuseppe on the history of San Tito)<br><br>The project shows how social work can go beyond assistance to become a real engine of social cohesion and territory valorization. As told by the participants themselves, it was 'a beautiful opportunity to develop skills' by realizing something visible and useful for the entire community.<br><br>To deepen themes related to social work and the valorization of skills, visit our skills calculator or discover the job offers in the social sector.

Frequently Asked Questions
How many pine cones were collected for the project of the feast of San Tito in Casorate Sempione?
According to the educators and the children of the CD of Iris Gallarate, about seven thousand pine cones were collected in the woods and in the Bassetti Park of Gallarate to make the decorations of Via Genova.
How long was the project to prepare the decorations for the feast of St. Titus?
The project lasted two years, as explained by the educators of the CD of Iris Gallarate, during which the boys worked two or three times a week to collect, clean, paint and assemble the over 150 decorations destined for Via Genova.
What salvaged materials were used in addition to pine cones for the decorations of Via Genova?
In addition to pine cones, salvaged objects such as fruit boxes, pitchers, pots, baskets, watering cans and old coffee makers were used, transformed into decorative elements for the floral compositions that will embellish Via Genova during the feast of San Tito.
