Precision farming cuts pesticide use in Switzerland (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 5 May 2026

A pilot project reduces pesticide use by 25% thanks to technology. See how agriculture is changing in Ticino and Grisons

Context

In brief

  • Pilot project reduces pesticides by 25%
  • Technology saves up to 80% in some situations
  • Economic and practical obstacles for large-scale application

Key facts

  • What: Pilot project on precision farming
  • When: 2026
  • Where: Three cantons in German-speaking Switzerland
  • Who: Farmers and phytosanitary services
  • Amount: Average reduction of 25% in pesticide use

A pilot project conducted in three cantons of German-speaking Switzerland demonstrates how digitalization can become a valuable ally for the environment. Thanks to precision farming, it has been possible to reduce the use of pesticides by an average of 25%. In some situations, the results have been even more significant. Thomas Käser, a vegetable producer who participated in the project, explains: 'I remove weeds from a lettuce field and apply phytosanitary products to each head, in a targeted manner.' Thanks to a series of cameras and intelligent systems, he is able to distinguish crops from weeds and intervene only where necessary. In this phase of production, Käser has managed to save up to 80% of pesticides.

Benefits and challenges

Alongside the benefits, the study has also highlighted numerous difficulties. Andreas Distel, head of the phytosanitary service of the canton of Aargau, states: 'These are expensive technologies and not all companies have the necessary means.' This is added to the characteristics of Swiss agriculture, made up of small fields and very diversified crops, which often make the purchase of such complex machinery unprofitable. New practical limits also emerge. Käser recounts: 'In our company we cultivate 30 different types of vegetables. After each use we have to wash the machinery before entering the next field.'

Operational details

Impact on Ticino Agriculture

Precision agriculture represents a challenge for the Canton of Ticino as well, where the small size of agricultural businesses and the diversity of crops make it difficult to adopt advanced technologies. However, the environmental benefits and long-term cost reduction could encourage a change. Businesses operating in Ticino could benefit from subsidies or funding for the purchase of machinery, making the adoption of these technologies more accessible.

Comparison with Traditional Agriculture

Traditional agriculture in Ticino is based on established methods that require more intensive use of pesticides and labor. Precision agriculture, on the other hand, allows for the optimization of resources and the reduction of waste. For example, the targeted use of pesticides not only reduces the environmental impact but also production costs. However, the transition requires a significant initial investment and adequate training for workers.

Future Scenarios

If precision agriculture were to spread in Ticino, a significant reduction in the use of pesticides and an improvement in the quality of crops could be seen. Agricultural businesses could become more competitive in the market, thanks to more efficient and sustainable production. However, the transition requires time and resources, and not all businesses will be able to immediately adopt these new technologies.

Key points

Concrete actions for agricultural producers

For agricultural producers in Ticino who wish to adopt precision agriculture, it is essential to follow a series of concrete steps. First, it is necessary to assess the economic feasibility of the investment. This includes estimating the initial costs and potential long-term savings. Subsequently, companies must purchase the necessary machinery, such as cameras and intelligent systems for monitoring crops. Finally, it is important to train staff in the correct use of new technologies.

Monitoring and optimization

Once the new technologies have been adopted, it is fundamental to monitor the results and make any changes to optimize the use of the technologies. This includes collecting data on crops and analyzing the results to identify any areas for improvement. Agricultural companies can also benefit from consultations with industry experts to optimize the use of technologies.

Useful tools

For agricultural producers in Ticino, a series of useful tools are available to adopt precision agriculture. These include software for monitoring crops, sensors for data collection, and intelligent irrigation systems. Additionally, companies can benefit from funding and subsidies for the purchase of advanced machinery.
