Gehri sounds the alarm over Ticino's deficit (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 25 September 2026

Before the '27 Budget, Chamber of Commerce President Gehri calls the cantonal deficit unsustainable and criticizes political inertia.

Context

In brief

  • Gehri speaks out before the '27 Budget
  • The president speaks of an unsustainable deficit
  • The Chamber of Commerce criticizes political inaction

Key facts

  • Who: president of the Chamber of Commerce
  • When: before the '27 Budget
  • What: position statement on cantonal finances
  • Assessment: unsustainable deficit

Gehri takes a position on Ticino's cantonal finances before the '27 Budget. The president of the Chamber of Commerce sums up his concern in a direct appeal to politicians: «Cantonal finances, dear politicians, wake up! Time has already run out».

The central point of the statement is the deficit, described as unsustainable. Alongside the financial dimension, Gehri also criticizes the timing of the political response. In the subheading of the position statement, in fact, the inaction is described as difficult to justify.

The intervention before the Budget

The timing indicated by the source is precise: the intervention comes before the '27 Budget. The text therefore links Gehri's position to the next step indicated for the cantonal finances, without describing measures already approved or decisions already implemented.

The source does not specify which measures were requested, which items are involved or the amount of the deficit. It contains no figures, percentages, rates, operational deadlines or indications of any changes for workers and businesses. The verifiable fact remains the political assessment of the deficit and the inaction.

For the cross-border worker who works in Ticino, the useful reference is therefore the discussion of the finances of the Canton of Ticino. The news does not announce any change to the G permit, withholding tax, tax transfers or double taxation. Nor does it communicate any changes concerning AVS, LPP, LAMal, INPS or border crossings such as Brogeda.

The connection with cross-border work remains the general one linked to the cantonal finances: the '27 Budget is the next point in time mentioned by the source, and the deficit is the issue raised by the president of the Chamber of Commerce. Any effect on salaries, taxes, services or procedures would require information that does not appear in the text.

For this reason, Gehri's appeal should be read as a political position on the cantonal finances, not as the announcement of a new tax measure or a change to the new 2026 tax agreement. The source identifies the problem and the point at which it will be discussed, but does not yet describe an operational decision.

Gehri's statement does not, on its own, change the rules applicable to cross-border workers. The text discusses cantonal finances, the '27 Budget and a deficit defined as unsustainable; however, it does not indicate increases in withholding tax, changes to revenue-sharing payments, new conditions for the G permit or measures concerning AVS, LPP and LAMal.

The practical value of the news is to distinguish a political alarm from a concrete measure. The president of the Chamber of Commerce signals that time may already have run out and considers inaction difficult to justify, but the source does not link these words to a legislative act, an administrative decision or an implementation timetable.

The third consists of the concrete effects, which are not specified at present. This separation prevents the reported urgency from being confused with a decision that has already been made.
