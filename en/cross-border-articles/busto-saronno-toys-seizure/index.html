Seizure of toys and unsafe devices in Lombardy

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 28 September 2026

Over 20 thousand toys and 300 electrical devices seized. Fines of 10 thousand euros and complaints for commercial fraud.

Context

In brief

  • Over 20mila toys seized in Busto Arsizio
  • More than 300 electrical devices without the required safety features
  • About 300 textile products seized in Saronno
  • Three penalties and two reports for fraud

Key facts

  • Locations → Busto Arsizio and Saronno
  • Goods → toys, electrical devices and textile products
  • Value → over 30mila euro
  • Penalties → approximately 10mila euro in total
  • Offense → commercial fraud, article 515 of the criminal code

Over 20mila toys intended for children, more than 300 electrical devices and about 300 textile products were seized during inspections carried out in recent days by the Guardia di Finanza of Varese at various commercial establishments in Busto Arsizio and Saronno. The tally also includes three merchants sanctioned and two reported for commercial fraud.

The inspections and seizures

The Busto Arsizio Group and the Saronno Company operated as part of the provincial inspection effort against the sale of non-compliant and potentially dangerous products. Irregularities emerged in goods displayed for sale in three stores.

In two commercial establishments in Busto Arsizio, run by Chinese nationals, officers seized about 20mila toys intended for children and more than 300 electrical devices. The items were found to lack the necessary safety requirements.

The source reports that some of the products lacked the CE marking, necessary to certify compliance with European standards concerning safety, health and environmental protection. On other items, the mark was present but non-compliant, with the risk of misleading buyers about the goods’ actual safety characteristics.

In Saronno, by contrast, Guardia di Finanza officers inspected a store run by Italian nationals. About 300 textile products lacking the minimum mandatory information about the characteristics of the items were identified and seized there. The total value of the seized goods exceeds 30mila euro.

At the end of the investigations, the three owners were reported to the competent Chamber of Commerce for administrative penalties, assessed at approximately 10mila euro in total. The two merchants from Busto Arsizio were also reported to the Public Prosecutor’s Office for commercial fraud, an offense provided for by article 515 of the criminal code, for selling products of a quality different from that declared.

According to the Guardia di Finanza, the operation protects consumers, combats fraud and aims to prevent the sale of potentially dangerous items, especially those intended for children, while ensuring fair competition.

Operational details

What Changes for the Consumer

The most concrete aspect of the matter lies in the distinction between product safety, mandatory information and commercial fairness. These are three aspects that appear together in inspections, but with consequences described differently.

From Marking to Minimum Information

For toys and electrical devices, the issue identified in Busto Arsizio concerns safety requirements. The absence of the CE marking, or the presence of a non-compliant mark, changes the picture described by the source: in the first case, certification of compliance with European standards is missing; in the second, the mark may create a misleading impression about the product's actual characteristics. The source links the issue to the protection of safety, health and the environment and to the risk of misleading buyers.

For the textiles from Saronno, on the other hand, the irregularity indicated is the absence of the mandatory minimum information about the characteristics of the articles. The text does not automatically equate this situation with that of the toys or electrical appliances: it describes different categories of goods and different violations. This distinction is useful for correctly interpreting the seizure without turning every irregularity into the same offense.

The second aspect concerns the outcome for the owners. All three were reported to the competent Chamber of Commerce for administrative penalties, while the report to the Public Prosecutor's Office concerned the two traders in Busto Arsizio. The distinction is explicit in the source and does not allow the two proceedings to be treated as though they were the same measure.

Article 515 of the criminal code is explained as the provision on fraud in the conduct of trade. The offense is committed when a trader delivers a product that differs, in terms of origin, provenance, quality or quantity, from the one declared or agreed upon. The stated penalty is imprisonment for up to two years or a fine of up to 2,065 euros, unless the act constitutes a more serious offense. The source adds that economic harm to the buyer is not necessary: the provision protects the fairness of commercial exchanges and consumer trust.

Key points

The operational process described by the source

The operational sequence concerns the activities of the Guardia di Finanza and makes it possible to reconstruct the steps reported in the operation. It is not a tax procedure nor a process for work permits: the available elements are those concerning inspections, seizures and reports.

From inspection to report

1. The Busto Arsizio Group and the Saronno Company carry out checks at three commercial establishments, as part of the provincial operation.

2. In the stores in Busto Arsizio, they identify toys and electrical devices lacking the necessary safety requirements. In Saronno, they identify textile products without the minimum mandatory information on the characteristics of the articles.

3. The goods are seized. The total value of the items exceeds 30 thousand euros.

4. At the end of the investigations, all three owners are reported to the competent Chamber of Commerce for the application of administrative penalties, totaling approximately 10 thousand euros.

5. For the two merchants from Busto Arsizio, a report to the Public Prosecutor's Office is added for commercial fraud, an offense provided for by Article 515 of the Criminal Code.

To read the procedure correctly, it is useful to separate the inspection of the goods from the outcome for the owners. The seizure concerns the articles identified as non-compliant or lacking the required characteristics; the administrative report concerns all three owners; the report for commercial fraud concerns the two merchants from Busto Arsizio. This is the only breakdown indicated by the source.

The text also provides two elements that consumers can verify: the presence of the CE marking, which must certify the indicated European standards, and the minimum mandatory information for textile products. No forms, deadlines, refund procedures or offices to which the consumer should turn are described; adding them would mean going beyond the data of the operation.
