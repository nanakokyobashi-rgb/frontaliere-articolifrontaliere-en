The strategy focused on targeted interventions, with a 40% increase in patrols in the most sensitive zones, supported by specific funding allocated through the law of March 15, 2023, which earmarked around 2 million CHF for security measures and surveillance.

The most recent incident occurred between October 10 and 12, when law enforcement intercepted and detained two foreign nationals suspected of carrying out multiple vehicle thefts and attempting to access parking areas between Gaggiolo and Brogeda. The pattern of these crimes shows that perpetrators mainly operate at night, between 11 pm and 4 am, taking advantage of the quiet hours to force open unattended vehicles or those without active alarms.

The two arrested individuals, both of foreign nationality, were found in possession of break-in tools valued at about 3,000 CHF and some electronic devices and stolen items, including smartphones and wallets. Their activities are part of a broader phenomenon involving recycling and illegal drug trafficking, often facilitated by the strategic position of the Switzerland-Italy border.

Local authorities are considering adopting more rigorous operational measures, including installing around 50 surveillance cameras in the most affected areas and increasing police checks during critical hours. The legislation enacted on January 1, 2022, already strengthened penalties for vehicle thefts, with fines ranging from 5,000 to 20,000 CHF and prison sentences up to 3 years, prompting law enforcement to intensify crackdown operations.

To address the issue effectively, law enforcement is implementing an operational checklist that includes: collaboration with Italian authorities, continuous monitoring of high-risk zones, raising awareness among residents and cross-border workers, and immediate information sharing among security agencies. In practical scenarios like Stabio, where 7 thefts occurred in September alone, these measures are proving essential to reduce risks and improve safety perceptions.

Compared to the past, the increased controls and use of advanced technology have contributed to a 15% reduction in car thefts compared to the same period last year, offering greater protection to cross-border workers and local communities.
