Operational details

Withholding tax and new cross-border taxation (2024-2033)

One of the pillars of the cross-border worker's life is understanding how tax collection works. Unlike an Italian employee residing in Italy, the cross-border worker who works in Switzerland is subject to Swiss withholding tax, not to the Italian IRPEF. This legislative choice was designed to avoid double taxation: in fact, Italy does not apply new taxation on income from work in Switzerland thanks to the tax credit in the EC framework of the tax return (form 730).

Withholding tax is withheld by Switzerland during the employment period. The Swiss tax administration at the federal level, through the AFC (Federal Tax Administration), administers the federal rates; the Canton of Ticino also applies its own cantonal rates. For the cross-border worker resident in Cremia who works in Ticino, the Canton of Ticino will apply the withholding tax according to its own bases.

The New Cross-border Workers Agreement, applied from 1 January 2024, introduced a significant change: cross-border workers are divided into two categories. Old cross-border commuters remain taxed only in Switzerland (art. 9 of the 2020 Agreement). The threshold of 10,000 euros is not a general exemption: art. 4 of Law 83/2023 limits it to the income referred to in the art. 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013. This protection means that a portion of your annual income is not subject to double taxation.

As regards Italian IRPEF rates (applied only to income exceeding the exemption), the rates are: 23% up to €28,000, 33% between €28,001 and €50,000, and 43% over €50,000. These amounts refer to your net income after applying the border exemption.

Swiss social security contributions: AVS, LPP, insurance

In addition to the tax, the cross-border worker must pay Swiss social security contributions, which represent a significant burden but provide access to a robust pension system. The AVS (Old Age and Survivors Insurance) provides a contribution rate of 5.3% paid by the employee, while the employer pays an identical amount. Contributions are also due for AI (disability insurance) included in the same 5.3%, AD (unemployment insurance) at 1.1% of the salary with a maximum ceiling of CHF 148,200, and a further 1.1% for AC (contingency insurance). For accident insurance (LAINF), the rate varies between 0.7% and 1.5% depending on the employment sector.

The LPP (Occupational pension fund, the second Swiss pillar) involves a contribution that varies from 7% to 18% of the salary depending on the age group, starting from 25 years. Registration with the AVS must take place within 5 days of starting work: this is a non-negotiable deadline and failure to register within the required time leads to administrative sanctions. The AVS number will be essential to manage all subsequent social security obligations.

As for health insurance (LAMal), the frontaliere has the right to opt: they can choose to maintain Italian coverage or enroll in a Swiss LAMal. Frequently Asked Questions
What taxes does a border worker living in Cremia and working in Ticino pay?
The tax at source is withheld exclusively from Switzerland (Canton Ticino) on income from work. Italy avoids double taxation by means of a tax credit in the EC framework of model 730. The €10,000 limit is not a general allowance: Article 4 of Italian Law No. 83/2023 applies it only to the income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law No. 147/2013. In addition to the tax, you pay AVS contributions 5.3% (employee), AI (included in 5.3%), AD/AC 1.1%, LAINF 0.7-1.5% and LPP 7-18% by age group.
Do I need a G permit to work in Ticino da Cremia?
Yes, the G permit is required by law. It is the visa that authorizes border work for Italian citizens in Switzerland according to the bilateral CH-IT agreements. It must be requested before starting work with the competent authorities, involving the Swiss employer, the Italian local authorities (Municipality of Cremia) and the Ticino cantonal authorities. Without the G permit, employment is not legal.
Which income does the €10,000 limit apply to?
Article 9’s transitional regime applies only to workers who meet the Agreement’s definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between 31 December 2018 and 17 July 2023; covered employment income is taxable exclusively in Switzerland. The ordinary regime applies in other cases, with Italian taxation and credit for Swiss tax where applicable. The €10,000 in Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is only the limit for income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law 147/2013, not a general allowance. Do I need to sign up for Swiss health insurance or can I keep Italian health insurance?
As a border worker, you have the right of option: you can keep Italian coverage or enroll in a Swiss LAMal (health insurance). If you choose Switzerland, adult deductibles range from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 depending on the contract. Contact a Ticino health insurance fund (Assicurazioni Populare, Santésuisse, etc.) for registration, coverage details and quotes.
When do I have to sign up for AVS and what happens if I don't do it on time?
Registration for the AVS must take place within 5 days of the start of work in Switzerland. This is a binding deadline set by federal law. Failure to register on time will result in administrative penalties and problems with the accreditation of pension contributions. The AVS number is essential to manage all social security contributions, the LPP (second pillar) and subsequent obligations with the Italian Revenue Agency.
