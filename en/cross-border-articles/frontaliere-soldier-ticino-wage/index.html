Context

In a nutshell

  • The New Frontier Agreement entered into force on 1 January 2024.
  • Switzerland and Italy signed the Double Taxation Convention on 9 December 1976.
  • Border workers in Ticino are subject to tax at source in Switzerland.

How much does a border welder earn in Ticino?

The border welder in Ticino is a professional who works in Switzerland, but resides in Italy. To understand how much you earn, we need to consider several factors, such as your experience, qualification, and salary.

The remuneration of the border welder According to the Double Taxation Convention between Switzerland and Italy, signed on 9 December 1976, frontier workers in Switzerland are subject to tax at source in Switzerland. The tax at source is 20% for salaries below 120,000 Swiss francs per year and 30% for salaries above 120,000 Swiss francs per year.

Concrete example Suppose a border welder in Ticino earns 80,000 Swiss francs per year. According to the Double Taxation Convention, the source tax in Switzerland would be 20% (80,000 x 0.20 = 16,000 Swiss francs).

Gross and net profit of a border welder in Ticino

Let's consider a concrete example of a border welder who works in Ticino and resides in Italy. Suppose your gross monthly income is CHF 8'000, with a contribution of CHF 1'500 for Swiss insurance. His monthly net income would be CHF 6,500.

Taxation in Switzerland

The border welder in question is subject to taxation in Switzerland, which provides for a 5.3% AVS/AI/IPG tax at source for employees. In addition, the AD/AC contribution is 1.1% for capital up to CHF 148'200. Suppose the border welder has an income of CHF 4'000, which will be taxed at 5.3% for a total of CHF 212. The AD/AC contribution will be 1.1% for a total of CHF 164.

Taxation in Italy

The border welder is also subject to taxation in Italy, which provides for a 23% tax for incomes up to €28,000. Suppose the border welder has an income of €20,000, which will be taxed at 23% for a total of €4,600.

Taxes in Switzerland

  • In Switzerland, taxes are calculated based on the individual's income and nationality.
  • Border workers in Ticino are considered to be resident in Switzerland if they reside in the Canton for at least 183 days per year.
  • Depending on their nationality, frontier workers may be considered to be resident in Switzerland or another country, which may affect their tax position.
