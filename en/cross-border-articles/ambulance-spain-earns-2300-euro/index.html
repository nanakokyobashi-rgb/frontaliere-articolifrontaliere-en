Dream working conditions... for those who drive an ambulance. It happens in Spain, where those who drive an ambulance earn about 2300 euros net with six days of work a year. This was revealed by Diego, driver of Samur, the emergency medical and civil protection service of the City of Madrid, to the podcast '17 grados'.

Ambulance drivers, Diego revealed, are qualified as emergency medical technicians (EMTs), which means that all of them are able to take care of the patient in whatever condition they arrive on board. Their work shifts last 24 hours, which is why the number of working days per month is significantly reduced. Each driver then, after his shift, will have to be 'rested' for five days before being called back into service.

"There are no distinctions, here we are all technicians," Diego remarked, confirming that precisely for this reason, along one's shift, one alternates between those who drive and those who follow the patient. The technicians are all in possession of the C license, i.e. the one that allows you to drive trucks, since the equipment transported on ambulances, in particular on mobile intensive care units, exceeds the weight allowed for a common car.
