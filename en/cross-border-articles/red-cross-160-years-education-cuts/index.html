Swiss Red Cross celebrates 160 years, but cuts persist (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 8 May 2026

Celebration at Lugano's Ciani grove, but concerns remain over cuts to educational staff

Context

In brief

  • Swiss Red Cross celebrates 160 years at Ciani Park in Lugano
  • Reduction from 35 to 13 layoffs planned
  • Decrease in educators for youth centers
  • 30% cuts for youth centers in Lugano and Mendrisio
  • 2023 cantonal regulation imposes a 20% reduction in educational staff

Key facts

  • What: Celebration of the 160th anniversary of the Swiss Red Cross
  • When: May 8, 2024
  • Where: Ciani Park, Lugano
  • Who: Swiss Red Cross, Sottoceneri section
  • Amount: 13 layoffs instead of the initially planned 35
  • Cuts: 30% reduction in educational staff for youth centers in Lugano and Mendrisio
  • Regulation: 2023 cantonal law imposes a 20% reduction in educational staff

Balloons, smiles, and sandwiches distributed among the people to celebrate the 160th anniversary of the Swiss Red Cross and its volunteers. But behind the party organized today at Ciani Park in Lugano remains the issue of cuts and the reduction of educational staff. Tables shaped like crosses, red and white balloons, and many smiles while distributing sandwiches. This is how Ciani Park in Lugano presented itself this morning, decorated to celebrate World Red Cross Day and the 160th anniversary of the association. A day of celebration open to the public, with numerous activities and moments of encounter to tell the work done in the territory.

The shadow of cuts

Alongside the celebrations, however, remains the issue of internal difficulties. Although layoffs have been reduced from the initially planned 35 to 13, the decrease in the number of educators continues to be felt. A situation that remains at the center of the concerns of the Sottoceneri section, even in a symbolic moment like the one celebrated today. 'The number of educators for the youth centers, where adults are found, has decreased,' specifies Debora Banchini Fersini, director of the Sottoceneri section, who adds: 'We are doing the best we can.'

Volunteers take center stage

The stars of the day were above all the volunteers, considered the beating heart of the Red Cross. In the Sottoceneri section, there are over 300 people engaged in the various activities of the association. Through the stands present at Ciani Park, the volunteers illustrated to the visitors the numerous services offered to the population, once again confirming the fundamental role of support and solidarity in the territory.

Impact of cuts

The 30% cuts for the youth centers in Lugano and Mendrisio have a significant impact. For example, the youth center in Lugano has seen a reduction from 15 to 10 educators, while that of Mendrisio has gone from 12 to 8. This reduction has led to greater pressure on the remaining staff, with an increase in working hours and a decrease in the quality of the services offered.

Operational checklist

To address the situation, the Sottoceneri section has implemented an operational checklist:

  • Resource assessment: Analysis of available resources and territorial needs.
  • Staff redistribution: Optimization of human resources to cover the most critical areas.
  • Continuous training: Update courses for the remaining staff to improve efficiency.
  • Collaboration with other associations: Partnerships with other organizations to share resources and skills.

Comparisons between practical scenarios

Before the cuts, the youth center in Lugano could count on 15 educators, with a ratio of 1 educator to every 10 young people. After the cuts, the ratio has increased to 1 educator to every 15 young people, making it more difficult to guarantee adequate support. Similarly, the center in Mendrisio has seen an increase in the workload for the remaining educators, with a direct impact on the quality of the service.

Cantonal regulation

The 2023 cantonal law imposed a 20% reduction in educational staff, but the Sottoceneri section had to face even more severe cuts due to financial difficulties. This situation has led to greater attention to resource management and the search for innovative solutions to maintain the quality of the services offered.

Conclusions

Despite the difficulties, the Swiss Red Cross continues to play a fundamental role in the territory, thanks to the tireless work of the volunteers and the ability to adapt to new challenges. The celebration of the 160th anniversary was an opportunity to reflect on the past and look to the future with determination and hope.

Operational details

Implications for cross-border workers

The reduction of educational staff at the Swiss Red Cross could have a significant impact on the services offered to cross-border workers residing in Ticino. In particular, youth centers, which accommodate adults, may experience a decrease in service quality due to staff reductions. This could affect the Swiss Red Cross's ability to support young cross-border workers in their integration and training journey. For example, at the Lugano center, the number of educators has decreased from 12 to 8, with a 33% increase in workload. In Mendrisio, the situation is similar, with a reduction from 7 to 5 educators, increasing the educator-user ratio from 1:15 to 1:20.

Comparison with the previous situation

Before the cuts, youth centers had a higher number of educators, allowing for more personalized and continuous support. With the reduction in staff, the remaining educators will have to manage a larger number of users, which could lead to a decrease in the quality of service offered. This is a crucial point for cross-border workers, who often face particular challenges related to double taxation and the management of health insurance. For example, the double taxation agreement between Italy and Switzerland, in effect since 2012, provides that cross-border workers pay taxes in Switzerland, but with a tax credit in Italy. However, managing these regulations can be complex without adequate support.

Future scenarios

If the situation worsens, cross-border workers may need to seek alternatives for educational and social support. This could include looking for private services or using other non-profit organizations. However, the Red Cross remains an important point of reference for many, and the hope is that current difficulties can be overcome over time. For example, in Bellinzona, some cross-border workers have already started turning to private centers, with costs up to 500 CHF per month, compared to the 100 CHF required by the Red Cross.

How to act

For cross-border workers using Red Cross services, it is important to stay informed about any changes to the services offered. Contacting the Sottoceneri section directly to obtain updates and detailed information can be a fundamental step in dealing with any changes. Additionally, actively participating in activities organized by the Red Cross can help maintain a strong bond with the community and receive support if needed.

Operational checklist

  • Contact the Sottoceneri section for updates on services.
  • Participate in activities organized by the Red Cross.
  • Explore alternatives such as private centers or other non-profit organizations.
  • Verify regulations on double taxation and health insurance.
  • Maintain open dialogue with educators to receive personalized support.

  • Mendrisio Center: The number of educators has decreased from 7 to 5, with a 33% increase in workload.
  • Bellinzona Center: Some cross-border workers have started turning to private centers, with costs up to 500 CHF per month.

Conclusion

The current situation requires a proactive approach from cross-border workers to ensure they continue to receive the necessary support. Staying informed, exploring alternatives, and maintaining open dialogue with the Red Cross are fundamental steps to addressing future challenges.
