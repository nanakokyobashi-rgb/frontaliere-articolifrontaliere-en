The Canton of Ticino is working on a single collection of data for LAMal, the federal law on health insurance. This novelty, recently announced, aims to simplify procedures for cross-border workers who work in Switzerland and reside in Italy. The new regulation provides for the collection of insurance data in a centralized manner, reducing bureaucracy and improving the efficiency of the system.

What changes for cross-border workers

Currently, cross-border workers must provide their insurance data to multiple entities, which can be complicated and time-consuming. With the single collection, data will be collected once and shared among the various interested parties, such as insurers and cantonal authorities. This will significantly simplify the procedures and reduce the risk of errors or omissions.

Advantages of single collection

The new procedure will offer several advantages to cross-border workers. First, it will reduce the administrative burden, allowing them to focus on work and daily life. Furthermore, the centralization of data will improve the transparency and traceability of information, ensuring greater security and reliability.

Practical Analysis: Implications for Cross-Border Workers

The single data collection of LAMal will have a significant impact on the lives of cross-border workers in Ticino. Let's look at the main practical implications and how this new development might affect your situation.

Greater Administrative Efficiency

One of the main advantages of single data collection is greater administrative efficiency. Currently, cross-border workers have to manage multiple procedures and deadlines, which can be complicated and time-consuming. With the new procedure, data will be collected once and automatically shared with the relevant parties, significantly reducing the bureaucratic burden.

Reduced Risk of Errors

Centralizing data will also reduce the risk of errors or omissions. Currently, it is possible that the data provided to one entity is not correct or up-to-date, which can cause problems with insurances or authorities. With single data collection, data will be verified and updated centrally, ensuring greater accuracy and reliability.

Greater Transparency

Single data collection will also improve the transparency of the system. Cross-border workers will be able to easily access their insurance information and verify that it is correct. This will ensure greater trust in the system and reduce the risk of disputes or misunderstandings.
