The context

Cross-border work is a growing phenomenon in Ticino, with many Italian workers going to work in Switzerland every day and vice versa. This flow of workers has led to a number of challenges for the tax authorities of both countries, who must ensure that taxes are paid properly and that workers are treated fairly.

The regulations

The agreement signed on 23 December 2020 provides for border workers' taxes to be paid on the basis of the principle of “tax residence”. This means that workers who reside in Switzerland for more than 183 days per year will be taxed in Switzerland, while those who reside in Italy for less than 183 days per year will be taxed in Italy.

Operational details

The agreement provides for a transitional regime for old border workers from 2024 to 2033 and a deductible of €10,000 for new border workers.

On 15 February, Italy and Switzerland signed an agreement that improves taxation for border workers working in the Ticino region. This agreement comes into force from 2024 and provides for a transitional regime for old border workers until 2033.

The agreement provides for the creation of a €10,000 deductible for new frontier workers, who will no longer have to pay taxes on their income. In addition, old border crossers will enjoy a deductible of €5,000 for the years 2024-2027 and €3,000 for the years 2028-2033.

For example, a frontier worker who works in Bellinzona and earns €50,000 per year will not have to pay taxes on his income, thanks to the €10,000 deductible. If, on the other hand, he worked in Lugano and earned €60,000 per year, he would not pay taxes on his income for €20,000, thanks to the €10,000 deductible and the fact that his income is less than €50,000.

To obtain the exemption, border workers must submit a tax return by 31 July of each year. In addition, they must provide documents attesting to their residence in Switzerland and their work activity.

Key points

Border workers must be aware of the new rules and proceed with the tax return within the established deadline. Under the agreement between Italy and Switzerland, border workers working in Switzerland and residing in Italy will have to submit their tax return by 31 March 2026.

Who are the border crossers concerned?

Border workers residing in Italy and working in Switzerland, including employees and the self-employed, are affected. These individuals will have to file the tax return for the period 2025, indicating the income received in Switzerland.

New rules for border workers

From 2026, border workers will have to submit a separate tax return compared to the Italian one. This declaration must be submitted by 31 March 2026 and must include the following data:

  • Income received in Switzerland
  • Costs incurred for work in Switzerland
  • Taxes paid in Switzerland

Frequently Asked Questions
When does the new tax agreement for border workers between Italy and Switzerland come into force?
The new agreement for the taxation of border workers, signed on 15 February 2023, will enter into force from 1 January 2024. This agreement aims to improve taxation for cross-border workers in the Ticino region, establishing new tax rules and benefits.
How is the tax residence of border workers determined under the new agreement?
Tax residency is determined based on the '183 days' principle. If a worker resides in Switzerland for more than 183 days per year, they will be taxed in Switzerland. Conversely, if you reside in Italy for less than 183 days per year, you will be taxed in Italy.
What tax benefits are envisaged for new frontier workers under the Italy-Switzerland agreement?
The new frontier workers will benefit from a deductible of €10,000 on their income. This means that they will not have to pay taxes on this portion of their earnings. For example, a frontier worker who earns €50,000per year will not pay taxes on the first €10,000.
Is there a transitional regime for old frontier workers and what allowances are applied?
Yes, there is a transitional regime for old frontier workers from 2024 to 2033. They will enjoy a deductible of €5,000 for the years 2024-2027 and €3,000 for the years 2028-2033, to lighten the tax burden during the transition.
What are the requirements for border workers to obtain tax exemption?
To benefit from the deductible, frontier workers must submit their tax return by 31 July of each year. In addition, it is necessary to provide documents attesting to their residence in Switzerland and their work activity.
