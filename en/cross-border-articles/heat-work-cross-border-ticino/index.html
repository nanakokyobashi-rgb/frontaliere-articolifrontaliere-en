Torrid heat and work: unions meet the Ticino Government for new measures (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 7 August 2026

UNIA, OCST, USS and VPOD unions call for intervention thresholds based on scientific indices to protect workers during heatwaves in Ticino

In a nutshell

  • Government opens to new measures after meeting on the risks of the heatwave
  • Discussion of the Valais model with scientific thresholds for professional sectors

Key facts

  • What: Meeting between trade unions and the Council of State on the conditions of workers during the cannibal waves
  • When: Today (date not specified in source)
  • Where: Ticino
  • Who: Representatives of the UNIA, OCST, USS and VPOD trade unions and delegation of the Council of State (Marina Carobbio Guscetti, Christian Vitta, Raffaele De Rosa)
  • Amount: More than one hundred admissions to the emergency room and dozens of unsustainable situations reported

After reporting the numerous problematic situations observed on construction sites and in kitchen work during the heatwaves, representatives of the UNIA, OCST, USS and VPOD trade unions met today with a delegation from the Ticino State Council to discuss the conditions of workers during prolonged exposures to the torrid heat. Giangiorgio Gargantini, regional secretary of UNIA, explained that they once again highlighted the need to have concrete and effective measures, inspired by the model adopted by the Canton of Valais, where intervention thresholds were determined based on scientific indices, different for different professional realities. The approach proposed by the unions is based on the adoption of differentiated intervention thresholds, as already implemented in the Valais Canton, where scientific indices are used to establish limits beyond which protection obligations are triggered, variable depending on the type of work activity. This method would make it possible to overcome the current uncertainty reported by Roncelli, according to which many workers cannot independently assess whether environmental conditions constitute a real risk to health.   • When: Expected start as early as next year
  • Where: Ticino, with national coordination
  • Who: Council of State, social partners, federal deputation, municipalities
  • Amount: Not yet specified (focus on process, not figures)

To translate the concerns that emerged during the meeting into concrete actions, the Council of State decided to activate several channels of intervention. First of all, the Health and Environment operational group (GOSA) will be formally launched, which will organise a series of structured meetings with the social partners (UNIA, OCST, USS, VPOD) to analyse in detail the sectoral criticalities and develop proposals for technical and organisational measures. At the same time, the Executive intends to bring the issue to the attention of the Ticino deputation to the federal chambers in Bern, recognizing that many of the regulatory levers to intervene on occupational safety during extreme weather events reside at the federal level, especially with regard to employer obligations and protection standards.

Frequently Asked Questions
Which unions attended the meeting with the State Council on heat and labour?
The meeting was attended by representatives of the UNIA, OCST, USS and VPOD trade unions, as stated by UNIA regional secretary Giangiorgio Gargantini and OCST project manager Evaristo Roncelli at the end of the meeting with the Ticino government delegation.
What precisely did the unions ask about the measures against heat?
The unions called for the adoption of concrete and effective measures, inspired by the model of the Canton of Valais, where intervention thresholds were determined based on scientific indices, different for different professional realities, in order to allow workers to recognize when conditions constitute an actual risk to their health.
What is the role of the Municipalities in the proposed measures to deal with extreme heat?
The Council of State will again urge the Municipalities because many municipal regulations today provide for a work start time that is not compatible with the summer needs of workers; anticipating the start of the shift would make it possible to secure those who must be exposed to the sun for a long part of the day, even if it could create early morning noise.
