Transit tax, Brussels turns up its nose

The transit tax will be introduced from 2026 and will affect foreign vehicles crossing Switzerland.

The transit tax will be applied to foreign vehicles passing through Switzerland, regardless of the type of vehicle or the reason for the trip. The tax will be calculated based on the weight of the vehicle and the distance traveled in Switzerland. According to the Swiss Confederation, the transit tax will be CHF 1.50 per tonne of weight for each kilometer travelled. For example, if a vehicle weighs 10 tonnes and crosses Switzerland for 100 kilometres, the transit tax will be CHF 150.

The transit tax will not be applied to Swiss vehicles or vehicles in transit to Switzerland without stopping. Furthermore, vehicles crossing Switzerland to reach the border with Italy will not pay the transit tax, since they do not stop in Switzerland.

The European Commission has expressed its opposition to the transit tax, as it could create obstacles to trade and the free movement of vehicles within the European Union. However, the Swiss Confederation stated that the transit tax is necessary to reduce hospital traffic and improve the quality of life of residents.

The transit tax will be managed by the Swiss Confederation, which has established an online payment system and a network of collection points for the tax. Foreign vehicles will have to pay the transit tax within 14 days of crossing the border.

When will the transit tax for foreign vehicles come into force in Switzerland?
The transit tax will come into force on January 1, 2026. From that date, all non-resident vehicles crossing the Swiss border will have to pay the required amount, regardless of the reason for the trip. The authorities have provided for a three-month transition period to allow travelers to adapt to the new payment procedures.
What is the amount of the transit fee and are there any differences between the rates mentioned in the article?
The amount set by Swiss legislation is 40 Swiss francs for each foreign vehicle crossing the border. The document also includes references to 20 CHF, but this is a proposal for a reduction limited to the vehicles of Ticino residents, which has not yet been approved. Therefore, for most travelers the final fare remains 40 CHF.
What are the possible penalties for those who do not pay the transit tax?
Anyone who crosses Switzerland without paying the transit tax risks an administrative penalty equal to twice the amount due, i.e. CHF 80, plus any interest on late payment. In recurring cases, the authorities can also suspend the vehicle registration document or block access to Swiss motorways until payment is made.
