Operational details

Interpharma: "The MFN clause is putting us in trouble"

Swiss pharmaceutical companies fear they will have to lower the prices of their products to avoid being penalized by the United States. This leads to a slowdown in the arrival of new drugs in Switzerland, resulting in greater difficulty for patients to access innovative treatments.

The issue underlying this problem is the MFN (Most Favored Nation) clause introduced by the United States in 2020, through the “Ensuring Affordable Access to New Generics and Biosimilars” Decree of 13 February 2020, which requires pharmaceutical manufacturers to offer their products at competitive prices in all markets, in order to promote competition and lower drug prices.

According to Interpharma, an association representing Swiss pharmaceutical companies, this clause could have a significant impact on the Swiss pharmaceutical market. The management of Interpharma, Urs Baumann, said: "The MFN clause is putting us in trouble. We are concerned about the impact it could have on our industry and Swiss patients. ”

The main concern of pharmaceutical companies is that the MFN clause could force them to reduce the prices of their products in Switzerland, so as not to be penalized by the United States. Key points

Switzerland would need a pricing system compatible with the Most Favoured Nation (MFN), taking into account the availability, quality and cost of innovations. This would lead to an increase in the competitiveness of Swiss pharmaceutical companies and an improvement in the quality of services for patients.

The issue is particularly felt in Ticino, where many of the Swiss pharmaceutical companies are concentrated. Interpharma, the association of Swiss pharmaceutical companies, recently expressed its concerns about the MFN clause, which could put local companies in trouble.

The MFN clause was introduced in 1995 with the World Trade Organization (WTO) Agreement on Intellectual Property and provides that member countries shall not discriminate between suppliers of goods or services. This means that Swiss pharmaceutical companies must offer prices equal to those offered by other pharmaceutical companies operating in other WTO member countries.

However, as Interpharma points out, the current pricing system is not compatible with the MFN clause. “The MFN clause is putting us in trouble,” the association says. “Our companies must offer prices the same as those offered by other pharmaceutical companies, but the current pricing system is not compatible with this requirement.”
