A network of scammers specialising in so-called shock calls has been dismantled in Canton Ticino: three people have been arrested.

Key facts

Place: Canton of Ticino Result: 3 arrests Type: Shock Call Scam

The authorities of the Canton of Ticino have made three arrests related to a series of telephone scams based on the 'shock calls' method. This system of deception aims to psychologically destabilize the victim through sudden and alarming communications, prompting them to pay sums of money to solve alleged urgent problems or family emergencies.

The operation of the network

The investigation made it possible to identify those responsible who operated on the Ticino territory, using manipulation techniques to target vulnerable people. The detainees were arrested after a monitoring activity that revealed the structure of the gang and the way in which the victims were selected. The operation ended with the intervention of law enforcement who secured the suspects, disrupting the criminal activity that was affecting several residents and patrons of the region.

Operational details

Implications for those who live and work at the border

Telephone scams represent a real risk for anyone who transits daily between Italy and Switzerland. Border crossers, who often operate checking accounts in both currencies and use international phone numbers, can become attractive targets for these criminal networks. The cross-border nature of many digital services makes it easier for fraudsters to disguise their identity or simulate calls from official institutions in the Canton of Ticino.

Mobility risks

Those who pass through crossings such as Brogeda or Chiasso and interact with different administrations may be more inclined to believe in calls that simulate urgent bureaucratic or fiscal problems. The use of technical terms related to work in Switzerland, such as source tax or G permit, could be used by criminals to give credibility to their story. In these scenarios, the psychological pressure of the 'shock call' serves to prevent the victim from reflecting or consulting official channels, leading to rapid and irreversible money transfers.

How to protect yourself from phone scams

To avoid falling into similar traps, it is essential to adopt some immediate verification procedures. If you receive an alarming call requesting money or sensitive data, the first action should be to stop communication. You should never provide bank details or passwords by telephone, regardless of the urgency declared by the interlocutor.

Verification steps

1. Close the call immediately if the request is suspicious. 2. Contact the aforementioned institution directly using the official numbers available on Swiss government websites. 3. Report the incident to the competent authorities of the Canton of Ticino. 4. Inform your banking institutions if sensitive data has been shared.
