Operational details

Unemployment among frontier workers is a complex and delicate issue. Switzerland must approach this issue with sensitivity and creativity, avoiding paying unemployment to foreign workers. The motion submitted to the Federal Council is an important step in this direction.

Frontier workers: no unemployment for Switzerland

Switzerland, especially the Ticino region, is an important hub for foreign workers, especially those from countries such as Italy, Romania and Albania. According to 2022 data, there are over 150,000 border crossers registered in Ticino, most of whom work in the construction, agriculture and service sector. However, unemployment among these workers is a growing problem.

Switzerland cannot allow frontier workers to remain unemployed. This not only represents an economic loss for the region, but can also lead to social and legal tensions. Unemployment can lead to increased pressure on safety and health services, as well as increase the risk of labour exploitation.

To address this challenge, the Federal Council has tabled a motion that aims to create alternatives to help border workers find work. The motion provides for the adoption of vocational training and job mobility support programmes. To help border workers, the Federal Council could consider alternatives such as vocational education or assistance to people in difficulty. It is important to work together to find effective solutions.

Switzerland has always played a key role in the context of cross-border work, with around 200,000 border workers working between the two borders every day. 📊

A concrete example is the municipality of Lugano, where the number of border workers has increased by 15% in the last five years. 📊

The Federal Council has already implemented some measures to support border workers. For example, since 2020, a regulation has been in force that allows border workers to work up to 10 hours a day, with a maximum of 40 hours per week. 📊

To further improve the situation, the Federal Council could consider vocational education. For example, in Bellinzona, a pilot project saw a 20% increase in border workers completing vocational training. 📊

Another solution could be assistance to people in difficulty. In Lugano, the Municipality has created an assistance service that offers legal and financial advice to border crossers. 📊
