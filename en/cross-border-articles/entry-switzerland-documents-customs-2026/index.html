Operational details

Implications for cross‑border workers

Entering Switzerland as a cross‑border worker requires careful preparation, especially for those working in the canton of Ticino. Cross‑border workers must ensure they have all necessary documents and understand customs rules to avoid problems. For example, for people living in Chiasso or Como and working in Lugano, it is essential to know that the value limit for declaring goods is 200 CHF.

Entry procedure

The entry procedure into Switzerland is relatively straightforward, but being prepared is important. Cross‑border workers must:

1. Hold a valid work permit, such as a G permit, which can be obtained from the Federal Office for Migration (SEM). 2. Have a valid identity document, such as an ID card or passport. 3. Declare goods that exceed a certain value, such as jewellery or items worth more than 200 CHF.

Operational checklist

Here is an operational checklist for cross‑border workers:

  • Valid work permit
  • Valid identity document
  • Declaration of goods over 200 CHF
  • Valid health insurance in Switzerland

Consequences of irregular entry

Irregular entry into Switzerland can have serious consequences, such as fines and expulsion. For instance, under Swiss law, irregular entry can be punished with a fine of up to 10,000 CHF. It is important to respect customs and entry rules to avoid problems.

"Switzerland has a very efficient border control system, so it is important to be prepared and follow the rules to avoid issues." - Federal Office for Migration (SEM)

Concrete example

A concrete example is a cross‑border worker who lives in Varese and works in Mendrisio. If this worker has a valid work permit and correctly declares his goods, he will not encounter problems entering Switzerland. However, if he does not respect customs rules and exceeds the 200 CHF limit without declaring it, he could face fines and penalties.

What to do concretely

To ensure that they have all the necessary documents, cross-border commuters can:

1. Check your work permit, e.g. cross-border commuter permit G, which must be valid for at least 3 months after the expiry date of your employment contract. 2. Check the validity of your identity document, such as an identity card or passport, which must be valid for at least 6 months after the intended date of entry into Switzerland. 3. Find out about Swiss customs regulations, such as duty-free personal property regulations and food restrictions.
