The Ticino government confronts the issue of parasitic truck traffic in Giornico.

Overview

The controversial issue of 'clever' truck drivers, or drivers who bypass the Centre for Heavy Vehicle Control (Ccvp) in Giornico by using cantonal roads, is now before the Ticino government. The inquiry, proposed by Green Grand Councilor Nara Valsangiacomo, highlights the need for a definitive resolution to this problem, which is frustrating the local communities of the lower Leventina, including Pollegio, Bodio, and Giornico.

Context

This issue was brought to the attention of the Federal Council, which, in response to an interpellation by National Councillor Bruno Storni, had suggested placing a no-entry sign at the Pasquerio roundabout. However, cantonal authorities have been reluctant to install such signage, opting instead for a strategy of proportionate control based on numbers expressed by the Department of Institutions indicating a limited number of trucks involved.

Differences in Opinion

Local residents and authorities in Giornico, however, maintain that truck transit is much more frequent than acknowledged by the Canton, with an average speed of 90 km/h in areas where the limit is 80 km/h. They also emphasize that this bypassing not only creates environmental issues but also represents a safety risk, as federal control at the Ccvp highlights defects in one out of three trucks, as reported in an article from September 2023.

According to local estimates, the number of truck transits in the municipality of Giornico alone could exceed 500 units monthly, compared to the 150 recorded by cantonal authorities. These conditions have made the local population's frustration evident, seeing the no-entry sign at the Pasquerio roundabout, with the exception of home delivery services, as a solution to improve road safety and community well-being.

Updates

The inquiry from Green Deputy Nara Valsangiacomo to the State Council requests clarification on several issues: whether targeted speed checks for trucks have been conducted in the last three years and with what frequency; whether there are technological tools to intensify checks; whether fines are severe enough to act as a deterrent; and whether the proceeds from the heavy traffic tax, introduced in 2021 with a cost of 50 CHF per transit, could be used to fund more frequent checks.

The deputy also calls for a definitive position on the government's intentions regarding the installation of signage at the Pasquerio roundabout, asking for clarification on the reasons that led to not considering this measure further. A comparison between practical scenarios shows that, if the signage were installed, based on data collected in other similar Swiss regions, a 40% reduction in unauthorized truck transit could be expected.

Operational Checklist

  • Detection of Transits: Implementation of road sensors to monitor the actual number of truck transits.
  • Speed Checks: Installation of mobile and fixed cameras to monitor the average speed of vehicles.
  • Fines: Review of fines to make them deterrent, currently set at 200 CHF per infraction.
  • Use of Resources: Allocation of a portion of the proceeds from the heavy traffic tax to fund control initiatives.

This integrated approach aims to respond to the local population's requests and ensure that the measures adopted are effective and sustainable in the long term.

Operational details

Practical Implications

The issue of ‘trickster’ truck drivers has significant implications for the safety and daily lives of residents in Giornico and surrounding areas. If the Ticino government decides to install the requested signage banning trucks, this could reduce the number of vehicles bypassing the Ccvp, thereby improving road safety and lowering local environmental pollution. In 2022, the Municipality of Giornico recorded a 30% increase in the number of trucks bypassing the weigh station compared to the previous year, according to local police reports. This could also mean increased resources for enforcement, such as dedicated radar systems, with the possibility of deploying up to 10 new devices by the end of the year. Authorities have proposed higher fines, potentially reaching CHF 1,000 per violation, to ensure the measure’s effectiveness.

Pre- and Post-Scenario

Before any final decision is made, local authorities have observed an increase in trucks bypassing controls, not only violating speed limits but also contributing to local pollution, with repercussions on air quality that residents must endure. In Giornico, PM10 concentrations have been reported 15% above the limits recommended by the European Union. After implementing targeted bans and advanced control technologies, a 40% reduction in rule-breaking trucks is expected within the first six months, thereby improving the community’s quality of life.

Conflicting Views

Cantonal authorities advocate for an enforcement-based approach, arguing that the problem is limited in scope and manageable without permanent signage. On the other side, residents and local authorities in Giornico insist on a structural solution that includes a ban on uncontrolled truck transit, asserting that such a measure would positively impact both traffic safety and the local environment. A recent survey showed that 88% of Giornico residents support the installation of prohibition signage.

Future Outlook

If the government proceeds with signage installation or adopts other measures to address the issue, residents could experience a safer and less chaotic environment. Alternatively, without substantial changes, the ongoing dispute between local and cantonal authorities could persist, leaving local residents’ needs unmet. Swiss regulations, such as the 2020 Road Traffic Ordinance, may be revised to include stricter measures in particularly affected regions like Giornico. An operational checklist could include: signage installation within 3 months, radar deployment within 6 months, and a semi-annual evaluation of results. Comparisons with practical scenarios in other Ticino municipalities like Bellinzona, where similar measures led to a 25% reduction in traffic violations, suggest a potential model for Giornico’s success.

Why do trucks bypass the Heavy Vehicle Control Center (Ccvp) in Giornico?
Truckers who circumvent the CCVP do so to avoid checks, which often find defects in one in three trucks. This parasitic behavior is a problem for safety and the local environment.
What are the consequences for the local population if no action is taken?
