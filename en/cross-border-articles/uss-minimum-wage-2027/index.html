Switzerland, request for a minimum salary of 5,000 francs (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 10 September 2026

The Swiss Trade Union (USS) is proposing an increase of 2.5% and a minimum wage of 5,000 francs for graduates by 2027.

Context

In brief

  • USS proposes a 2.5% increase for 2027.
  • Request for a minimum wage of 5,000 francs for graduates.
  • Focus on sectors such as healthcare and transport.

Key facts

  • What: Proposal for a generalized wage increase.
  • When: Target year 2027.
  • Where: Switzerland.
  • Who: Swiss Trade Union Federation (USS).
  • Amount: 2.5% increase requested; 5,000 francs for graduates.

The Swiss Trade Union Federation (USS) has released an official note proposing a plan for 2027 that aims for a net improvement in workers' economic conditions. The main request concerns a generalized wage increase of 2.5%, motivated by the weak wage evolution recorded over the last decade and an economic situation that appears more favorable today than in the past.

The need for a 2.5% increase

According to the USS vision, higher wages are not just a matter of well-being, but represent an indispensable tool to defend workers' purchasing power. The proposal aims to correct the lag in real wages compared to productivity growth. If productivity were fully reflected in average remuneration, the monthly salary would be 500 francs higher.

For workers operating in specific sectors, the situation appears even more critical. The USS points out that in sectors such as healthcare, the hotel and catering industry, and transport, wage increases have not been sufficient to compensate for the rising cost of living. This disparity requires a structural intervention that can guarantee more solid economic stability for those working in these sectors, which are essential to the Swiss system.

Key points

How to monitor wage trends

Although the USS proposal is geared towards 2027, the debate over the protection of purchasing power and adjustment to productivity is current. Workers and frontier workers operating in the Swiss labour market need to be aware of the dynamics that affect their payrolls. Monitoring productivity growth compared to average wages is a fundamental exercise to understand one's position in the labour market.

# Remuneration Analysis Steps

For workers who wish to deepen their economic situation, it is useful to follow these steps:

  • Check your position with respect to the average salaries in the sector.
  • Check whether the wage increases received are in line with inflation and the cost of living.
  • Monitor the evolution of trade union proposals for any regulatory or contractual developments.
  • Use digital tools to calculate the difference between gross and gross net salary, considering mandatory deductions such as AVS, LPP and tax at source.

Although the implementation of this proposal by 2027 is not yet guaranteed, the awareness of working workers is the first step to achieve an improvement in economic conditions. Staying up to date on the dynamics of the Swiss labour market and on the copper of potential reforms can make the difference in planning your economic stability.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the USS proposal for 2027?
The Swiss Trade Union (USS) proposes a generalised wage increase of 2.5% for the year 2027, with the aim of improving economic conditions and defending workers' purchasing power.
Why is a minimum wage of CHF 5,000 required?
The request for a minimum salary of 5,000 francs per month is addressed to workers in possession of a diploma. Currently, only two-thirds of graduates and 60% of women reach this threshold.
Which sectors are most affected by wage non-adjustment?
The USS indicates that in sectors such as healthcare, hospitality, catering, and transportation, wage increases have not been sufficient to offset the rising cost of living.
