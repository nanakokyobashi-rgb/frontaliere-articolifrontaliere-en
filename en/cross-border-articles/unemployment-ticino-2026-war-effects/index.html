Ticino unemployment: SECO, too early to see war effects

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 7 May 2026

The Swiss State Secretariat for Economic Affairs states that it's too early to assess the impact of the war in Iran on the Ticino job market

Context

In brief

  • Iran war has not yet impacted Ticino unemployment
  • Swiss companies resilient to structural changes
  • Tourism could be affected by fuel price increases
  • Ticino unemployment rate in March 2026: 3.1% vs 2.9% national average

Key facts

  • What: Assessment of the impact of the war in Iran on the labor market
  • When: 7 May 2026
  • Where: Switzerland, with a focus on Ticino (Lugano, Bellinzona, Locarno)
  • Who: Jérôme Cosandey, head of the labor department at SECO
  • Amount: Unemployment rate slightly higher than the 2025 average (2.8%)

The State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO) has stated that it is still too early to assess the impact of the war in Iran on the Ticino labor market. Jérôme Cosandey, head of the labor department at SECO, emphasized that economic slowdowns affect the labor market with a delay of several months. Therefore, immediate reactions were not predictable.

Impact on tourism

Tourism, which represents 10.3% of Ticino's GDP, could be directly affected by the increase in fuel prices. According to data from the Federal Statistical Office, in 2025 Ticino welcomed 2.8 million tourists, an increase of 4.2% compared to the previous year. However, as in the previous case, it is too early to identify possible repercussions on the labor market. Tourist flows could be diverted, but the effects are not yet quantifiable.

  • Monitor energy costs and evaluate possible savings
  • Analyze tourist demand for the next 6-12 months
  • Plan marketing strategies to attract tourists despite the price increase

Resilience of Swiss companies

Cosandey highlighted that Swiss companies have generally proven to be resilient. For example, in the Ticino manufacturing sector, which employs around 25,000 people, layoffs have been contained, with an unemployment rate of 2.7% in March 2026. German automotive companies, on the other hand, recorded a 15% increase in layoffs in the first quarter of 2026. The Swiss unemployment rate remains slightly higher than last year's average, but compared to the coronavirus pandemic and the financial crisis, the job market is currently more solid.

Short-time work and disruptions

Regarding short-time work, the picture is mixed. While preliminary requests decreased in March and April, dropping from 12,000 to 9,500, over the last three years, their level remains high. Moreover, on 6 January, SECO introduced the Asal 2.0 payment system for the funds, addressing significant disruptions with numerous people registered as unemployed who received their benefits late. Cosandey assured that payments are now up to date and that since the beginning of April, there have been notable improvements in terms of speed. The funds are continuously hiring new staff to manage the situation.

⚠️ Comparison of practical scenarios:

  • Optimistic scenario: If the war in Iran is resolved within six months, oil prices could stabilize, reducing the impact on tourism and the labor market.
  • Pessimistic scenario: If the conflict persists, fuel prices could rise further, with a negative impact on the tourism sector and other industries dependent on transportation.

Practical Analysis: Implications for Cross-Border Workers

Cross-border workers employed in Ticino may be affected by potential repercussions of the war in Iran on the labor market. However, as highlighted by the SECO, it is still too early to assess these effects. It is important to monitor the situation and stay updated on any regulatory or economic changes that could influence their employment.

Comparison with the Previous Situation

Before the war in Iran, the Ticino labor market was relatively stable, with an unemployment rate of 2.9% in 2022, lower than the 3.4% in 2021. The coronavirus pandemic and the financial crisis had severely impacted the sector, but the situation had gradually stabilized. For example, in the municipality of Lugano, the unemployment rate had dropped to 1.8% in 2022, while in Bellinzona it was 2.5%. Now, with the introduction of the new Asal 2.0 payment system, there have been some disruptions, but the SECO has assured that the situation is under control.

Future Scenarios

If the war in Iran were to have a significant impact on the labor market, cross-border workers might face new challenges. It is crucial that they stay informed and prepared for any changes. The resilience of Swiss companies, however, offers a certain margin of security, reducing the risk of mass layoffs. For example, Ticino companies have shown a good ability to adapt during the pandemic, with a GDP decline of only 1.7% in 2020, compared to 6.3% in the European average.

Key points

Concrete actions for cross-border workers

For cross-border workers employed in Ticino, it is crucial to adopt practical measures to address potential changes in the labor market, especially in the current unstable geopolitical context.

Monitor the news

Keeping up-to-date with the latest news regarding the war in Iran and its economic impact is fundamental. Use reliable and official sources, such as the SECO website or the Ticino Department of Economic Affairs, to obtain accurate information. For example, monitoring the performance of the Swiss franc against the euro can be useful for those with salaries denominated in foreign currency.

Consult a tax expert

If changes in your employment status are anticipated, it is essential to consult a tax expert to understand the tax implications. This includes managing withholding tax, which in Ticino ranges from 10% to 30% depending on income, AVS contributions (8.4% of salary) and LPP (professional pensions), and any tax refunds. For example, a cross-border worker residing in Chiasso with an annual salary of 80,000 CHF might pay approximately 16,800 CHF in withholding tax.

Prepare for potential changes

Being ready for potential changes in the labor market can make a difference. This includes creating an emergency fund, diversifying skills, and seeking new job opportunities. For example, an emergency fund of 3-6 months of living expenses can help cover needs in case of unemployment.

Use useful tools

Use tools such as the salary calculator to estimate your net income and plan your finances. Additionally, consult guides and online resources to stay informed about the latest regulatory and tax developments. For example, the Ticino Cantonal Compensation Fund website offers useful information on social contributions.

Operational checklist

  • Update your CV and professional skills.

Compare practical scenarios

Considering practical scenarios can help you prepare better. For example, a cross-border worker employed in Lugano with an annual salary of 100,000 CHF might see a significant impact if the labor market changes. In this case, having a savings plan and an emergency fund could make a difference.

Contact the competent authorities

In case of doubts or need for assistance, contact the competent authorities, such as SECO or the compensation funds, to obtain information and support. For example, the Ticino Cantonal Compensation Fund offers tax and pension consulting services.

Conclusion

While it is still too early to assess the impact of the war in Iran on the Ticino labor market, cross-border workers can adopt concrete measures to prepare for potential changes. Staying informed, consulting experts, and using useful tools are fundamental steps to face future challenges with serenity.
