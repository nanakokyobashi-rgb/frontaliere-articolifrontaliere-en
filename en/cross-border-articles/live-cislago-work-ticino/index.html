Cross-border workers from Cislago to Ticino: taxation, the New Agreement, withholding tax, work permits, and social security for those who choose to live in Italy and work in Switzerland.

The New Cross-Border Workers Agreement: Changes Starting in 2024

The decision to live in Cislago, a municipality in the province of Como in Lombardy, and work in Ticino is an increasingly informed choice for cross-border workers. Those who choose this path benefit from the New Cross-Border Workers Agreement, signed on December 23, 2020, and effective as of January 1, 2024. This agreement introduced a system of tax allowances that significantly alters the tax burden for those residing in Italy.

For a new cross-border worker (hired after July 17, 2023), the annual tax exemption is €10,000. For those who were already cross-border workers before that date, a transitional regime applies with a €7,500 exemption until 2033. This exemption directly reduces your taxable income in Italy: the income you report to the Italian Revenue Agency is already net of this amount.

The key point is that the withholding tax on your employment income is withheld exclusively by Switzerland, not by Italy. There is no double taxation in the traditional sense. The rules are governed by the Double Taxation Treaty signed on March 9, 1976: Italy recognizes the tax credit through Section CE of the tax return (Form 730 or Individuals).

When you fill out your Form 730, you will report the taxes paid in Switzerland and your gross foreign income in Section CE. This foreign tax credit is subtracted from the Italian IRPEF calculated according to progressive tax rates: 23% up to €28,000, 33% from €28,001 to €50,000, and 43% above €50,000. In your specific case, you apply these tax brackets to the difference between your gross income and the tax-free allowance (€10,000).

Permit G: The Essential Document for Cross-Border Workers

To live legally in Cislago and work in Switzerland, you must obtain the Permesso G (cross-border worker permit). You can apply for it at the Como Police Headquarters by submitting your signed Swiss employment contract, a valid ID, and a certificate of residence issued by the Municipality of Cislago.

The G permit is valid for multiple years and authorizes your permanent residence in Italy while you work in Switzerland, allowing you to cross the border regularly at the border crossings (Chiasso, Brogeda, Gaggiolo). It is your proof of legal status: without it, you risk violations and losing the right to work across the border.

Processing times typically range from 30 to 60 days from the date of application. Prepare a complete application package: employment contract, identification document, certificate of residence, and completed forms. The Como Police Headquarters will provide you with a detailed checklist. Important: Renew your permit 30 days before the expiration date indicated on the document itself.

Social Security Contributions and Benefits: AVS, LPP, LAMal

As a cross-border worker from Cislago to Ticino, you are subject to Swiss social security contributions. You cannot opt into the Italian INPS system for your Swiss employment income; contributions must go to the Swiss system.

AVS (Old Age, Survivors’, and Disability Insurance): Your contribution rate is 5.3% of gross income. This is the basic Swiss pension system. You accrue rights to a retirement pension starting at age 65 (men) or gradually from age 64 (women, during the transition period). Each year of recorded contributions increases your future pension.

LPP (Occupational Pension Fund, Second Pillar): The contribution rate ranges from 7% to 18% gross, depending on your age group. It is mandatory from your first day of work. Contributions are deposited into an individual account with your employer’s pension fund and generate a supplementary pension.

LAMal (Health Insurance): As a Category G cross-border worker, you have the right to choose. You can obtain insurance from a Swiss insurer or remain in the Italian INPS system. You must notify your choice within 90 days of your first day of work. Many cross-border workers opt for Swiss insurance because it guarantees full coverage in Switzerland and access to healthcare networks. Deductibles for adults range from CHF 300 (high deductible, lower premiums) to CHF 2,500 (low deductible, higher premiums).

Essential documents: AHV membership number (provided by your pension fund), LPP statements from your employer, and a signed KVG option declaration submitted within 90 days.

Operational details

Tax Basics: How Your Paycheck and Annual Take-Home Pay Change

Your Swiss paycheck includes two key components that determine your take-home pay: cantonal withholding tax and social security contributions. The withholding tax in the Canton of Ticino is not provisional: it is the final tax that your employer withholds directly from your salary. You will owe nothing further to the Swiss tax authorities on your earned income.

The withholding tax rate varies depending on your income, marital status, and personal circumstances. In the Canton of Ticino, for a medium-to-high income, the effective rate is around 19–22% of gross income. At the same time, you pay social security contributions (AVS 5.3%, LPP 7–18%, LAINF approx. 1%). Your take-home pay is your gross pay minus withholding tax and contributions.

In Italy, when you report your foreign income, you apply the €10,000 tax-free allowance (or €7,500 if you are a long-term cross-border worker). This means that your Italian taxable income is your Swiss gross income minus the tax-free allowance. On this basis, you calculate IRPEF: 23% up to €28,000, 33% from €28,001 to €50,000, 43% above €50,000. But immediately afterward, you deduct the foreign tax credit (the taxes paid in Switzerland). In the vast majority of cases, the Swiss tax credit exceeds the calculated Italian IRPEF, resulting in a ristorno (refund).

Tax Refunds: Recover Overpaid Taxes

A tangible benefit for cross-border workers is the annual tax refund. This stems from the fact that the effective Swiss tax rate (withholding tax + social security contributions, which are partially deductible) is generally lower than the average Italian IRPEF rate. The difference between what you pay in Switzerland and what would be due in Italy (after applying the tax-free allowance and tax credits) constitutes your refundable tax credit.

Procedure for obtaining the tax refund: 1. Gather all your annual Swiss pay stubs and the withholding tax certificate issued by your employer or the tax authority of the Canton of Ticino. 2. Fill out Form 730 (for Individuals) with your Italian tax information, indicating in Section CE your gross foreign income, the foreign tax paid, and the tax exemption applied. 3. Submit the tax return via the Italian Revenue Agency’s online service by April 30 of the following year. 4. The refund will be credited to your Italian IBAN within 4–6 months, depending on processing times.

Typical refund amounts range from €1,000 to €4,000+ per year, depending on your gross Swiss income and personal deductions (dependent family members, mortgages, medical expenses). Always keep your Swiss documentation for at least 5 years in case of an audit.

Comparison: Is It Better to Stay in Cislago or Move to Ticino?

Many cross-border workers ask themselves this question: Is it better to maintain residency in Cislago or to physically move to Ticino? The answer depends on personal, family, and work-related factors.

Living in Cislago:

  • Significantly lower living costs (rent, home purchases, utilities)
  • Maintaining Italian family, social, and cultural ties
  • Managing two tax systems (Italy + Switzerland), but with frequent tax refunds
  • Daily or weekly commuting to border crossings (travel times vary depending on traffic, season, and work location)
  • Multi-year G permit; administrative renewal every 2–5 years
  • Established community of cross-border workers; specialized professionals (accountants, lawyers)

Moving to Ticino:

  • Residence in Switzerland: requires a C permit (permanent resident), which is more difficult to obtain
  • No commuting; more time and less stress related to transportation
  • A single tax system (Switzerland); Swiss tax return only
  • Much higher housing costs (rent, real estate, municipal taxes)
  • Full access to the Swiss healthcare system, schools, and public services
  • Social integration within the Swiss-Italian community

The decision isn’t purely financial. If your employer offers long-term contracts (3–5+ years) and stability, commuting from Cislago becomes less of a burden. If, on the other hand, you face contractual instability, moving to Ticino (if possible) offers you greater administrative and tax control. Also consider the impact on your family (spouse, children): moving means new schools, new systems, and a new social environment.

Step-by-Step Guide: Your First Year as a Cislago–Ticino Cross-Border Worker

If you’ve just signed an employment contract in Ticino and live in Cislago, follow this roadmap.

Weeks 1–2: Signing the Contract and Initial Notifications 1. Sign the contract with your Swiss employer. Obtain essential information: your AVS membership number from the relevant Ticino fund, the name and contact information of the LPP fund, and instructions for choosing your LAMal coverage (Swiss or Italian). 2. Gather your documents: Italian tax ID number, valid ID, certificate of residence (to be requested from the Municipality of Cislago). 3. Plan your initial budget: Your first paycheck usually arrives 15–30 days after you start; calculate your cash needs until that date.

Weeks 2–3: Applying for a G Permit 1. Go to the Como Police Headquarters with your signed employment contract, ID, and certificate of residence. Submit your application for a G permit (cross-border worker permit). 2. Notify the Municipality of Cislago of the start of your new job (this is an administrative requirement sometimes needed to update your personal records). 3. Allow 30–60 days for processing. Once issued, the G permit is valid for multiple years (duration varies). Keep it in a safe place; without it, you cannot legally cross the border.

Week 3–4: AVS Registration and Opening a Swiss Bank Account 1. Your employer will notify the relevant AVS office in Ticino of your employment. You will receive a temporary AVS number by mail within 1–2 weeks. 2. Open a Swiss bank account if your employer requires it. Many Swiss citizens and employers in Ticino require a Swiss IBAN (CH) for direct deposit of your salary. Contact Swiss banks (UBS, CS, cantonal banks) or financial institutions that serve cross-border workers. 3. Once you receive your permanent AVS number (within 3–4 weeks), provide it to your employer so they can adjust your payroll deductions.

Weeks 4–6: Choosing Health Insurance and Tax Filing 1. Receive health insurance quotes from multiple Swiss providers (if you choose the Swiss option). Compare premiums, deductibles, and coverage. Many cross-border workers choose insurers that operate across borders for convenience. 2. Within 90 days of your first day of work, notify your employer and the insurer of your LAMal choice. If you opt for Italy, notify the INPS. 3. Notify your tax advisor or the Italian Revenue Agency (if applicable) of the start of your employment abroad. You may need to update your Italian tax status.

Months 2–3: Monitoring Pay Stubs and Payments 1. Receive your first pay stub. Verify the gross amount, the cantonal withholding tax, the AVS/LPP contributions, and the IBAN for the deposit. If you notice any errors, report them to your employer immediately. 2. Keep ALL pay stubs in either paper or digital format. You’ll need them for your tax return. 3. By December 31 of the year, your employer must provide you with the annual withholding tax certificate (confirming your payments).

November–December: Preparing Your Tax Return 1. Gather: all pay stubs, the annual withholding tax certificate, documentation for any deductions (dependent family members, mortgages, union dues, etc.), and your Swiss and Italian IBANs. 2. If this is your first year as a cross-border worker, consider hiring an accountant who specializes in cross-border workers. The initial cost (€300–600) is recoverable through the annual tax refund in most cases. 3. Prepare the supporting documentation: pay stubs, tax certificates, LAMal option declaration, and, if applicable, LPP certificates.

Late January–March: Filing the tax return and receiving the tax refund 1. Fill out Form 730 (Individuals) or the Redditi P.F. form, depending on your situation. In Section CE, indicate your gross foreign income, the tax paid in Switzerland, and the tax-free allowance (€10,000 or €7,500). 2. File your tax return electronically by April 30 of the following year via the Italian Revenue Agency’s Entratel website. 3. Within 4–6 months of filing, you will receive the outcome: a tax refund or a notice of tax due (rare if everything is correct). The refund will be credited to your Italian IBAN.

Annual Process: Management Cycle 1. Every December, ask your employer for the new withholding tax certificate for the current year. 2. In February/March, update and file your new tax return with the latest annual data. 3. Periodically review your AVS statement (you’ll receive updates every 1–2 years). Verify that your payments are recorded correctly. 4. Renew your G permit 30 days before the expiration date indicated on the document.

Clear Advantages of Choosing Cislago–Ticino

In summary, living in Cislago and working in Ticino offers a practical and beneficial balance:

  • Swiss income: Among the highest in Europe. Even for entry-level positions, Swiss gross pay significantly exceeds comparable Italian salaries.
  • Italian housing costs: Rents, home purchases, utilities, and services are much lower than in Ticino. This difference allows for significant savings.
  • Annual tax refund: In the vast majority of cases, you’ll recover overpaid taxes. This isn’t speculation—it’s the standard mechanism of cross-border taxation.
  • Established Italian community: Cislago has a strong network of cross-border workers. You can easily find professionals (accountants, lawyers, insurance agents) who specialize in these matters.
  • Administrative flexibility: The G permit allows you to maintain your Italian residence without any requirement to physically reside in Switzerland. You can commute or live permanently in Cislago.
  • Bilateral access: You benefit from Italy-Switzerland agreements and services (healthcare, social security, mobility).

The key to success is planning from the very first month: don’t delay registering for AVS, choosing your LAMal plan, or applying for the G permit. Once the initial bureaucratic procedures are sorted out, managing them becomes an annual routine.
