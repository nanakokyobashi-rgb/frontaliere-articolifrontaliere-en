G-bewilligung: procedure and costs for border workers (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 3 September 2026

Permit G: CHF 65 cost, 5 years validity. Complete application guide at the Migrationsamt for border workers Ticino-Italy.

Context

In brief

  • G permit valid for 5 years for cross-border workers in Switzerland
  • Application cost: 65 CHF at the Migrationsamt
  • Required documents: identity, employment contract, proof of income
  • Renewal possible within 12 months before expiration

Key facts

  • What: G permit for cross-border workers (Grenzgänger)
  • When: Valid for 5 years from issuance
  • Where: Migrationsamt (cantonal immigration office)
  • Who: Cross-border workers residing in Italy who work in Switzerland
  • Amount: CHF 65 for the application
  • Renewal: Possible 12 months before expiration

The G permit is an essential tool for cross-border workers who regularly work in Switzerland. Unlike other forms of work authorization, the permit is specifically designed for those who reside in Italy and cross the border daily to work for Swiss companies in the Canton of Ticino. The application procedure is not complex but requires attention to administrative details. The Migrationsamt, the competent office for immigration, handles all requests for issuance and renewal. With a cost of only 65 CHF, the permit represents a modest investment to ensure a regularized and recognized work position by Swiss authorities.

Operational details

The Permesso G greatly simplifies the administrative life of the cross-border commuter. Those who cross the border daily at Chiasso, Brogeda or other crossings in the Ticino region thus have a document recognized by the Swiss authorities and the tax offices of both countries. One of the most important practical implications concerns taxation: as a cross-border commuter with Permesso G, the withholding tax on employment income is made solely in Switzerland. Italy, for its part, recognizes the tax credit to avoid double taxation. This mechanism is configured in the income tax return 730, in the CE section specifically for cross-border commuters. Differences between Permesso G and Permesso B Many cross-border commuters confuse Permesso G with Permesso B. The distinction is fundamental: Permesso G is for those who reside in Italy and work in Switzerland while maintaining their residence in Italy (cross-border commuter). Permesso B is for those who move and change their residence to Switzerland. For a Ticino-Italy cross-border commuter, Permesso G is almost always the correct choice and offers greater administrative and tax advantages compared to the alternative. Practical scenario: how Permesso G influences the pay slip Suppose a cross-border commuter starts a new job with a company in the Canton of Ticino. Once the Permesso G has been issued by the Migrationsamt, the Swiss employer will apply the AVS/AI/IPG contribution (5.3% for the employee) and the contributions for accident insurance (LAINF, 0.7–1.5% depending on the professional risk). The withholding tax, calculated on a federal and cantonal basis, will not be reported on the pay slip as double taxation: it is already the final tax for the cross-border commuter. Italy, in the 730 declaration, will recognize the Swiss tax credit, thus avoiding the same income being taxed twice.

Practical Procedure: How to Apply for the G Permit

The application for the G Permit is not complicated, but it must follow official channels. Here are the essential steps:

Step-by-Step Application Procedure

1. Gather Documents — Before presenting yourself at the Migrationsamt, prepare a valid identity document (passport or Italian ID card), a work contract signed by the Swiss employer (copy), proof of income (preliminary pay slips or employer's statement), and the application form provided by the Migrationsamt itself.

2. Submit the Application — Go to the Migrationsamt of the canton where you will work (in this case, Canton Ticino). The application must be submitted on paper or possibly online according to local procedures. The office will guide you on the fastest submission method for your situation.

3. Pay the Fee — The application cost is 65 CHF. This amount is due at the time of application, generally payable directly at the office or through the methods indicated by the Migrationsamt.

4. Processing Time — The Migrationsamt normally processes the application within a few weeks. Once approved, you will receive the G Permit by mail. It is advisable to contact the office to know the specific times in your situation.

Validity and Renewal

The G Permit is valid for 5 years from issuance. It does not expire suddenly: you have plenty of time to plan the renewal without rush. Renewal can be requested within the 12 months preceding the expiry date. It is advisable to start the renewal procedure at least 2-3 months before the expiry date, to avoid administrative problems or periods without valid authorization. The renewal cost is the same: 65 CHF. Many cross-border workers renew the permit even before the 5 years if the employer or workplace in Switzerland changes.

Frequently Asked Questions
How long does Permit G last?
Permit G is valid for 5 years from issue. You can start the renewal process within 12 months before the expiration date. It is advisable to start the process 2-3 months before the expiration date to avoid interruptions of the authorization.
What is the cost to apply for the G Leave?
The application fee is CHF 65, payable at the time of submission to the Migrationsamt. The same amount of CHF 65 is also due for renewal of the permit every 5 years.
What is the difference between Leave G and Leave B?
The G Permit is for those who reside in Italy and work in Switzerland while maintaining their residence in Italy (border worker). Permit B is for those who move and change residence in Switzerland. For a Ticino-Italy border crossing, the G Permit is the right choice and offers greater administrative and tax advantages.
What documents do I need for the application?
Valid identity document (passport or Italian identity card), employment contract signed by the Swiss employer (copy), proof of income (preliminary pay slips or employer's statement), and the completed application form provided by the Migrationsamt.
How does the G Leave affect my tax situation?
With Permit G, withholding tax takes place only in Switzerland. Italy recognizes the tax credit in 730 (EC framework) to avoid double taxation. AVS/AI/IPG (5.3% employee) and LAINF contributions are managed in Switzerland.
