The new agreement between the Italian Revenue Agency, the Guardia di Finanza, and the Cremona Prosecution represents a significant step in the fight against tax evasion. This agreement, which does not yet have a specific date, aims to strengthen collaboration between the different authorities to ensure greater transparency and tax compliance. For Ticino cross-border workers, this could mean an increase in checks and greater attention to tax declarations.

Impact on cross-border workers

Cross-border workers who work in Switzerland and reside in Italy, particularly in Lombardy, may be subject to stricter checks. The agreement provides for greater sharing of information between Italian and Swiss authorities, which could influence the management of withholding taxes and refunds.

What changes for cross-border workers

With the new agreement, cross-border workers will need to pay particular attention to the correct declaration of income and the management of withholding taxes. It is essential to ensure that all information is accurate and complete to avoid penalties. Additionally, cross-border workers should be aware of the new procedures and possible checks that may be carried out.

Frequently Asked Questions
What changes for cross-border workers with the new agreement?
The new agreement between the Italian Revenue Agency, the Guardia di Finanza, and the Prosecutor's Office of Cremona provides for greater collaboration between Italian and Swiss authorities. This could lead to more frequent and in-depth checks for cross-border workers, who will need to pay particular attention to the correct declaration of income and the management of withholding taxes.
Which documents should I keep to be compliant?
Cross-border workers should keep documents such as employment contracts, payment receipts, tax declarations, documents related to withholding taxes, and documents related to refunds. These documents are essential to demonstrate tax compliance in case of checks.
How can I ensure I am compliant with the new regulations?
To ensure compliance with the new regulations, cross-border workers should verify the accuracy of their tax declarations, keep all documentation related to income and taxes, consult an accountant or tax expert, and stay updated on new procedures and regulations.
