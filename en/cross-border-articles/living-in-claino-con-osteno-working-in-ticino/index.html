The municipality of Claino with Osteno offers a strategic residential solution for many workers who cross the border daily to reach Ticino. Living in this area of Lombardy provides a scenic landscape and geographical proximity to major Swiss working centers such as Lugano and Mendrisio. For those who choose this lifestyle, managing transfrontier work requires a thorough understanding of the current tax and social security regulations. Switzerland, which is not a member of the European Union or the European Economic Area, applies specific provisions regulated by the Convention against Double Taxation signed on 9 December 1976. The income tax on dependent employment is withheld directly in the Swiss country, avoiding double taxation in Italy through the mechanism of the tax credit managed through the CE model 730. Operational details

Daily organization and cross-border connections

Living in Claino with Osteno implies a careful planning of daily commutes to reach the workplaces in Ticino. Road connections connect the area to border crossings and major arteries such as the A2 motorway and the Lombard road network. The mobility of the border crossing requires the management of travel times, especially in the morning and evening rush hours, when the flow of vehicles to Switzerland suffers slowdowns near customs crossing points. In order to optimise the costs of travelling by car, including parking and the Swiss motorway vignette, commuters carefully evaluate the different transport options available on the territory.

Health and social security coverage for residents

A fundamental chapter for those who reside in Claino con Osteno and work in Ticino concerns the management of compulsory health coverage. Border workers in possession of the G permit fall under the right of option, which allows them to choose between the Swiss health system through LAMal and the Italian health system. Opting for LAMal means taking out health insurance with deductibles for adults of between CHF 300 and CHF 2,500. Operational Procedures and Compliance for Frontalier

The worker who decides to settle in Claino with Osteno to pursue a professional activity in Ticino must follow a series of precise administrative steps. The first requirement is to obtain the G work permit, whose procedure involves presenting the employment contract documentation with a Ticino company. Once the activity is started, it is necessary to monitor one's fiscal position in Italy by correctly filling out the tax return through the 730 model, including data on income from Switzerland and applying the tax credit to avoid double taxation.

Checklist of Fundamental Steps

  • Verify the status of old or new frontalier based on the date of previous or subsequent to the 17 July 2023.
  • Request the G permit for frontalier from the competent authorities of the Canton Ticino before the actual start of the professional activity.
  • Exercise the right to opt for the LAMal sick fund within the terms provided by the current legislation.
  • Keep the documentation related to taxes paid at source in Switzerland for the compilation of the CE in the 730.

Frequently Asked Questions
How is the income of a border worker living in Claino con Osteno taxed?
Employee income tax is only withheld at source in Switzerland. Italy eliminates double taxation by recognizing a tax credit through the EC framework of model 730, applying the Italian IRPEF rates provided for the income brackets.
What are the differences between old and new border crossers for those who live in Claino con Osteno?
Old frontier workers, i.e. those who worked in Switzerland already before 17 July 2023, benefit from a transitional regime until 2033 with an exemption of 7,500 euros. The new frontier workers subject to the New Agreement in force from 1 January 2024, on the other hand, enjoy a deductible of 10,000 euros.
How does LAMal health insurance work for border workers from Claino con Osteno?
Border workers in possession of the G permit have the right of option to choose between the Swiss LAMal health system and the Italian one. By choosing LAMal, the insurance provides deductibles for adults ranging from a minimum of 300 to a maximum of 2,500 Swiss francs.
