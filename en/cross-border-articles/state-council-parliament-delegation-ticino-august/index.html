Operational details

The refreshment mechanism and its meaning for border workers

Refreshments represent one of the fundamental mechanisms that link the Swiss tax system to the Italian border municipalities and directly affect the daily reality of the Ticino border worker. For those who work in Switzerland and reside in Italy, understanding the dynamics of refunds means understanding how income taxation is redistributed between the Swiss authorities and the Italian municipalities of the provinces of Varese, Como and Brescia.

Refunds constitute the portion of the tax at source that Switzerland pays to the Italian municipalities of residence of border workers according to the bilateral agreements between the two States. When this transmission is cautiously suspended — as occurred and discussed in today's meeting in Bellinzona — the financial flow to the beneficiary Italian municipalities is temporarily interrupted. This interruption has a direct impact on municipal finances and, consequently, on the local public services that border workers resident in Italy use daily: from road maintenance to public transport services, from educational services to local welfare.

Key points

How cross-border workers can monitor the evolution of the issue

For the cross-border worker who resides in Italy and works in Switzerland, monitoring the evolution of the issue regarding remittances means staying informed about official communications from the Swiss Confederation, the Ticino Parliamentary Delegation, and the Lombardy Region. During the autumn session of the Swiss Parliament — which starts on September 14 and concludes on October 2 — the Ticino Parliamentary Delegation will presumably bring updates on this bilateral negotiation. The mayors of border municipalities (Chiasso, Mendrisio, Lugano, Varese, Como) are often the first to disseminate news on negotiation outcomes, as they are directly interested in maintaining remittances in their budgets.

A useful information channel is the following: the official website of the Ticino Parliamentary Delegation will publish reports from the autumn session in which this issue will be discussed. Furthermore, press releases from the Lombardy Region and the interested mayors will be disseminated through Italian local media (Como and Varese provinces) and Swiss media (Ticino media). The cross-border worker can subscribe to the mailing lists of their municipalities of residence in Italy to receive official updates on this matter.

Impact on the payslip and income tax return of the cross-border worker

The suspension of remittances does not directly impact the payslip of the cross-border worker from an immediate quantitative perspective: the Swiss source tax continues to be applied according to the current federal, cantonal (Ticino), and municipal rates. However, the correct functioning of double taxation Italy-Switzerland and the Italian tax credit remains linked to the regular transfer of data between Swiss administrations and the Italian Tax Authority.

If the suspension of remittances reflects broader administrative misalignments between the two states, the cross-border worker may experience delays in completing their income tax return (section CE of form 730) or in collecting the tax credit owed. It is therefore advisable to regularly verify the status of your tax communications through the tax calculator dedicated to cross-border workers, simulating the impact of your taxation according to the current source tax rates and comparing the amount withheld in Switzerland with the credit due in Italy.

It is also appropriate for the cross-border worker to communicate with their Swiss employer to verify that they are correctly applying source tax according to federal legislation and Ticino cantonal legislation, without variations caused by the administrative suspension of remittances (which concerns exclusively the transfer of funds between states, not the calculation of the withholding from payslip).

Future developments and monitoring of the resolution

During the autumn session, the Ticino Parliamentary Delegation will meet with Federal Councillor Ignazio Cassis and, for the first time, the new General Director of the Postal Service, Pascal Grieder. These meetings could bring clarification on the timing of the resolution of the precautionary suspension and on coordination between the various administrative levels. Cross-border workers can stay updated through official communications from the Ticino Parliamentary Delegation, available on the official website of the Swiss Parliament, and through bulletins from Italian mayors.

Given the strategic importance of the year-end deadline, it is essential to monitor developments in the coming weeks. If the suspension were to continue beyond this deadline, the cross-border worker could decide to contact their Italian municipality of residence to report the situation and receive information on any expected delays in managing remittances and related public services.
