Torre Velasca: history and restoration at Villa Recalcati

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 5 October 2026

The Order of Architects of Varese recounts the 2021–2025 restoration and the history of Torre Velasca through architecture, design, and redevelopment.

The conference hall of the Province of Varese, at Villa Recalcati, hosted a meeting dedicated to Torre Velasca, the famous monumental building in Milan. The evening was promoted by the Order of Architects of Varese as part of the Olivari Openings series and attracted a substantial audience. The account followed three threads: the building's origins, the renovation project, and the history of the handle that became a symbol of the tower.

The event opened with official greetings from Giorgio Caporaso, president of the design commission, and Michela Nurra, president of the Young People and Innovative Tools commission. Then Stefano Suriano, an architectural historian, took the audience inside the project by Studio BBPR and engineer Arturo Danusso. His interpretation highlighted a dual modernity: in the construction solutions and in the building's social significance.

A housing model beyond the engineering challenge

Torre Velasca was not presented solely as a technically complex work. According to Suriano's account, the tower brought together three different structures—commercial, office, and residential—and also proposed compact residential solutions. These included 40-square-metre one-bedroom apartments, a choice described as revolutionary compared with the standards and habits of living in the city center during the postwar period.

The transition from the project to the present came with architect Giacomo Albo's contribution. Albo illustrated the phases of the renovation carried out between 2021 and 2025, documented with thousands of photographs requested by the latest purchaser. The redevelopment adapted the tower to contemporary times while respecting the original structure. The evening concluded with engineer Antonio Olivari, of the eponymous company, and with the story of the handles designed in the 1950s by Gio Ponti and Lodovico Barbiano di Belgiojoso, a member of Studio BBPR. Since then, that design has been known as «Maniglia Velasca».

Operational details

From the housing model to conservation

The most significant point in the account is not a single date, but the way the speakers connected use, construction, and conservation. Torre Velasca was considered both as a technical building and as a housing model. The presence, in the same tower, of commercial spaces, offices, and homes describes a complex structure; the 40-square-meter one-bedroom apartments add a compact residential option to this complexity. According to the account of the evening, it is this juxtaposition that makes the project innovative on the social level as well.

Philology as a working criterion

As for the restoration, the keyword is «philology». Before the works, a meticulous inventory was made of everything inside the tower. The subsequent phase did not erase that survey: it led to the restoration of most of the original elements, from the wood paneling to the historic details of the staircases. The criterion described by Giacomo Albo therefore brings together two needs: adapting the building to contemporary times and remaining faithful to the original design.

The economic dimension makes the construction site even more imposing. The redevelopment costs doubled the property's original purchase price of 150 million euros. This figure is inseparable from the documentation: the thousands of photographs requested by the last buyer recount the lengthy construction phase and accompany the transformation without replacing the existing structure.

Frequently Asked Questions
What were the main phases of the restoration of the Velasca Tower?
The restoration, illustrated by the architect Giacomo Albo, took place between 2021 and 2025. The intervention was preceded by a meticulous inventory of all the internal elements. The criterion followed was of a philological nature: the works aimed to adapt the building to contemporary times while preserving the original design, with the restoration of boiserie and historical details of the stairs, documented through thousands of photographs requested by the last buyer.
What is the economic value of the redevelopment operation?
The property was the subject of a major investment: the redevelopment costs incurred doubled the original purchase figure of the tower, which amounted to 150 million euros. This economic figure reflects the size of the construction site and the technical complexity necessary to maintain respect for the original structure during the adaptation to modern living and working standards.
What is meant by 'Maniglia Velasca'?
The 'Velasca Handle' is a design object designed in the 1950s by Gio Ponti and Lodovico Barbiano from Belgiojoso, a member of the BBPR studio, who designed the entire tower together with engineer Arturo Danusso. Produced by the Olivari company, this handle has become a symbol of the building itself and has remained in the company's catalogue for almost fiftyyears, until 2005.
