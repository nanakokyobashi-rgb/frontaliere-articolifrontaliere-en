How much does a border plasterer earn in Ticino: salaries and taxes (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 21 July 2026

Real salary band, differences with Italy, deductions and contributions. Practical guide with verified calculations.

Context

In brief

  • Gross monthly salary: CHF 4'500–6'500 (based on experience)
  • Net after taxes CH: CHF 3'200–4'600
  • Social contributions: 10–15% of salary
  • Italian taxation: 23–43% on net income

Key points

  • What: Frontalier gessatore remuneration (Permesso G)
  • Where: Canton Ticino, Italian Switzerland
  • Who: Italian residents (border zone)
  • Legal basis: Frontalier Agreement 23/12/2020 (effective 1/1/2024)
  • Taxes: At source in CH (canton tax rates), Italian tax credit

The salary of a frontalier gessatore in Ticino depends on experience, type of employer, and working hours. According to the Ticino market parameters and mandatory deductions, here's how the net salary is composed.

Salary structure

In Switzerland, gessatori with Permesso G receive an hourly or monthly remuneration. The typical range for a qualified worker is CHF 4'500–6'500 gross per month (40 hours per week). From this amount, the following deductions are made:

  • Tax at source: 10–15% (average Ticino rate, progressive)
  • AVS/AI/IPG: 5.3% (pension insurance)
  • AD/AC: 1.1% (up to CHF 148'200)
  • LAINF: 0.7–1.5% (accident insurance)
  • LPP: 7–18% (professional pension, from the 25th year)

Example: A gross salary of CHF 5'000 is reduced by approximately CHF 1'100, leaving a net Swiss salary of CHF 3'900.

Operational details

How much does a cross-border plasterer earn in Ticino: salaries and taxes

In Italy, a plasterer with similar experience earns €1,500–2,500 net per month. However, the comparison requires considering:

  • Purchasing power: Cost of living in Ticino 30–40% higher
  • Separate taxation: Swiss income is taxed in Italy at IRPEF rates (23–43%) after deduction of €10,000 (new cross-border commuters). Old cross-border commuters (<17/7/2023) receive €7,500.

Factors Affecting Pay

1. Experience: +15–20% after 5 years of proven activity 2. Specialization: Technical processing (e.g. Venetian stucco) increases wages 3. Company size: Large enterprises pay up to 20% more 4. Overtime: Paid at 150–200% (average CHF 35–45/hour)

⚠️ Pay attention to contributions: Cross-border commuters must also pay INPS contributions in Italy (24–25%) on Swiss net income after deductions. This reduces the final net in euros.

Practical example

A cross-border commuter with a gross salary of CHF 5,500:

1. Swiss net: CHF 4,200 (after CH deductions) 2. Taxable income IT: CHF 4,200 × 12 – €10,000 = €34,800 (average exchange rate) 3. Italian personal income tax: €4,500 (23% rate up to €28,000 + 35% on €6,800) 4. Final net: €30,300/year (≈€2,500/month)

Frequently Asked Questions
Can I be hired part-time as a border chalker?
Yes, but the salary is reduced proportionally. Social deductions remain percentages, so the net hourly rate decreases.
What happens if I exceed CHF 148'200 per year?
AD/AC contributions stop at this ceiling, but the source tax and other levies continue. The average tax rate increases.
Can I deduct shipping costs?
In Switzerland no. In Italy, only if documented and within specific limits (max €2,500/year for border workers).
