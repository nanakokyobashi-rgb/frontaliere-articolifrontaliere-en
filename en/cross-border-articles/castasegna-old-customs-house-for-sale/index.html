Castasegna: the old customs house for sale worries Bregaglia (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 9 August 2026

The Confederation has put the old Castasegna customs house up for sale. The Municipality of Bregaglia activates with Chur to avoid the loss of federal presence.

Context

In a nutshell

  • The Confederation has put the old Castasegna customs house up for sale.
  • The price has not yet been made public by Bern.
  • The Municipality of Bregaglia has asked Chur for help.
  • The building was built in 1860 and expanded over time.
  • What: Sale of the old Castasegna customs house by the Confederation
  • When: News spread in August 2026, with meetings planned after the summer
  • Where: Castasegna, Val Bregaglia, Canton of Graubünden
  • Who: Municipality of Bregaglia, Mayor Fernando Giovanoli, Confederation, Canton, Federal Office of Customs and Border Security
  • Amount: Price not yet specified by Bern

The Municipality of Bregaglia opposes the prospect of losing the old Castasegna customs house, one of the last no longer necessary customs buildings that the Confederation has decided to put on the market with the intention of proceeding with the sale. The news mobilized local authorities, concerned about the reduction of state and federal presence within the valley. Before proceeding publicly, Bern contacted the Municipality and Canton directly to verify any institutional interest in the purchase. Faced with this move, the municipal administration of Bregaglia immediately took action asking for support and coordination in Chur. Operational details

The federal decision to alienate real estate assets no longer needed for direct customs activities raises questions about the long-term consequences for employment and the presence of public personnel in the Alpine valleys. According to the municipal administration of Bregaglia, the sale of historic properties of this type is often a first step that risks leading to a progressive reduction in jobs related to services in the territory. Often the reason is given that, in the absence of adequate living or residential spaces, there are no people interested in holding certain public positions in decentralised areas. For this reason, the mayor stressed the strategic importance that the Canton or the Confederation retain ownership of suitable buildings to allow police and border guards to regularly carry out their operational and surveillance duties.

Future scenarios and alternative destinations of spaces

After the summer break, an official meeting is scheduled between the Municipality of Bregaglia and the representatives of the Canton of Graubünden to evaluate what to do and define a common strategy on the management of the property. In the event that the building does not remain in public hands and the institutional purchase option is definitively phased out, the local administration hopes that the spaces will be converted to respond to the housing shortages experienced in the region. Key points

The case of the old customs house in Castasegna highlights the delicacy of the processes of dismission of public real estate in peripheral Swiss areas, where each building represents a bastion of sovereignty and continuity of services. The local population and local administrators continue to monitor the evolution of the administrative situation, awaiting the direct confrontation between the Municipality and the Canton, which will take place immediately after the summer season. Evaluations will range from the possibility of a joint public intervention to maintain the building in the state domain to the analysis of alternative proposals for the urbanistic and residential valorization of existing volumes, taking into account the specificities of the real estate market in the Bregaglia Valley compared to other Alpine regions bordering more densely solicited by speculative pressures.

Frequently Asked Questions
Why was the old Castasegna customs house put up for sale?
The Confederation has decided to put the old Castasegna customs house on the market as the building is no longer considered necessary for the current customs activities, falling within the plan for the disposal of the no longer indispensable federal real estate assets.
Who put the property up for sale and what is the price?
The property was put up for sale by the Confederation through the Federal Office of Customs and Border Security. The sale price has not yet been made public by Bern, which has previously consulted the Municipality and the Canton.
What are the concerns of the Municipality of Bregaglia?
The Municipality fears the loss of federal and cantonal presence in the valley, fearing that the sale of the property could lead to a reduction in jobs and weaken the presence of law enforcement and cantonal police.
What alternative uses are evaluated if the building does not remain public?
If the building does not remain in public hands, Mayor Fernando Giovanoli hopes that the spaces will be used as homes to cope with the local shortage, avoiding speculation similar to that of the Engadine, or converted into hotel rooms.
