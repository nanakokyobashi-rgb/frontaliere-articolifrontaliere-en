Fire at Malpensa Terminal 1: Firefighters' Intervention

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 2 May 2026

Context

In brief

  • Malpensa is the main international hub for thousands of cross-border workers at multinationals in Ticino
  • Four practical alternative airports exist within 2 hours of Como, Varese and Lugano
  • EU Regulation EC 261/2004 entitles passengers to up to €600 in compensation for cancellations and significant delays

On 2 May 2026, an electrical fault in a telephone exchange in the service tunnel of Malpensa Terminal 1 caused a small fire. Fire crews from Busto Arsizio and Somma Lombardo responded within twenty minutes and contained the incident. No passengers were involved. Operations at Terminal 1 were partially disrupted for several hours before returning to normal.

For cross-border workers (frontalieri) employed at multinational companies in Lugano, Mendrisio or Chiasso — in pharmaceuticals, finance or technology — airport disruptions are an occupational reality. A sudden cancellation can mean a missed client meeting, a delayed project delivery or a wasted business trip. Having a clear plan before the disruption happens makes all the difference.

Alternative airports: distances and drive times from Como, Varese and Lugano

When Malpensa is disrupted — whether by fire, air traffic control strike or severe weather — these are the viable alternatives:

  • Orio al Serio (BGY) – Bergamo: around 80 km from Como, 75 km from Varese, 100 km from Lugano. Average drive time: 60–75 minutes outside rush hour. Strong range of European low-cost routes; less suited for long-haul travel.
  • Linate (LIN) – Milan: around 50 km from Como, 55 km from Varese, 70 km from Lugano. Drive time: 45–60 minutes. Good for business routes to Rome and European capitals; expanded capacity since 2025.
  • Lugano-Agno (LUG): under 10 km from central Lugano and about 35 km from Chiasso. Limited but growing European destinations; convenient for those already in Switzerland needing to connect to Zurich or Geneva.
  • Geneva (GVA): around 220 km from Lugano via the A2/A9 motorway, roughly 2 hours' drive. Full intercontinental hub — useful for North American or Asian routes when other airports are congested.

Practical checklist: what to do in the first 30 minutes of a disruption

1. Check your flight status immediately on FlightRadar24 or the airline's app — delay alerts appear before official cancellation notices. 2. Contact your employer's HR or travel desk within the first half hour: many Swiss employers hold corporate travel insurance that covers rebooking costs. 3. Do not leave the airport without getting written confirmation from check-in or gate staff of the disruption reason — this document is essential for any EC 261/2004 claim. 4. Research the fastest alternative route (see table above) and book via the airline's app or Google Flights, filtered by departures within the next 4 hours. 5. Keep all receipts for extraordinary expenses (meals, taxi, hotel): EC 261/2004 obliges the airline to reimburse these when the cancellation is attributable to the carrier.

Operational details

EC 261/2004: passenger rights every frequent-flying frontaliere should know

The EU regulation on air passenger protection is one of the most powerful tools available to business travellers, yet it remains widely underused. Here are the key points for those flying from Malpensa.

When the right to financial compensation applies:

  • Flight cancelled with less than 14 days' notice: €250 (routes ≤1,500 km), €400 (1,500–3,500 km), €600 (>3,500 km)
  • Arrival delay of more than 3 hours (short haul), 4 hours (medium haul), 5 hours (long haul)
  • Denied boarding due to overbooking

When the airline is exempt: "Extraordinary circumstances" — severe weather, political instability, or in some cases structural fires at the airport — can release the carrier from financial compensation. However, the duty of care (meals, hotel, alternative transport) always applies, even in force majeure situations. An infrastructure fire such as the one on 2 May at Malpensa sits in a grey area: if the cancellation is the carrier's own operational decision rather than the result of an official airport closure, compensation is owed.

How to file a claim: 1. Within 6 years of the flight date (Italian limitation period), send a written complaint to the airline citing the flight number, date, and Article 7 of Reg. EC 261/2004. 2. If no response within 6 weeks, escalate to ENAC (Italy's civil aviation authority) or use a claim service such as AirHelp or Flightright, which work on a percentage of the recovered amount. 3. For passengers with tickets issued by a Swiss employer, check whether the employment contract specifies an internal reimbursement process — some Ticino multinationals handle claims centrally through travel management.

Who covers the cost: employer vs personal insurance

Disruption management differs between business and private travel for cross-border workers.

Business travel (company assignment):

  • The cost of an alternative ticket is borne by the Swiss employer, who then recovers from the airline via EC 261/2004 or via a corporate travel insurance policy.
  • Meal and hotel expenses incurred because of the delay are standard business expenses.
  • If the trip is booked via a corporate travel desk, the employee should not need to advance significant costs.

Private travel (Permit G holder, weekend return):

  • The burden falls on the traveller's own travel insurance (if held) or directly on EC 261/2004 compensation.
  • Some premium credit cards issued by Swiss banks — such as PostFinance or Raiffeisen — include basic cover for flight delays exceeding 4 hours.

Key points

Step-by-step: what to do when your Malpensa flight is cancelled

Panic is the worst response to a travel disruption. Following a clear procedure helps you recover mobility and protect your rights in the shortest time possible.

Phase 1 — First 15 minutes: Check the airline's app or airport monitors. If you see "Cancelled" or "Delayed >2h", go immediately to your airline's dedicated assistance desk (not the generic check-in counter) and request the EC 261/2004 assistance form to be completed and stamped. Photograph or download the document.

Phase 2 — Between 15 and 60 minutes: Search for an alternative flight. Business travellers should call the company travel desk or appointed travel agency first. Private travellers should use Google Flights or Kayak, which show real-time seat availability on nearby airports (Orio, Linate, Lugano-Agno). If the airline can rebook you on an alternative flight within 2 hours, it is often worth waiting.

Phase 3 — Beyond 60 minutes: If rebooking is delayed or impossible, consider the train. From Malpensa Airport station, a regional train reaches Milan Centrale in 40 minutes: from there, high-speed trains serve the whole of Italy and the EuroCity reaches Lugano in about 1 hour. Keep your rail ticket — it may be reimbursable under EC 261/2004 if the modal transfer was made necessary by the flight cancellation.

Phase 4 — After returning: Prepare your reimbursement claim within 30 days of the event. Required documents: original boarding pass (or screenshot), cancellation confirmation by email, receipts for extraordinary expenses, and any invoice for the replacement flight. The statutory limitation period is 2 years in Switzerland (CO art. 127) and 2 years in Italy (Consumer Code) — but claiming early is always advisable.

Frequently Asked Questions
If my Malpensa flight is cancelled because of an airport fire, am I entitled to EC 261/2004 compensation?
It depends. If the fire qualifies as an extraordinary circumstance beyond the carrier's control, the airline may be exempt from financial compensation (€250–€600). However, the duty of care — meals, hotel accommodation and alternative transport — always applies regardless of the cause. If the cancellation is the carrier's own operational decision and not the result of an official airport closure order, compensation is owed. Always submit a written claim to ENAC or via a claim service such as AirHelp to have your specific case assessed.
Which airports can I use as alternatives to Malpensa when travelling from Como or the Lugano area?
The most practical alternatives are: Orio al Serio (BGY, around 75–80 km, 60–75 min drive), best for European low-cost routes; Linate (LIN, around 50–55 km, 45–60 min), good for business destinations; Lugano-Agno (LUG, under 10 km from Lugano), convenient for those already in Switzerland. Geneva (GVA, around 2 hours) is only practical for intercontinental flights. For Zurich, the direct train from Lugano is often faster than flying.
How does a Swiss employer typically handle a missed business trip due to an airport disruption?
Swiss companies with a structured travel policy generally require employees to advance extraordinary costs (hotel, taxi, alternative flight) and claim them back via expenses. Some multinationals have a central travel desk that handles rebooking directly. The employer then recovers the costs from the airline via EC 261/2004 or through corporate travel insurance. Always check the travel section of your collective agreement or company regulations for the exact procedure.
