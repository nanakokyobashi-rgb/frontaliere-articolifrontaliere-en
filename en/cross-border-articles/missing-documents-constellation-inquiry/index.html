Constellation Inquiry: Missing Official Documents (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 20 July 2026

The 2015 folders on the works at the Le Constellation bar in Crans-Montana are gone. The point about the investigation and the documentary implications.

Context

In Brief

  • Documents from 2015 related to the Le Constellation bar have gone missing.
  • The files concerned the renovation work on the premises.
  • A 2016 document confirms the Municipality's knowledge of the works.
  • No searches have been conducted at the town hall to date.

Key Facts

  • What: Missing investigation documents
  • When: 2015-2016 period
  • Where: Municipality of Chermignon, Crans-Montana (VS)
  • Who: Prosecutor, former Municipality, former town clerk
  • Status: Investigation in progress

Operational details

Procedural and administrative implications

The disappearance of public documents in an investigation of this importance not only constitutes an obstacle for the judiciary, but highlights the vulnerabilities inherent in the handover during administrative mergers between Swiss municipalities. For those who work in Switzerland, this story underlines the importance of always keeping a copy of any document or authorisation that concerns their professional activity or tax position. In the case in question, the presence of a document from 2016, which emerged later, shows that the Municipality was aware of the sound insulation works carried out in the bar, potentially contradicting the hypothesis of a total lack of traceability.

Document management, from work permits such as the permesso G to professional certifications, is an obligation that falls on both public bodies and individual workers. In situations of document uncertainty, proof of the fact becomes the determining element. If a public body is unable to produce official documents, the risk of opaque management of procedures inevitably reflects on the transparency of authorisation processes. For a border worker, maintaining an orderly personnel file of payrolls, contracts and official communications with the Federal Tax Administration is essential to avoid disputes within dichiarazione dei redditi. Key points

How to protect your documentation

In a context where dematerialisation and digitisation do not always guarantee the long-term availability of documents, it is essential to adopt good personal archiving practices. Regardless of the sector in which you operate, the systematic storage of any document received from the employer or the cantonal authorities in Ticino is a necessary act of prudence. If you work in Switzerland, it is advisable to scan and digitally archive any relevant documents, including work certificates and communications relating to the previdenza professionale LPP.

In the event that you find discrepancies or lack of documentation regarding your position, the first action to be taken is the formal request for an extract or certified copy from the issuing body. Regarding taxation, verifying your position through the calcolatore fiscale allows you to have a clear picture of the payments made, regardless of the physical availability of paper folders. It is also advisable to regularly monitor the portal of the Federal Tax Administration to ensure that the data relating to the tax at source is correctly recorded. Frequently Asked Questions
What documents were lost in the Constellation investigation?
The official documents dating back to 2015 relating to the renovation work have been lost, in particular those concerning the sound insulation of the Le Constellation premises in Crans-Montana.
Why is the disappearance of the folders considered a problem?
The documents are considered keys to shedding light on the responsibilities related to the New Year's fire. Their absence makes it impossible to accurately reconstruct the authorisation process followed by the Municipality for the work carried out on the premises.
What emerged regarding the knowledge of the works by the Municipality?
A document from 2016 has emerged attesting that the City Council was aware of the sound insulation work carried out in the bar, despite the disappearance of the main files from 2015.
