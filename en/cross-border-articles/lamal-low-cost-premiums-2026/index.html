Elizabeth Baume-Schneider opposes low-cost LAMal with lower premiums (cross-border guide)

Di Valerie Linc · Published on 8 May 2026

Federal Councillor Elizabeth Baume-Schneider opposes the proposal for a low-cost LAMal with lower premiums, defending the current system.

Context

In brief

  • Elizabeth Baume-Schneider opposes a low-cost LAMal
  • Lower premiums do not guarantee adequate coverage
  • Current system protects cross-border workers

Key facts

  • What: Proposal for a low-cost LAMal with lower premiums
  • When: Discussion ongoing in 2026
  • Where: Switzerland, with impact on Ticino cross-border workers
  • Who: Federal Councillor Elizabeth Baume-Schneider
  • Amount: Current premiums vary depending on the canton and health insurance company

Federal Councillor Elizabeth Baume-Schneider has clearly expressed her opposition to the proposal to introduce a low-cost LAMal with lower premiums. In an interview with TVS, Baume-Schneider emphasized that a reduction in premiums could compromise the quality and scope of the services offered. 'A low-cost LAMal is not the solution,' she stated, defending the current system that ensures adequate health coverage for all residents, including cross-border workers.

Impact on cross-border workers

Cross-border workers who work in Ticino and reside in Italy are particularly interested in this discussion. Currently, cross-border workers are subject to withholding tax and must comply with Swiss regulations regarding health insurance. A low-cost LAMal could significantly influence their finances and the quality of medical care they have access to. Baume-Schneider emphasized that the current system, although it may seem expensive, offers comprehensive protection that a low-cost version could not guarantee.

Operational details

Practical Analysis

The proposal for a low-cost LAMal raises several practical issues for cross-border workers. First, it's important to understand how current premiums are calculated and what services are covered. Currently, cross-border workers must pay insurance premiums based on their income and family situation. A low-cost LAMal could offer lower premiums, but with a reduction in covered services.

Premium Comparison

To make a comparison, let's consider the current average premiums in Ticino. For example, a cross-border worker residing in Italy and working in Ticino might pay monthly premiums ranging from 300 to 500 CHF, depending on the chosen health insurance provider. A low-cost LAMal could reduce these premiums by 10-20%, but with limited coverage.

Covered Services

The current LAMal system covers a wide range of services, including medical visits, hospital stays, medications, and therapies. A low-cost LAMal might exclude some of these services, leaving cross-border workers with additional costs. For example, it might not cover specialized therapies or expensive medications, forcing cross-border workers to pay out of pocket.

Savings Scenarios

If a cross-border worker chooses a low-cost LAMal, they might save on monthly premiums but could face unexpected costs for uncovered services. For example, if a cross-border worker needs a specialized therapy not covered by the low-cost policy, they might have to pay thousands of CHF out of pocket. This could negate the initial savings on premiums.
