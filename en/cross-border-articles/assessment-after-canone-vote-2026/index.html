On May 9, the Ordinary General Assembly of SSR Italian-speaking Switzerland will be held at the Palazzo dei Congressi in Lugano. This event represents a crucial moment to review the vote on the radio-television fee held on March 8, which saw a 45% turnout of eligible voters, with 52% of the votes in favor of maintaining the fee. CORSI President Giovanna Masoni Brenni stated that the challenges continue, particularly with the Enavant project, which addresses fundamental issues such as the role of the regions and the allocation of resources. Concern remains high in Italian-speaking Switzerland, as also emerged in the recent Delegates' Assembly.

Enavant and its implications

The Enavant project, with a budget of 1.2 billion Swiss francs for the 2025-2027 triennium, is at the center of discussions. Among the critical points are verticalization, representation in key positions, and cuts to regional budgets. President Masoni Brenni emphasized the importance of constructive dialogue within SSR SRG.

"It is essential that the regions have an active voice in decisions that directly concern them."

Operational details

Practical Analysis: What Changes for Cross-Border Workers

The ordinary general assembly of the SSR Svizzera italiana on May 9 represents an opportunity to assess the current situation and future directions of the SSR. For cross-border workers employed in Ticino, it is crucial to understand the implications of these decisions, especially in light of recent regulatory changes and new corporate strategies.

Comparison Before and After the Vote

Before the March 8 vote, there were concerns about verticalization and the allocation of resources. For example, many cross-border workers residing in the municipalities of Chiasso, Mendrisio, and Lugano feared a reduction in local services in favor of greater centralization. After the vote, the Enavant project became central, with a focus on themes such as editorial autonomy and representation in key positions. This comparison shows how priorities have changed and what new challenges the SSR must face.

Future Scenarios

One of the future scenarios could see greater regional involvement in the management of resources. This could have a positive impact on editorial autonomy, allowing for greater customization of content for different linguistic regions. However, there are also concerns about cuts and the implementation timelines of the new strategies.

For example, the SSR has announced a savings plan of 100 million Swiss francs by 2025, which could affect local services. For cross-border workers, this could mean a reduction in Italian-language programs and a greater emphasis on bilingual content.
