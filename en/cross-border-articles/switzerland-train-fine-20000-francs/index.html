Switzerland: 20,000 francs fine for traveling without a ticket (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 6 May 2026

A 40-year-old man from the Canton of Schwyz was fined 20,000 francs for traveling without a ticket and forging documents.

Context

In brief

  • Record fine of 20,000 francs for traveling without a ticket
  • False identity and fraud uncovered
  • Criminal record aggravates the penalty

Key facts

  • What: Traveling without a ticket and document fraud
  • When: Last year
  • Where: Canton of Schwyz, Switzerland
  • Who: Swiss Federal Railways (SBB)
  • Amount: 20,000 francs

In Switzerland, traveling without a ticket on public transport is an offense monitored with extreme rigor. However, the case of a 40-year-old resident in the Canton of Schwyz is incredible. The Swiss Federal Railways (SBB) provide an administrative sanction of around 90 francs for the first offense, but for this passenger, the final bill has soared to the astronomical figure of 20,000 francs.

It all started when the man, last year, was intercepted on board an SBB train without a valid ticket. In an attempt to avoid the fine, the forty-year-old showed the controllers a stolen ID card, subsequently signing the report with a false signature. The fraud was uncovered by a technological mishap: the rightful owner of the document, unaware of the incident, received an official notification via email from the SBB regarding the fine issued on the train. Suspicious of the charge for a journey he had never taken, the user immediately filed a complaint, triggering the investigations that revealed the crime of impersonation.

Operational details

Implications for cross-border workers

The case of the 40-year-old from the Canton of Schwyz highlights the importance for cross-border workers to always be in order with their documents and transport tickets. Travelling without a ticket in Switzerland can result in heavy fines, and the consequences can be even more severe if one attempts to evade responsibilities through fraudulent means.

Comparison with the previous situation

Before this case, fines for travelling without a ticket in Switzerland were known to be strict but predictable. The standard fine for a first offence was around 90 francs, with progressive increases in case of repeat offences. However, this case has shown that the consequences can be much more severe if further crimes are committed, such as the forgery of documents.

Concrete scenarios

Imagine a cross-border worker who regularly travels to Switzerland for work. If they were to forget to buy a ticket, the fine would be around 90 francs. However, if they attempted to avoid the fine through fraudulent means, such as the use of false documents, the consequences could be much more serious, as demonstrated by this case. It is therefore fundamental to always be in order with documents and transport tickets to avoid unpleasant legal consequences.

Concrete procedures

To avoid incurring fines, cross-border workers should follow some concrete procedures: 1. Always buy the ticket before boarding the train. 2. Keep identity documents safe to avoid theft or loss. 3. In case of a check, always show valid documents and transport tickets. 4. In case of error or forgetfulness, address the situation with transparency and honesty, avoiding committing further crimes.

Key points

What to do in case of a fine

If a cross-border worker receives a fine for traveling without a ticket, it's important to act promptly. First, it's necessary to verify the validity of the fine and, if necessary, file a complaint. It's crucial to provide all the requested information and cooperate with the competent authorities.

Deadlines and procedures

The deadlines for paying fines vary depending on the specific case. It's important to respect the deadlines indicated in the fine notice to avoid further sanctions. In case of doubts or need for assistance, you can contact the Swiss Federal Railways (SBB CFF FFS) or a lawyer specialized in Swiss law.
