Key facts

  • What: Seizure of 7500 euros.
  • When: Not specified.
  • Where: Valico di Piattamala (Italy)/Campocologno (GR).
  • Who: Tirano's financial guard.
  • Amount: 7500 euros.

The 62-year-old Italian, originally from the province of Naples, was on his way to Switzerland when he was stopped by financiers from Tirano's Guardia di Finanza. The agents found that the man was indebted to the Italian tax authorities and denounced him for fraudulent subtraction from the payment of taxes.

Border controls in Switzerland

The Finance Guard of Tirano worked in collaboration with the Customs of Bellinzona to carry out border controls. The financiers verified the identity and documentation of passengers transiting from the Piattamala crossing, in Italy, to Campocologno, in Switzerland.

The regulations in force

The legislation in force provides that individuals who travel to Switzerland with more than 10,000 euros must declare their arrival to the Italian Revenue Agency within 12 months from the date of their arrival in Switzerland. In the event of non-compliance with this legislation, individuals may be reported for fraudulent subtraction from the payment of taxes.

Operational details

The article does not provide specific information on the implications for frontier workers, but the seizure of €7,500 may have consequences for those who work and reside in Switzerland. Border crossers can check their taxes and insurance to avoid similar problems.

Tirano, a municipality located in the district of Sondrio, is an important commercial and transport hub for border workers working in Switzerland. According to 2022 statistics, around 3,000 border workers from Tirano regularly work in Switzerland, mainly in the construction and services sector.

However, the seizure of money can have serious consequences for these workers. For example, in 2020, a frontier worker from Tirano had his bank account frozen due to a bureaucratic error that led to an amount of 1200 euros being seized. The worker had to wait over 6 months to resolve the situation and get the money back.

To avoid similar problems, border crossers should regularly check their taxes and insurance. In Switzerland, the Tax Act (LIE, LR 2, of 20 March 1988) stipulates that frontier workers must pay income taxes in Switzerland, regardless of their residence. The Insurance Act (LAS, LR 2, of 20 March 1988) stipulates that frontier workers must be insured for occupational risks in Switzerland.

What is the limit of cash to declare when going to Switzerland from Italy?
Current legislation provides that individuals who travel to Switzerland from Italy with more than 10,000 euros in cash must declare their arrival to the Italian Revenue Agency within 12 months of the date of arrival. Failure to comply with this rule may lead to complaints of fraudulent subtraction of taxes.
What are the penalties for not declaring money at the Switzerland-Italy border?
Those who do not declare their arrival in Switzerland with more than 10,000 euros can be reported for fraudulent subtraction from the payment of taxes. In addition to the seizure of money, as in the aforementioned case, the legislation provides that you may have to pay a fine of 10,000 euros for failure to comply.
How do the Italian and Swiss authorities cooperate in border controls for border guards?
The Italian Guardia di Finanza, like that of Tirano, works in close collaboration with the Swiss authorities, for example the Customs of Bellinzona, for border controls. They verify the identity, documentation and tax debts of passengers. In 2022, this collaboration led to the seizure of over 10 million euros in cash.
