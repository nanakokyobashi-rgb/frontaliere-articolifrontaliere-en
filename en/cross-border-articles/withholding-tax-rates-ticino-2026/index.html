quellensteuer is the tax on the source of earned income levied in Switzerland at the time of payment. For the Ticino-Italy frontier worker, this is the crucial moment: Switzerland withholds directly from the paycheck; Italy does not apply any withholding and subsequently recognizes the tax credit in declaration 730 (EC framework) to avoid double taxation, in accordance with the Italian-Swiss Convention of 9 March 1976.

The New Frontier Agreement, signed on December 23, 2020 and applied from January 1, 2024, introduced an annual deductible on employee income. This reduction applies to taxable income before the calculation of withholding tax.

Who is subject and what are the recognized rebates

All income from employment (wages, salaries, bonuses, allowances, bonuses) of a person working in Switzerland and residing in Italy are subject to quellensteuer. The withholding varies depending on the canton where you physically work. In the Canton of Ticino, the calculation takes place monthly on the basis of the gross salary net of specific subsidies:

The main subsidies recognized are: mandatory AHV/AI/IPG contributions (5.3% paid by the employee), payments to the second pillar BVG (rate 7-18% according to age group from 25 years), LAMAL health insurance premiums in employee share, and documented commuting expenses (public transport, fuel). The paycheck always shows the detail of each monthly rebate. If you notice discrepancies, first check that the annual deductible has been correctly applied by the employer.

Operational details

Calculation of the quellensteuer and mechanism of the deductible

The quellensteuer is calculated every month (or per pay period) on the basis of the gross salary, after deducting the recognized bonuses and, from the entry into force of the New Agreement (1 January 2024), the annual deductible divided into monthly instalments. Old cross-border workers (art. 9 of the Agreement) are taxed on their wages only in Switzerland; the IRPEF exemption of €10,000 is valid from 2024 for cross-border workers whose income is taxed in Italy.

The annual gross amount, the monthly bonuses, and the applied deductible appear in the Lohnausweis (annual pay slip document) that the employer delivers by February 28 of the following year. This document is essential for the Italian 730 declaration, because the Revenue Agency uses the total annual quellensteuer paid to calculate the tax credit.

The NOV threshold: CHF 120'000 and what changes beyond this amount

The acronym NOV stands for "Notation Ohne Voranmeldung" (communication without notice). This Swiss technical term indicates an income limit within which the withholding tax is considered definitive and does not require further formalities in the declaration. When a cross-border worker earns less than CHF 120,000 gross per year, the quellensteuer paid in Switzerland completely covers the tax due; in Italy, the cross-border worker simply applies the tax credit in the EC framework of 730 and, in most cases, receives a refund because Switzerland has already withdrawn the tax definitively.

If your income exceeds CHF 120,000, the situation changes. Italian IRPEF rates are progressive: 23% up to €28,000, 33% up to €50,000, 43% over €50,000. The Swiss quellensteuer, calculated on a different basis and with different rates, could be lower than the IRPEF payable in Italy on the total income (sum of Italian income, if any, and Swiss income). When making the 730 declaration, the cross-border worker will have to check whether there is any further Italian tax to be paid.

Example: a cross-border worker with CHF 150,000 gross Swiss tax (approximately €160,000 at the current exchange rate) may have paid a Swiss quellensteuer lower than the IRPEF due in Italy on that same income. The tax credit covers the Swiss withholding tax, but if the IRPEF is higher, the cross-border worker pays the difference to the Revenue Agency when making the return.

Key points

Practical procedure: how to verify withholdings and prepare the 730 declaration

Every cross-border worker should receive a monthly pay slip (Lohnausweis) detailing: gross, withholdings, allowances, contributions, and net. The first thing is to check that they are correct:

1. Declared gross: is it consistent with the contract? 2. Rebates: have AVS/AI/IPG (5.3%), LPP (7-18% depending on age), KVG, commuting expenses been recognised? 3. New Agreement Deductible: has it been applied? You should see a reduction of approximately €625 per month (€7,500 ÷ 12) or €833 per month (€10,000 ÷ 12) if you are an eligible cross-border commuter. 4. Net: Is this the correct difference between gross, withholdings and allowances?

If you notice any discrepancies, report it immediately to your employer's personnel department in Switzerland. If the problem persists, you can contact the AFC (Federal Tax Administration), which supervises the procedures at the Swiss federal level.

Italian declaration 730 and tax credit

Every year between May and June, the cross-border worker must complete the Italian declaration 730. In the EC framework (Income from other States), he declares the total income received in Switzerland during the previous year (you will find it in the December Lohnausweis) and the overall paid quellensteuer. The Revenue Agency automatically recognizes the tax credit to avoid double taxation.

The refund depends on the comparison between the amount paid in Switzerland and the amount due in Italy:

  • Refund: if the quellensteuer exceeds the Italian IRPEF due on the same income (frequent situation when the income is less than CHF 120,000).
  • Additional payment: if the quellensteuer is lower than the IRPEF (possible situation when the income exceeds CHF 120,000 or if you have other Italian incomes).
  • Even: if they coincide.

For those who remain below CHF 120,000, reimbursement is almost certain. For those who exceed this threshold, it depends on the marginal IRPEF rates applied to your overall income and the structure of the Italian progressive system.

Frequently Asked Questions
Is the quellensteuer retained even if I work for an Italian company but from Switzerland?
No. The quellensteuer applies when the place of work (source of income) is geographically in Switzerland. If your company is Italian and you work from home in Italy (also in smart working), the withholding is Italian, not Swiss. If, on the other hand, your company is Swiss and you work physically in Switzerland, or you have a contract with a Swiss employer who pays you from Switzerland even for remote work, then the quellensteuer is Swiss and Italy does not withhold anything. What matters is the location of the employer and/or the place of execution of the work.
What are the rate differences between Ticino, Graubünden, Valais and Bern in 2026?
The quellensteuer rates vary by canton and by income bracket. The Canton of Ticino has its own rates, as do Graubünden, Valais and Bern. In 2026, rates remain stable. For exact rates specific to the canton where you work, check the cantonal tax administration's website (e.g. DFF Ticino) or use Frontaliere Ticino's calculator for an automatic estimate based on your salary.
How do I know if I fall within the NOV threshold of CHF 120'000?
Check the annual gross total indicated in the December Lohnausweis (document you receive from the employer by February 28 of the following year). Add up all the monthly payments and any bonuses and bonuses. If the total is less than CHF 120'000, stay in the NOV and the Swiss quellensteuer is sufficient; in the case of 730 Italian you only apply the credit. If you exceed CHF 120'000, you will check on the 730 return if an additional Italian taxation emerges.
Who qualifies for the transitional regime in Article 9?
Article 9’s transitional regime applies only to workers who meet the Agreement’s definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between 31 December 2018 and 17 July 2023; covered employment income is taxable exclusively in Switzerland. The ordinary regime applies in other cases, with Italian taxation and credit for Swiss tax where applicable. The €10,000 in Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is only the limit for income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law 147/2013, not a general allowance. If I change employer during the year, how is the deductible calculated?
The annual deductible of €10,000 applies to the total calendar year, regardless of how many employers you have had. If you work for company A for 6 months and for company B for 6 months, the total deductible is divided proportionally between the two employers, or concentrated at the first: it depends on how the employers coordinate and on the procedures of the AFC. Clarify with the staff offices of both employers to avoid overlap or omissions.
