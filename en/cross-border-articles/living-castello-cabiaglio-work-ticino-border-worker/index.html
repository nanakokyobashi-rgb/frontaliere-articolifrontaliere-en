Castello Cabiaglio, in the province of Varese, has for years been a favorite destination for cross-border workers who choose to reside in Italy and commute to the Canton of Ticino. Its geometric proximity to the border crossings (Brogeda, Gaggiolo, Ponte Tresa) and a generally lower cost of living than Ticino make it logistically interesting. €10,000 for new ones) and further clarifying the coordination system between Swiss and Italian taxes.

For those who reside in Castello Cabiaglio and work as an employee in Ticino, a fundamental principle remains unchanged: the tax on income from work is withheld by Switzerland, from the employer. Italy, on the basis of the Italian-Swiss Convention of 9 March 1976, recognizes this primary taxation and grants a tax credit in the EC framework of the 730 declaration, thus avoiding double taxation. At the same time, the cross-border worker pays contributions to the Swiss AVS/AI/IPG system (5.3% paid by the employee) in addition to the LAINF premium for accidents (0.7–1.5%) and the LPP fund (private pension, 7–18% based on age).

Who is the category G cross-border worker?

The category G cross-border worker is a foreign worker who resides permanently in Italian territory (registered resident) and carries out employed work in Switzerland with a permanent or fixed-term contract. The status requires a work permit (G Permit) issued by the SEM (State Secretariat for Migration) through the canton of employment, subject to authorization and sponsorship from the Swiss employer.

Operational details

Concrete advantages of living in Castello Cabiaglio as a cross-border commuter

The proximity to the passes is the main advantage. A cross-border commuter who resides in Castello Cabiaglio, unlike those who choose more distant areas in the Varesotto or Lombardy, reduces the times and costs of daily commuting to the main Ticino employment hubs (Lugano, Bellinzona, Mendrisio, Chiasso). The cost of rent and housing utilities in the province of Varese generally remains lower than in the Canton of Ticino, allowing a greater portion of the Swiss gross salary to be preserved.

A further advantage, explicitly introduced by the New Cross-border Workers Agreement 2024, concerns the right of option regarding health insurance. G cross-border workers can choose to join the Swiss KVG (with adult deductibles of CHF 300–2,500 depending on the model) or opt for maintenance in the Italian National Health Service. This flexibility allows those permanently residing in Italy to continue to benefit from local medical care, potentially reducing healthcare costs.

Finally, those who work as cross-border workers in Switzerland benefit from salaries and benefit packages that are generally higher than in the Italian market, which largely offsets mobility costs and progressive Italian tax rates.

Disadvantages and hidden costs

Daily commuting, although feasible from the location of Castello Cabiaglio, involves concrete expenses: fuel, wear and tear on the vehicle, potential motorway tolls (A2, A9), parking in work municipalities, and time taken away from private life. A cross-border worker who discovers one morning that his monthly net earnings, after all the Swiss deductions and progressive Italian taxation, are reduced by a significant fraction when commuting is added.

The Italian IRPEF rate remains a second obstacle. The threshold of 10,000 euros of the art. 4 of Law 83/2023 concerns exclusively income from employment carried out abroad, in a border area or in a neighboring country, continuously and exclusively, by people resident in Italy, referred to in the art. 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013; it is not a general exemption nor an exemption from Swiss withholding tax. If a cross-border worker earns CHF 60,000 gross per year (approximately €59,000 in euros), the Italian taxable part after the exemption undergoes the full progression, generating a higher overall tax than it would appear at first sight.

Social security contributions and administrative obligations

A cross-border worker who works in Ticino must pay into the Swiss system:

  • AVS/AI/IPG: 5.3% paid by the employee
  • LAINF accident insurance: 0.7–1.5%
  • LPP (mandatory pension fund): 7–18% paid by the employer (the employee does not contribute directly, but the cost reflects on the negotiated salary)
  • Unemployment AD/AC: 1.1% (ceiling CHF 148'200)

These contributions are administered by the AFC (Federal Tax Administration) and the competent cantonal administrations. In parallel, Italy generally requires registration with INPS special management, even if the Italian-Swiss coordination introduced by the New Agreement aims to simplify the framework and reduce double payments.

Every tax year, the cross-border worker must complete the Italian 730 declaration, attaching Swiss tax documentation (tax certificate, final pay slip, any SVZ documents) and recovering the tax credit in the EC framework. Omitting or incorrectly declaring exposes you to controls and sanctions.

Key points

How to move to Castello Cabiaglio and obtain the border permit: step-by-step procedures

Phase 1: Preliminary documentation (before job search)

Before contacting employers in Ticino, prepare the basic documents:

  • Valid Italian passport or identity card
  • Curriculum vitae
  • Professional qualification certificates/diplomas, duly translated if necessary
  • CAIS certificate (Certificate of the history of services) from INPS, if you have already paid Italian contributions (useful for social security coordination)
  • Declaration of living capacity in Italy (rental contract, cadastral survey, letter from the owner)

Phase 2: Job offer and application for G permit at SEM

Having received the official offer from the Swiss employer, the latter (or possibly the worker with legal support) presents an application for a G permit at the SEM responsible for the Canton of Ticino. The question includes:

  • Signed employment contract or offer letter with start date
  • Declaration of need from the employer (certification that the role cannot be covered by a CH resident)
  • Valid identity document of the worker
  • Proof of Italian residence (registry extract, rental contract)

Processing times vary (on average from 2 to 6 weeks) depending on the SEM load. Once approved, you will receive your cross-border work permit.

Phase 3: Registration with the tax administration of the Canton of Ticino

After obtaining the permit, contact the cantonal tax office (AFC/ESTV at the federal level, administered by the canton) to register as an AVS insured. You will receive a Ticino AVS insured number, essential for making contributions and declarations.

Phase 4: Communication to the INPS database and Italian social security declaration

Notify INPS of your status as a cross-border employee in Switzerland. Depending on the competent management (Traders, Para-subordinates, Self-employed, etc.), minimal coordination contributions may be due. The New Deal 2024 aims to reduce these burdens, but it is essential to check with an accountant.

Step 5: Choice of health insurance (LAMal or Italian SSN option)

Within the required timeframe (usually within the first month of employment), communicate to your employer the choice between:

  • Swiss KVG (standard): deductibles CHF 300–2,500 per year for adults, full coverage in Switzerland, limited European coordination
  • Italian SSN option: permanence in the National Health Service, access to Italian care, but requires coordination with right of option recognized by the New Agreement

This choice has implications on costs, preventative coverage, and access to specialist facilities.

Phase 6: First Italian 730 declaration (subsequent tax year)

Submitted in May/June of the following year, the 730 declaration must include:

  • All income from employment in Switzerland (gross amount)
  • Swiss tax certificate (tax document of the cantonal SVZ or of the employer)
  • Use of the CE framework to declare the Swiss tax paid, obtaining the tax credit
  • The threshold of 10,000 euros is not a general exemption: art. 4 of Law 83/2023 limits it to the income referred to in the art. 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013.

The tax credit reduces the final Italian tax. If calculated correctly, the overall taxation (Swiss + Italian coordinated) is in line with the intentions of the Italian-Swiss Convention of 9 March 1976.

Frequently Asked Questions
Which income does the €10,000 limit apply to?
Article 9’s transitional regime applies only to workers who meet the Agreement’s definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between 31 December 2018 and 17 July 2023; covered employment income is taxable exclusively in Switzerland. The ordinary regime applies in other cases, with Italian taxation and credit for Swiss tax where applicable. The €10,000 in Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is only the limit for income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law 147/2013, not a general allowance. FTA factsheet · Agreement, Article 9 · Normattiva, Article 4.
Where is income tax calculated and paid for a border worker living in Castello Cabiaglio?
Income tax is withheld in Switzerland by the employer (Swiss source tax). Italy, on the basis of the Italian-Swiss Convention of 9 March 1976, recognises this primary taxation and does not apply further tax on the income of employees of Switzerland. In the EC framework of the Italian declaration 730, the border worker declares the Swiss tax paid and obtains it as a tax credit, eliminating the risk of double withdrawal.
What are the social security contributions that a border worker pays in Ticino?
A border worker employed in Ticino pays: (i) AVS/AI/IPG 5.3% paid by the employee; (ii) LAINF (accidents) 0.7–1.5%; (iii) LPP (mandatory pension fund) 7–18% paid by the employer based on age (from 25 years); (iv) AD/AC (unemployment) 1.1% up to a maximum of CHF 148,200. These contributions are administered by the AFC (Federal Contributions Administration) and the competent cantonal administrations.
Can I choose to remain in the Italian National Health Service instead of subscribing to LAMal?
Yes. The New Frontier Agreement 2024 recognizes the right of option for G frontiersmen: you can keep the Italian medical insurance (SSN) or join the Swiss LAMal (with adult deductibles CHF 300-2,500 depending on the model). This choice must be communicated to the employer within the scheduled time and has implications for costs and access to care.
How does the Italian tax credit work if I work in Switzerland and already pay taxes there?
The Italian-Swiss Double Taxation Convention (signed on 9 March 1976) states that income from employment in Switzerland is taxed exclusively in the State where you work. In the EC framework of the Italian declaration 730, you declare the amount of the Swiss tax paid and obtain it as a tax credit. If the Swiss tax is higher than the Italian one calculated on the same income, you do not pay additional taxes in Italy.
