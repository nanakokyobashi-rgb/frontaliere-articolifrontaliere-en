Choosing to live in Montescheno, a Lombardy municipality on the border with Ticino, and work in Ticino is a practical choice for those seeking a balance between the Swiss labor market and lower residential costs than in Switzerland. But what does it mean to be a cross-border worker in this geographical situation? From taxation to administrative procedures, the rules changed with the New Agreement on Cross-Border Workers, applied as of January 1, 2024, introducing new rights and specific obligations.

Montescheno: a choice at the border

Montescheno is strategically located in relation to the main centers in Ticino. Those who work in Lugano, Mendrisio, Bellinzona or in the Riviera commute daily through crossings such as Brogeda, Gaggiolo and other border crossing points in the area. The geographical proximity to Ticino makes daily commuting easier, but it remains essential to understand which tax and social-security benefits arise from the current regulatory framework.

The G permit (cross-border commuter) is the legal instrument that enables this labor mobility. As for the transitional regime under Article 9 of the Agreement, it applies only to those who meet the treaty definition and have worked in the Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between December 31, 2018 and July 17, 2023; the covered employment income is taxed exclusively in Switzerland. In other cases, the ordinary regime applies, with Italian taxation and a credit for Swiss tax. The 10.000 euro amount under Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is merely the limit for the income referred to in Article 1, paragraph 175, Law 147/2013, not a general tax-free allowance. This two-speed structure reflects the New Agreement's intention to distinguish between workers with established experience in cross-border commuting and new entrants to the cross-border market.

Operational details

For the cross-border worker resident in Montescheno and employed in Ticino, taxation is one of the central issues in the choice of transfer. Italy applies the right to tax the income of its residents, but the Italian-Swiss Convention (9 March 1976) and the New Agreement define how this right is limited and coordinated with the Swiss system, avoiding double taxation.

How taxation works in the border regime

Withholding tax on earned income is withheld in Switzerland by the Ticino employer according to the applicable federal and cantonal rates. Italy does not levy a parallel tax, thus avoiding double taxation and ensuring that the net income received is not eroded by two simultaneous levies. Those who receive a Swiss salary see the withholding tax carried out directly from their paycheck on a monthly basis.

The income, once declared in Italy through the EC framework of the 730 form, benefits from the tax credit on Swiss payments already made. This mechanism guarantees that you do not pay the tax twice in the same tax year, with the difference between the amount paid in Switzerland and the Italian rate (if higher) automatically regulated by the Revenue Agency.

Mandatory social security: AVS and LPP

The Swiss social security system is mandatory for anyone who works in Switzerland. A cross-border worker based in Montescheno must register for AVS (Old Age, Survivors and Disability Insurance) with a contribution rate of 5.3% paid by the employee, integrated by the employer. This contribution increases pension rights at the Swiss institution, regardless of Italian residence.

The second pillar, the occupational pension (LPP), is equally mandatory for salaries above a minimum threshold. The contribution rate varies by age group, with a range from 7% to 18% starting from the age of 25. These payments build a supplementary income that integrates the basic AVS pension.

Health insurance: right of option

Health insurance (KVG) is mandatory in Switzerland. Even though they reside in Italy, cross-border commuters have the right to opt for Swiss coverage with deductibles for adults ranging from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 per year. Alternatively, those who choose to maintain Italian insurance must explicitly check the coverage in case of medical needs in the Ticino area, to avoid surprises in the reimbursement.

Key points

The transition from an Italian resident to a Ticino cross-border worker requires a series of specific and sequential obligations. From the formal request for the G Permit to the correct annual tax declaration, each phase has precise terms and well-defined responsibilities.

G Permit request procedure

The G Permit must be requested from the Ticino cantonal authorities (Canton Migration Section) with the active support of the employer. The latter must submit a notification to the cantonal employment office indicating the hiring of the cross-border worker. The process typically ends within 5-10 working days, barring administrative complications or additional checks requested by the authorities.

Documentation normally required: formal letter of job offer, copy of valid Italian identity document, proof of residence in Montescheno (utility bill, rental or sale contract, registry certificate). Once the Permit is approved, the initial duration is normally one year, with the possibility of renewal for up to five consecutive years.

Tax return and annual deductible

In the first year of work in Ticino, the cross-border worker declares the income received in Switzerland to the Italian tax authorities. Form 730 (or Unico if you earn from self-employment) must report in the EC framework the income from foreign employment and the tax amounts already paid in Switzerland. The Revenue Agency automatically applies the tax credit, amortizing the Swiss tax against the Italian tax potentially due.

The threshold of 10,000 euros of the art. 4 of Law 83/2023 concerns exclusively income from employment carried out abroad, in a border area or in a neighboring country, continuously and exclusively, by people resident in Italy, referred to in the art. 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013; it is not a general exemption nor an exemption from Swiss withholding tax. Once the annual threshold is exceeded, the excess is subject to Italian taxation according to ordinary rates (23%, 33%, 43% based on total income).

Frequently Asked Questions
What does it mean to live in Montescheno and work in Ticino as a border worker?
It means residing as an Italian citizen in Montescheno and working with a Ticino Swiss employer with a local contract. It applies for Permit G and subjects the income to a double tax regime (Italian and Swiss) coordinated by the Italian-Swiss Convention (9 March 1976) and the New Agreement applied since 1 January 2024.
Who qualifies for the transitional regime in Article 9?
Article 9’s transitional regime applies only to workers who meet the Agreement’s definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between 31 December 2018 and 17 July 2023; covered employment income is taxable exclusively in Switzerland. The ordinary regime applies in other cases, with Italian taxation and credit for Swiss tax where applicable. The €10,000 in Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is only the limit for income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law 147/2013, not a general allowance. What Permit do you need to work in Ticino while living in Montescheno?
Permit G (border). It must be requested from the Ticino cantonal authorities with the support of the employer. The procedure takes about 5-10 working days. Initial duration: one year, renewable up to five years thereafter.
Do I need to register with the Swiss AVS if I live in Italy?
Yes. Those who work in Switzerland are compulsorily enrolled in the AVS (Old Age, Survivors, Disability Insurance) with a contribution rate of 5.3% to be paid by the employee. This increases Swiss pension rights regardless of residence. The second pillar (LPP) is also mandatory with a rate of 7-18%.
Can I choose Swiss health insurance (LAMal) from Montescheno?
Yes. As a frontier worker residing in Italy you have the right of option for a Swiss coverage (LAMal) with excess from CHF 300 to CHF 2500 per year. Alternatively, you can keep Italian insurance by checking cross-border coverage and reimbursement in Ticino.
