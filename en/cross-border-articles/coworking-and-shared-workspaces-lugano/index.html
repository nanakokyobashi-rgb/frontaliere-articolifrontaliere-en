The use of shared workspaces in Lugano represents a flexible solution for professionals working in Ticino, including cross-border workers. Managing one's own activity requires a clear understanding of the current regulatory framework. The New Cross-Border Agreement, signed on December 23, 2020, has been fully operational since January 1, 2024, following Italy's ratification through Law 83 of June 13, 2023. For those considering teleworking or using shared hubs, it is essential to bear in mind that Switzerland is NOT an EU/EEA member, a factor that influences mobility dynamics and administrative management. Bodies such as SECO, SEM, USTAT and the Federal Tax Administration (AFC/ESTV) define the framework within which to operate. The structure of Swiss contributions is precisely defined: the employee pays 5,3% for AVS/AI/IPG and 1,1% for AD/AC, with a maximum reference ceiling of CHF 148'200. Additional charges include LAINF (0,7–1,5%) and LPP (7–18% based on age bracket, starting at age 25). Unlike what happens in other contexts, withholding tax is deducted in Switzerland, while Italy applies a tax credit to avoid double taxation through section CE of form 730.

Regulatory considerations

The distinction between old and new cross-border workers is fundamental. Old cross-border workers remain taxed only in Switzerland (art. 9 of the 2020 Agreement). For the purposes of art. 9 of the Agreement, “old cross-border workers” are people who met the tax requirements and who worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais on July 17, 2023 or during the period from December 31, 2018 to July 17, 2023. For employment income covered by art. 9, taxation remains exclusively at source in Switzerland; for other workers, the ordinary regime applies, with taxation in Italy, a credit for Swiss tax and Swiss withholding at 80% of ordinary rates. The 10.000 euros under art. 4 of Law 83/2023 are merely the income threshold referred to in art. 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013 and not a general tax-free allowance for new cross-border workers. The Italian IRPEF rates applied to income received are 23% up to €28'000, 33% on the portion between €28'001 and €50'000 and 43% on the portion exceeding €50'000.

Operational details

The adoption of shared work spaces allows you to optimize productivity, but requires attention to social security and tax implications. KVG, or health insurance, is mandatory for those who work in Switzerland, with the right of option for cross-border workers G and deductibles for adults between CHF 300 and CHF 2,500. It is important to remember that tax rates and contributions, including those for pensions, are established by federal or cantonal laws and managed by the AFC/ESTV, not by the UFAS or the BFS, which is exclusively responsible for collecting statistical data. For those who operate in coworking, the stability of the connection and the availability of network services are standard, but the management of tax residence remains a crucial point. The Convention for the avoidance of double taxation between Italy and Switzerland, signed on 9 March 1976, remains the pillar of reference. The transition to working remotely or in shared spaces does not exempt the worker from compliance with the procedures established for withholding tax. A cross-border worker who uses a shared office in Lugano must ensure that his position is in line with the employer, especially regarding the tax and social security limits linked to teleworking. Correct management of the EC framework in 730 allows you to avoid double taxation, making the tax credit the main tool to compensate for what has already been paid in Swiss territory. Compliance with INPS and the Revenue Agency as regards the Italian part, combined with correct contributions to Swiss funds (AVS/AI/IPG), guarantees the protection of pension rights. The use of shared spaces does not change the nature of the employment contract, but facilitates compliance with the presence requirements established by the employer, facilitating commuting to passes such as Brogeda or Gaggiolo. Frequently Asked Questions
What are the contribution rates for an employee in Ticino?
Compulsory Swiss contributions include 5.3% for AVS/AI/IPG and 1.1% for AD/AC (with a cap on salary of CHF 148'200). The LAINF varies between 0.7% and 1.5%, while the LPP varies from 7% to 18% depending on the age group, with an obligation to contribute from 25 years of age.
How is double taxation for border workers avoided?
The tax is withheld at source in Switzerland. To avoid double taxation, Italy recognizes a tax credit that must be declared in the EC framework of model 730. It is essential to keep all documentation relating to taxes paid in Switzerland.
What changes with the New Frontier Agreement of 2024?
The New Agreement, signed on 23 December 2020, is applied since 1 January 2024. For Article 9 of the Agreement, “old frontier workers” are people who met the tax requirements and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais on 17 July 2023 or during the period from 31 December 2018 through 17 July 2023. Article 9 keeps covered employment income taxable exclusively at source in Switzerland; other workers follow the ordinary regime, with ordinary taxation in Italy, a credit for Swiss tax and Swiss withholding at 80% of the ordinary rates. The €10,000 figure in Article 4 of Italian Law No. 83/2023 is only the income limit referred to in Article 1(175) of Law No. 147/2013; it is not a general allowance for new frontier workers. Sources: ESTV factsheet (Italian) · Agreement, Art. 9 · Law No. 83/2023, Art. 4.
