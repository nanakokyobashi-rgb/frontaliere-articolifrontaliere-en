Fire in Asso: double-rope maneuver in the gorge

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 4 October 2026

Vegetation fire in Asso: Firefighters and SAF personnel operated in a gorge. Approximately 100 square meters were damaged.

Context

In brief

  • Vegetation fire in Asso around 3 p.m.
  • Firefighters from Canzo, Cantù and Como in action.
  • SAF reached the gorge using a double-rope maneuver.
  • Approximately 100 square meters of vegetation damaged.

Key facts

  • What → vegetation fire
  • Where → Asso
  • When → around 3 p.m.
  • Crews → Canzo, Cantù and SAF specialists from Como
  • Vehicles → fire engine, wildland firefighting pickup truck and water tanker
  • Damaged area → approximately 100 square meters

The rescue operation was carried out today in Asso around 3 p.m. for a vegetation fire that was developing in a gorge. The Firefighters' crews from Canzo operated with a fire engine and a wildland firefighting pickup truck. Their colleagues from Cantù brought a water tanker, while SAF specialists, Speleo Alpine River Rescue, arrived from Como.

The rugged terrain made operations particularly difficult. The flames were spreading rapidly inside the gorge, making it necessary to reach even the most difficult areas. To fight the fire, SAF personnel carried out a complex and risky double-rope maneuver. The specialists thus reached the most inaccessible areas and transported the equipment needed for extinguishing the fire.

The presence of crews from Canzo and Cantù, together with the SAF specialists from Como, made it possible to tackle the fire with the vehicles indicated in the operation. The reported damage to the vegetation amounts to approximately 100 square meters.

Operational details

A difficult-to-reach gorge

The most delicate aspect of the operation was the combination of the terrain's configuration and the speed at which the flames were spreading. The terrain is described as rugged, and the fire was advancing into a gorge. For this reason, the operations were not limited to using the vehicles that had arrived on site: it was necessary to reach the most difficult points and bring the equipment needed to extinguish the fire there.

This task was carried out by the SAF specialists, namely the Speleo Alpino Fluviale personnel who arrived from Como. The double-rope maneuver was described as complex and risky. It made it possible to reach the most rugged areas and transport the equipment needed to extinguish the fire, right into the area where the flames were advancing.

The emergency response also included the firefighter teams from Canzo, with a fire engine and a wildland firefighting pickup, and their colleagues from Cantù, who were there with a water tanker. The joint effort supported the rope specialists' maneuver and enabled the operation to reach the most difficult points in the gorge.

The source therefore describes an operation built around two immediate needs: reaching the affected vegetation and having the equipment needed to extinguish the flames. Both were addressed in the same operation.

Key points

Fire suppression and mop-up

Thanks to the joint efforts of the teams, the flames were extinguished. After the fire was extinguished, the affected area was mopped up: this is the next step documented in the operation in Asso.

The mop-up prevented the fire from spreading further. This result followed the difficulties posed by the terrain and the gorge, where the flames were spreading rapidly and where the SAF specialists had to reach the most rugged areas using a double-rope maneuver.

The final figure reported by the source concerns the extent of the damage: only approximately 100 square meters of vegetation were damaged. The affected area was therefore subjected first to fire suppression and then to mop-up, with further spread halted after the joint operation.

The work on site concluded with the mop-up of the affected area, after the teams from Canzo and Cantù and the SAF specialists from Como had brought the flames under control. The final tally given is limited to approximately 100 square meters of damaged vegetation.
