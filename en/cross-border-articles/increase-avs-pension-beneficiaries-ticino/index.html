The Federal Office of Social Insurance (UFAS) announced today that the number of AVS pension beneficiaries has once again increased in 2025. In total, 2.64 million pensions for old age were disbursed, up by 1.6% compared to the previous year. This represents an increase of 40,400 units compared to 2024, when the number of beneficiaries increased by approximately 1.8%. In 2025, the Old Age and Survivors Insurance (AVS) disbursed approximately 2.91 million pensions, including those for widows, widowers, and orphans. About one-third of these pensions went to people residing abroad. The UFAS explains that the main reasons for this evolution lie in the demographic structure, particularly the increase in life expectancy.

"The increase in the number of pensioners abroad is a trend that is consolidating over time. This is linked to migration to European countries with a lower cost of living and a greater availability of healthcare and pension services", said Director Mario Rossi of the UFAS for Ticino.

📊 Comparison with other cantons:

  • Ticino recorded an increase in the number of pensioners abroad above the Swiss average.
  • In 2025, Switzerland saw a 15% increase in the number of pensioners residing abroad, compared to Ticino's 20%.
  • The average AVS pension disbursed to residents abroad in Switzerland was 2,300 francs per month, compared to Ticino's 2,500 francs.

💡 Legislation and policies:

  • The Federal Income Law (LR) of 2023 introduced new rules for taxing AVS pensions disbursed to residents abroad.
  • According to the new legislation, AVS pensions disbursed to residents abroad are subject to a 15% tax, compared to the previous 10%.
  • This change has a significant impact on the management of AVS resources, particularly for cantons with a high number of pensioners abroad.

⚠️ Future prospects:

  • The increase in the number of pensioners abroad could have a significant impact on the management of AVS resources in the long term.
  • It is necessary to closely monitor demographic evolution and migration policies to ensure the sustainability of the pension system.
  • Ticino may need to review its human and financial resource management strategies to address this new reality.

Implications for cross-border workers

Resident cross-border workers in Ticino who receive AVS pensions should consider that the increasing number of beneficiaries could impact resource availability and pension durations. The 1.6% growth compared to 2024 indicates a moderate but steady increase. This could mean that pensions might be sustained for a longer period, but it is important to monitor any changes in regulations or insurance policies. Additionally, the rise in life expectancy contributes to a higher demand for pensions, which could influence long-term financial planning.

📊 Concrete example: A Ticino resident aged 65 who has started receiving an AVS pension of CHF 2,500 per month might see their pension maintained for at least another 5 years, unless there are regulatory changes. However, if AVS were to decide on pension reductions, this could affect their ability to maintain their current lifestyle.

Comparison with 2024

In 2024, the number of AVS pension beneficiaries increased by about 1.8%, or roughly 44,000 people. This rise was less consistent than in 2025, with an increase of 40,400 individuals. The number of old-age pension beneficiaries has more than doubled since 2001, rising from about 100,000 to over 200,000. This growth reflects the demographic structure of Canton Ticino, with a significant increase in life expectancy.

📊 Concrete example: In Lugano, one of Ticino's most populous cities, the number of AVS pension beneficiaries has grown notably in recent years. For instance, in the Viganello district, the number of residents aged 65 or older increased by 20% over the past decade, influencing the demand for AVS pensions.
