Historical Museum at Palazzo Civico: cross-party motion in Lugano

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 3 October 2026

Ten city councillors ask to evaluate a historical museum on the second floor of Palazzo Civico, which will be liberated in 2028 with the move to Cornaredo.

Context

  • Ten municipal councillors support the request

Ten municipal councillors in Lugano are asking for the creation of a historical museum in the spaces of Palazzo Civico. The motion is cross-party and its first signatory is Danilo Baratti, of Verdi e Indipendenti. The building houses the municipal chancellery, the wedding hall, the municipal councillors’ offices and the Municipal Council chamber.

The starting point is the transfer, planned in a few years’ time, of part of the city administration to Cornaredo, in the East Tower of the Polo sportivo e degli eventi, the Pse. The motion focuses in particular on the second floor, which would be vacated by the relocation of the Municipal Administration offices planned for 2028. The parliamentary motion calls for the future of those spaces not to be left undecided.

According to the proponents, the intended use of the spaces should be discussed before any decisions are made about their use. The text points to the risk that, in the absence of preliminary consideration of a public use, the City could end up granting them to the first commercial bidder. The motion also links this risk to the need to cover the substantial rent of the East Tower.

A decision about the building’s future

The document defines Palazzo Civico as a public building with strong symbolic value and calls for a debate to be opened on its future. The request concerns the use of some of the spaces that could become available, not the abandonment of the entire building. Lugano Deputy Mayor Roberto Badaracco has in fact stated that the building will not be decommissioned.

According to the position expressed by the deputy mayor, Palazzo Civico will remain a landmark and an institutional seat of the City. For example, sessions of the legislature will continue to be held in the building. The motion therefore forms part of the discussion on how to pair this institutional function with a public and cultural use of any spaces that may become available.

However, the administrative step is not presented as already completed. The proposal speaks of a future relocation and spaces that would be vacated; the museum is presented as an option to be considered. The discussion is thus linked to two elements: the relocation to Cornaredo and the determination of the use of the second floor.

Key points

The follow-up and issues still open

For the time being, the motion opens an assessment but does not establish the launch of a museum. Roberto Badaracco said he was willing to examine the proposal further. He did, however, recall that the transfer of some of the staff currently working at Palazzo Civico has not yet been decided. This leaves it to be determined which rooms will actually be available and when, without turning the plan indicated in the motion into an already operational certainty.

The timing of the Pse also remains to be verified. The parliamentary motion links the relocation of the second-floor offices to 2028, but the deputy mayor pointed out that it is not certain that the Pse towers will already have been built in two years. The possibility of freeing up the spaces therefore depends on a transfer to Cornaredo and on the availability of the East Tower, elements that the source presents as still requiring verification.

Archives, storage facilities and management

If the planned relocation were to take place, according to the motion the second floor would make it possible to exhibit other objects currently kept in archives and storage facilities that are not easily accessible to the public. This reference broadens the proposal beyond the works and objects already visible in the corridors. The proposed historical museum would therefore also potentially contain materials that are not currently on display, if the vacated spaces were assigned this function.

However, the question of management remains open. Badaracco warned: «A permanent museum would entail the presence of a permanent curator». The presence of a curator is one of the points to be considered in the assessment.

The follow-up indicated by the motion is therefore a debate on the future of Palazzo Civico and a closer examination of the proposal. No definitive decision on the exhibition setup is reported: on the one hand, the transfer of the staff is still uncertain; on the other, the timing of the Pse towers remains to be verified. The discussion will have to bring together the question of the building’s cultural purpose and the functions already present in the building. The next step documented by the source therefore remains an assessment, while the spaces, timing and arrangements for any potential museum are still under discussion.

Frequently Asked Questions
Who submitted the motion for a historical museum at Palazzo Civico?
The motion was presented by ten municipal councillors of Lugano, with first signatory Danilo Baratti of the Verdi e Indipendenti group. The act is cross-party and asks to evaluate the setting up of a historical museum in the spaces that will be available on the second floor of the building.
When would the spaces for the possible museum be freed up?
According to the motion, the second floor of the Civic Palace would be liberated in 2028, in conjunction with the transfer of part of the municipal administration to the East Tower of the Sports and Events Centre (PSE) in Cornaredo. However, Deputy Mayor Badaracco specified that the transfer has not yet been decided and the timing of the PES remains to be verified.
What are the historical precedents mentioned in the motion?
The motion recalls that in Lugano there was a historical-archaeological museum between 1906 and 1963, housed first in the Palazzo degli Studi and then in Villa Ciani. Also mentioned is the digital experience on the reconstruction of the Castle of Trevano and the photographic exhibition "Lugano Belle Époque" currently set up in the Civic Palace and extended until January 8 of next year.
What are the conditions for building the museum?
Deputy Mayor Roberto Badaracco said he was willing to deepen the proposal, but pointed out that a permanent museum would require the presence of a permanent curator. In addition, the availability of the spaces depends on the transfer of the offices to Cornaredo and the construction of the East tower of the PSE, elements that are still uncertain. The Civic Palace will in any case remain the institutional headquarters and will not be decommissioned.
