On January 1, 2024, the new agreement between Switzerland and Italy on the taxation of border crossers entered into force, transforming the tax regime for those who reside in the Italian border territory and work on Swiss territory, in particular in the Canton of Ticino. Those who move to Ferrera di Varese to access Ticino jobs will face the daily commute through cross-border crossings (such as Brogeda or other entrances to the Canton of Ticino), a practical aspect that is accompanied by significant tax benefits introduced by the agreement signed on December 23, 2020 and ratified by Italy through Law 83 of June 13, 2023.

What changes for border crossers from 2024

The previous regime provided for double taxation managed through the Italian tax credit. Now, with the new agreement, Switzerland applies a withholding tax directly on wages: this withholding tax is withheld ONLY by the Swiss authorities, while Italy avoids applying it further when filing tax returns, recognizing a tax credit under EC model 730. For the frontier, it means simplified tax management and a reduction in the administrative burden at the declarative stage.

Border crossers are divided into two categories according to the date of commencement of cross-border activity. The transitional regime of Article 9 of the Agreement applies only to those who meet the conventional definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between 31 December 2018 and 17 July 2023; the covered employment income is taxed exclusively in Switzerland. In other cases, the ordinary regime applies, with Italian taxation and credit for Swiss tax. The 10,000 euros of Article 4 L. 83/2023 are only the limit for income referred to in Article 1, paragraph 175, L. 147/2013, not a general deductible. Operational details

Payroll and Tax return implications

Swiss social contributions (AHV, AI, IPG) are paid by the employee at a rate of 5.3% on the taxable base. These include unemployment insurance (AD/AC) contributions of 1.1% (with a ceiling of CHF 148,200), the Accident Insurance Act (LAINF) between 0.7% and 1.5%, and the Occupational Pension Act (BVG), which ranges from 7% to 18% according to the age group from 25. Although these contributions are supported by the worker, the Italian frontier worker benefits from easier management thanks to the withholding tax applied in Switzerland.

In Italy, the frontier worker must still submit the tax return (model 730 or model Incomes). In the EC framework of 730, it declares the income earned in Switzerland and the tax credit for the tax already paid in Switzerland, thus avoiding a new Italian tax. Key points

Health insurance (KVG) for cross-border commuters

Cross-border commuters with Permit G have the right to choose between Swiss health insurance (LAMal) and ordinary Italian insurance through INPS. The Swiss KVG offers coverage with adult deductibles between CHF 300 and CHF 2500, while Italian insurance has a different framework. This choice must be made consciously because it impacts annual healthcare costs and treatment coverage in both countries. The option must be communicated to the competent authorities at the time of requesting the G Permit.

Annual tax deadlines and INPS checks

The Italian tax return must be submitted by May 31st of the year following the tax year (or following any extension deadlines established). The INPS, as regards the insurance position, recognizes Swiss AVS/AI payments through the bilateral Convention of 9 March 1976: coordination is automatic once the cross-border worker is regularly registered with the competent Swiss authorities. However, it is advisable to check your social security position with INPS annually to avoid contribution gaps that could penalize the calculation of your future pension.

Is it true that only Switzerland pays the tax at source, not Italy?
Yes, exactly. Under the new agreement, the tax at source is only withheld by the Swiss authorities. Italy DOES NOT apply a second withholding tax on the declared tax base. However, Italy still applies ordinary personal income tax, recognizing the tax credit for what was paid in Switzerland in the EC framework of 730. For this reason, it is important to correctly declare both the gross income and the Swiss tax paid in the Italian declaration.
What are the Swiss social contributions that keep me off my paycheck?
The main ones are: AVS/AI/IPG (5.3%), AD/AC Unemployment Insurance (1.1% with a ceiling of CHF 148,200), LAINF accident insurance law (0.7–1.5%), and LPP occupational pension law (7–18% according to age group, from 25 years). Italian IRPEF applies in stages: 23% up to €28,000, 33% from €28,001-€50,000, 43% above €50,000. The exact amount depends on your gross income and age group; use the calculator for an accurate estimate.
Do I have to have the G Permit to be recognized as a border worker?
Yes. The G Permit is the border work permit issued by the SEM (Secretariat of State for Migration) and managed by the Canton of Ticino through the Department of Justice. The Swiss employer submits the request to the competent authorities. Once obtained, you are officially cross-border and access the tax and social security regime described by the 2024 agreement. Without Permit G, your status is different (it could be Permit B for temporary employees), with different tax implications.
