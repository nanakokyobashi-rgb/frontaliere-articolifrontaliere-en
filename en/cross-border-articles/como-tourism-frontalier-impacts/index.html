Tourism in Como is experiencing an unprecedented growth period, with a 25% increase in arrivals in 2023 compared to 2022, according to data from the Como-Lecco Chamber of Commerce. However, this expansion does not seem to bring benefits to peripheral districts like Camnago and Albate, where unemployment rates stand at 12%, against 7% in the city center. Tomorrow evening, Monday, May 4 at 20:30, the Yacht Club of Como will host a public meeting dedicated to the topic "Tourism and Peripheries," one of the strategic nodes for the present and future of the territory. The event is organized by Fratelli d'Italia and will focus on a crucial theme: on the one hand, unprecedented tourism growth, on the other, increasingly struggling districts, often excluded from economic benefits and penalized in daily dynamics.

Alessandro Nardone, City Coordinator of Fratelli d'Italia, will open and moderate the meeting. Speakers will include Fabio Dadati, entrepreneur in the tourism-hospitality sector and member of the Chamber of Commerce of Como-Lecco with delegation to tourism and culture, Pasquale Diodato, President CNA of Lario and Brianza, Claudio Casartelli, President Confesercenti Como, and Matteo Planta, President Nautica Lago di Como Scarl. Stefano Molinari, Provincial President of Fratelli d'Italia, will conclude.

Particular relevance is given to the presence of category associations, protagonists of the local economic system, whose contribution represents a central element to address the challenges related to tourism and commerce in a concrete way. During the evening, representatives of the districts will also intervene, bringing to attention problems and points of view often overlooked but fundamental to understanding the real impact of the transformations underway on the daily life of the people of Como.

Comparison with Ticino scenarios

In Ticino, municipalities like Lugano and Chiasso have implemented specific regulations to manage tourism and cross-border work. For example, Lugano has introduced a system of residence permits for cross-border workers, with a maximum cap of 30% of foreign workers in the tourism sector. This has allowed balancing economic growth with the quality of life of residents. Moreover, Chiasso has launched urban regeneration projects to enhance the peripheries, with an investment of 5 million Swiss francs in 2023.

In parallel, Fratelli d'Italia has prepared a public survey, already online at the address comoturistica.it, through which citizens can express opinions, report critical issues, and propose solutions on the relationship between tourism and quality of life in the districts. The survey is a fundamental tool to collect concrete data and develop targeted policies.

Key points

Unprecedented Tourism in Como: Struggling Neighborhoods and Excluded Cross-Border Workers

For cross-border workers living and working in Como, the meeting at the Yacht Club represents an opportunity to better understand how tourism is affecting the city and what the challenges and opportunities are for the future. Participating in the meeting can be a way to express your opinions and contribute to developing solutions that improve the quality of life for all citizens.

Impact of Tourism in Como

Tourism in Como has reached unprecedented levels, with over 1.5 million visitors per year. This increase has led to a growing impact on residential neighborhoods, particularly those near the lake. For example, in the Camerlata neighborhood, rents have increased by 20% in the last two years, making it difficult for local residents to find affordable housing.

Comparison with Ticino

In comparison, Ticinese municipalities like Lugano and Mendrisio have implemented policies to balance tourism with the needs of residents. In Lugano, for example, a 2.5% tourist tax has been introduced, which is reinvested in public services. Additionally, the canton has stipulated that 30% of new housing must be allocated to local residents.

Regulations and Key Dates

In Italy, Law No. 13 of 2017 introduced regulations for short-term rentals, but in Como, enforcement has been slow. The city is now considering following Milan's example, where a 3% tourist tax and a 90-day limit per year for short-term rentals have been introduced.

Concrete Examples

In Como, the San Giovanni neighborhood has seen a 15% increase in vehicular traffic due to tourism, with resulting parking and pollution problems. In contrast, in Mendrisio, traffic has been reduced by 10% thanks to sustainable mobility policies.

Comparisons Between Practical Scenarios

If Como were to adopt a tourist tax similar to Lugano's, it could generate approximately 3.75 million euros per year, which could be used to improve public services and housing for residents. Additionally, by limiting short-term rentals as in Milan, pressure on the local rental market could be reduced.
