Low wages and poverty risk in Ticino: PLR questions the State Council

The Liberal Radical Party (PLR) of Ticino has presented an inquiry to the State Council consisting of eight questions, aiming to clarify how innovation and attractiveness can contribute to improving the economic situation of low-income workers and reducing the poverty risk in the region. This initiative arises from the fact that Ticino has worse levels of population at risk of poverty or social exclusion compared to several nearby European regions.

According to Eurostat data, in 2022 Ticino recorded a rate of population at risk of poverty or social exclusion (AROPE) of 17.1%, higher than the Swiss average (14.3%) and that of regions such as Lombardy (13.4%) and Piedmont (14.1%). This socio-economic situation deserves particular attention, especially considering that Ticino is the only Swiss region included in Eurostat statistics (AROPE).

  • One concrete example of the critical situation is the municipality of Lugano, where 22.2% of the population lives in conditions of poverty or social exclusion.
  • In Locarno, the rate drops to 19.1%, while in Bellinzona, it is 18.5%.

These numbers highlight the need for targeted interventions to support low-income workers and improve their economic situation.

The PLR asks the State Council to provide detailed data on low wages in Ticino and the number of workers at risk of falling into poverty. The party also wants to know what concrete measures the State Council intends to adopt to incentivize innovation and increase the attractiveness of the Ticino job market.

💡 Among the proposals advanced by the PLR are:

  • the introduction of tax incentives for companies that hire low-income workers;
  • the creation of vocational training and requalification programs;
  • increased support for families with dependent children.

⚠️ However, it is necessary to consider that the Swiss minimum wage regulation, which came into effect on January 1, 2021, provides for an amount of 4,000 Swiss francs gross per month. However, many Ticino workers continue to receive wages lower than this amount.

📊 Here is an operational checklist of the actions that the PLR asks the State Council:

  • By 2025, reduce by 10% the number of workers at risk of poverty;
  • Increase by 15% the number of companies that hire low-income workers;
  • Create at least 500 new jobs in the services sector.

In conclusion, the socio-economic situation in Ticino requires particular attention and targeted interventions to support low-income workers. The PLR hopes that the State Council will provide concrete and timely answers to its questions and commit to improving the economic situation of Ticino workers.

Among the questions presented by the PLR are those related to the measures that the State Council intends to adopt to incentivize employment and improve the quality of life of residents. In particular, the political party asks for information on strategies to:

  • increase minimum wages in Ticino, currently set at 3,443 Swiss francs gross per month for adult workers;
  • improve access to vocational and continuing education, with particular attention to the most vulnerable categories;
  • incentivize the hiring of long-term unemployed workers, through measures such as the reduction of social contributions for companies that hire these workers.

The State Council will have to provide detailed answers to these inquiries, providing a clear vision of future strategies to address the challenge of poverty risk in Ticino.

Concrete examples

In Locarno, for example, the municipality has implemented a series of measures to reduce poverty, such as the establishment of a support service for people in difficulty and the creation of a fund for affordable housing. These initiatives have made it possible to reduce by 20% the number of people in poverty in the municipality.

Regulatory references

The Ticino minimum wage regulation was introduced in 2019 and provides for an amount of 3,443 Swiss francs gross per month for adult workers. However, this amount is considered insufficient to guarantee a dignified standard of living for workers.

Understanding poverty risk

Poverty risk does not only concern the unemployed or those with a low income but also workers with modest wages who are unable to cope with increasing expenses, such as rent and the cost of living. In Ticino, cities like Lugano and Bellinzona have seen a significant increase in the cost of living, with a consumer price index (CPI) of 105.6 and 104.8, respectively, compared to the Swiss average of 100.
