Stable unemployment in Switzerland and Ticino in August 2024

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 7 September 2026

The unemployment rate in Switzerland remained stable at 3% in August, with an increase of 0.2 points on an annual basis. In Ticino, the rate is 2.9%, with an increase of 0.1 points on a monthly basis and 0.3 points on a yearly basis.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Unemployment in Switzerland is stable at 3% in August.
  • On an annual basis, the increase is 0.2 percentage points.
  • In Ticino, the rate is 2.9%, with an increase of 0.1 points on a monthly basis and 0.3 points on a yearly basis.

Key facts

  • What: Unemployment rate in Switzerland
  • When: August 2024
  • Where: Switzerland, Ticino
  • Who: State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO)
  • Amount: 3% in Switzerland, 2.9% in Ticino

Unemployment in Switzerland remained stable at 3% in August, as reported by the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO). On an annual basis, however, there is a slight increase of 0.2 percentage points. At the regional level, the unemployment rate in Ticino was 2.9%, with an increase of 0.1 points on a monthly basis and 0.3 points on a yearly basis. The data does not take into account people who have exhausted the right to receive benefits and who live on savings or are beneficiaries of assistance.

Regional data

In Ticino, the unemployment rate is 2.9%, with an increase of 0.1 points on a monthly basis and 0.3 points on a yearly basis. In Graubünden, the rate is 1.1%, stable compared to July and with an increase of 0.1 points on an annual basis. The data are based on the number of people actually registered with the Regional Employment Offices (RPOs), a different criterion from that of the International Labour Office (ILO), which for the 2nd quarter of the year calculated a rate of 4.9% for Switzerland.

Operational details

Rising unemployment on an annual basis in Switzerland and Ticino could have implications for cross-border workers. A stable unemployment rate on a monthly basis indicates some stability in the labour market, but the annual increase suggests that there may be future challenges. Border workers need to be aware of these trends and prepare adequately to deal with any changes in the labour market.

Impact on frontier workers

For frontier workers, a stable unemployment rate on a monthly basis can offer some job security. However, the increase on an annual basis could indicate the need to be more flexible and adaptable. Cross-border workers should consider upgrading their skills and seek lifelong learning opportunities to remain competitive in the labour market.

Comparison with other regions

In Graubünden, the unemployment rate remained stable at 1.1%, with an increase of 0.1 points on an annual basis. This comparison shows that although Ticino has seen an increase, other regions such as Graubünden maintain a relatively low unemployment rate. Border workers working in Ticino may want to consider job opportunities in other Swiss regions to diversify their employment options.
