Bilateral Agreements III: Council of States test on Monday

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 26 September 2026

The package of Switzerland-EU agreements reaches the Council of States on Monday: the source expects a marathon debate, with sparks in the exchange.

Context

In brief

  • The Switzerland-EU package reaches the Council of States
  • The first examination is expected from Monday
  • The discussion will be a marathon debate
  • Sparks are expected in the confrontation

Key facts

  • Subject → package of agreements between Switzerland and the EU
  • When → from Monday
  • Where → Council of States
  • Debate climate → marathon, with sparks expected

Starting Monday, the package of agreements between Switzerland and the EU will be on the desks of the Council of States. It is the first test bench indicated for the Bilaterals III, with the dossier ready to enter the parliamentary body's discussion.

The eve is described as eventful: the source predicts a marathon debate and expects sparks. However, the outcome is not anticipated, nor are the positions that will clash reported. The concrete fact remains the start of the examination on Monday.

The available material therefore defines the general subject and the place of the passage, but not the content of the individual agreements. It does not list chapters, amounts, amendments, decisions already adopted, or an entry-into-force date. The news concerns the parliamentary timing of the package.

For the reader of Frontaliere Ticino, this scope avoids attributing effects to the news that are not declared. The text does not link the Bilaterals III to changes in the G permit, AVS, LPP, or LAMal. It does not announce measures on tax rebates, withholding tax, INPS, or double taxation. No new 2026 tax agreement appears either.

The verifiable point for those working across the border

Those who are cross-border commuters in the Canton of Ticino can read the article as an update on the Council of States' agenda, not as an operational communication regarding their own position. Even the Brogeda crossing is not mentioned as part of the news. No document, form, tax rate, or payroll adjustment is indicated.

The lexicon used by the source must be maintained in its precise meaning. "First test bench" describes the relevance of the passage to the Council of States; "marathon debate" signals a long discussion; "sparks" announces a potentially heated confrontation. None of the three formulas is equivalent to a final decision or a change that has already entered into force. Without the detail of the contents, it cannot be established whether the package affects cross-border work, taxation, or social security. The available report allows for establishing the venue, timing, and tone of the confrontation, and nothing else.

Operational details

Two aspects to keep separate

For a cross-border worker who works in Canton Ticino, the move to the Council of States should be read alongside the rules already applicable, without automatically turning it into a tax change. The source discusses Bilaterals III and a Switzerland-EU package; it does not present the dossier as a new 2026 tax agreement. This distinction avoids attributing immediate effects to a payslip or a permit.

The New Cross-Border Commuter Agreement has its own timeline: it was signed on 23 dicembre 2020 and has been in force since 1 gennaio 2024. These dates belong to the framework already applicable and are not indicated by the source as part of the announced review. The comparison can be read as follows:

Where caution is needed

The same caution applies to AVS, LPP and LAMal. These are areas that a cross-border worker may associate with agreements between Switzerland and the EU, but the source does not describe changes to contributions, social security or health insurance. A subsequent update should therefore be read by checking whether it actually mentions one of these subjects and whether it specifies the persons concerned and effective dates.

The same applies to tax rebates: those looking for the dedicated framework can consult the guide to ristorni Ticino-Italia, while keeping that topic separate from the parliamentary debate described in the article.

Key points

What to do after the announcement

The text allows for a reading procedure, not an administrative process. It does not indicate any forms, documents, rates or personal deadlines to apply. The cross-border worker can therefore use the parliamentary step as a checkpoint and wait for any future communications to clarify the content, recipients and effective dates.

Checklist for the cross-border worker

1. Note the timeframe: starting Monday, the package will be examined by the Council of States. This is the journalistic deadline indicated by the source, not an individual tax deadline.

2. When reading an update, distinguish the debate from any possible decision. The source speaks of expected sparks and a marathon debate, but does not communicate a definitive outcome.

3. If references appear to withholding tax, tax rebates or double taxation, compare them with the framework already in force. The withholding on employment income remains indicated in Switzerland, and the Italian tax credit is entered through section CE of the 730. The title Bilaterali III alone is not enough to infer a tax change.

4. If a future text mentions G permit, AVS, LPP or LAMal, check that it explicitly identifies the category concerned. The comparatore Permesso G vs B is available for the initial document check; for health insurance, see guide LAMal/CMI.

This sequence prevents confusing news about the institutional agenda with a change already applicable to the cross-border worker. It also helps keep separate the package of Switzerland-EU agreements, the Nuovo Accordo Frontalieri and the rules currently affecting taxation, social security and insurance.
