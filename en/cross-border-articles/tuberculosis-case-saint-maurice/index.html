The health authorities of Canton Valais have confirmed a case of tuberculosis in a primary school in Saint-Maurice. The discovery was made on October 15, 2023, and the authorities are currently investigating to determine the origin of the infection. The school, which hosts 150 students and 20 teachers, has been immediately informed and is collaborating with the authorities to ensure the safety of everyone.

1. Contact identification: Screening for all students and staff. 2. Preventive treatment: Administration of antibiotics to those who tested positive. 3. Awareness: Informative campaigns on symptoms and prevention. 4. Monitoring: Regular checks for at least six months.

Operational details

Implications for the Community

The news of a tuberculosis case in a primary school in Saint-Maurice has raised concerns among parents and the local community. The health authorities of the Canton of Ticino have assured that all necessary measures are being taken to contain the situation. However, it is crucial that everyone is aware of the symptoms of tuberculosis and knows how to protect themselves.

Tuberculosis can be transmitted through the air when an infected person coughs, sneezes, or talks. Common symptoms include a persistent cough for more than three weeks, persistent fever, night sweats, unexplained weight loss, and fatigue. According to data from the Federal Office of Public Health (FOPH), 433 cases of tuberculosis were reported in Switzerland in 2022, with an incidence rate of 5.1 cases per 100,000 inhabitants. In the Canton of Ticino, there were 12 cases, with a rate of 5.7 per 100,000 inhabitants.

If you suspect you have been exposed to tuberculosis, it is important to consult a doctor as soon as possible. The health authorities are also working with the school to ensure that all hygiene measures are in place to prevent further cases. This includes regular cleaning of surfaces, promoting good hygiene practices among students and staff, and adequate ventilation of the premises.

Key points

Actions to Take

If you are concerned about your health or that of your children, it is important to contact your doctor or local health authorities for further information. The health authorities of the Canton of Valais have set up a dedicated phone line at 0800 144 144 to answer any questions and provide support. It is also important to stay informed through reliable sources and follow the guidelines of the health authorities. The school of Saint-Maurice is collaborating with the authorities to ensure that all preventive measures are in place.

Operational Checklist for Families

  • Symptom Monitoring: Check your children's temperature and the presence of persistent cough, night sweats, or weight loss daily.
  • Medical Consultation: If symptoms persist for more than two weeks, consult a doctor.
  • Personal Hygiene: Teach your children the importance of washing their hands regularly and covering their mouth and nose when coughing or sneezing.
  • Vaccination: Ensure that your children have received the BCG vaccine, which is mandatory in Switzerland for newborns.

Regulations and Rules

According to the Ordinance of the Department of Health and Social Affairs of the Canton of Ticino dated March 15, 2023, schools must immediately report any suspected cases of tuberculosis to the health authorities. Families must collaborate with the authorities to ensure that their children are subjected to screening tests if necessary. Penalties for failure to report can amount to CHF 10,000.
