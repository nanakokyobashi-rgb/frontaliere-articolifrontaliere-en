The Premio Ecologia Città di Varese – Salvatore Furia 2026 is being awarded this year to Enrico Giovannini. The committee's decision concerns a figure who has transformed awareness of the environmental crisis into a reflection on the responsibilities of society, institutions and the economic system.

Giovannini is an economist and statistician. At the University of Rome Tor Vergata, he teaches Economic Statistics and Analysis and Policies for Sustainable Development as a full professor, in addition to teaching at the National School of Administration. Over the course of his career, he has been a two-time minister, Chief Statistician of the OECD and president of ISTAT.

He is co-founder and scientific director of the Italian Alliance for Sustainable Development, ASviS. The founding of the Alliance in 2016 represents a key milestone in a commitment to promoting the United Nations 2030 Agenda and a vision of development capable of bringing together the environmental, economic and social dimensions.

The committee's rationale

The choice recognizes the scientific, institutional and public-outreach work through which Giovannini has placed sustainable development at the center of public debate. Well-being, in the perspective highlighted by the committee, does not coincide with economic growth alone: it includes quality of life, social cohesion, the protection of natural resources and the opportunities left to future generations.

Salvatore Furia's legacy

The award thus recalls the figure of its founder, the naturalist and science communicator Salvatore Furia, who in 1973 linked knowledge of nature with education and the growth of the community. According to Nicoletta San Martino, councillor of the Municipality of Varese with responsibility for Environmental Protection, Social Sustainability and the Circular Economy, Furia's teaching instilled in young people a tangible sense of responsibility toward their local area:

“To many young people, in addition to knowledge of nature, Furia passed on the ability to observe nature, learn about their own area and feel responsible for protecting it.”

The 2026 rationale therefore fits into his idea of environmental culture: understanding natural balances, recognizing human responsibility and turning awareness into responsible behavior. The choice links the recipient's profile to the tradition of the award: from knowledge of nature to collective responsibility, without separating the environment, society and the economy. The committee considers this transition consistent with Furia's cultural legacy.
