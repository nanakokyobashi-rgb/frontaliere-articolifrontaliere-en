Locarno, Lugano, Mendrisio vie for 2030 Cultural Capital (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 10 May 2026

Locarno management agrees to joint candidacy but asks for measures to avoid a marginal position

Context

In brief

  • Locarno approves cultural alliance with Lugano and Mendrisio
  • Joint bid for Swiss Capital of Culture 2030
  • Requests for measures to avoid marginalization

Key facts

  • What: Joint bid for Swiss Capital of Culture 2030
  • When: May 8, 2026
  • Where: Locarno, Lugano, Mendrisio
  • Who: Management of Locarno, Lugano, Mendrisio
  • Amount: Not yet specified

The Commission of Locarno has approved the joint bid with Lugano and Mendrisio to become the Swiss Capital of Culture in 2030. However, the Management of Locarno has set important conditions to ensure that the city is not marginalized within the alliance. The decision was made during a meeting held on May 8, 2026, as reported by Sascha Cellina on laRegione.ch.

The cultural alliance between Locarno, Lugano, and Mendrisio represents a significant opportunity for Ticino, but the Management of Locarno has emphasized the need to ensure that the city is not disadvantaged compared to the other two locations. This agreement could have a positive impact on cross-border workers who work in these cities, offering new cultural and leisure opportunities.

Implications for cross-border workers

The approval of this alliance could lead to greater cultural integration between the three cities, with events and initiatives involving both residents and cross-border workers. This could include art exhibitions, music festivals, and other cultural activities that may interest cross-border workers living in Italy and working in Switzerland.

Furthermore, the alliance could facilitate mobility between the three cities, making it easier for cross-border workers to participate in cultural and social events in all three locations. This could improve the quality of life for cross-border workers, offering them more opportunities for integration and socialization.

Comparison with the previous situation

Before this alliance, each city organized cultural events independently, which could limit the scope and impact of these initiatives. With the alliance, the resources and expertise of the three cities can be combined to create larger-scale and more attractive events, benefiting both residents and cross-border workers.

However, it is important that Locarno is not marginalized within the alliance. The Management of Locarno has requested specific measures to ensure that the city has a leading role and that its interests are adequately represented. This is fundamental to ensuring that cross-border workers living and working in Locarno can fully benefit from the opportunities offered by the alliance.
