Working in the Canton of Ticino while living in Mornago represents a consolidated choice for professionals in the province of Varese. The Italy-Switzerland double taxation agreement, signed on 9 March 1976, eliminated the risk of paying taxes in both countries. For cross-border workers, a precise rule applies: the withholding tax is withheld by Switzerland, not by Italy. Italy recognizes the tax credit through the EC framework of the 730 declaration.

The G Permit is the fundamental document for those who live in Mornago and work in Ticino. It is not a temporary visa, but a work permit that allows unlimited access to the Ticino job market without sectoral restrictions. The request is submitted to the SEM (State Secretariat for Migration) with the employment contract signed by the Swiss company and the Italian identity document.

January 1, 2024 marked the entry into force of the New Cross-Border Agreement, agreed on December 23, 2020 and ratified by Italy with Law 83 of June 13, 2023. The allowance represents the gross annual tax-free income in the Italian tax system.

The Swiss pay slip contains several elements. Gross applies immediately to AVS (employee rate 5.3%), AI (disability, included in the AVS rate), IPG (unemployment earnings, included) and ADD (ordinary unemployment, 1.1% up to the ceiling of CHF 148,200 per year). In addition to mandatory contributions, there is the LPP (Occupational pension fund, second pillar), which varies from 7% to 18% of the paycheck depending on age: an employee aged 25–34 years pays around 7%, while one aged 55–64 reaches 18%. LAINF (accident insurance), managed by SUVA, is paid by the employer at rates between 0.7% and 1.5% depending on the sector of activity.

The social security framework for cross-border workers

The AVS is not a tax, but a social security contribution that generates pension rights. Those who work in Switzerland accumulate years of contributions valid for the Italian and Swiss pension thanks to international agreements. The LPP (second pillar), on the other hand, is a supplementary pension managed by the company's private funds or by pension institutions, and generates a life annuity or capital upon reaching the retirement age.

The benefits for those who live in Mornago and work in Ticino also include the possibility of choosing between Swiss health insurance (LAMal) and Italian health insurance (CMI). The KVG covers the entire Swiss territory and guarantees high-level medical services; deductibles for adults vary from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 per year depending on the plan chosen. The choice must take place within 3 months of the start of the employment relationship.

Operational details

Taxation and withholding tax in Switzerland

Swiss withholding tax is the basis of the tax system for cross-border commuters. Unlike many European countries, Switzerland withholds taxes directly from payroll and remits them to the cantonal administration. For those who live in Mornago and work in Ticino, this means that there is no direct double taxation: Italy does not withhold additional national taxes on income from foreign salaried employment, but recognizes the Swiss tax credit in the income tax return through section CE of Form 730 or the Redditi PF tax return.

The withholding tax rate varies by canton. Canton Ticino applies combined federal and cantonal rates: the federal component is established according to a progressive table (from approximately 6% to approximately 13% depending on income), and the cantonal component is added to it. A person earning CHF 50.000 annually will have a total withholding (tax + social contributions) significantly lower than Italian taxation on an equivalent income in euros.

The New Cross-Border Commuter Agreement, applied from January 1, 2024, profoundly changed the tax treatment. The transitional regime under Article 9 of the Agreement applies only to those who meet the treaty definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino, or Valais between December 31, 2018 and July 17, 2023; the covered employment income is taxed exclusively in Switzerland. In all other cases, the ordinary regime applies, with Italian taxation and a credit for Swiss tax. The 10.000 euros under Article 4 of Law 83/2023 are only the threshold for the income referred to in Article 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013, not a general tax allowance. Withholding tax is administered by the AFC (Federal Tax Administration) at the federal level, while Canton Ticino manages the cantonal surcharge through the Cantonal Tax Office. The Swiss employer automatically withholds these amounts and remits them to the competent authorities; the cross-border commuter does not have to file a separate tax return in Switzerland (except in cases of multiple employment or additional income).

Mandatory social contributions: AVS, LPP, ADD, LAINF

Swiss social contributions are separate from ordinary taxation and generate pension and protection entitlements. AVS (Old-Age and Survivors' Insurance and Disability Insurance, AI) represents the first pillar and has an employee contribution rate of 5.3% (IPG, unemployment compensation, is included). The employer pays the corresponding amount, thereby creating an overall rate of 10.6%. This is not a tax withholding, but rather an entitlement: each year of Swiss contributions also counts toward the Italian pension, thanks to international treaties.

ADD (unemployment insurance, with a rate of 1.1%) covers the cross-border commuter in the event of losing employment in Switzerland. The contribution ceiling is set at CHF 148.200 annually: those who earn more do not pay additional ADD. AC (natural disaster insurance) is part of the same contribution.

LPP (occupational pension fund, second pillar) is mandatory from age 25. The rate varies according to age: from 25 to 34 years it is approximately 7%, from 35 to 44 approximately 10%, from 45 to 54 approximately 14%, from 55 to 64 approximately 18%. This progressive structure reflects the principle that younger employees contribute less because they have more time to accumulate capital. LPP is managed by private pension institutions (foundations, insurance companies) and generates an annuity or capital upon reaching retirement age (65 for men, 64 for women in Switzerland, but the Italian cross-border commuter retains access to entitlements through INPS as well).

LAINF (insurance against occupational accidents and occupational diseases) is managed by SUVA and financed by the employer at a rate between 0.7% and 1.5%, depending on the sector of activity. It covers workplace accidents, occupational diseases, and provides medical benefits, compensation and, in the event of death, benefits for family members.

Comparison of Italian and Swiss rates

In theory, the same gross salary results in a very different net amount depending on whether it is earned in Switzerland or Italy. Italian IRPEF has progressive rates: 23% up to €28.000, 33% between €28.001 and €50.000, and 43% above €50.000. In Switzerland, federal withholding tax is lower (6–13% depending on income), and Canton Ticino adds an additional cantonal component. Although Switzerland also has AVS/AI/IPG (5.3%) and LPP contributions (7–18%), the total is often lower than or comparable to Italian IRPEF alone on middle incomes.

Health insurance: LAMal and the right to choose for cross-border workers with a G Permit

Cross-border workers with G Permits have an explicit right to choose their health coverage. They can choose to join LAMal (Swiss health insurance, mandatory for Swiss residents) or remain in the Italian system with CMI (Maternity-Disability-Hygiene Coverage). The choice must be communicated in writing within 3 months of the start of the employment relationship. Many cross-border workers opt for LAMal because healthcare facilities in Ticino are accessible without geographical barriers, and the covered benefits include general practitioners, hospitalization, prescription medicines, and specialist services throughout Switzerland.

LAMal deductibles for adults range from CHF 300 (minimum deductible, with a higher premium) to CHF 2.500 (maximum deductible, with a lower premium). The deductible choice is annual and allows the cost of coverage to be adjusted according to personal needs. Those who anticipate low medical expenses can choose a high deductible and keep the monthly premium down; those with chronic conditions or who frequently use services choose a low deductible. Cost-sharing (copayment) is set at 10% after the deductible has been reached (up to an annual maximum of CHF 700 for outpatient medical costs only).

The Italian CMI, an alternative for those who remain in the Italian system, is administered by INPS and covers essential primary and hospital medical services. However, it does not always extend to specialist care in Switzerland: a cross-border worker with CMI who needs an urgent specialist appointment in Lugano might have to pay for it out of pocket, unless INPS has agreements with facilities in Ticino.

Practical procedure: step-by-step for starting employment

1. Applying for the G Permit The application must be submitted to SEM (State Secretariat for Migration) in the canton where the employer has its registered office, or directly to the Ticino migration authority if the employer is based in Ticino. The following are required:

  • Employment contract signed by the employer
  • Italian identity document (passport or valid identity card)
  • If applicable, certificate of residence in Mornago

Processing times vary from 4 to 8 weeks. Many cantons allow work to begin even before the official notification, on the basis of a declaration.

2. Registration with AVS Once hired, the employer registers the new employee with the Ticino cantonal AVS office within 5 days. The cross-border worker receives an AVS number (13 digits) and a confirmation letter. This number is unique and follows the cross-border worker throughout their career in Switzerland. The cross-border worker does not need to submit a separate application; the company handles the procedure.

3. Choosing health insurance The employer provides a list of LAMal insurers with which it usually has arrangements, or the cross-border worker may independently choose a Swiss insurer. If they opt for LAMal, they must pay the monthly premium (varying between CHF 300 and CHF 500 depending on age, the chosen deductible, and the insurer). If they prefer to keep the Italian CMI, they must notify the Swiss insurer of this in writing within 3 months to avoid automatic registration.

4. Notification to the Italian Revenue Agency By January 31 of the following year, the employer or Swiss tax intermediary must report the cross-border worker's data to the Italian MEF system (Ministry of Economy and Finance): gross income paid, taxes withheld, and days worked. This communication is necessary so that the Italian Revenue Agency becomes aware of the foreign income and applies the tax credit.
