The agreement timeline

As of January 1st, 2024, the New Cross-Border Workers Agreement is in force. Signed on December 23, 2020, it was ratified in Italy by Law 83 of June 13, 2023. The correct reference is 2024, not 2026.

For anyone considering living in Arcisate and working in Ticino as a cross-border worker, the basic tax rule is clear. Withholding on the cross-border worker's employment income takes place only in Switzerland, not in both countries. Italy avoids double taxation through the tax credit provided for in the CE section of Form 730. The issue therefore concerns withholding tax and cannot be understood as double Italian-Swiss withholding.

Existing and new cross-border workers

The timeline is not enough: the definition of the two categories also matters. Existing cross-border workers, who were already in that status before July 17, 2023, benefit from an exemption of €7'500 under the transitional regime covering the period 2024–2033. For new cross-border workers, the stated allowance is instead €10'000. These are different values and periods and should not be combined into a single threshold.

The framework is based on the Convention for the Avoidance of Double Taxation between Italy and Switzerland, signed on March 9, 1976. Switzerland is not a member of the EU or the EEA: work in Canton Ticino must therefore be understood within a bilateral tax relationship, not as an ordinary movement of work within the Union.

The logistical aspect remains distinct from the tax aspect. The comparison with IRPEF

Italian legislation also distinguishes tax rates over time. From 2026, under Law 199/2025, Article 1, paragraph 3, IRPEF is 23% up to €28'000, 33% between €28'001 and €50'000, and 43% above €50'000. For income in 2024–2025, the rates indicated are instead 23%, 35%, and 43%.

These brackets describe the Italian IRPEF framework; they do not override the specific rule for cross-border workers, namely withholding only in Switzerland and a tax credit to avoid double taxation. G permit, healthcare and data on Canton Ticino

The move is not limited to the relationship between salary and tax. The framework to consider includes the G permit, social security and health insurance, while statistics describe the overall scale of cross-border employment in Canton Ticino. These are areas connected to the decision, but they are not interchangeable.

LAMal and G permit

LAMal is health insurance. Cross-border workers with a G permit have the right of option and, for adults, the specified deductibles range from CHF 300 to CHF 2'500. This figure defines a healthcare component of the comparison. It is not equivalent to an income tax rate and does not, by itself, alter the withholding tax rule.

To understand whom to contact, the area must be distinguished: federal and cantonal withholding tax rates are established by the applicable laws and administered, respectively, by AFC/ESTV at the federal level and by the cantonal tax administrations. Social security, including the AVS/AI area, instead falls within the remit of UFAS; BFS is limited to collecting statistical data. Consequently, neither UFAS nor BFS determines tax rates.

This distinction helps assess advantages and disadvantages without oversimplifying them.
