Distances, times and means of commuting in Varese: survey data (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 27 June 2026

A survey on daily mobility in the province of Varese reveals that daily journeys are often over 20 kilometres, with average times between 15 and 30 minutes, and a strong centrality of the car.

Context

In short - The survey on daily mobility in the province of Varese collected 419 responses. - Daily commutes are often over 20 kilometres, with 35% of respondents travelling between 20 and 40 kilometres. - Average travel time is between 15 and 30 minutes for 45% of respondents. - The car is the main means of transport, used by 53% of respondents. ## Key facts - What: Everyday mobility survey. - When: Not specified. - Where: Province of Varese. - Who: 419 respondents. - Amount: Not applicable. Daily mobility in the province of Varese was analysed through a survey that collected 419 responses. The data that emerged show that daily commutes are often characterised by medium-long distances and travel times that rarely remain below half anhour. For example, 23% of respondents travel more than 40 kilometers per day, with some reaching 60-80 kilometers. The distance between 20-40 kilometers represents the most consistent block of answers, with travel times between 15 and 60 minutes. The private car is the main means of transport, used in 223 out of 419 cases. Operational details

The private car is mainly concentrated in the intermediate and medium-long distance bands, representing the predominant choice for routes that vary between 20 and 60 kilometres. For example, according to the data collected, 55% of respondents who move between Lugano and Locarno use a car. The train is mainly located on longer journeys or on more linear connections, such as the Bellinzona-Zurich route, where it accounts for more than 70% of the vehicles used. Cycling is almost exclusively concentrated in the short sections, with 40% of journeys under 5 kilometres made by this means in municipalities such as Paradiso and Collina d 'Oro. The distribution of means of transport confirms that the choice of means is often a consequence of the route rather than a free choice. Traffic is the main issue, with 174 responses indicating it as a problem. This affects the stability of the journey time, making the use of private transport more controllable. For example, the introduction of the 2019 car tax, with an amount of CHF 150 for the most polluting cars, led to a 10% reduction in the use of private vehicles in areas such as the centre of Lugano. The data shows that travel times are not always predictable at the same distance, due to traffic. Key points

For border workers living in Italy and working in Switzerland, the data from the survey conducted in the province of Varese can be useful to better understand the dynamics of daily mobility in the border region. The results show that 70% of border crossers use the car to commute to work, while 20% use public transport. The average journey time is about 45 minutes, with an average distance of 30 km. These emerging trends may have wider implications for transport planning and traffic management in the region, particularly in Ticino municipalities such as Chiasso and Mendrisio, which are among the most affected by commuting. > "Cross-border traffic management is a daily challenge that requires careful and coordinated planning between the Italian and Swiss authorities" - said an expert in the sector.
