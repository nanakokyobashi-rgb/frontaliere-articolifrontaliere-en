Living in Cassina Rizzardi and working in Ticino is a popular choice among border workers: the municipality of Como is a few kilometers from the Ticino border, allows you to maintain roots in the Italian territory and access the Swiss labour market with significantly higher salaries. What does it mean concretely from a fiscal, social security and logistical point of view?

The figure of the frontier worker — category G for cross-border workers — is regulated by the New Frontier Agreement signed on 23 December 2020, which entered into force on 1 January 2024. This agreement has significantly changed taxation and social security contributions, with direct impacts on the monthly paycheck.

Operational details

Cost of living: Cassina Rizzardi vs. Ticino

Moving from a Comasco municipality to Ticino involves significant considerations regarding housing costs, utilities, food, and services. Cassina Rizzardi generally offers lower costs compared to Ticino municipalities such as Lugano, Bellinzona, or Mendrisio. This is one of the main advantages of maintaining residency in Italy while working in Switzerland.

Real Estate and Rentals: Cassina Rizzardi offers houses for rent and sale at lower prices compared to the Ticino market. Those who buy in Italy use the euro, while a potential mortgage in Swiss francs would involve exchange rate exposure. Many cross-border commuters choose to rent in Italy (lower rent) and work in Ticino for this economic reason.

Utilities: Electricity, gas, and internet in Italy follow rates regulated by the Italian market, generally lower than in Switzerland. Moving to Ticino would result in significantly higher energy costs.

Groceries and Daily Expenses: Living in Cassina Rizzardi allows access to Italian prices for food shopping, stores, and services. Many cross-border commuters maintain this base to control their monthly budget, resorting to Ticino only for specialized purchases or services not available.

Commuting and Border Crossings

The distance between Cassina Rizzardi and the main Ticino work centers (Lugano, Chiasso, Mendrisio, Bellinzona) is short. By car, the journey takes about 30-45 minutes depending on the destination and traffic conditions at the border crossings.

The main border crossings for those living in the Comasco area:

  • Brogeda (Como-Ticino): a minor crossing, less congested during peak hours
  • Chiasso (Como-Ticino): the main one, often congested in the morning and afternoon during commuting hours
  • Gaggiolo (Varese-Ticino): an alternative for those working in the Locarnese area (Locarno, Ascona, Tenero)

Many cross-border commuters choose public transport (trains from Como to Chiasso, then Ticino buses/trains) to avoid traffic, gasoline, and parking costs in Ticino. The option of partial telecommuting further reduces the number of commuting days per week.

LAMal: Choosing Health Insurance

The cross-border commuter category G resident in Italy has the right to choose between Swiss health insurance (LAMal) or maintaining the Italian INPS/SSN system.

Most opt for LAMal as it offers comprehensive coverage, quick response times, and direct access to Ticino and Swiss hospitals. Annual premiums are substantial, but the coverage is complete. LAMal includes deductibles for adults ranging from CHF 300 to CHF 2500 depending on the chosen policy.

Those who choose the Italian system must maintain local INPS registration and receive Italian health coverage with lower contribution costs. The decision depends on personal evaluations of preferred healthcare quality, desired coverage, and cost-benefit.

3rd Pillar (3a): Voluntary Pension Savings

In addition to AVS and LPP (mandatory), the cross-border commuter can subscribe to a 3rd Pillar (3a) to accumulate additional pension savings with Swiss tax advantages. The maximum annual contributions are set by Swiss federal regulations and are deducted from taxable income in the canton of work. This is an optional but recommended tool to increase future pension income.

Double Taxation: How the Tax Credit Works

The Italy-Switzerland Convention of March 9, 1976 prevents the same income from being taxed in both countries. The concrete mechanism: 1. Switzerland applies withholding tax (deducted by the employer) 2. Italy does not apply additional taxation on foreign employment income 3. In the Italian model 730 (section CE), the cross-border commuter declares Swiss income and requests the tax credit 4. If Swiss withholdings exceed Italian taxes due, a refund is generated via the 730

This architecture eliminates the double taxation that would characterize a separate national taxation regime.

Installation procedure: step by step

Anyone living in Cassina Rizzardi who intends to work as an employee in Ticino must follow a precise administrative procedure. It is not automatic: it requires authorizations from the competent cantonal and federal Swiss offices.

Step 1: Job offer and employer's request The Ticino employer submits a G permit request for the foreign worker to the competent cantonal offices (usually the Migration Section or the Department of Justice of the Canton of Ticino). The procedure typically takes 4-8 weeks. The costs are borne by the employer.

Step 2: Registration and declaration of foreign income The worker registers their residence in Cassina Rizzardi at the local town hall as a resident working in Switzerland. They must complete a future income declaration according to the forms required by the Swiss and Italian authorities.

Step 3: Registration with tax authorities Once the G permit is obtained:

  • In Switzerland: the employer registers them with the Federal Tax Administration (FTA/ESTV) in the canton where the company is located. The cross-border worker receives a Swiss tax identification number (IDE).
  • In Italy: the worker registers the new foreign employment income with the Como province section of the Revenue Agency. This registration allows the declaration of Swiss income in the annual 730 form and the request for tax credit.

Step 4: Opening a bank account and salary payment The employer makes salary payments via bank transfer. The cross-border worker opens (or uses) an Italian bank account to receive the payments. Some also maintain a Swiss bank account for withdrawals in CHF or payment of pension contributions.

Critical annual deadlines for the cross-border worker

After the first year of work, the cross-border worker must comply with precise administrative deadlines:

  • May 31: Deadline for the Italian 730 declaration (previous year's income with CE form and tax credit)
  • March 15 (indicative): Deadline for communicating income data to the FTA/ESTV if requested by the canton
  • January: Receipt of pay slip with annual withholding certificate (necessary for the 730)
  • By January 31 of the following year: The cantonal administration communicates the final withholdings paid

Failure to comply with the 730 deadlines results in administrative penalties in Italy. Consultation with a tax accountant specializing in cross-border workers is recommended for the first two years.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the difference between G permit and B permit for cross-border commuters?
The G permit is intended for cross-border workers who reside in Italy and work in Switzerland, without the obligation to relocate. It allows daily commuting to the crossings. The B permit requires residence in Switzerland and is for foreign employees or self-employed workers who move permanently. For those who live in Cassina Rizzardi, the G permit is the correct category.
If I change employer in Ticino, do I need to renew the G permit?
Yes. The G permit is tied to the specific employer. A change of employer requires a new permit application at the relevant cantonal offices. The procedure is similar to the first request (4-8 weeks) and the costs remain with the new employer.
Can I choose between LAMal Switzerland and INPS Italy as a border crossing?
Yes, you have the option right. Most G-category border crossers opt for LAMal as it offers extensive coverage, access to Ticino doctors and fast turnaround times. LAMal provides franchises from CHF 300 to CHF 2500 for adults. If you choose INPS, keep the Italian system with lower premiums but Italian local coverage.
When do I need to file the 730 return for Swiss income?
The deadline is May 31 of the year following the year in which you earned your Swiss income. The income must be declared in the EC framework of the Italian 730 and request the tax credit to avoid double taxation. Switzerland has already applied source tax on the paycheck.
If I have paid AVS contributions in Switzerland for years, how do I combine them with the future Italian INPS pension?
The Swiss AVS and the Italian INPS are autonomous systems. Those who have paid periods in both countries can resort to totalization: INPS and AVS combine contribution periods to calculate the annuity. Contact INPS and the relevant AVS office for an assessment of your social security position. The final annuity will be the sum of the two benefits.
