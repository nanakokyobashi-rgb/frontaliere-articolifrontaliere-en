Living in Cucciago and working in Ticino as a cross-border commuter

Cucciago is a Swiss municipality located in the Canton of Ticino, a few kilometers from Lugano. It is a quiet and picturesque residential area, ideal for those who wish to live in a peaceful environment while still being close to the city. Thanks to its strategic location, Cucciago offers its residents the opportunity to work in Ticino and enjoy all the benefits that come with it.

In brief

  • Distance from Lugano: 15 km
  • Travel time: 20 minutes by car
  • Cost of living: Lower than Lugano
  • Recommended areas: Historic center, residential neighborhood

Cost of living

The cost of living in Cucciago is generally lower than in Lugano. For example, the average price of a 3-room apartment in Cucciago is around CHF 350,000, while in Lugano it can exceed CHF 500,000. Food and service prices are also more contained in Cucciago.

Transportation

Cucciago is well connected to Lugano and other Ticino municipalities via the road and rail network. Cucciago railway station is on the Lugano-Milan line and offers direct connections to the main Swiss and Italian cities.

Work

The Canton of Ticino is an important economic region, with one of the lowest unemployment rates in Switzerland. The main industries are tourism, finance, and technology. Those who work in Ticino can enjoy a higher average salary compared to other Swiss regions.

Regulations

Cross-border commuters working in Ticino must have a Swiss work permit. The permit can be requested online on the Canton of Ticino website. The cost of the permit is CHF 100 per year.

Operational details

Transportation

Cucciago is well connected with Lugano and the rest of Ticino thanks to an efficient bus network. The nearest train station is in Lugano, which offers connections to major Swiss and Italian cities. By car, Cucciago can be reached via the A2/A9 motorway. The travel time from Lugano is about 20 minutes, while from Milan it is about an hour and a half. For those who prefer cycling, there are several bike paths that connect Cucciago to Lugano and other locations in Ticino.

Accommodation

In Cucciago, cross-border workers can find various accommodation options, from apartments to houses. Property prices are generally lower compared to Lugano, but it is important to also consider the costs of daily transportation. For example, a 3-room apartment in Cucciago can cost around 1,500 CHF per month, while the same type of apartment in Lugano can cost over 2,500 CHF.

Work

Ticino offers various job opportunities for cross-border workers, especially in the tourism, healthcare, and finance sectors. Lugano is the main economic center of the canton and hosts numerous international companies. The average salary in Ticino is about 5,500 CHF per month, but can vary significantly depending on the sector and job position.

Regulations

Cross-border workers who work in Ticino must have a Swiss work permit. The permit can be requested by the Swiss employer or directly by the worker at the Labor Office of the canton of Ticino. The cost of the permit is 300 CHF per year. Additionally, cross-border workers must also have Swiss health insurance.

Key points

Cost of living and taxes

The cost of living in Cucciago is generally lower than in Lugano, but still higher than in other Italian border towns. Property prices are more affordable and the quality of life is high. As for taxes, cross-border workers are subject to withholding tax in Switzerland and must declare their income in Italy as well. It is important to be aware of the tax conventions between Switzerland and Italy to avoid double taxation.

Tax conventions

The tax conventions between Switzerland and Italy are important to avoid double taxation. Switzerland signed a tax convention with Italy in 1992, which came into force in 1993. This tax convention provides that cross-border workers are subject to withholding tax in Switzerland and must declare their income in Italy as well.

Double taxation

Double taxation is a problem that can occur when working in a country different from the one in which you reside. In this case, cross-border workers who work in Ticino and reside in Italy could be subject to paying taxes in both countries. However, the tax convention between Switzerland and Italy provides that cross-border workers are subject to withholding tax in Switzerland and must declare their income in Italy as well. This means that cross-border workers should not be subject to paying taxes in both countries.
