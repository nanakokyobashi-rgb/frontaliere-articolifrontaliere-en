Switzerland votes on neutrality and food

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 27 September 2026

Today Switzerland votes on neutrality and a food initiative (70% self-sufficiency in 10 years); nine cantons are also deciding on cantonal issues, including cross-border mobility.

Switzerland is voting today on two federal initiatives: the one on neutrality and the one on food. In addition to the two national issues, voters in nine cantons are being called upon to vote on cantonal matters. The report is by Keystone-ATS.

Neutrality and sanctions

The initiative on neutrality calls for a stricter application of Swiss neutrality. The text seeks to introduce a far-reaching ban on sanctions into the Constitution and provides for Switzerland to use its neutrality to play a mediating role.

If the yes vote wins, Switzerland could resort to economic sanctions only when they were decided by the UN Security Council. Measures against sanctions evasion would remain possible. Cooperation with military alliances would instead be permitted only in the event of a direct and armed attack.

The proposal is supported by the UDC and some center-left representatives. Former UDC Federal Councillor Christoph Blocher contributed to its drafting. According to the latest polls, only about one third of those eligible to vote said they were in favor, and the text is expected to face an uphill battle at the polls.

Food and cantonal vote

The latest polls also indicate a clear no to the food initiative: fewer than one third of respondents were in favor. Launched by environmentalist Franziska Herren and her association Acqua pulita per tutti, the proposal calls on the Confederation to aim for a level of food self-sufficiency of at least 70% within ten years.

The Confederation should also promote a diet based more heavily on plant-based products. The agricultural and food sectors should adapt, while subsidies should be structured according to the same principles. The Confederation would also be required to guarantee everyone in Switzerland a supply of food and clean drinking water.

The cantonal issues range from spatial planning to education, from contraception to hooliganism, as well as infrastructure for cross-border mobility. Among the most eagerly awaited votes are the proposal for a substantial reduction in taxes in the Canton of Vaud and the extension of voting rights to sixteen-year-olds in Graubünden.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the goal of the food initiative?
The Food Initiative aims to achieve at least 70% food self-sufficiency within ten years, promoting a more plant-based diet and adapting agricultural and food subsidies to the same principles.
What does the neutrality initiative envisage with regard to economic sanctions?
If approved, the neutrality initiative would allow Switzerland to apply economic sanctions only when they are decided by the UN Security Council; measures against the circumvention of sanctions would remain possible, while cooperation with military alliances would only be allowed in the event of a direct and armed attack.
Which cantonal issues are being voted on in the nine cantons and which is related to cross-border commuters?
In the nine cantons, votes are held on spatial planning, education, contraception, hooliganism and infrastructure for cross-border mobility. This last item is the only cantonal topic that has a direct link with those who cross the border, although the source does not specify the canton, the work or the effects already decided.
