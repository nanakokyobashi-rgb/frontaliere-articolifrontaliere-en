About fifty workers, mostly from the province of Varese, come to Ticino every autumn to take part in the grape harvest on the hills of Mendrisiotto. They are cross-border workers engaged in two distinct activities: grape harvesting and loading. Their number makes visible a seasonal presence that is renewed year after year, with a stable core and a turnover of new arrivals.

The work thus brings together those who join for the shorter phase and those who remain throughout the entire vineyard cycle. It is not a homogeneous group in terms of age, experience or length of commitment: the grape harvest brings new applicants and established returnees together in the same field.

Two durations for the same work

The duration is not the same for everyone. For those who harvest grapes, the seasonal contract usually lasts a month and a half. Those who follow the entire vineyard work cycle, on the other hand, work from April to November. The workday also varies according to the needs of the field: it ranges from quick three-hour shifts to more demanding seven- or eight-hour sessions. The stated pay is about fifteen net francs per hour. The hourly figure can be explored in more detail in simulatore busta paga.

Putting the team together requires connections. Alongside the «hard core» of veterans who faithfully return every year, applications arrive via CV in Ticino. The turnover is about ten new people each season, and the workers’ ages range from 18 to 70.

The personal stories show the group’s variety. Maurizio spent 43 years in a factory as a lithographer and, as a retiree, considers the vineyard an experience capable of rejuvenating him. Matteo, 52, from Varese, works in the vineyard from April to November: he has fifteen seasons in Ticino and twenty-seven in total behind him. In his view, in the past the grape harvest had a more festive atmosphere, whereas today it seems more industrial and lacking in warmth. His comment describes a tradition that is still demanding and irreplaceable, but stripped of some of its romanticism.

Operational details

One harvest, two commitments

For a cross-border worker, the most useful figure is not just the hourly rate. The report describes two ways of working in the vineyard: a role focused on harvesting and a job covering the entire production chain. Duration therefore becomes the first factor to consider in relation to one's availability.

Key points

Before applying

For a cross-border worker from the Varese area considering grape harvesting in Ticino, the choice can be organized starting from the data described for the Mendrisiotto. The point is not to assign everyone the same schedule, but to understand which part of the work fits their availability.

Four practical steps

1. Separate harvesting from the production chain. Grape harvesting corresponds to a seasonal contract that usually lasts a month and a half. Working throughout the vineyard production chain, on the other hand, means being employed from April to November.

2. Set the time frame. Before applying, determine whether you are looking for a commitment limited to the harvest or availability covering the period indicated for the entire production chain. These are two different durations, even though they belong to the same vineyard work.

3. Prepare your CV. The source reports that new applications arrived via CV in Ticino. For those joining the team for the first time, this is the step described for introducing themselves alongside the veterans who return each season.

4. Focus on pace and pay. Workdays can range from quick three-hour shifts to seven- or eight-hour sessions. The financial reference is approximately 15 net francs per hour: the length of the workday and the period of employment should therefore be considered together.

This sequence helps assess the work correctly without flattening very different profiles. The team includes newcomers, veterans and workers between 18 and 70 years old. The experiences described also confirm that there is no single way to reach the vineyard: some arrive there after 43 years in a factory, while others have been returning for fifteen seasons in Ticino.
