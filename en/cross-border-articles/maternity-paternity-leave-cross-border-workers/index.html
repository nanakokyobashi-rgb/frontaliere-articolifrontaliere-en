The birth of a child radically changes a cross-border worker’s employment and social security situation. Between Canton Ticino and the Italian border provinces, tens of thousands of workers cross border points such as Brogeda or Gaggiolo every day to reach their workplace. When a child arrives, the situation becomes more complicated: it is necessary to understand which national system pays the leave, which rate applies to the benefits, and how to coordinate applications between Switzerland and Italy.

The starting point is the Swiss contribution structure. For employees, the AVS/AI/IPG contribution is 5.3% of gross salary. Within this acronym, the IPG component finances loss-of-earnings benefits, including those related to maternity and paternity. Added to this are AD/AC (unemployment and insolvency insurance) at 1.1% with a ceiling of CHF 148'200, LAINF (accident insurance) between 0.7% and 1.5%, and LPP (occupational pension provision) between 7% and 18% depending on the age bracket, from age 25.

For a cross-border worker with a G permit, tax residence remains in Italy, but employment income is taxed at source in Switzerland. Italy avoids double taxation through a tax credit, which must be indicated in section CE of Form 730. This mechanism also applies to leave benefits paid by Swiss entities: the cross-border worker is not subject to double withholding, but must correctly manage the Italian tax return.

The relevant regulatory framework

The New Cross-Border Workers Agreement was signed on December 23, 2020 and applies from January 1, 2024. Italian ratification took place through Law 83 of June 13, 2023. Legacy cross-border workers remain taxed only in Switzerland (Article 9 of the 2020 Agreement). The transitional regime under Article 9 of the Agreement applies only to those who meet the conventional definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between December 31, 2018 and July 17, 2023; the covered employment income is taxed exclusively in Switzerland. In other cases, the ordinary regime applies, with Italian taxation and a credit for Swiss tax. The 10.000 euros under Article 4 of Law 83/2023 are only the limit for the income referred to in Article 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013, not a general exemption. The Double Taxation Convention between Italy and Switzerland dates back to March 9, 1976. Switzerland is not a member of the European Union or the European Economic Area, so coordination of leave does not take place through EU regulations, but through specific bilateral agreements. For those who work in Ticino and live in Italy, this means that maternity and paternity benefits primarily follow the Swiss system for the employment-related aspect, while the Italian system may intervene for benefits linked to residence.

Operational details

What changes in practice for a new cross-border parent

The first issue to resolve is where to submit the leave request. If the employment relationship is in Switzerland, the IPG daily allowance is paid by the Swiss employer, who then claims it against the competent body. The cross-border worker must therefore follow the Swiss procedure, regardless of whether he resides in Italy. The Italian INPS does not intervene for leave linked to the Swiss employment relationship, except in specific cases of benefits linked to residence or previous Italian contribution periods.

For a cross-border mother, maternity leave in Switzerland includes a period of prohibition from work after giving birth and an allowance equal to a percentage of the salary, financed by the IPG. The father is entitled to paternity leave, which is also funded by the IPG. Parental leave, however, can have different durations and methods depending on whether it is requested in Switzerland or Italy, and here the coordination between the two systems comes into play.

Comparison between the two systems

The substantial difference lies in the fact that the Swiss system links the allowances to the actual salary and contributions paid, while the Italian system provides for amounts and durations defined at a national level. For a cross-border worker, the reference income is the Swiss one, which is generally higher than the Italian border average. This has a direct effect on the amount of benefits: the higher the salary, the higher the IPG benefit.

From a tax perspective, leave benefits received by a cross-border worker follow the rules of Swiss withholding tax. There is no double withholding: Italy applies the tax credit in the EC framework of 730. However, the management of the Italian declaration requires attention, because the Swiss allowances must be indicated correctly to avoid errors in the calculation of the credit.

Another practical aspect concerns health insurance. Cross-border commuters with a G permit have the right to opt for the Swiss KVG or can maintain registration with the Italian National Health Service. During leave, this choice affects access to care for the mother and newborn. Deductibles for adults in Switzerland vary between CHF 300 and CHF 2'500.

Retirement planning should not be overlooked. AVS contributions continue to accrue during leave, because the IPG benefit is subject to contributions. The BVG can also be affected by the leave, depending on the pension fund regulations. For a cross-border worker who thinks about the long term, verifying contribution continuity is essential to avoid creating social security gaps.

Key points

How to move: the step-by-step procedure

For a cross-border worker who is about to become a parent, the operational process is divided into some precise phases. The first is the communication to the Swiss employer: the request for leave must be presented with the notice required by the contract or company regulations. The employer is the person who physically provides the IPG allowance and then claims it against the competent body.

The second phase concerns documentation. You need the birth certificate, confirmation of the employment relationship and, for cross-border workers, proof of the G permit. The documentation must be presented in Switzerland, even if the worker resides in Italy. Any translations or legalizations may be requested depending on the specific case.

Deadlines to meet

Maternity leave in Switzerland starts automatically after giving birth, but the formal request must be made promptly. Paternity leave must be requested within the deadlines established by Swiss legislation. For parental leave, the time windows are wider, but vary depending on whether the Swiss or Italian system applies.

On the tax front, the cross-border worker must remember that the allowances received in Switzerland must be indicated in the Italian declaration in the EC framework of 730, to benefit from the tax credit. Failure to indicate involves the risk of effective double taxation, because Italy may not recognize the credit.

Frequently Asked Questions
Who pays maternity leave for a border worker working in Switzerland?
Maternity leave for a frontier worker in Switzerland is funded by the IPG, whose contribution rate is 5.3% borne by the employee (together with AVS and AI). The allowance is paid by the Swiss employer, who then rivals the competent body. The Italian INPS does not intervene for leave related to the Swiss employment relationship.
Are leave allowances received in Switzerland also taxed in Italy?
No. Border workers' earned income is taxed at source in Switzerland. Italy avoids double taxation through the tax credit, to be indicated in the EC framework of model 730. The leave allowances follow the same principle: no double withholding, but correct indication in the Italian declaration.
What Swiss contributions apply to a border worker's salary?
For border employees, the Swiss contributions are: AVS/AI/IPG at 5.3%, AD/AC at 1.1% with a ceiling of CHF 148'200, LAINF between 0.7% and 1.5%, and LPP between 7% and 18% depending on the age group, from 25 years. These contributions also fund leave allowances.
Can a border worker with a G permit choose Swiss health insurance?
Yes. Border workers with a G permit have the right of option: they can choose the Swiss LAMal or maintain their registration with the Italian National Health Service. Adult deductibles in Switzerland range from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500. During leave, this choice affects access to care for the mother and the newborn.
