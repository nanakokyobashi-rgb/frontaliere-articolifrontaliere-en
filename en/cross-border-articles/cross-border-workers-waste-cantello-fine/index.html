Cross-border worker fined for dumping waste in Cantello (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 15 May 2026

A Swiss citizen was sanctioned for abandoning waste in Italy. Reports of cross-border workers dumping trash across the border are increasing.

Context

In brief

  • A cross-border worker was fined for abandoning waste in Cantello
  • The fine is 100 euros
  • Reports of waste from Switzerland in Italy are increasing

Key facts

  • What: Abandoned waste
  • When: May 14, 2026
  • Where: Cantello, Italy
  • Who: Local police of Cantello
  • Amount: 100 euros

A Swiss citizen was identified and sanctioned for abandoning waste in the territory of Cantello, on the border with the Canton of Ticino. The local police issued a fine of 100 euros for incorrect waste disposal. The man was caught abandoning bags inside a public bin in the town. Thanks to an investigation carried out by the officers, it was possible to trace his identity and notify the sanction.

The episode would not be isolated. Some residents report an increase in waste from Switzerland abandoned in various areas of Varesotto, particularly in the Valsolda area, within the local park of supra-municipal interest of the Valle della Bevere. Among the most frequent cases would be red bags used for waste collection in Canton Ticino, found in Italian bins or along the roads.

Testimonies and surveillance

A testimony collected from residents tells: 'One evening I saw a car with a Ticino license plate stop to unload bags into the containers.' 'I informed the Municipality and was advised to report everything to the local police, also because in the area there are cameras useful for identifying the culprits.' And it is precisely thanks to the surveillance systems that, in at least one case, the sanction was reached.

Operational details

Analysis of the implications

The increase in reports of waste abandoned by cross-border workers raises important questions about waste management and individual responsibility. This phenomenon not only creates environmental problems but can also damage relations between local communities and cross-border workers.

Environmental impact

Abandoned waste has a negative impact on the environment, particularly in border areas where waste management can be more complex. Abandoned waste can contaminate the soil and water, damaging local flora and fauna. Furthermore, it can pose a risk to public health, attracting parasites and animals that can spread diseases.

Relations between communities

Reports of waste abandoned by cross-border workers can create tensions between local communities and cross-border workers. It is important that cross-border workers respect local regulations and collaborate with authorities to maintain a clean and safe environment. Local communities, on the other hand, must be understanding and offer support to cross-border workers to help them understand and respect the regulations.

Individual responsibility

Every individual, regardless of nationality, has the responsibility to properly manage their waste. Cross-border workers must be aware of the differences in regulations between Switzerland and Italy and adopt responsible behaviors to avoid making mistakes. This includes informing themselves about local regulations, following the instructions for proper waste disposal, and collaborating with the authorities if necessary.

Key points

What to do to avoid penalties

To avoid penalties for abandoning waste, cross-border workers must follow some fundamental guidelines. First of all, it is important to inform themselves about the local regulations regarding waste management. Each municipality has its own rules and indications, and cross-border workers must know and respect these regulations to avoid making mistakes.

Inform yourself about local regulations

Cross-border workers can turn to local information services, such as municipalities or associations, to obtain clarifications about the regulations regarding waste management. It is important to know the differences between the various types of waste and the disposal methods provided by the municipality in which they reside or work.

Follow the indications for proper disposal

Once informed about the local regulations, cross-border workers must follow the indications for the proper disposal of waste. This includes separating waste according to the categories provided, using the appropriate containers, and respecting the collection dates. Cross-border workers must also avoid abandoning waste in unauthorized areas and collaborate with the authorities if necessary.

Collaborate with the authorities

In case of error or need for clarification, cross-border workers must collaborate with the local authorities. This includes reporting any problems or doubts regarding waste management and following the authorities' indications to avoid making mistakes. Cross-border workers can also turn to local associations or information services to obtain support and advice.
