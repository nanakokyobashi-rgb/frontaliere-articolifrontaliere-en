On November 29, funding for the 13th AVS pension will go to a vote. In Bern, a broad alliance made up of PS, Centro, Verdi, PEV and USS presented the arguments in favor of an increase in VAT. The news report, bearing the Keystone-ATS byline, presents a common position: without a solid funding solution, the entire AVS risks running into difficulty.

For the cross-border commuter reader, the scope of the news is clear: it concerns AVS and pension funding. These topics therefore cannot be cited as consequences described by the vote. To follow only the pension-related aspect, one can consult rendita AVS.

The proposal on the table

The mechanism put forward by the alliance is an increase described as moderate: 0,4 percentage points in VAT, up to 8,5%. According to Samira Marti, co-president of the Socialist parliamentary group, adequately funding the 13th monthly payment through this measure would provide stability and security for pensions. The National Councillor from Basel-Landschaft also recalled that nine out of ten people receive more from the AVS than they pay in.

Centro, with its president Philipp Matthias Bregy, linked the vote to the AVS's financial sustainability. The 13th AVS pension entails additional annual costs of between 4 and 5 billion francs. The reserves, although still significant, are not unlimited. Moreover, the fund's assets are invested and generate returns that help finance the pensions: if the reserves fall, these returns also decline over the long term.

For this reason, Centro supports the compromise drawn up by parliament. From 2028, the measure should bring in about 1,5 billion francs more each year and slow the erosion of the reserves. Bregy ruled out, however, that the project could cover the funding needs on its own: in his assessment, further solutions will have to come with the AVS 2030 reform. The same alliance therefore presents a yes vote as a stabilization measure, not as the definitive financing of the AVS.

Three levels to keep separate

The first level concerns VAT. Marti argues that food and medicines, described as essential goods, would not be affected by the moderate increase. Hence his conclusion: low and middle incomes would be protected. This is the alliance's wording; it is not an individual calculation for those who work across the border.

Regarding the relationship between costs and benefits, Léonore Porchet estimated that a person with an average income would pay about 85 francs more in VAT each year and, once reaching retirement age, would receive an additional pension of about 2000 francs per year. The vice-president of the Greens acknowledged that the tax system is proportionally more advantageous for higher incomes than an increase in wage contributions, adding, however, that it entails significant compensation.
