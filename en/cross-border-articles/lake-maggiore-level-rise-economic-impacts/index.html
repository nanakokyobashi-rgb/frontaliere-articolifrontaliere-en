Lake Maggiore rise: economic and environmental impacts

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 30 April 2026

The Po River Basin Authority sets the maximum level at 140 cm, but the area fears irreversible damage to the economy and biodiversity.

Context

In brief

  • Lake Maggiore level set at 140 cm above zero hydrometric.
  • 20-30 million cubic meters more for irrigation.
  • Local administrators fear economic and environmental damage.

Key facts

  • What: Rise in Lake Maggiore level.
  • When: April 29, 2026.
  • Where: Lake Maggiore, Varesotto and Piedmont shores.
  • Who: Po River Basin Authority.
  • Amount: 140 cm above zero hydrometric of Sesto Calende.
  • Additional volume: 20-30 million cubic meters for irrigation.

The Po River Basin Authority has set the maximum level of Lake Maggiore at 140 centimeters above the zero hydrometric of Sesto Calende. This decision, made on April 29, 2026, has raised concerns among local administrators of the Varesotto and Piedmont shores of Verbano. Despite the agreement between Italy and Switzerland guaranteeing an additional volume of 20-30 million cubic meters for the irrigation of the plain, the mayors of Verbania, Baveno, and Cannobio have defined the choice as 'erroneous and not shared with the territory'.

Environmental concerns

The environment assessor of Angera, Milo Manica, emphasized the fragility of the wet areas and the need to review the current agricultural model. 'Water storage for crops is often considered, but no one ever thinks about converting these crops,' Manica stated. The rise in the lake level could cause the disappearance of reeds and the loss of historical habitats such as those of Isolino Partegora, already documented in the 19th century and now almost extinct.

Operational details

Impact Analysis

The rise in the water level of Lake Maggiore will have significant consequences for both the environment and the local economy. Historical natural habitats, such as reed beds and Isolino Partegora, could disappear, with serious repercussions for biodiversity. The disappearance of reed beds, for example, would put species like the Lopinga butterfly and Lataste's frog at risk.

Economic Impacts

The tourism sector is one of the most concerned. The reduction of beaches, caused by the rise in the lake's water level, could reduce the tourist attractiveness of Lake Maggiore. Beaches are a determining factor for the local economy, and their reduction could have a negative impact on the revenues of the lakeside municipalities.

Management of the Shores

Managing the shores during periods of high water will be more difficult. The impossibility of retreating vegetation due to the presence of roads and campsites could cause damage to local infrastructure and ecosystems. This could lead to additional costs for the maintenance and protection of the shores.

Reactions of Local Administrations

Local administrations have expressed their opposition to the decision through all available legal and political means. The mayors of Verbania, Baveno, and Cannobio have declared that they will continue to oppose the decision, criticizing the Piedmont Region for not firmly representing the interests of the lakeside municipalities.

Key points

Concrete actions

To address the impacts of the rising water level of Lake Maggiore, it is necessary to adopt concrete measures. Local authorities should work together to develop a sustainable water resource management plan. This plan should include the conversion of unsustainable agricultural crops and the protection of natural habitats.

Steps to follow

1. Impact assessment: Conduct an in-depth study to assess the environmental and economic impacts of the lake's rising water level. 2. Stakeholder engagement: Actively involve stakeholders, including lakeside municipalities, environmental associations, and tourism operators, in planning mitigation measures. 3. Development of a management plan: Develop a sustainable water resource management plan that includes the conversion of agricultural crops and the protection of natural habitats. 4. Implementation of measures: Implement the mitigation measures outlined in the management plan, constantly monitoring the results and making necessary adjustments.

Conclusion

The decision to raise the water level of Lake Maggiore will have significant consequences for the local environment and economy. It is crucial to adopt concrete measures to mitigate negative impacts and ensure a sustainable future for the region. Local authorities must work together to develop a sustainable water resource management plan and protect natural habitats.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the main concerns of local administrators regarding the rise in the level of Lake Maggiore?
The main concerns are the environmental impacts, such as the disappearance of reeds and the loss of historic habitats, and the economic impacts, particularly the reduction of beaches and the difficulty in managing the shores during periods of high water.
What alternative proposals have been suggested to mitigate the impacts of the lake's rising level?
Milo Manica has proposed looking at the experiences of the Ticino Park, where the resource is accumulated underground through the marcite and the recharge of the aquifers. This system would allow the level of the lake and the survival of rare species such as the Littorella to be safeguarded.
