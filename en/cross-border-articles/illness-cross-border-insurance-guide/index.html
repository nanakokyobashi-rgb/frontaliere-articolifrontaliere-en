Context

Choosing Your Health Insurance — LAMal vs CMI

Cross-border workers must exercise the right of option within three months of starting work: choose Swiss LAMal or the Italian National Health Service (SSN) through the CMI exemption. The choice is definitive for the entire uninterrupted cross-border activity.

LAMal — Swiss Health Insurance

With LAMal, residents of Italy pay the EU/EFTA premium calculated for Italy: in 2026 adults aged 26 and over pay CHF 279.00–487.20 per month without accident coverage, according to the insurer. The ordinary deductible is CHF 300 per year plus 10% co-payment up to CHF 700; residents of Italy cannot choose optional deductibles or alternative models. LAMal-insured workers can receive care in Switzerland and in Italy by registering with the ASL using the certificate issued by the Swiss health insurer and paying only the Italian co-payment (ticket), if any.

CMI — Italian National Health Service

The CMI option keeps you in the Italian National Health Service (SSN). For cross-border workers under the new regime (hired from 17 July 2023) there is no specific health contribution; for “old” cross-border workers (active between 31 December 2018 and 17 July 2023, taxed only in Switzerland), Law 213/2023 provides for a 3–6% share of net Swiss salary, with a minimum of 30 and a maximum of 200 euros for each month worked (Lombardy chose 3%). In Switzerland, the European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) covers medically necessary care during a stay: CHF 92 every 30 days of care, or CHF 33 for minors. Planned care requires prior ASL authorization (S2); work accidents and occupational diseases remain covered by the Swiss employer’s accident insurance.

Operational details

Sick Pay, Dismissal Protection, and Workplace Accidents

Understanding your rights when illness strikes is essential, because Swiss rules directly affect your income and job security.

Sick Pay — The Bernese Scale and IJM Insurance

Swiss law provides continued salary payment during illness, with the duration depending on years of service. The widely used Bernese scale (Berner Skala) provides:

  • 1st year of service: 3 weeks at full pay
  • 2nd year: 1 month
  • 3rd–4th year: 2 months
  • 5th–9th year: 3 months
  • 10th–14th year: 4 months
  • 15th–19th year: 5 months
  • 20+ years: 6 months

Many employers subscribe to IJM insurance (daily sickness allowance insurance), which may provide 80% of salary for up to 720 days. The employee contribution is around 0.8% of salary, but the rate varies by employer and contract; check your employment contract to confirm the applicable regime.

You must present a medical certificate (certificato medico) from the 3rd day of absence. Some employers require it from the 1st day — review your company regulations.

Dismissal Protection During Illness

Swiss law provides protection against dismissal during illness, but it is time-limited:

  • 1st year of service: 30 days of protection
  • 2nd–5th year: 90 days
  • 6th year and beyond: 180 days

During these periods, your employer cannot terminate your contract. A dismissal issued during a protected period is void, subject to the applicable conditions.

Workplace Accidents and Occupational Diseases — LAINF Coverage

If you are injured at work or develop an occupational disease, you are covered by LAINF, the Swiss accident insurance: premiums for occupational accidents are paid by the employer, while those for non-occupational accidents may be deducted from the employee’s salary. LAINF provides:

  • 80 % of your insured salary from the 3rd day of incapacity
  • Full coverage of medical costs related to the accident or disease
  • Disability pensions for permanent impairment
  • Death benefits for survivors

Commuting accidents are covered as non-professional accidents for employees working at least 8 hours per week for the same employer. Below that threshold, an accident on the commute is considered professional. Report every accident promptly to your employer.

Rare and Chronic Diseases and Parental Leave

Accessing Care for Rare or Chronic Conditions

Cross-border workers with rare diseases or complex chronic conditions can access relevant specialist care under their chosen system. With LAMal, care is available in Switzerland and Italy; in Italy, register with the ASL using the certificate issued by the Swiss health insurer and pay only the Italian co-payment (ticket), if any. The ordinary participation applies in Switzerland: CHF 300 deductible plus 10% co-payment up to CHF 700 per year.

If you chose the SSN option, planned care in Switzerland requires prior ASL authorization (S2); the EHIC covers medically necessary care during a stay. Work accidents and occupational diseases remain covered by the Swiss employer’s accident insurance.

LAMal for residents of Italy: care access and insurance models

For chronic conditions, LAMal allows treatment both in Switzerland and in Italy, where care goes through the ASL and only the Italian co-payment (ticket), if any, is due. Residents of Italy cannot choose family-doctor, HMO or Telmed models, nor optional deductibles. Compare the insurers’ premiums with the insurance comparator.

Maternity and Paternity Leave

Swiss maternity and paternity provisions apply to cross-border workers:

  • Maternity leave: 14 weeks (98 days) at 80 % of salary, up to a maximum of 220 CHF per day.
  • Paternity leave: 14 daily allowances within 6 months of the birth, at 80 % of salary, up to a maximum of 220 CHF per day.

The conditions are distinct: the parent must have been insured under AVS during the nine months immediately before the birth and must have worked for at least five months during that period. Use the parental leave guide for the wider rules.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the compulsory health coverage for cross-border workers in Switzerland?
Cross-border workers must choose Swiss LAMal or the Italian National Health Service (SSN) through the CMI exemption. The formal request is due within three months of starting work; without it, Swiss insurance remains mandatory retroactively.
What is the process for changing from LAMal to CMI or vice versa after the initial choice?
The choice is definitive throughout uninterrupted cross-border activity: a change of employer or canton and a change in income or premium do not reopen it. It reopens only when Swiss work resumes after subjection in another state or when the worker becomes a pension beneficiary. For new family members after marriage or birth, a LAMal-insured person may request exemption within three months of the event.
How does the reimbursement of healthcare costs work in the event of an emergency in Switzerland?
With LAMal, care is available in Switzerland and Italy; in Italy, register with the ASL using the certificate issued by the Swiss health insurer and pay only the Italian co-payment (ticket), if any. With the SSN option, the EHIC covers medically necessary care during a stay in Switzerland with CHF 92 every 30 days, or CHF 33 for minors; planned care requires prior ASL authorization (S2).
