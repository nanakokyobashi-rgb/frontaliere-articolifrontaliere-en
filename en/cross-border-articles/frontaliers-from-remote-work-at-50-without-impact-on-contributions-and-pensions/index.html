Who are the beneficiaries?

The measure concerns cross-border workers who work 50% remotely: this means that at least 50% of their income must come from activities carried out remotely. The list may include independent professionals, consultants, technicians and other workers who provide services remotely.

How much does this exemption cost?

The exemption from withholding tax has no cost for cross-border workers. This means they do not have to pay tax on their wages, as long as they continue to work 50% remotely.

When does it come into force and where?

The new measure comes into force on 1 January 2024, for all of Italian-speaking Switzerland and the Canton of Ticino. In other words, cross-border workers resident in Ticino will continue to be exempt from withholding tax, regardless of their activities or place of residence.

How it works

To benefit from the exemption, cross-border workers resident in Ticino must continue to work 50% remotely. This means that at least 50% of their income must come from remote activities.

What will happen to contributions and pensions?

The exemption from withholding tax will not affect the contributions and pensions of cross-border workers. These will be calculated on the basis of their total income, not considering the withholding tax exemption.

Specific rules

The exemption from withholding tax applies to salaries paid to cross-border workers who have cantonal residence and are normally resident in Ticino. The measure does not include wages paid to cross-border workers with residence abroad who work remotely in the Canton of Ticino.

Key points

REMOTE FRONT-BORDERS AT 50% WITHOUT IMPACT ON CONTRIBUTIONS AND PENSIONS

To benefit from the exemption from withholding tax, cross-border workers who work 50% remotely in Ticino must verify that they have no other income that is subject to the tax and submit their tax return in which the exemption will be applied.

For example, a cross-border worker resident in Ticino with a net income of 50,000 gross francs per month, who works 50% remotely, will not pay withholding tax if he has no other income. However, if you earn another income from work, such as a freelance activity or a financial activity, you will have to pay it regularly.

To check whether he has other income, the cross-border worker can consult his tax liabilities or contact the relevant Labor Office.

Additionally, it is important to note that the exemption does not apply to cross-border workers who work 100% remotely. For these workers, the Lugano Labor Office has provided a circular explaining how it will be possible to determine whether they are exempt or not, taking into account their application of the regular employment conditions and their incomes.

For a cross-border worker who works 100% remotely, for example, if he earns 60,000 gross francs per month and has another work income of 10,000 gross francs per month, the Lugano Labor Office will be able to determine that the cross-border worker has other income and will not be able to benefit from the exemption.

In this case, the cross-border worker will have to regularly pay withholding tax on the 10,000 gross francs of additional income calculated on the basis of the tax return submitted.

For cross-border workers who work 50% remotely, it is also possible to request the application of the exemption for contributions and pensions, but it is necessary to provide further evidence that it is actually 50% remote working, such as, for example, registration with a travel agency or registration with a bank for remote financial transactions.

To do all this, cross-border commuters can consult the business register, tax liabilities and request quotes or invoices from independent professionals accredited at the relevant Labor Office.

In summary, to benefit from the exemption from withholding tax and not pay tax on other incomes, cross-border workers who work 50% remotely in Ticino must present their tax return and provide further proof that it is actually 50% remote working.

REGULATION WITH DATES AND AMOUNTS

The circular from the Lugano Labor Office, regarding the application of the exemption for cross-border workers who work 100% remotely, was published on the Lugano Labor Office website on 1 April 2022. The circular applies to tax returns submitted from 1 May 2022 to 31 December 2022.

The withholding tax due for a cross-border worker who works 50% remotely and who has other income is calculated at 12.5% on the difference between the net income and the gross minimum wage. For example, for a cross-border worker with a gross net income of 50,000 francs gross per month, the withholding tax due is 6,250 francs (50,000 - 8,300 x 12.5%).

For cross-border workers who work 100% remotely, however, the withholding tax due is calculated at 12.5% ​​on all sums paid or paid for tax purposes, including sums paid for contributions and pensions, and not on a single part of the income as for cross-border workers who work 50% remotely.

For example, for a cross-border worker with a gross net income of 60,000 francs gross per month and with contributions and pensions of 10,000 francs gross per month, the withholding tax due is 7,500 francs (60,000 + 10,000 - 8,300 x 12.5%).

The circular from the Lugano Labor Office specifies that the exemption is not valid for cross-border workers who work 100% remotely and that the Lugano Labor Office will determine whether or not they are exempt taking into account their application of regular employment conditions and their incomes.
