On 23 December 2020, the New Cross-Border Agreement between Italy and Switzerland was signed. Italy ratified it with Law 83 of 13 June 2023 and the text entered into force on 1 January 2024. For those who live in Merone and work in the Canton of Ticino as cross-border workers, these dates define the framework to be verified before evaluating a transfer.

The dates that separate the two regimes

The threshold indicated is 17 July 2023. The threshold of 10,000 euros is not a general exemption: art. 4 of Law 83/2023 limits it to the income referred to in the art. 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013. The first check therefore concerns one's position on the established date, not just the place of residence or the route to work.

On income from work the rule indicated is clear: for cross-border workers the withholding tax is withheld in Switzerland. Italy avoids double taxation through the tax credit reported in the EC framework of 730. The step concerns the tax return and can be explored in detail in the tax return, without transforming the credit into a second taxation of the same income.

The Italy-Switzerland Convention against double taxation was signed on 9 March 1976. Switzerland is not a member of the EU or the EEA: this reference must be distinguished from the agreement which entered into force in 2024.

Swiss tax rates, including federal and cantonal ones, are established by federal and cantonal laws. At the federal level, the administration is the responsibility of the AFC/ESTV, while the cantonal tax administrations also intervene. For Merone's reader, the verification therefore starts from the classification of the regime and from the reading of the Swiss pay slip together with the Italian tax framework.

The cost of insurance and the deductible must therefore be kept separate in the spending plan: they are healthcare elements, not AVS deductions or withholding tax. Frequently Asked Questions
Who qualifies for the transitional regime in Article 9?
Article 9’s transitional regime applies only to workers who meet the Agreement’s definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between 31 December 2018 and 17 July 2023; covered employment income is taxable exclusively in Switzerland. The ordinary regime applies in other cases, with Italian taxation and credit for Swiss tax where applicable. The €10,000 in Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is only the limit for income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law 147/2013, not a general allowance.
