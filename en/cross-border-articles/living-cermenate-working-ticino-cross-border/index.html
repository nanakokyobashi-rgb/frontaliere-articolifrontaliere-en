Cermenate, a municipality in the province of Como, is among the most natural locations for those looking to work in Ticino while maintaining Italian living costs. The proximity to the Ticino border, the economic fabric of the area and the availability of jobs in the Swiss sector make this area a point of reference for cross-border commuters.

For those who choose to live in Cermenate and work in Ticino as cross-border workers, the regulatory context has changed significantly starting from 1 January 2024, with the entry into force of the New Cross-border Workers Agreement signed between Italy and Switzerland in December 2020. This agreement introduced new tax and social security rules which directly impact the pay slip, refunds and the choice between insurance systems.

What does it mean to live in Italy and work in Ticino?

The G permit is valid for five years with a contract lasting more than one year; with a shorter contract it lasts as long as the contract. The G Permit is a prerequisite for legally operating on the Swiss labor market. It is not possible to work in Ticino without this document.

Unlike those who reside in Ticino, the Italian cross-border worker pays taxes directly in Switzerland via withholding tax, and Italy does not apply a second tax on the same income. Coordination between the two countries takes place through the Italian-Swiss Convention of 9 March 1976 and the tax credit in the EC framework of the Italian declaration (form 730).

As for social security contributions, the cross-border worker pays AVS/AI/IPG (5.3% dependent on dependents), AD/AC (1.1%, with a cap of CHF 148,200), LAINF (0.7–1.5%) and LPP (7–18% depending on the age group, from the 25th year). At the same time, Italy coordinates with INPS, but the coordination base is Switzerland. This means that your Ticino social security position is a priority: the AVS is registered by the Swiss federal body, while the INPS receives a coordination communication.

Operational details

Tax and social security analysis: from withholding to refunds

The New Frontier Agreement, applied since 1 January 2024, has modified the tax regime and the rights to refunds (reimbursement) that each border crossing may request. The two main categories are well distinguished and produce significant practical effects on economic availability.

Old and new frontier workers: the post-2024 tax regime

Old cross-border workers remain taxed only in Switzerland (Art. 9 of the 2020 Agreement). This means an automatic reduction of the tax base every year for the next ten years, with direct effect on the cantonal taxation of Ticino.

The €10,000 limit is not a general allowance: Article 4 of Italian Law No. 83/2023 applies it only to the income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law No. 147/2013. “Old frontier workers” qualify under Article 9 only if they met the tax requirements and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais during the stated period; covered employment income is taxable exclusively in Switzerland. Old cross-border workers (Art. 9 of the Agreement) are taxed on their salary only in Switzerland; the €10,000 IRPEF allowance has applied since 2024 to cross-border workers whose income is taxed in Italy. 83/2023 applies only to income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law No. 147/2013. The exemption applies to Swiss taxable income before calculation of cantonal source tax.

Refunds are the procedure by which frontier workers recover the taxes withheld in Switzerland that were not due (or were due to a lesser extent). Italy grants the tax credit to avoid double taxation.

Key points

Practical Procedure: What to Do Before and After the Transfer

A transfer to Cermenate to work in Ticino requires a sequential planning on four fronts: documents, administrative registrations, insurance choices, and tax obligations. The procedure is not complex, but times must be respected to avoid interruptions of coverage or fines.

Step 1: Verify the Permesso G

Before looking for work in Ticino, it is necessary to check the possibility of obtaining the Permesso G. This document is issued by the cantonal authorities of Ticino (migration office) and requires: a work contract in Ticino (or a written offer from the employer), residence in Cermenate (Italian anagrafe registration), mandatory insurance (LAMal for border workers, or coordination with INPS).

Step 2: Anagrafe and Residence Registration in Cermenate

The transfer must be communicated to the Comune of Cermenate within the times provided by Italian regulations (usually 8-10 days after moving). The residence certificate is mandatory to request the Permesso G.

Step 3: Choice of Health Insurance (LAMal vs INPS)

Border workers with a Permesso G have the right to opt: they can choose to be subject to Swiss LAMal (health) or remain under Italian INPS. This choice directly affects costs and health coverage. LAMal (Switzerland) offers complete coverage in Switzerland and Italy (reciprocity EU), with adult deductibles CHF 300-2,500 and variable costs depending on age, canton, and coverage choice. It is recommended if you work stably in Ticino. INPS (Italy) guarantees coordination with Switzerland and extended Italian coverage in reciprocity; it is recommended if you frequently visit Italy or plan to return. The choice must be communicated within the required terms (usually within 30 days of starting work or changing situation) and is not reversible during the year, or with administrative costs.

Step 4: Opening a Bank Account and Salary Transfer

It is recommended to open a bank account that supports CHF-EUR transactions for salary payments. Many Italian banks offer accounts with this service; some Swiss banks have branches in Italy or specific services for border workers. Check with your bank the commission costs on CHF-EUR conversion and the time of crediting.

Step 5: Registration with the Italian Revenue Agency and Declaration of Border Workers

Within the year (February-May, depending on the deadline 730), it is mandatory to submit the income statement (model 730 or Redditi PF) indicating the Swiss income and qualifying as a border worker in the CE box. This allows you to benefit from rebates and tax coordination. Many Italian accountants familiar with the CE box are recommended to entrust one specializing in border workers.

Step 6: Application of the Deduction and Rebates

The €10,000 limit is not a general allowance: Article 4 of Italian Law No. 83/2023 applies it only to the income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law No. 147/2013. In subsequent years, check the rebates elaborated (usually credited together with the 730 refund in June-July).

The €10,000 threshold in Article 4 of Italian Law No. 83/2023 applies exclusively to employment income earned continuously and exclusively by Italian residents in a foreign border area or neighbouring country, as referred to in Article 1(175) of Law No. 147/2013; it is neither a general allowance nor an exemption from Swiss withholding tax.

Frequently Asked Questions
Can I live in Cermenate and work legally in Ticino?
Yes, through Permit G (border). This document, issued by the Ticino cantonal authorities, is mandatory to work in Ticino while maintaining residence in Italy. The G permit is valid for five years with a contract of more than one year; with a shorter contract it lasts as long as the contract. Without Permit G, the work is illegal and subject to penalties.
Where will I pay taxes if I live in Cermenate and work in Ticino?
Taxes are withheld in Switzerland (via withholding tax on the paycheck). Italy does not apply a second tax on the same income, thanks to the Italian-Swiss Convention of 9 March 1976 and the coordination through the EC framework of declaration 730. There is no double taxation.
Which income does the €10,000 limit apply to?
Article 9’s transitional regime applies only to workers who meet the Agreement’s definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between 31 December 2018 and 17 July 2023; covered employment income is taxable exclusively in Switzerland. The ordinary regime applies in other cases, with Italian taxation and credit for Swiss tax where applicable. The €10,000 in Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is only the limit for income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law 147/2013, not a general allowance. Do I have to choose between LAMal (Switzerland) and INPS (Italy) for health insurance?
Yes. As a G border crossing, you have the right of option: you can join the Swiss LAMal (full coverage in CH and IT, CHF 300-2,500 deductibles for adults) or stay with Italian INPS (coordination with CH in reciprocity). The choice must be communicated within 30 days of the start of work and remains binding for the year.
What social security contributions to border workers in Ticino?
As an employee in Ticino, you pay: AVS/AI/IPG 5.3%, AD/AC 1.1% (with cap CHF 148.200), LAINF 0.7–1.5%, LPP (second pillar) 7–18% according to age from 25th year. These are paid by Switzerland. Italy coordinates through INPS, but the primary payment is Swiss and guarantees social security coverage even when returning to Italy.
