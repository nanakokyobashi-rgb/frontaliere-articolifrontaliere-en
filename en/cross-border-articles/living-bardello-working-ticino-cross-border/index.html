Those who reside in Bardello with Malgesso and Bregano and work in the Canton of Ticino access the Swiss job market with the status of category G cross-border workers. The New Cross-border Workers Agreement, applied from 1 January 2024, regulates the tax and contribution regime for all cross-border workers between Italy and Switzerland. A crucial aspect concerns the place of tax withholding: the withholding tax is withheld in Switzerland from the worker's paycheck, not in Italy. This mechanism avoids double taxation thanks to the tax credit in the Italian 730 model (CE framework), where the declared income can benefit from an offsetting compared to the taxes already paid in Switzerland. Italy recognizes Swiss taxation as primary for income from work carried out in Swiss territory.

Transitional regime: old and new cross-border workers

The New Cross-border Agreement distinguishes between two categories of cross-border workers based on the start date of the cross-border activity. This transitional protection guarantees fiscal continuity during the transition to the new regime. The threshold of 10,000 euros of the art. 4 of Law 83/2023 concerns exclusively income from employment carried out abroad, in a border area or in a neighboring country, continuously and exclusively, by people resident in Italy, referred to in the art. 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013; it is not a general exemption nor an exemption from Swiss withholding tax. The difference of €2,500 between the two categories reflects the legislator's effort to mitigate the tax impact on historic cross-border commuters, while establishing a higher threshold amount for new ones. For those who reside in Bardello and move to Ticino after this date, the tax return procedure must accurately record the date of starting work with the Swiss employer, since from this moment the contribution obligations and access to the respective exemption begin.

G Permit: procedure and requirements

Access to employed work in Ticino requires the G Permit (border workers), issued by the Swiss Secretariat of State for Migration (SEM). The G Permit is strictly linked to the employment relationship: the procedure requires the employer to submit the request to the SEM providing documentation on the contract, remuneration and duration of employment. Those who reside in Bardello must guarantee stable residence in Italy (proven by a registry certificate) and absence of relevant criminal records. The G Permit is valid for the entire Canton of Ticino and for any transfers of the employer within Swiss borders, with an administrative cost and processing times managed by the SEM. The permit is valid for the entire duration of the employment contract and is automatically renewed if the professional relationship persists.

Operational details

Cross-border worker pay slip: what changes

The pay slip of a cross-border worker in Ticino includes deductions for Swiss contributions which have no parallel in Italian salaries. The AVS/AI/IPG contributions (pension, disability, unemployment) amount to 5.3% paid by the employee, while the employer pays additional contributions. In addition to these, there are contributions for accident insurance (LAINF) in the amount of 0.7–1.5% and, for employees aged 25 and over, contributions for the second pillar (LPP) varying from 7% to 18% depending on the age group. The total Swiss payroll withholdings are significant, but must be understood in the context of the right to the Italian tax credit. When the cross-border worker fills out the Italian form 730 declaring the gross income received in Switzerland (converted into EUR at the average annual exchange rate), he applies the Italian federal taxes based on the IRPEF progression (23% up to €28,000, 33% between €28,001 and €50,000, 43% over €50,000 for the first national bracket). However, thanks to the tax credit in the EC framework, the taxes already paid in Switzerland are deducted from the Italian tax due, effectively eliminating double taxation.

Let's imagine a hypothetical scenario: a cross-border worker with a gross salary of CHF 5,000 per month in Ticino suffers Swiss deductions of around CHF 400–450 per month (AVS/AI, LAINF, LPP, insurance), receiving a net amount of around CHF 4,550–4,600. In Italy, declaring the gross income in EUR annually (for example, approximately €4,800–4,900 average gross), the initial IRPEF would be approximately €1,000–1,200. However, in the EC framework of 730, the taxpayer applies the tax credit equal to the Swiss taxes already paid (calculated on the same income basis), reducing or canceling the net Italian tax. The result is that the cross-border worker does not pay double taxes, but only one tax in Switzerland, as provided for by the Italian-Swiss Convention signed on 9 March 1976.

KVG: insurance obligations and options

KVG (Swiss compulsory health insurance) is mandatory for all workers active in Switzerland. Category G cross-border commuters have the right to choose: they can enroll in Swiss health insurance or maintain Italian coverage with foreign protection, provided that the Italian insurance is recognized and adequately covers Swiss territory. If you choose to remain insured in Italy, the coverage must extend to the area of ​​work (Ticino) and residence (Varese). If you opt for Swiss insurance, the minimum deductible for adults is CHF 300, with maximum deductibles of up to CHF 2,500. The choice must be made within the terms established by the first registration and communicated to the employer for administrative processing.

Key points

Procedure: how to start working as a cross-border worker

Step 1: Sign an employment contract with a Ticino company. Before any administrative step, it is necessary to have a binding offer of employment from a Swiss employer in Ticino. The contract must specify duties, remuneration, duration (determined or indefinite) and start of work. This document is essential for applying for the G Permit at the SEM.

Step 2: Submit an application for Permit G. The Ticino employer submits an application for authorization to the SEM attaching the employment contract, cross-border worker's personal data extract, passport/ID, and documentation on the economic stability of the company. Processing times vary, but SEM typically reports results within 4-8 weeks. While waiting, the cross-border worker CANNOT start working in Switzerland.

Step 3: AVS registration and KVG registration. Once the G Permit has been obtained, the employer proceeds with the mandatory registration with the Federal Office of Statistics (BFS) for the issue of the AVS number (Swiss social insurance number), and with the choice of health insurance (Swiss option or maintenance of Italian coverage). The AVS number is communicated to the cross-border worker within a few days and must be provided to the employer for payroll processing.

Step 4: Communication to the Italian INPS. Before the first salary payment, the cross-border worker communicates the new activity to the Italian INPS through a specific form, declaring start of work, employer, estimated salary. This allows INPS to register the position and coordinate it with Swiss payments to avoid duplication of contributions.

Step 5: Annual tax return. Every year, by 30 April, the cross-border worker fills out the Italian form 730 declaring the gross income received in Switzerland (converted into EUR), applying the tax credit in the EC framework. At the same time, if due, pay the balance of the national tax. Frequently Asked Questions
If I work in Ticino and reside in Bardello, do I pay taxes in both countries?
No. Tax at source is only withheld in Switzerland on the paycheck. Italy avoids double taxation through the tax credit in model 730 (EC framework). You declare the gross income received in Switzerland, apply the credit for Swiss taxes already paid, and the result is a single taxation, not a double one.
Which income is covered by the €10,000 limit?
