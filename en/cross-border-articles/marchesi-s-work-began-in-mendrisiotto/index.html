The transition to the new role

Piero Marchesi's work should start from Mendrisiotto. This is the request of Lorenzo Cavagliotti, who opens his speech with best wishes to the new State Councilor. Marchesi inherits a Department grappling with complex and crucial issues for the economic and territorial development of the Canton.

In the text, the first indication of priorities comes from the words attributed to the new Director:

«The valleys and peripheral regions».

The comment interprets this choice as a possible reduction in traffic and mobility in the emergency ranking. For Mendrisiotto, the author observes, it would be a negative start.

The next node concerns the motorway network. Marchesi has publicly argued that motorways are a pillar of Swiss infrastructure. During the 2023 campaign, in his role in Bern, he supported the strengthening of the national network. The project, Cavagliotti recalls, was opposed by all the municipalities of Mendrisiotto and the affair was accompanied by a defeat for the UDC parliamentary group and for Minister Albert Roesti.

The author then recalls the Collina d'Oro - Bioggio route, which crosses the Vedeggio plain. The description is highly critical: no improvements to the landscape and territory, 250 million francs in cost and major traffic inconveniences, attributed to the duration of the construction site. In the same passage USTRA, POLUME and MEME are mentioned.

The picture ends with the recent vote of the Grand Council in support of a resolution which aims to clarify and evaluate the noise and environmental impact of MEME. It is on this terrain, between mobility, territory and quality of life, that the intervention asks the new State Councilor to measure the work in the Mendrisiotto.

Operational details

The point for those who cross the border

A concrete but circumscribed connection

For the border worker, the relevance of the news is concrete only in terms of territorial mobility. The source talks about congestion, increasing traffic, pollution, environmental load and quality of life. However, it does not indicate schedules, queues, closures, detours, individual costs or routes. For this reason, the intervention cannot be transformed into a guide to the daily journey.

The perimeter is also net in tax and social security terms. The text does not announce news on G permit, AVS, LPP, LAMal, rebates, source tax, INPS or double taxation. The source therefore does not allow to link the new appointment to a change in these matters. For the border worker working in the Canton of Ticino, the news concerns the way in which the territory is governed, not a change already communicated to his personal position.

The before/after comparison should be read as a political comparison. Before the new assignment, Marchesi supported the strengthening of the national highway network; now the author asks him to make the Mendrisiotto a priority and to review support for indiscriminate strengthening. There is no decision announced to change traffic: it is a call to change the order of priorities.

If mobility remained subordinate to the other priorities, the problem reported by the author would remain open. If, on the other hand, large projects were accompanied by local listening and evaluation of the quality of life, the choice could be measured together on mobility, economic development and protection of the territory. These are the three criteria indicated in the text; neither promises nor results already obtained are promised.

For those looking for operational data, it is advisable to separate the reading of the article from the consultation of the travel tools: tempi di attesa ai valichi and storico del traffico ai valichi do not replace the source and do not add a forecast on Mendrisiotto to it.

Key points

From news to verification

A four-step procedure

1. Start with the exact object. The appointment of Marchesi and the vote of the Grand Council are elements reported in the source; the call to make Mendrisiotto a priority, stop USTRA and review support for the motorway network is instead the author's position.

2. Keep projects separate. The 250 million francs refer to the Collina d 'Oro - Bioggio section, together with the inconvenience attributed to the duration of the construction site. For MEME, the source cites only the resolution asking for clarification and assessment of the sound and environmental impact. Do not pass on the cost or effects of one project to the other.

3. Do not fill in the missing data. The text does not provide an operational date, a closure, an alternative itinerary, a journey time, or a wait at a crossing. These elements cannot be derived from the political appeal nor attributed to the resolution of the Grand Council.

4. Follow the debate through the questions indicated in the article: will Mendrisiotto be heard and involved? Will mobility be considered together with economic development and the protection of the territory? The reference to the coming years describes a challenge, not an administrative deadline.

For the border worker, this separation avoids confusing a discussion about infrastructure with topics that the source does not address. The waiting time and traffic tools can be consulted separately, without presenting them as article data. To keep mobility distinct from personal economic situation, use the calcolatore stipendio.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the priority indicated by the new State Councillor Piero Marchesi?
According to the source, Marchesi indicated that his priorities are the valleys and peripheral regions of the Canton, a signal that could shift attention away from large national mobility projects towards the more peripheral areas.
What is the cost quoted for the Collina d 'Oro - Bioggio route and what is it linked to?
The source reports that the Collina d 'Oro - Bioggio section has a cost of 250 million francs and is associated with inconvenience to traffic due to the duration of the construction site, without significant improvements to the landscape and territory.
What did the Grand Council ask about the MEME project?
The Grand Council voted a resolution calling for clarification and assessment of the sound and environmental impact of MEMES, clearly distinguishing this project from other infrastructure interventions and their costs.
