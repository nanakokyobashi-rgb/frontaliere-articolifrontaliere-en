Cross-Border Workers, 'Health Tax' in Place by September

By Laura Bianchi (editorial byline of the newsroom, AI-generated text) · Published on 25 April 2026 · Updated on 8 November 2026

Lombardy Region confirms the timeframe for the economic contribution that will support the salaries of healthcare personnel in border areas with Switzerland.

Context

TL;DR

  • The Lombardy Region introduces a contribution of 3% on the net salary of cross-border workers to support the salaries of health personnel.
  • Doctors and nurses working in the border areas with Switzerland will receive a 20% salary increase.
  • The contribution will be used to guarantee and strengthen health in border areas.
  • The measure was approved despite criticism from cross-border workers and their union representatives.

Key facts

  • Contribution: 3% of the net salary
  • Salary increase: 20% for doctors and nurses
  • Destination of contribution: Support the salaries of healthcare personnel
  • Areas affected: Border areas with Switzerland
  • Effective date: By September
  • Total investment: Approximately 45 million euros
  • Number of workers affected: Approximately 7,000

The Chiasso customs announced that the Italian legislation to increase the salary of doctors and nurses working in the areas bordering Switzerland will come into force in the coming months. The announcement comes directly from the Lombardy Region. The measure derives from state law 213 of 2023 and was made operational by the decree of the Ministry of Health of 14 November 2025 which regulates the sharing of healthcare costs for cross-border workers in Switzerland. "The objective is to implement it by September. This is an important measure, in application of a state law, which has the aim of retaining healthcare workers in our territory, avoiding their transfer to Switzerland". The Lombardy Region councilor for local authorities and mountains, Massimo Sertori, said this on Thursday during the hearing in the VIII Commission of the Regional Council. “Doctors and nurses who work in the Lombardy areas on the border with Switzerland will obtain a 20% salary increase”, quantifiable as approximately 10,000 euros gross per year for doctors and 5,400 euros for nurses. According to what was reported at the hearing, the workers affected would be around 7,000, for a total investment of around 45 million euros. "State law - continued Sertori - provides that the resources are obtained with a contribution of 3% to 6% of the net salary paid by the so-called "old" cross-border workers. Lombardy has opted for the minimum threshold of 3%", Sertori himself reports. In essence, for a net monthly salary of 4,000 euros - explains the Lombard politician - the payment will be 120 euros per month; funds that will be used to guarantee and strengthen healthcare in the border areas, therefore for the benefit of the cross-border workers themselves and their families". The ministerial decree in fact provides for a range between 3% and 6% of the net salary, with minimum and maximum monthly limits established by the law. But it is precisely this contribution that has sparked a strong controversy, Sertori himself explains that Switzerland has not provided tax data. The measure, renamed by many "health tax", has therefore provoked a harsh reaction from cross-border workers and their representatives unions, who consider it an unfair levy on workers. In recent weeks the issue has returned to the center of the regional political conflict, until the vote of the Lombardy Council on 21-22 April, when the request for total repeal put forward by the centre-left oppositions was rejected. It is in this climate that the issue also entered the electoral campaign for the municipal elections of 24 and 25 May 2026, especially in the border territories of Lombardy and Piedmont, where the number of cross-border workers is high and the discontent is particularly visible. The issue also risks weighing on the relations between the electorate and the centre-right parties, the same ones that supported the law in the Region. The point, however, is not just political. You can even agree to pay but only in exchange for a public health service capable of providing rapid and concrete responses. If, however, the contribution is added to waiting lists lasting months, if not years, and still forces citizens and families to turn to the private sector for visits and tests - they reason - then that levy risks being perceived as yet another tax with no real return. The real issue, therefore, is not just how much you pay, but what you receive in return.

Operational details

The impact of the new measure on health for cross-border commuters is significant. The 20% salary increase for doctors and 5% for nurses represents a significant improvement, but is accompanied by a 3% increase in their net salary. This means that cross-border workers who earn 4,000 euros net per month will have to pay 120 euros per month as a contribution. This contribution will be used to support the salaries of healthcare personnel in border areas, ensuring a quality healthcare service. However, the strong controversy surrounding this measure has led to a negative reaction from cross-border workers and their trade union representatives. The health tax was considered an unfair tax on workers, and the issue also found space in the electoral campaign for the municipal elections of 24 and 25 May 2026. The issue became a point of discussion between the electorate and center-right parties, which supported the law. The reaction of cross-border workers was motivated by the fact that the contribution might not lead to an immediate improvement in the public health service, but rather to double taxation. This led to a reflection on how cross-border workers perceive the value of the public health service and their desire for a faster and more concrete service.

Law 213/2023, art. 1, paragraphs 237–238, does not subject all cross-border workers without distinction. This includes: (a) residents of Italy who work and stay in Switzerland and use the SSN; (b) the cross-border workers referred to in art. 9, par. 1, of the Italy-Switzerland Agreement, with taxable incomes only in Switzerland, who have exercised the right of option for health insurance; and (c) dependent family members of the two categories. Paragraph 238 provides for a progressive quota of 3–6% of the Swiss net salary, with a minimum of €30 and an ordinary maximum of €200 for each month worked from 2024. The ceiling can be doubled in cases of non-payment or non-communication provided for by the art. 1, paragraph 100, Law 207/2024. The Region of residence determines the rates and methods every year.
