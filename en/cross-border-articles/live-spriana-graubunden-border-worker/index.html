G Permit: legal basis for cross-border employment

The G Permit is the legal authorization that allows an Italian citizen residing in Italy to work as an employee in Switzerland. It is requested from the cantonal migration authority of the canton where the work is performed. With a contract lasting more than one year, the permit is valid for five years; with a contract lasting less than one year, it is valid for the duration of the contract.

Once the G Permit has been obtained and work has begun, the cross-border worker is legally required to register with the Swiss social security system (AVS, Old-Age and Survivors' Insurance) from the first day of employment. AVS/AI/IPG contributions amount to 5.3% of gross income and are borne by the employee; the employer pays an additional 5.3%. In addition to AVS, the cross-border worker is required—or may be exempt, for some categories—to enroll in the second pillar, the Occupational Pension Scheme (LPP). LPP contributions vary by age bracket: from age 25 onward, the annual percentage is between 7% and 18% of insured income, paid jointly by the employee and employer.

The employer also pays contributions for accident insurance (LAINF, 0.7%-1.5%) and unemployment insurance (AD/AC, 1.1% up to CHF 148.200 in annual salary). Health insurance (LAMal) in Switzerland is legally mandatory; however, a cross-border worker residing in Italy may exercise the right of option to retain Italian health coverage (public SSN) or enroll in private Swiss LAMal. Adult deductibles in Switzerland range from CHF 300 to CHF 2.500 per year; the choice must be made within 30 days of the first employment contract.

Operational details

Accumulation of AVS pension rights and Italy-Switzerland coordination

An important aspect is that the AVS contributions paid in Switzerland credit Swiss pension rights for the cross-border worker, even if he is permanently resident in Italy. The 1976 Italy-Switzerland Convention guarantees coordination between the two social security systems: contributions paid in one country are recognized in the other for the calculation of the pension income. Once the cross-border worker reaches retirement age, he receives an AVS pension paid directly by the competent Swiss body (Federal Office of Social Insurance/UFAS through the cantonal fund), regardless of his Italian residence. It is not necessary to move to Switzerland to collect your pension; the annuity can be collected directly into an Italian bank account.

The LPP (second pillar) accumulated in Switzerland remains segregated in the pension fund of the fund affiliated to the employer; upon retirement, the cross-border worker can choose whether to collect the accumulated capital (redemption option) or opt for a life annuity according to the rules of the fund (regulation under cantonal competence). There is no double payment: the cross-border worker contributes ONLY in Switzerland for the work carried out in Switzerland, and credits ONLY in Italy for any periods worked in Italian territory.

Obligations with INPS and the Italian Revenue Agency

Although the Spriana-Graubünden cross-border worker works entirely in Switzerland, he remains obliged to report his condition to the Italian Revenue Agency and, where required, to the INPS for social security coordination issues. There is no "double contribution": the cross-border worker pays AVS/LPP contributions ONLY in Switzerland for the work carried out in Switzerland. He remains registered with the Italian INPS (as a citizen/resident) but without payments for periods worked abroad (except for specific cases of mixed Italy-Switzerland work). Coordination between INPS and the Swiss social security authorities (UFAS) is automatic through data exchange protocols, avoiding double contributions and guaranteeing the recognition of periods in pension calculations.

Procedure step-by-step: becoming a border worker Spriana-Graubünden

Phase 1: Basic documentation Collect Italian residence certificate (issued by the Spriana municipality) and employment contract signed with a registered employer in the Graubünden (Switzerland). The contract must be dated and signed by the employer.

Phase 2: Requesting the Permesso G Contact the cantonal migration office of the canton of Graubünden, or have your employer submit the application on your behalf. Required documents:

  • Italian residence certificate (conform copy)
  • Employment contract signed
  • Copy of valid passport or ID card

Once obtained, you will have the right to work legally in Switzerland.

Phase 3: Automatic enrollment in AVS From the first day of work, your employer will automatically enroll you in AVS at the competent compensation office (assigned based on the workplace canton). You will receive a unique AVS number and a registration notification. No further action is required from you; enrollment is automatic and mandatory.

Phase 4: Choosing health insurance (LAMal) Within 30 days of starting work, decide whether to maintain Italian coverage (public SSN, no additional costs) or enroll in a private Swiss LAMal. If you choose LAMal, select a Swiss insurance fund and a deductible (CHF 300-2500). Inform your employer (usually through the LPP or HR manager) of your choice. If you maintain Italian coverage, explicitly communicate this decision; you will not pay LAMal contributions in Switzerland.

Phase 5: Registration with the Italian Revenue Agency Inform your accountant (or directly with the Revenue Agency through the 730/Unified Model) of your new border worker status. Provide:

  • Complete employer details in Switzerland (company name, address, CHE ID number)
  • AVS enrollment number (obtained in Phase 3)
  • Copies of the first two pay slips (to verify amounts and deductions)

Frequently Asked Questions
Do I have to enroll in the AVS even though I live in Italy and will receive an Italian pension?
Yes, you are required to enroll in the AVS starting on your first day of work in Switzerland. The AVS (Old Age and Survivors’ Insurance) is the first pillar of the Swiss pension system, similar to Italy’s INPS public pension. By contributing 5.3% of your gross income, you accrue Swiss pension benefits. Even if you reside in Italy, you will still receive your AVS pension upon retirement (at age 65 for men), paid directly by the Swiss agency. It is not necessary to move to Switzerland; the 1976 Italy-Switzerland Agreement guarantees the recognition of these rights even for residents of Italy.
Who is covered by the transitional provisions of Article 9 of the Agreement on Cross-Border Workers?
The transitional regime applies exclusively to workers who meet the conventional definition of a cross-border worker and who were employed in the cantons of Ticino, Graubünden, or Valais during the period from December 31, 2018, to July 17, 2023. For these individuals, income from employment remains taxable exclusively in Switzerland, unlike under the standard regime, which provides for taxation in Italy with a credit for taxes paid in Switzerland.
Are the 10,000 euros provided for in Law 83/2023 a general deductible for cross-border workers?
No, this is not a general exemption. The 10,000-euro limit mentioned in Article 4 of Law 83/2023 applies exclusively to the types of income referred to in Article 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013. Therefore, this threshold should not be confused with a universal tax deduction applicable to all cross-border workers; rather, it is limited to specific types of income provided for by current legislation.
How does the LAMal option work for cross-border workers residing in Italy?
Cross-border workers residing in Italy are required to have health insurance. They may exercise their right to choose within 30 days of the start of their first employment contract in Switzerland: they may choose between health coverage under the Italian National Health Service or enrollment in a Swiss health insurance plan (LAMal). This choice is binding and determines the insurance plan that applies during the period of employment in Switzerland.
What are the mandatory social security contributions for a cross-border worker in Switzerland?
Cross-border workers are required to pay AVS (Old Age and Survivors’ Insurance) contributions, equal to 5.3% of gross income, plus the employer’s share. They are also required to enroll in the second pillar (LPP), with contribution rates ranging from 7% to 18% of insured income depending on age, as well as the payment of contributions for accident insurance (LAINF) and unemployment insurance (AD/AC).
