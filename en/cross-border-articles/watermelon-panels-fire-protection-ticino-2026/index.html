The tragedy of Crans-Montana has brought attention back to a crucial issue: the choice of construction materials that can make a difference in fire protection. Now, a promising proposal comes from the Federal Polytechnic of Zurich: fire-resistant panels made with watermelon seed powder. Mixed with sawdust, urea, struvite, and water, the powder of watermelon seeds can become the base of a material that takes a long time to catch fire. Not only that: according to the researchers, the composite can even extinguish itself.

How does it work?

Ronny Kürsteiner, a PhD student at the Polytechnic of Zurich, explains the mechanism. The behavior of the material, he reports, depends partly on the struvite: when heated, it decomposes and releases gas. This process has a double effect: on the one hand, it removes heat because decomposition itself requires it; on the other hand, it helps to remove oxygen from the surface. In this way, explains Kürsteiner, 'a protective layer of non-flammable gas is formed.'

Implications for Ticino

The discovery by the Zurich Polytechnic could have significant implications for the Canton of Ticino, where fire safety is a priority. Panels made from watermelon seeds could be used in new constructions or for the restoration of existing buildings, offering greater protection against fires. Additionally, the possibility of recycling the material could reduce the environmental impact of constructions, an aspect increasingly relevant in urban planning.

Comparison with Traditional Materials

Traditional panels used in Switzerland, such as those made of chipboard bonded with cement, already offer good fire protection. However, the new material developed by the Zurich Polytechnic presents significant advantages in terms of sustainability and recyclability. This could make watermelon panels a preferable choice for new constructions, especially in areas at high risk of fires.

Future Scenarios

If watermelon panels were to be adopted on a large scale, they could become a key element in the construction of public and private buildings in Ticino. Their self-extinguishing capability could reduce the risk of fires and improve building safety. Furthermore, the possibility of recycling the material could encourage the use of these panels in sustainable construction projects.

Comparison with the Previous Situation

Before this discovery, materials used for fire protection were mainly based on cement and other chemical compounds. Watermelon panels represent an innovative alternative that could revolutionize the sector. Their self-extinguishing capability and sustainability make them a promising choice for the future.
