Petrol over €2, the Swiss full is no longer convenient (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 26 August 2026

Prices aligned between Lombardy and Ticino: petrol at €2.01 and diesel at €2.14. Excise discount expired on 26 August.

Context

In brief

  • Gasoline in Lombardy and Switzerland now aligned at over €2 per liter
  • Excise discount of 17 cents ends August 26
  • For Ticino commuters, it is no longer worthwhile to go to Switzerland

Key facts

  • What: fuel price comparison Lombardy‑Switzerland
  • When: August 26, 2026, excise discount expiry (TCS data updated August 20)
  • Where: Province of Varese, border with Canton Ticino
  • Who: Ministry of Enterprises and Made in Italy Fuel Price Observatory, Touring Club Schweiz (TCS)
  • Amount: Lombardy gasoline €2.011/liter, Switzerland gasoline €2.02/liter (95), Swiss diesel €2.27/liter

On August 26 the 17‑cent‑per‑liter excise discount that the Italian government had extended by two days expires. This is the moment when gasoline prices in Lombardy reach those in Switzerland, making the traditional trip of Ticino commuters to cross‑border filling stations less convenient.

The Ministry of Enterprises and Made in Italy Fuel Price Observatory certifies the situation in the region: self‑service gasoline at €2.011 per liter, self‑service diesel at €2.135 per liter. TCS data, updated August 20, show Swiss 95‑octane gasoline at €2.02 and diesel at €2.27.

Those who work in the Province of Varese or other border areas with Canton Ticino now face a significant change: until a few days ago crossing the border to fill up was generally convenient thanks to the price differential between Swiss and Lombardy prices. Today that convenience has disappeared. If you want to assess the overall impact on your budget, discover how commuting costs affect the cost of living in cross‑border work.

Operational details

Highways and impact on cross-border workers

The situation on the main arteries is critical, especially for those who daily travel the A2 (Como-Lugano-Locarno) or the A9 (Como-Chiasso). The progressive increases in recent days, documented by the Ministry's Fuel Observatory, transform the cost of commuting. A cross-border worker who drives 60 km per day (30 km each way from the border to the workplace) may spend 70-80 euros per week on gasoline at these prices.

The end of the excise discount further complicates the picture. This contribution, which reduced the price by 17 cents per liter, was a support measure that the Italian government had kept in force, extending it until August 26. Its removal opens the debate on the need for new interventions. Before the expiry, some Varese petrol stations still offered gasoline below two euros, albeit by a few cents: from today, even these margins are likely to disappear.

What the Italian government is doing

On August 26, exactly when the discount ends, the Italian Council of Ministers will address the issue of measures to contain fuel costs. Among the hypotheses on the table is the possible extension of the measures already in force, but also the introduction of instruments aimed at lower-income brackets, possibly linked to the social card.

Key points

Procedures and control tools

The first step for an informed choice is to check real‑time fuel prices. The Ministry of Enterprises and Made in Italy’s Fuel Observatory publishes daily regional averages; the Touring Club Schweiz updates data on Swiss distributors. Both sources provide an accurate picture of the situation, although prices can vary significantly from one station to another.

A cross‑border worker living, for example, in Mendrisio or Chiasso and working in Varese should:

1. Verify the average fuel price in the province of Varese and in Canton Ticino before deciding where to fill up 2. Calculate the additional cost (time + fuel for the detour) to reach a Swiss distributor 3. Consider whether the possible saving (now marginal) justifies the extra trip

Until a few weeks ago, the answer was almost always yes. Today convenience has become uncertain, almost nil.

Impact on the cross‑border worker’s budget

The figure is concrete: if a cross‑border worker fills up once a week and drives on average 300‑400 km (mix of home‑work trips and local errands), his weekly consumption is about 20‑25 litres. At €2 per litre, that means €40‑50 per week on gasoline. Before the excise discount and with previous prices, the difference between Lombardy and Switzerland could be worth €5‑10 per week (if Swiss fuel was cheaper). Today that saving is almost erased.

Nevertheless, the context remains dynamic. The Italian Council of Ministers on 26 August could announce new fuel‑support measures, or the Swiss government could intervene if inflationary pressures persist. The cross‑border worker who finds this cost variable relevant should monitor developments in the coming weeks.

Frequently Asked Questions
Is it still convenient to run gasoline in Switzerland as a border crossing?
Today, August 26, 2026, the economic advantage is almost zero. Self-service petrol in Lombardy costs €2.011 per litre, compared to €2.02 for Swiss 95 petrol as measured by the TCS on 20 August. Considering the time and fuel required to reach a Swiss distributor, the choice becomes a matter of very tight margins. If you previously saved 5-10 euros per week, today you can expect almost non-existent savings or even higher costs due to the extra trip.
What is the reason for the increase in price in Lombardy?
The source attributes the increases to a general pressure on fuel markets, documented by the Ministry's Price Observatory. The excise discount of 17 cents per litre, which the government had extended until 26 August, is expiring today, removing support that had held back prices. The Council of Ministers will address the issue on 26 August itself, considering whether to extend the interventions or introduce new ones.
What are the prices on the Italian motorways?
Prices are even higher than regional averages: petrol at 2,091 euros per litre, diesel at 2,207 euros according to the Observatory. On the A22 of the Brenner, Supreme diesel even reached 3,159 euros per liter. Although the Ticino border crossing does not run along the Brenner, these prices show the volatility on the Italian road network and the risk of increases also on the A2 and A9 that connect Como to Ticino.
Will the Italian government do anything to lower prices?
The Council of Ministers on 26 August will address the issue, with the possibility of extending the measures already in force (such as the discount on excise duties) and possible new instruments for the lower income groups, possibly through social cards. No specific details on concrete measures have been announced yet, so it is appropriate to await the outcome of the government meeting.
How do you calculate the real impact of fuel cost on the border carrier's budget?
If you travel 300-400 km per week (mix of home-work and local travel), the consumption will be about 20-25 litres. At 2 euros per liter, it means 40-50 euros per week of gasoline. Add the costs of the fuel needed to reach a Swiss distributor (if it is not close to home) and the time lost on the extra journey. By multiplying by 4 weeks per month, you can estimate the impact on your monthly budget and evaluate it in relation to your net cross-border earnings.
