Managing healthcare expenses for those who work in Switzerland and reside in Italy is an issue that affects thousands of cross-border workers in Canton Ticino every year. The starting point is the Convention for the Avoidance of Double Taxation between Italy and Switzerland, signed on March 9, 1976. This convention establishes that tax on employment income is withheld at source in Switzerland. Italy avoids double taxation by granting a tax credit, which can be claimed by completing the CE section of Form 730.

For cross-border workers with a G permit, Swiss LAMal health insurance provides for the right to choose. This means that the worker can choose whether to maintain Swiss coverage or join the Italian healthcare system. Adult deductibles range from 300 to 2.500 Swiss francs, a factor that directly affects how much is paid out of pocket before the insurance starts to cover costs.

The relevant regulatory framework

The New Cross-Border Workers Agreement, signed on December 23, 2020 and applied from January 1st, 2024, reshaped the tax treatment. Italy ratified it through Law 83 of June 13, 2023. Legacy cross-border workers remain taxed only in Switzerland (Article 9 of the 2020 Agreement). The transitional regime under Article 9 of the Agreement applies only to those who meet the treaty definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino, or Valais between December 31, 2018 and July 17, 2023; the covered employment income is taxed exclusively in Switzerland. In other cases, the ordinary regime applies, with Italian taxation and a credit for Swiss tax. The 10.000 euros under Article 4 of Law 83/2023 are only the limit for the income referred to in Article 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013, not a general allowance. With regard to Swiss social security contributions, the rates are set by federal and cantonal laws and administered at federal level by the Federal Tax Administration (AFC/ESTV) and by the cantonal tax administrations. The rates are not set by UFAS, which deals with AVS/AI social security, or by BFS, which collects statistical data. The AVS/AI/IPG contribution is 5,3% for employees, AD/AC is 1,1% with a ceiling of 148.200 francs, LAINF ranges between 0,7% and 1,5%, while LPP ranges from 7% to 18% depending on the age bracket, starting at age 25.

On the Italian side, IRPEF applies three brackets: 23% up to 28.000 euros, 33% between 28.001 and 50.000 euros, and 43% above 50.000 euros. These figures are essential for understanding how beneficial it is to deduct healthcare expenses on Form 730, because the tax effect depends on the taxpayer's marginal rate.

Operational details

Practical implications for the cross-border worker

Anyone residing in Italy and working in Ticino must understand how to treat healthcare expenses incurred in Switzerland. The general rule is that documented medical expenses can be deducted in the Italian tax return, using Section CE for the tax credit. The mechanism works as follows: Swiss employment income is declared in Italy, Italian tax is calculated, and the credit for taxes already withheld at source in Switzerland is deducted.

Reimbursable or deductible healthcare expenses include medical services, medicines, hospital stays and other items provided for by Italian law. The critical point is the documentation: a Swiss receipt or invoice is required, preferably indicating the service and the amount in francs. Conversion into euros must be carried out using the average exchange rate for the tax period, a step requiring attention because the CHF/EUR exchange rate can also vary significantly during the year.

The role of LAMal and the right of option

The LAMal right of option is a strategic element. A cross-border worker with a G permit can choose between Swiss coverage and enrollment in the Italian National Health Service. The choice has direct consequences: with LAMal, deductibles ranging from 300 to 2.500 francs are paid, while under the Italian system, services are accessed with the co-payments provided for by national legislation. The decision should also be assessed based on where healthcare services are concentrated: those receiving most of their care in Italy might find the Italian system more convenient, while those relying on Swiss facilities will prefer to keep LAMal.

Switzerland is not a member of the European Union or the European Economic Area. This detail is important because it excludes the automatic application of European rules on cross-border healthcare. Relations between Italy and Switzerland in healthcare and tax matters are governed by specific bilateral agreements, such as the Convention of March 9, 1976 and the New Cross-Border Workers Agreement of 2020.

Key points

Operational procedure for reimbursement

The path to obtain reimbursement or deduction of Swiss healthcare costs in Italy is divided into precise steps. The first step is to collect all the documentation: invoices, receipts, payment receipts issued by healthcare facilities, doctors or Swiss pharmacies. Each document must indicate the service, the date, the amount in francs and the details of the person who incurred the expense.

The second step is the conversion into euros. Italian legislation provides for the use of the average exchange rate for the tax period. It is advisable to check the applicable exchange rate and keep track of the method used, in order to be able to justify it in the event of an inspection by the Revenue Agency.

Compilation of form 730

The third step is to fill in the EC form 730. In this form, the income produced in Switzerland is declared and the tax credit for taxes paid at source is calculated. Health expenses must be indicated in the section dedicated to deductible charges. The amount in euros, converted as described, must be reported with the codes required for healthcare expenses.

The fourth step is testing the limits. Deductible healthcare expenses are subject to a deductible, as required by Italian law. Not all expenses are fully deductible: some items have specific limits. It is advisable to check the documentation before sending to avoid errors that could trigger checks or integration requests.

Frequently Asked Questions
What Swiss healthcare expenses can I deduct in Italy?
Documented medical expenses incurred in Switzerland, such as medical services, medications and hospitalizations, can be deducted in the Italian tax return. The original documentation is required with an indication of the service, the date and the amount in francs. The deduction takes place in the CE framework of the model 730.
How does the border tax credit work?
Tax at source is only withheld in Switzerland. Italy avoids double taxation by recognizing a tax credit, to be indicated in the EC framework of 730. Swiss income is declared in Italy, Italian personal income tax is calculated and the credit for taxes already paid at source is subtracted.
Should you keep the LAMal or register with the Italian health system?
Border workers with a G permit have the right of option. LAMal provides deductibles for adults from 300 to 2,500 francs. The choice depends on where the services are concentrated: those who care mainly in Italy can find the Italian system convenient, those who use Swiss facilities will prefer LAMal. Switzerland is not an EU/EEA member, so European standards do not automatically apply.
