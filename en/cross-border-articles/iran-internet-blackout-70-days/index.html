Key facts

  • What: Internet blackout
  • When: 70 consecutive days
  • Where: Iran
  • Who: NetBlocks
  • Amount: 1656 hours of disconnection

The internet blackout in Iran has reached a significant milestone, reaching its 70th consecutive day. According to the monitoring group NetBlocks, this is one of the longest national-level blocks ever recorded. Digital connectivity has been disrupted for a total of 1656 hours, a period that has had a significant impact on the country's population and economic activities.

"Today marks the 70th day of the internet blackout in Iran, surpassing 1656 hours. Digital connectivity is vital in times of crisis, and limiting the service harms those who need it most: people with disabilities, students, small businesses, and the general population," reads the post on NetBlocks' X profile. This long period of disconnection has highlighted the crucial importance of the internet for daily life and economic activities, especially in times of crisis.

Impact on the population

The blackout has severely affected various groups, including students, small businesses, and people with disabilities. The lack of internet access has made it difficult to access essential services, communication, and remote education. Small businesses, in particular, have suffered significant losses due to the inability to operate online.

International reactions

The international community has followed the situation in Iran with concern. Human rights organizations and monitoring groups have expressed worry over the restrictions imposed on the freedom of information and expression. The blackout has also raised questions about digital security and the need to ensure internet access as a fundamental right.

Future prospects

As the blackout continues, the prospects for a quick recovery of connectivity remain uncertain. Iranian authorities have not provided clear information on the timeline for restoring the service. Meanwhile, the population continues to seek alternative solutions to access the internet, such as using private networks or mobile devices with data connections.

Important documents: Have important documents available in paper format, such as work contracts, identity documents, and banking information. This can be useful if you are unable to access online services. 4. Emergency budget: Maintain an emergency budget for any unexpected expenses.
