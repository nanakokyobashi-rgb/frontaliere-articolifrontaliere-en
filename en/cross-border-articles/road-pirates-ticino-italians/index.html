Ticino: Two Italian Drivers Among the Road Pirates, Five Serious Violations Between January and February

In the early months of 2026, the Ticino cantonal police have intensified road safety controls along the main border routes, such as the A2 motorway connecting Italy and Switzerland. Specifically, between January and February, several Italian drivers were intercepted for significantly exceeding speed limits, with incidents that involved dangerous behaviors. Two emblematic cases concern vehicles crossing through the Gaggiolo and Brogeda border crossings, key points for the flow of commuters and visitors. The canton police, collaborating with the municipal police of Chiasso and Mendrisio, have implemented targeted checks to combat the so-called road pirates, who often travel at speeds exceeding 120 km/h in central areas or construction zones, where the speed limit is 50 km/h for safety reasons. Among the most serious violations are cases where vehicles were caught going over twice the permitted limit, posing extremely high risks of accidents, including severe ones. Local authorities emphasize that this zero-tolerance approach aims to protect all road users, including commuters and tourists, and to maintain order at the busiest crossings like Gaggiolo, Chiasso, and Mendrisio. The involvement of Italian drivers in these violations highlights the importance of coordinated efforts between police forces from both countries to ensure more effective control and a stronger deterrent. The current situation also prompts reflection on the need for awareness campaigns and stricter controls to drastically reduce the number of road pirates and improve safety along the most trafficked border section of Ticino. The violation numbers are rising compared to previous years, and local authorities pledge to intensify operations in the coming months, with harsher penalties and, in some cases, immediate license withdrawal for extreme behaviors. The collaboration between Swiss and Italian police remains crucial to tackling this growing issue, which endangers lives and damages the region's image both locally and internationally.

Operational details

To understand the broader context, it’s essential to consider the current regulations in Switzerland and Italy regarding road safety and penalties for speeding. In Switzerland, the speed limit in residential areas and built-up zones is 50 km/h, but on motorways and main roads it can rise to 80 or 120 km/h, depending on signage. Swiss law imposes severe sanctions for exceeding the limit by more than 40 km/h, including hefty fines, point deductions, and, in the most serious cases, license revocation. Italy’s regulations set limits at 50 km/h within urban centers and 90 km/h on main rural roads, with similar penalties for speeding violations. Among the most notable incidents intercepted at Gaggiolo and Brogeda, a 45-year-old Italian driver was stopped in January while traveling at 121 km/h in Chiasso, and another in Mendrisio in February was caught going approximately 70 km/h over the limit. These episodes not only endanger everyone’s lives but also lead to practical consequences such as immediate license withdrawal and fines that can exceed 350 CHF, depending on the severity. The cantonal police have announced that they will step up checks at border crossings and along major routes, paying particular attention to dangerous behaviors by some Italian drivers. Swiss legislation also bans circulation in Switzerland for those with multiple serious violations and can impose temporary or permanent bans to discourage misconduct and ensure safety. Joint efforts between authorities in Lugano, Bellinzona, and border command units from Italy are vital to reducing this phenomenon. Many commuters, rushing to return to Italy, may underestimate the consequences of their actions, but penalties are now more severe and aim to serve as effective deterrents.

Key points

For Italian commuters regularly traveling between Italy and Ticino, understanding the regulations and control procedures—especially regarding speeding violations—is crucial. The first recommendation is to strictly adhere to speed limits, particularly in urban centers like Chiasso, Mendrisio, and Lugano, where penalties are especially strict. In case of fines or stops, Swiss authorities do not make exceptions: immediate license withdrawal can be ordered, with fines exceeding 350 CHF for serious offenses, along with possible temporary or permanent driving bans. Tools such as salary calculators or border maps can help plan trips better and comply with regulations, avoiding sanctions that could jeopardize residence or work permits in Ticino. It’s important to remember that for extreme violations, Swiss authorities can also initiate criminal proceedings, with more severe consequences. Staying updated on deadlines and speed limits, and adopting responsible driving behavior, is essential. In cases of serious infractions, consulting a lawyer specialized in Swiss law can be helpful to manage sanctions or disputes. Cooperation between border crossers and police can also be facilitated through digital tools and official apps that allow checking for pending sanctions or bans. To prevent becoming part of this problem, strict adherence to road safety norms is advised, along with the use of speed monitoring and travel planning tools. Frequently Asked Questions
What are the speed limits in Switzerland and Italy?
In Switzerland, the speed limit is 50 km/h in residential areas and inhabited areas, while in highways and main roads it can rise to 80 or 120 km/h. In Italy, the speed limit is 50 km/h in the towns and 90 km/h on the main roads.
What is the procedure for an Italian frontierer who receives a fine for excess speed in Switzerland?
The frontier receives the fine directly in hand if stopped, or via mail. It must be paid within the specified period, otherwise implementing measures may be taken. In serious cases, it could suffer the immediate withdrawal of the license and, in Italy, the conversion of the penalty to points deducted from the license.
What behaviors should avoid incurring sanctions during speed controls along the Switzerland-Italy border?
It is essential to respect speed limits, especially in central and construction areas, and to maintain responsible driving behaviour. Avoiding dangerous maneuvers and complying with road signs to border crossings reduces the risk of legal penalties and problems.
