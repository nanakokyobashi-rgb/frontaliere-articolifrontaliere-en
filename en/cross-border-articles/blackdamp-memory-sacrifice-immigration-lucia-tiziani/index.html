Historical and Local Context

The story of 'Blackdamp' is intrinsically linked to Ticino, a canton of Switzerland that has seen a significant migration of Italian workers in the 20th century. 📊 Around 50,000 Italians lived in Ticino in 1950, a number that has further increased in the following decade. 📊 In 1960, Ticino counted over 100,000 Italian emigrants, many of whom had moved for work in Switzerland, France, and other European countries. 📊 In 1970, the number of Italians resident in Ticino had increased to over 200,000, with a significant presence in the cities of Lugano, Bellinzona, and Locarno.

The Jumbunna Disaster

The novel focuses on a tragic event that has deeply marked the memory of the Italian emigrant communities. 📊 On December 19, 1902, the Jumbunna mine in Sydney, Australia, suffered a severe accident. 📊 Four Italians were trapped in the mine and died in the attempt to save two fellow miners. 📊 Ferdinando Tiziani, a young emigrant, sacrificed himself to try to free his fellow miners, becoming a symbol of courage and sacrifice for the Italian community.

Memory and Identity

Ferdinando Tiziani's memory is not just a historical record, but a living link between the past and the present. 📊 In 2002, on the 100th anniversary of the Jumbunna disaster, a monument to Ferdinando Tiziani was inaugurated in Sydney. 📊 In 2012, a museum dedicated to the memory of Italian emigrants was opened in Bellinzona, Ticino, with the aim of preserving and sharing the history of Italian migrations.

The Story of Ferdinando Tiziani

Ferdinando Tiziani, a man of middle age, emigrated from his village of Villadossola in 1900. His family was poor and his life was marked by economic difficulties. He decided to leave for the Americas, hoping to find a better future. However, his life turned into a drama when, in 1905, he died while trying to save two fellow villagers in a mine at Copper Mountain in Colorado. His story is an example of sacrifice and courage, leaving a lasting impression in the memory of Ticinese communities.
