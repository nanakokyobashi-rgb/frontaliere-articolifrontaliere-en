Cerano d'Intelvi is one of the main municipalities of the Valle d'Intelvi, in the province of Como, where several thousand border crossers choose to live to work in offices, hospitals, factories and companies in the Canton of Ticino. The decision to move from an Italian resident to a Swiss commuter has concrete implications on taxation, permits, social insurance and economic planning. Since January 1, 2024, with the entry into force of the new bilateral agreement between Italy and Switzerland signed on December 23, 2020, the rules on deductions, deductibles and restorations have undergone significant changes that directly affect the payroll and annual tax recoveries. This guide accompanies the Cerano-Ticino frontier worker through the main legal, fiscal and administrative aspects of the cross-border working condition.

Why Cerano d'Intelvi attracts Ticino border crossers

Cerano d'Intelvi is located in the Intelvi Valley, a hilly area where the cost of the Italian house is generally lower than the Ticino prices, especially in the districts of Lugano, Mendrisio and Bellinzona. The access to the main crossings—Brogeda, Chiasso and Gaggiolo—makes the daily commute feasible and organized for those with a stable occupation in Ticino. The community has been consolidated for decades, with solid local infrastructure (schools, shops, municipal services, post offices) and already hosts several thousand border crossers working in the Canton of Ticino, thus creating a network of shared experience, contacts and practical knowledge of procedures. This characteristic makes Valle d'Intelvi a natural destination for those looking for a balance between low housing costs and stable access to the Italian-speaking Swiss labour market.

Operational details

Cross-border commuter taxation: withholding tax and tax credit

For the Italian cross-border worker working in Ticino, taxation is the pillar of annual economic planning. Withholding tax is withheld in Switzerland, not in Italy. This means that the gross salary declared by the Ticino employer is subjected to a Swiss tax withholding directly on the pay slip each month. Italy, by virtue of the Convention for the avoidance of double taxation signed on 9 March 1976, recognizes cross-border workers with a tax credit when they declare their income in the EC framework of form 730 (annual income declaration).

The amount of the Swiss withholding tax depends on the gross income bracket. Up to CHF 148'200, the federal and cantonal rate varies approximately from 0% to 3%, with brackets that progressively increase as the salary increases. Above CHF 148'200, the rates applied are significantly higher. In addition to the withholding tax, the cross-border worker automatically pays these social and security contributions:

  • AVS/AI/IPG: 5.3% of the salary (old age insurance, disability, unemployment benefits)
  • AD/AC: 1.1% (unemployment insurance, integration subsidies), with a maximum limit of CHF 148'200
  • UVG: 0.7–1.5% (accident insurance)
  • LPP: 7–18% depending on age (second pension pillar, mandatory from age 25)

These amounts are deducted from your paycheck each month and represent social and pension protections guaranteed by Swiss law, not additional taxes.

Deductibles and refunds from 2024: how the new agreement changes recoveries

From 1 January 2024, the new agreement introduced two distinct annual exemption brackets which divide cross-border commuters into two categories:

  • The threshold of 10,000 euros of the art. 4 of Law 83/2023 concerns exclusively income from employment carried out abroad, in a border area or in a neighboring country, continuously and exclusively, by people resident in Italy, referred to in the art. 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013; it is not a general exemption nor an exemption from Swiss withholding tax.
  • Old cross-border commuters remain taxed only in Switzerland (art. 9 of the 2020 Agreement).

This difference has direct consequences on refunds (tax refunds). A higher deductible reduces the overall income subject to Italian taxation, thus lowering the IRPEF due and increasing final recoveries. In concrete terms, the cross-border worker fills out the EC form of form 730 by declaring the gross income from employment in Ticino, net of the deductible. The Italian Revenue Agency therefore calculates the IRPEF according to the brackets:

  • 23% up to €28,000
  • 33% from €28'001 to €50'000
  • 43% over €50,000

Subsequently apply the tax credit (equal to the Swiss tax already paid). The final result determines whether the cross-border worker receives a refund or will have to pay a tax supplement.

Healthcare and KVG: the choice between the Italian and Swiss systems

The cross-border worker with a G permit has the right to opt for KVG (Swiss health insurance). You can choose between remaining in the Italian healthcare system (with mutual coverage for treatments also in Ticino through the INPS-SVS agreement) or enrolling in a Swiss health insurance company. KVG deductibles for adults vary from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 per year depending on the policy and fund chosen. This choice has not only health and organizational implications, but also fiscal and administrative ones, since KVG premiums are partially deductible from Swiss taxes and have effects on the Italian tax return.

Key points

G permit: how to obtain and renew it

The first administrative step towards life as a cross-border worker is to obtain the G permit. Contrary to what many believe, the cross-border worker does NOT request the permit directly: it is the Ticino employer who sends a formal request for authorization to the SECO (Secretariat of State for the Economy) of the canton of Ticino. Once SECO approves the request, communication of the permit reaches both the employer and the employee. The G permit is valid for 5 years and can be renewed at the end without difficulty if the occupation continues. It does not involve costs for the worker and allows you to work legally in Ticino while residing as a resident in Italy (Cerano d'Intelvi or other municipalities in the Valle d'Intelvi).

Tax return: calendar, forms and annual procedure

Every year the Italian cross-border worker must submit a tax return to the Italian Revenue Agency. The deadline is November 30th for income from the previous calendar year. In the declaration (form 730 or PF income form) you must indicate:

  • Gross income from employment in Ticino
  • Withholding tax paid in Switzerland (documented by year-end pay slip extract)
  • Request for tax credit in the EC framework

The Revenue Agency processes the data, calculates the Italian IRPEF by applying the brackets and the tax credit, applies the exemption provided for by the new agreement. If a refund emerges (refund due), it is paid to the IBAN indicated by the taxpayer within 4-6 months of processing the application.

Management of AVS, LPP and monitoring of contributions

Old age insurance (AVS) is mandatory from the first day of work in Ticino. The 5.3% contribution is paid directly by the employer as a payroll deduction; the worker does not have to do anything. Every year, the cross-border worker receives an AVS account statement from the competent cantonal body (Ahv-IV-Stelle Ticino) which documents the accredited contributions. It is essential to keep these documents both for the Italian declaration and to verify the correct contribution towards the future pension income over time.

For the second pillar (LPP, mandatory second pillar), the employer is required by law to enroll the employee in a pension fund within the first month of employment. The rate varies from 7% to 18% depending on the age of the worker and the chosen fund, and is deducted monthly from the paycheck. The cross-border worker receives an account statement every year documenting the accumulated fund. This data is crucial for planning future retirement income and for verifying that contributions proceed regularly over the years. Switzerland is outside the European Union, so the Swiss pension systems (AVS, LPP) are not automatically coordinated with the Italian ones (INPS): the cross-border worker remains registered with the Italian INPS for the contributions paid before moving to Ticino, while the years of Swiss work accumulate in the AVS/LPP system.

Annual checklist: key deadlines and obligations

To avoid errors and maximize tax benefits, the cross-border worker should follow this precise timetable:

  • January–February: Collect all annual Swiss tax certification (payment statements, paid withholding tax, AVS/AI/IPG, AD/AC, LAINF, LPP contributions) directly from the final pay slip and employer documents
  • February–March: Calculate the Italian net income and check if there are additional deduction elements (medical expenses, mortgage interest, family expenses)
  • October–November: Fill out form 730 online via the Revenue Agency website (maximum deadline 30 November)
  • January following year: Receive the final certification and the final result (refund, payment due, or breakeven)

Many cross-border commuters rely on an Italian accountant or a firm specialized in Ticino-Italy border tax law (present in the border municipalities in the province of Como). This professional manages the declaration, maximizes tax recoveries and ensures compliance with the regulations of both countries.

Frequently Asked Questions
I move to Cerano d 'Intelvi to work in Ticino: how do I get the G permit?
The Ticino employer requires SECO authorisation to hire you as a border worker. You don't have to make a personal request. Once approved, you will receive the G permit (valid for 5 years, renewable free of charge). The permit allows you to legally reside in Italy (Cerano d 'Intelvi) and work without restrictions in the Canton of Ticino.
How many taxes and contributions are withheld from my border worker's salary?
In Switzerland: source tax around 0-3% (varies by income bracket up to CHF 148'200), AVS/AI/IPG 5.3%, AD/AC 1.1%, LAINF 0.7-1.5%, LPP 7-18% by age. In Italy: no direct withholding. Report your income in 730 and get a tax credit for what you paid in Switzerland.
Who qualifies for the transitional regime in Article 9?
Article 9’s transitional regime applies only to workers who meet the Agreement’s definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between 31 December 2018 and 17 July 2023; covered employment income is taxable exclusively in Switzerland. The ordinary regime applies in other cases, with Italian taxation and credit for Swiss tax where applicable. The €10,000 in Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is only the limit for income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law 147/2013, not a general allowance. How does tax recovery work? Am I entitled to a refund?
Fill out the Italian Form 730 by November 30, indicating Swiss gross income and paid source tax (paycheck). The Agency calculates the Italian personal income tax (23-35-43% per bracket) and applies the Swiss credit. If Italian IRPEF < Swiss tax paid, you receive a refund (4-6 months). If Italian IRPEF > Swiss tax, you pay the difference.
Can I choose between Italian healthcare and Swiss LAMal?
Yes, the G permit gives you the right of option. You can stay in the Italian health system (also covered in Ticino via INPS-SVS agreement) or subscribe to LAMal Switzerland with a CHF 300-2500 deductible. Both choices have tax implications: consult your employer or an accountant for the best solution.
