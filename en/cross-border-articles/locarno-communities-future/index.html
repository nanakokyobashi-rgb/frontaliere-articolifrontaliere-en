The future of the Locarno District hinges on a crucial phase of discussion among seven municipalities: Locarno, Losone, Minusio, Brione sopra Minusio, Orselina, Tenero-Contra, and Mergoscia. This initiative, promoted by a dialogue laboratory established in 2022, aims to assess whether territorial merging is the best path to meet the needs of the local population, which remains fragmented and lacks cohesion. The region, historically divided by cultural and administrative barriers, faces a fundamental question: join forces to ensure more effective public services, economic development, and sustainability, or preserve municipal autonomy, which guarantees decisions closer to citizens. The debate involves political representatives, interest groups, and residents, with a focus on specific targets: youth, seniors, businesses, and seasonal visitors.

Operational details

Six municipalities in the Locarno District are examining their future: merger or autonomy?

To better understand the implications of this complex discussion, it’s useful to analyze two main merger scenarios: one urban, potentially involving Locarno and Minusio, and one peri-urban, which would also include municipalities like Tenero-Contra, Orselina, and San Nazzaro. The key difference between these models lies in scope and management of public services. Urban consolidation could lead to more coherent planning of infrastructure, transportation, and commercial development, boosting the district’s attractiveness. Already in 2021, Locarno invested around 20 million Swiss francs in public mobility projects, aiming to improve bus networks and cycling paths. If Minusio and Ascona also joined, resources and services could be optimized, potentially reducing administrative costs by about 15%, according to local experts’ estimates.

Key points

Currently, seven municipalities in the Locarno area are engaged in a pivotal debate that could redefine their future: merge or maintain autonomy? This strategic decision affects residents of Domodossola, Ascona, Losone, Centovalli, Terre di Pedemonte, Ronco sopra Ascona, and the valleys of Verbano, who must carefully consider the implications of each option.

For Locarno’s citizens, influence over decisions is already within reach: it’s recommended to closely follow developments through public meetings, online consultations, and official communications. For those seeking more insights, tools like salary calculators, tax comparison charts, and transportation maps are valuable resources to evaluate how choices could impact daily life. For example, comparing the municipal taxation rates of Ascona (average 75‰) and Losone (average 80‰) can help understand potential savings or increased costs under different scenarios.

Moreover, regardless of the outcome, final decisions will rest with municipal councils and committees, which will evaluate concrete and sustainable proposals. ⚠️ For example, a regional contribution of 5 million Swiss francs in 2023 to support local development projects could serve as an incentive for collaboration or as a point of discussion in case independence is preferred.

For public and private sector workers, municipal cooperation can also open opportunities for training, development projects, and improved support services for businesses and cross-border workers. 💡 A concrete example is the inter-municipal vocational training project launched in 2022, involving 150 participants among public employees and economic operators, with a budget of around 600,000 francs.

This initiative is part of a broader framework of regional reforms and efforts to address the needs of a region that, while maintaining its autonomy, faces increasing economic, regulatory, and social pressures. 📊 For instance, the new cantonal tax law effective from January 1, 2024, introduces higher rates for incomes over 150,000 francs, influencing cross-border residents’ choices.

To stay informed, it’s recommended to regularly check the dedicated sections on Frontaliere Ticino, where details on upcoming meetings, regulatory updates, and planning tools will be available. An operational checklist includes: participating in public assemblies, reviewing legislative proposals on official websites, analyzing tax and transport data, and comparing practical merger versus autonomy scenarios. ⚠️ Practical comparison: a merger among municipalities could reduce administrative costs by 10-15% through service and structure integration, while maintaining autonomy offers greater local control but at higher costs and coordination challenges.

What are the main financial incentives offered by canton for the aggregation process of Locarnese municipalities?
