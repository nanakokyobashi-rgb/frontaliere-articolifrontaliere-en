Strong franc: how the CHF-EUR exchange rate affects cross-border salaries

The Swiss franc keeps strengthening against the euro. For cross-border workers spending in Italy, the exchange rate can mean hundreds of euros more or less each month.

Key facts

  • Reference rate: 0.9462 CHF per EUR, ECB figure for 18 September 2026
  • Cross-border workers in Ticino: 78,561 in Q1 2026
  • Illustrative conversion: CHF 5,000 ÷ 0.9462 = about EUR 5,284
  • Caution: the ECB rate is indicative and excludes fees and the worker’s individual tax method

A strong franc has an immediate effect when a salary is paid in CHF and expenses are paid in EUR. On 18 September 2026, the ECB reference rate was 0.9462 CHF per EUR. At that rate, CHF 5,000 mathematically equals about EUR 5,284 before fees: this is a currency illustration, not a forecast of take-home pay. Ticino’s official statistics counted 78,561 cross-border workers in Q1 2026. To estimate the effect on your own situation, consider the conversion date, provider fees, actual salary and the tax rules that apply to you.

Operational details

A favourable rate can increase purchasing power in Italy, but the result depends on volatility and on when francs are converted. For Ticino companies, the rate affects the relative cost of cross-border labour; for workers, the ECB rate is only a reference and the bank or exchange service may offer a different rate. Tax treatment also requires care. Italian taxes may require conversion into euros under the method applicable to the individual case; the daily ECB figure should not automatically be used as a tax base. For mortgages, savings and household budgets, compare scenarios and record the conversion date and costs.

Key points

To manage exchange-rate risk, keep an illustrative calculation separate from tax planning: record the rate, date, amount and fees for each conversion and use the simulator only as an estimate. Holding CHF and EUR accounts can spread conversions over time but does not remove the risk. Check your provider’s conditions and ask a professional which method applies to your tax return. Frequently Asked Questions
How much is CHF 5,000 at an indicative rate of 0.9462?
CHF 5,000 divided by 0.9462 CHF per EUR equals about EUR 5,284 before fees. This is an illustrative conversion and is not automatically the net amount on a payslip.
Does the ECB rate determine Italian tax?
No. The ECB rate is a comparison reference. The tax conversion depends on the method applicable to the individual case and the required documentation.
What happens if the franc weakens?
With the same CHF salary, a weaker franc reduces the EUR equivalent. The monthly impact depends on the amount, the applied rate and fees; there is no fixed loss that applies to everyone.
How can I manage EUR/CHF volatility?
Compare service costs, avoid concentrating every conversion on one day and keep an adequate reserve in the currency of your expenses. For tax matters, follow the method applicable to your case.
