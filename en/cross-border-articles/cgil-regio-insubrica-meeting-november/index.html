The request after the meeting

Giuseppe Augurusa, CGIL trade unionist for cross-border workers, relaunched the issue of the health tax at the end of the annual assembly of the Regio Insubrica. The body brings together institutions and economic and social realities of the border territories.

For those who work across the border, the point raised by the union concerns the healthcare contribution expected for some of the old cross-border workers. Augurusa defines it as a health tax and disputes the fact that, despite having been in force for two and a half years, it still does not have the implementing decrees. The consequence indicated is a condition of uncertainty, made more complex by different positions between the Regions and institutions involved.

According to the CGIL, the issue cannot be separated from the institutional relations between Italy and Switzerland. It is intertwined with the refunds intended for border municipalities, a dossier that has already produced a distinction between 2026 and 2027.

For 2026, an agreement brought the refunds from 89 to 108 million euros. Augurusa evaluates the result positively, but draws attention to the following year: Ticino has precautionally blocked the refunds on Lombardy at 50%, while it has assigned them in full to Piedmont.

This choice, according to the union, is linked to the Swiss protest against the Italian introduction of the health tax. The Swiss counterpart considers it incompatible with the agreements between the two countries. For local authorities, therefore, the greatest certainty concerns 2026; the 2027 framework remains open.

The passage of the annual presidency of the Regio Insubrica to Piedmont is seen as a possible point of reopening. The CGIL representative observes that the Piedmont Region has a position on the tax that is very close to that of the trade unions. Added to the same opportunity is the reactivation of the inter-ministerial table, which occurred a year and a half after its establishment. The table met in recent weeks and a new meeting is scheduled for early November.

Hence the request to include in the comparison the various still unresolved dossiers on cross-border work. In the words of the union, the Regio should carry out a liaison and guidance function towards Italian and Swiss politics and legislators. The objective is to recompose relations on border issues, starting from the health tax. Augurusa's final hope is modest: a peaceful period.
