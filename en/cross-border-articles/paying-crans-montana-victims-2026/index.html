Switzerland disagrees on Crans-Montana fire compensation (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 6 May 2026

Switzerland is considering covering healthcare costs for Italian victims of the Crans-Montana fire, but the idea is dividing opinions in Bern.

Context

In brief

  • Switzerland might cover healthcare costs for Italian victims of the Crans-Montana fire.
  • The European regulation provides an exception for exceptional cases.
  • The idea divides the Swiss Parliament, with criticism from the PLR.

Key facts

  • What: Possible coverage of healthcare costs for Italian victims.
  • When: Discussions ongoing, proposal advanced by President Parmelin.
  • Where: Switzerland and Italy, with a focus on Crans-Montana.
  • Who: Swiss and Italian governments, Swiss Parliament.
  • Amount: Not yet specified.

Switzerland is considering covering healthcare costs for Italian victims of the Crans-Montana fire on January 1, 2026. During his meetings with Giorgia Meloni and Sergio Mattarella, Swiss Confederation President Guy Parmelin mentioned that Switzerland could cover hospitalization costs for Italian patients. The European regulation on the distribution of healthcare costs indeed provides an exception in this regard.

"The regulation has so far been applied to all European countries and not just Italy," Parmelin stated. "However, there is a possibility of an exception, and we want to evaluate the consequences if the Federal Council wants to use it. Consequences not only from a financial point of view but also regulatory. We will conduct this analysis."

Analysis of implications

The idea of covering healthcare costs for Italian victims has sparked heated debate in Bern. While the Swiss government seems open to the possibility of using the exception provided by European regulations, the Swiss Parliament is divided. National Councilor of the Liberal Radical Party (PLR) Cyril Aellen criticized the proposal, arguing that the issue should be addressed with ordinary law.

"In cases like this, Italians should pay for their citizens and Swiss for theirs. This seems much more sensible to me," Aellen declared. "However, they are trying to make Switzerland pay more than Italy would pay for us, since the services between the two countries are not equivalent."

Aellen fears that other countries may in the future ask for an exceptional regime when they find an advantage: "Every time there is a problem, France or other countries will say: there is a precedent, you paid the Italian bills. Now pay ours."

Possible scenarios

If Switzerland decides to cover healthcare costs for Italian victims, it would set a significant precedent. This could lead other countries to ask for similar treatment in analogous cases. However, the Swiss government has emphasized that the goal is to ensure that affected families and injured parties do not have to bear any costs.

"Our goal is to find the best solution for the victims," Parmelin stated. "On both sides, the fire has stirred many emotions, which I can understand. But in terms of bilateral relations, the discussions we have conducted at the ministerial level, there have been no tensions."

Implications for cross-border workers

For cross-border workers who work in Switzerland and reside in Italy, this discussion could have significant implications. If Switzerland decides to cover healthcare costs for Italian victims, it could pave the way for greater cooperation between the two countries in terms of cross-border healthcare assistance. However, criticism from the Swiss Parliament could slow down the process.

Conclusion

The final decision will be up to the Swiss Federal Council, which will have to carefully evaluate the financial and regulatory consequences. Meanwhile, the competent authorities of both countries will remain in contact to find a solution that satisfies all parties involved.

Operational details

Analysis of Criticisms

The criticisms raised by National Councilor Cyril Aellen reflect a broader concern within the Swiss Parliament. Aellen argues that the proposal to cover healthcare costs for Italian victims could set an unwanted precedent. In his view, ordinary law should be applied uniformly, without exceptions.

"Due to the emotion stirred by the Crans-Montana drama, there is a will to handle things specifically. However, victims [of tragedies] are numerous in our country. They deserve fair treatment — Aellen emphasized —. A country's reputation is not built by granting favorable treatments."

Comparison with the Previous Situation

Before this proposal, healthcare costs for Italian citizens involved in accidents in Switzerland were generally covered by their respective national healthcare systems. The possibility of an exemption represents a significant change from the established practice. This could impact bilateral relations between the two countries, improving collaboration in healthcare assistance.

Procedure for Covering Expenses

If the Swiss Federal Council decides to approve the proposal, the procedure for covering healthcare costs should be clearly defined. This could include the creation of a special fund for victims of cross-border accidents or the signing of specific bilateral agreements. This approach could enhance Switzerland's reputation as a supportive and collaborative country, despite the criticisms from Parliament.

Conclusion

The final decision will rest with the Swiss Federal Council, which will have to carefully evaluate the financial and regulatory consequences. Meanwhile, the competent authorities of both countries will remain in contact to find a solution that satisfies all parties involved.
