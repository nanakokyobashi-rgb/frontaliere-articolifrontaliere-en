For Intragna border workers who find work in Ticino, the change is decisive: the tax at source is withheld in Switzerland, and Italy completely avoids double taxation on income from border work. Operational details

Cost of living: living in Intragna is cheaper than Ticino

One of the main advantages of moving to Intragna rather than Ticino is the immediate economic impact. The rent for a two-bedroom house in Intragna is around €400-600 per month, while in Lugano the same square meters cost €900-1,400. Utilities, food, catering and leisure services follow the same pattern: around 30-40% cheaper than in Ticino municipalities. However, the cross-border worker has to compensate for this advantage with the costs of daily commuting.

For those who use a personal car, the calculation is simple: fuel (around €0.30 per kilometre), vignette A2/A9 (CHF 40 per year), maintenance and wear. A daily Intragna-Lugano journey (about 50 km return) costs on average €12-15 in petrol per day, plus €50-100 monthly in indirect costs. Those who choose public transport (regional trains, cross-border buses) can sign up for monthly passes between CHF 200 and CHF 350, depending on the destination area and the operator. Many cross-border workers in Intragna find the public cost more convenient because it allows them to work during the journey.

The net economic benefit therefore depends on the gross salary in Switzerland. A cross-border worker earns around 20-30% more in Ticino than he would earn for the same role in Lombardy or Piedmont. After all the Swiss deductions (cantonal tax, AVS 5.3%, LPP 7-18%, AD/AC 1.1%, LAINF 0.7-1.5%) and the payment of transport costs, the monthly net of a cross-border commuter from Intragna remains significantly higher than those living in Ticino: the lower Italian cost of living more than offsets the commuting costs.

G permit and social insurance registration

The G permit (Grenzgänger, Cross-border worker) is the legal instrument that allows an Italian citizen to work daily in Switzerland without having to move. It's completely free — no taxes, security deposits or administrative fees. Once a Ticino employer hires the cross-border worker, the procedure starts automatically through SEM (Swiss Secretariat of State for Migration) and the competent Italian police headquarters. Release times are generally 4-8 weeks.

On the first day of work in Switzerland, the employer communicates the hiring to the social security bodies (AVS). The cross-border worker automatically receives an AHV number (Swiss social security identification number) by post. Registration for the KVG (Swiss compulsory health insurance) must be carried out by the cross-border worker himself within the first month of work. There are two options: sign up for a KVG with a Swiss company (adult deductibles vary from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 per year depending on the coverage), or maintain Italian health coverage and invoke the right of option provided by the new Agreement. The majority of cross-border commuters from Intragna choose the Swiss KVG to avoid administrative complications between the two healthcare systems.

Swiss social security contributions are automatically deducted from the salary: AVS/AI/IPG 5.3% for employees, AD/AC 1.1% up to CHF 148,200 annual salary, LAINF (accident insurance) between 0.7 and 1.5%. Added to these are the LPP (professional pension fund) quotas, which vary from 7% to 18% depending on the age group of the worker (from 25 years of age onwards).

Key points

Concrete checklist: the steps to become a cross-border worker from Intragna

Phase 1: Finding a job in Ticino The first step is to identify a Ticinese company willing to hire with a G permit. Consult specialized job listings for Ticino offers, or directly contact manufacturing, commercial, healthcare, or public administration companies in Ticino. Many large companies (hospitals, factories, professional firms) actively hire cross-border workers.

Phase 2: Starting the G permit procedure Once the employment contract is signed, the Swiss employer submits an application to SEM (State Secretariat for Migration). The request is forwarded to the Italian police headquarters (questura) of the province of residence (VCO). In the meantime, the cross-border worker cannot yet start working. Authorization times: generally 4-8 weeks, sometimes up to 12 if there are additional verifications.

Phase 3: Opening a Swiss bank account This is mandatory to receive your monthly salary. Ticinese banks (Raiffeisen, UBS, BancaStato, others) have streamlined procedures for cross-border workers. Required documents: valid Italian identity document, Italian residence certificate, employment contract, AHV number (received after registration).

Phase 4: Choosing health insurance (LAMal or Italian option) Within the first month of work, the cross-border worker must take out a LAMal policy with a Swiss insurer (Helsana, CSS, Visana, Concordia, others) or declare that they are maintaining Italian coverage. If LAMal is chosen, the options are: basic models (CHF 300-600 annual deductible), semi-co-insurance (CHF 800-1,200), or low-cost models with a CHF 2,500 deductible. The choice affects the monthly premium (usually CHF 300-450).

Phase 5: Filling out the Italian tax return (Form 730) By April 30 of the following year, the cross-border worker fills out Form 730 through a CAF (Tax Assistance Center) or independently online (Agenzia delle Entrate website). Attach the Swiss withholding tax statement provided by the employer. Complete section CE (foreign income) by indicating foreign employment income and taxes already paid in Switzerland. This step is crucial: it enables the tax credit mechanism provided by the 1976 Italy-Switzerland tax treaty.

Tax refunds and recovery of excess taxes

The €10,000 limit is not a general allowance: Article 4 of Italian Law No. 83/2023 applies it only to the income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law No. 147/2013. If so, they are entitled to a refund. The process happens automatically through the Agenzia delle Entrate after the tax office compares Swiss data with Italian data. Refunds generally arrive between May and September of the following year, via credit to the Italian account.

Many cross-border workers from Intragna recover between CHF 500 and CHF 3,000 annually, depending on their income level and the applied Swiss cantonal tax expertise. Frequently Asked Questions
Which income does the €10,000 limit apply to?
Article 9’s transitional regime applies only to workers who meet the Agreement’s definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between 31 December 2018 and 17 July 2023; covered employment income is taxable exclusively in Switzerland. The ordinary regime applies in other cases, with Italian taxation and credit for Swiss tax where applicable. The €10,000 in Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is only the limit for income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law 147/2013, not a general allowance. Is the G permit really free for those who work in Ticino from Intragna?
Yes, completely free. There are no administration fees, security deposits, or paid renewals. The procedure is managed by SEM (State Secretariat for Swiss Migration) and the Italian police headquarters of the VCO province. Times: Normally 4-8 weeks after the employer submits a request. Once approved, the G permit allows you to work every day in Ticino and return daily to Intragna without restrictions.
How much does Intragna-Ticino daily transport cost and which crossings should be used?
With personal car: fuel ~€0.30 per km + vignette A2/A9 CHF 40 per year + maintenance. For an Intragna-Lugano journey (~50 km round trip), daily cost ~€12-15. By public transport: monthly subscription CHF 200-350 depending on the destination. The main passes for Intragna are Brogeda and Gaggiolo, with waiting times of 10-15 min during normal hours, 20-30 min in the morning (7-9 am) and evening peaks (17-19). Intragna-Lugano: 35-45 min total; Intragna-Bellinzona: 50-60 min.
What social and social security insurance covers the border worker with a G permit?
The employer automatically registers the frontier worker with the Swiss AVS (Old Age and Disability Insurance) and the LPP (Occupational Pension Fund). The border worker must choose a Swiss LAMal (compulsory health insurance) or maintain Italian coverage. Contributions withheld from salary: AVS/AI/IPG 5.3% for employees + AD/AC 1.1% up to CHF 148,200 + LAINF 0.7-1.5% + LPP 7-18% according to age group (from 25th year). The Italian INPS coordinates with the Swiss authorities to avoid double registrations.
How do I recover taxes if I have paid too much in Switzerland on my return?
Fill in the Italian form 730 by 30 April of the following year, attaching the employer's Swiss withholding tax statement. Complete the CE chart indicating foreign income and taxes already paid. The €10,000 limit is not a general allowance: Article 4 of Italian Law No. 83/2023 applies it only to the income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law No. 147/2013. If you have paid more than you should, the refund is disbursed between May and September. Many border workers from Intragna recover CHF 500-3,000 per year.
