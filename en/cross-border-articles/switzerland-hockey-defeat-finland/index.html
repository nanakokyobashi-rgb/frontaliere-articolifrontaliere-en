For the thousands of Italian workers who cross into Ticino every morning, this result lands differently. Because in this part of Switzerland, hockey is not just a sport — it is a shared cultural language.

Hockey in Ticino: a social institution

Whether you hold a Permit G or B, following HC Lugano or HC Ambrì-Piotta is one of the most effective ways to integrate into the professional and social fabric of Ticino. Conversations about the Friday night game, debates about the summer transfer window, and animated office discussions after a Lugano-Ambrì derby are part of daily working life in this region in a way that is hard to overstate.

HC Lugano — founded in 1941, playing at the 8,500-seat Cornèr Arena — is one of the most supported clubs in the Swiss National League. HC Ambrì-Piotta, with its new Valascia arena holding 6,200 fans, has built an identity around underdog resilience that makes it arguably the most emotionally resonant club in Swiss sport.

For a cross-border worker living in Como, Varese, or anywhere along the border strip, knowing the standings, the key players and the results gives you immediate common ground with Swiss colleagues who might otherwise remain professionally distant.

Operational details

Season tickets and match-day tickets: what cross-border workers need to know

The Swiss National League regular season runs from September to March, with playoffs extending into April or May. Attending games from Italy is entirely practical — here is what to expect on cost and logistics.

Approximate prices for the 2025/26 season:

At HC Lugano, a season ticket for the standing sections (Curva) typically costs between CHF 350 and 550 for the full regular season. Central seated sections start around CHF 800. Single-match tickets for popular sections generally range from CHF 25 to 45.

At HC Ambrì-Piotta, the new arena has made season tickets slightly more accessible (around CHF 280–420 for standing sections), but demand is very high. Historic members have priority, and individual match tickets often sell out during presale.

By comparison, equivalent Italian Serie A football tickets rarely exceed EUR 20-30 — Swiss hockey is more expensive, but the atmosphere and proximity to the ice are genuinely exceptional.

Practical scenarios

Scenario A — Permit G worker based in Lugano: Buys a lateral-section HC Lugano season ticket (roughly CHF 600 for the season). With around 34 home games, the per-game cost drops to under CHF 18. Lugano-Agno airport area car parks cost about CHF 5; the Cornèr Arena is also reachable by tram from Lugano station.

Scenario B — Permit B worker resident in Lugano: Same ticket prices apply. Additional options include family packages (approximately 40% discount for under-16s) and some employers who organise corporate matchday events as an informal staff benefit.

The main practical difference for cross-border workers is transport timing, not ticket cost. Budget an extra 30-60 minutes versus a resident, and always check the last train back before you go.

Following hockey from Italy without a season ticket

Not every game requires physical attendance. The National League has broadcast agreements with MySports (available via streaming, accessible from Italy with a direct subscription or VPN) and Blue Sport (Sunrise's sports platform, requires a Swiss subscription). For free coverage, the clubs' official apps and YouTube channels publish highlights within hours of the final whistle.

For the most important games — playoff semi-finals and finals — tickets are also available on resale platforms such as Ticketcorner and StubHub CH. Prices rise above face value, but for a special occasion trip from Italy, it can be worth it.

Frequently Asked Questions
Can I buy an HC Lugano or HC Ambrì-Piotta season ticket as an Italian resident?
Yes. Both clubs sell season tickets to Italian residents without restrictions. Select 'abroad' as your residence at checkout. European credit cards are accepted. Season ticket campaigns typically open in May or June for the following season.
What time do games end and can I catch a train back to Italy?
Games generally start at 19:45 and finish by 22:30. From Lugano, trains run towards Como and Varese past midnight. Check sbb.ch for timetables as late evening frequency varies between weekdays and weekends.
HC Lugano or HC Ambrì-Piotta — which club matters more for workplace integration in Ticino?
It depends on where you work. In Lugano and the Lugano district, support is split between the two clubs but HC Lugano has the broader fan base. In the Sopraceneri region and around Ambrì, local support is near-universal. Following both clubs at least superficially is the most inclusive approach.
Is it possible to watch Swiss National League games online from Italy?
Yes. MySports broadcasts the National League and is accessible via streaming from abroad with a direct subscription. RSI (Swiss Italian television, available free via streaming) covers the later playoff rounds. Club apps also provide live audio commentary and highlight packages.
