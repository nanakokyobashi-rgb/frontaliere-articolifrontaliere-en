Kindergarten Gallarate: crisis not inevitable, plan by June (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 7 May 2026

Anna Zambon of the Gallarate PD asks the new president Agostini for guarantees for families and staff. Criticism of the administration for past management.

Context

In brief

  • Nursery schools crisis in Gallarate, plan by June
  • New president Agostini, criticism of past management
  • Risk of school closures

Key facts

  • What: Crisis at Gallarate Nursery Schools Foundation
  • When: Recovery plan expected by June 2026
  • Where: Gallarate, Lombardy
  • Who: Anna Zambon (Pd Gallarate), Alessandro Agostini (new president)
  • Amount: Not specified

The crisis at Gallarate's nursery schools is not inevitable. This is the strong message from Anna Zambon, a member of Gallarate's Pd, following the appointment of Alessandro Agostini as the new president of the Nursery Schools Foundation. The situation is delicate and concerns families, staff, and the entire city. The recovery plan, expected by June, must be respected to provide clarity on the future of the schools, classes, services, and staff. Zambon does not hesitate to criticize past management, calling it inadequate, and urges the administration to take political responsibility. The crisis did not start today and cannot be blamed on the new president, nor can it be presented as an inevitability. The challenge now is to avoid a setback in services with the possible closure of a school and many classes.

…

Operational details

The crisis of the kindergartens in Gallarate is an issue that concerns not only the city, but also the cross-border workers living in Ticino and working in Lombardy. The possible closure of school buildings and classes could have a significant impact on families who choose Gallarate for its proximity to the Swiss border. The situation requires special attention from local and regional authorities to ensure that educational services remain accessible and of high quality. Cross-border families, in particular, may face additional difficulties if adequate measures are not taken to prevent the closure of fundamental educational facilities.

The implications for cross-border workers

Cross-border workers living in Ticino and working in Lombardy could find themselves in a difficult situation if the kindergartens in Gallarate were to close. The proximity to the Swiss border makes Gallarate a popular choice for many families seeking a balance between work and family life. The closure of school buildings and classes could force families to look for alternatives in other cities, significantly impacting their lifestyle and daily routines. It is crucial that local and regional authorities work together to find solutions that ensure the continuity of educational services for all families, including those of cross-border workers.

Key points

To address the crisis of the Nursery Schools in Gallarate, it is crucial that families, particularly those of cross-border workers, are informed and involved in the decision-making process. Local authorities must ensure that all information is accessible and understandable, so that families can make informed decisions about the educational future of their children. It is important that families are aware of the deadlines and the necessary steps to participate in discussions and public consultations. Cross-border worker families, in particular, may need additional support to navigate the Italian educational system and understand the implications of the decisions made by local authorities.

Next deadlines

The recovery plan for the Nursery Schools of Gallarate must be presented by June 2026. Families and staff must be actively involved in the planning and discussion process. It is important that local authorities ensure that all stakeholders have the opportunity to express their concerns and suggestions. Cross-border worker families, in particular, must be encouraged to participate and make their voices heard. Local authorities must also ensure that information is available in multiple languages, so that all families can access the information they need.
