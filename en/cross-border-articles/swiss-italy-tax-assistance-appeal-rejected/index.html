Swiss-Italy Tax Assistance: Appeal Rejected (cross-border guide)

Di Samuele Valente · Published on 23 July 2026

The Swiss Federal Court has declared inadmissible the appeal of an Italian taxpayer against the exchange of required banking information

Context

In the matter of tax administrative assistance, a matter of fundamental importance pursuant to art. 84a LTF does not arise when the taxpayer disputes only the application to the concrete case of already established jurisprudential principles relating to subsidiarity, the probable relevance of the information requested and the prohibition of fishing expedition. With judgment 2C_285/2026 of 4 June 2026, the Swiss Federal Court declared inadmissible the appeal brought by an Italian taxpayer against the decision by which the Swiss authorities had authorised the transmission to Italy of banking information requested as part of the administrative tax assistance provided for in Article 27 of the Double Taxation Convention between Switzerland and Italy.

Operational details

Specifically, before the Federal Court, the applicant argued, on the one hand, the violation of the principle of subsidiarity, considering that the Italian authority had not exhausted the investigative tools available in the national territory and, on the other, the absence of the requirement of the "probable relevance" of the information requested, qualifying the request as a fishing expedition based on mere assumptions. However, the Court ruled out that the dispute raised questions of law of fundamental importance. The judges recalled that the criteria for identifying an exploratory request and those relating to the likely relevance of the information have already been defined by case law.

Key points

Similarly, the relationship between the principle of subsidiarity, good faith and mutual trust between Contracting States is also consolidated. According to the Federal Court, the applicant did not seek to clarify new aspects of the law of administrative assistance, but only challenged the way in which already known principles had been applied to the concrete case. The ruling confirms the restrictive orientation followed by Swiss jurisprudence in accessing the Federal Court in matters of international tax cooperation and definitively leaves the transmission of the information requested by the Italian tax authority free.

What was the main decision of the Swiss Federal Court regarding tax administrative assistance?

This decision authorised the transmission to Italy of banking information required in the context of administrative tax assistance, confirming the restrictive orientation of Swiss jurisprudence on international tax cooperation.
What arguments did an Italian taxpayer use to oppose the transmission of banking information?
The Italian taxpayer contested the violation of the principle of subsidiarity, arguing that the Italian authority had not exhausted internal investigative tools. In addition, he objected to the absence of the requirement of 'probable relevance' of the information, qualifying the request as a 'fishing expedition' based on mere assumptions and not on concrete data.
Why did the Swiss Federal Court dismiss the taxpayer's claim for tax assistance?
The Federal Court ruled out that the dispute raised questions of law of fundamental importance. He recalled that the principles of subsidiarity, probable relevance of information and prohibition of 'fishing expedition' are already consolidated in case law. On what legal basis does the exchange of tax information between Switzerland and Italy take place?
The exchange of banking and tax information between Switzerland and Italy takes place on the basis of Article 27 of the Double Taxation Convention between the two countries. This convention regulates tax administrative assistance, allowing the transmission of data at the request of the competent authorities, as established by case law.

No, the ruling does not establish a new precedent, but confirms the restrictive orientation already followed by Swiss jurisprudence in accessing the Federal Court in matters of international tax cooperation.
