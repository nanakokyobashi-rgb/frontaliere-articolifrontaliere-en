Slowing Swiss labour market: what's changing for frontier workers (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 7 July 2026

Swiss unemployment rate stable at 3.1% seasonally adjusted in June. The estimate for 2026 and 2027 has been revised upwards due to energy prices.

Context

In brief

  • Seasonally adjusted unemployment rate in Switzerland remains stable at 3.1% in June 2024
  • Slight decrease in the overall figure (2.9% vs 3.0% in May), but the seasonally adjusted figure confirms the slowdown
  • SECO attributes the dynamics to high energy costs and revised upwards estimates for 2026 and 2027

Key Facts

  • What: Slowdown in the Swiss labor market confirmed by seasonally adjusted data
  • When: June 2024, with a press conference on 6 July
  • Where: Switzerland (data SECO)
  • Who: SECO, Martin Godel (responsible for labour market and unemployment)
  • Amount: Seasonally adjusted rate 3.1% (0.3 points above neutral); estimate 2026 at 3.1% and 2027 at 3.0%
  • Overall: 2.9% in June vs 3.0% in May
  • Additional signal: Increase in occupational categories with vacancy reporting obligation

The Swiss labor market is showing signs of slowing down, as confirmed by data released on Monday, July 6 by the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO). During the conference call commenting on the June 2024 statistics, Martin Godel, head of the labor market and unemployment insurance sector at SECO, pointed out that the seasonally adjusted unemployment rate remains stable at 3.1%. A value that, according to Godel, is 0.3 points higher than the level considered neutral for an economy with a "normal workload".

The overall unemployment figure, without the effect of seasonality, recorded a slight decrease: from 3.0% in May to 2.9% in June. However, the seasonally adjusted rate — the one that best captures the real market trend — does not show substantial improvement. Indeed, according to the forecasts of the SECO, the situation could worsen in the coming years due to the still high energy prices.

The role of energy costs and forecasts SECO

The experts at SECO have already taken the difficult economic environment into account in their assessment of the Swiss economy, which was carried out in mid-June. Energy prices, which have remained high for an extended period, are holding back growth both globally and in Switzerland. For this reason, estimates for the average unemployment rate have been revised slightly upwards: they are now forecast at 3.1% for 2026 and 3.0% for 2027.

Vacancies on the rise: a warning sign

Another indicator of concern is the increase in the number of professional categories affected by the obligation to report vacancies. This reflects the growth in the number of unemployed in Switzerland in recent years. According to Godel, the phenomenon is expanding and involves more and more sectors, a sign of a declining demand for labor compared to supply.

For cross-border commuters working in Ticino, these data could have indirect repercussions. A slowing labor market could reduce employment opportunities, especially in sectors most exposed to economic fluctuations. However, at the moment there are no concrete indications of layoffs or staff reductions that directly affect the audience of cross-border workers.

What to watch in the coming months

Cross-border commuters working in Ticino should keep an eye on the development of the local and Swiss labour market. In particular, it is useful to follow: - The evolution of job vacancies in the areas of expertise - The decisions of Ticino companies that hire cross-border workers, especially in areas such as health, construction and services - Any updates on recruitment policies from cantonal and federal authorities

Operational details

Frontalieri in Ticino: which sectors could be affected by the slowdown

The data released by SECO is not directly related to cross-border workers, but their indirect impact on the Ticino job market could be significant. Here's an overview of how transnational workers could be involved in the ongoing dynamics.

Sectors at risk and opportunities for cross-border workers

The slowdown in the Swiss job market is transversal, but some sectors are more exposed than others. According to SECO experts, high energy costs are slowing down energy-intensive sectors such as manufacturing and logistics services. Retail trade and hospitality could also be affected by reduced consumption.

However, Ticino represents a special case: its economy is strongly linked to transfrontal commuting, especially in the healthcare, construction, and services sectors. These sectors, while not exempt from risks, could be less vulnerable due to labor demand from Italy.

Comparison with the pre-pandemic situation

Before the pandemic, the unemployment rate in Switzerland stood at around 1.8-2.0%. After the peak of 2.9% in 2021, the market had started a slow recovery. Now, with the seasonally adjusted figure at 3.1%, we are approaching the levels of 2009, a period of global financial crisis.

For cross-border workers, this means that:

  • Job opportunities in Ticino could become more competitive
  • Companies could be more selective in hiring
  • Salaries could come under downward pressure, especially in less specialized sectors

Practical cases: what could happen to a cross-border worker in Ticino

A cross-border nurse working in Lugano or Mendrisio may not be immediately affected, thanks to the chronic shortage of healthcare personnel in Switzerland. However, if the slowdown were to persist, even this sector could see a reduction in hiring or an increase in temporary working hours.

A cross-border construction worker, on the other hand, could be more exposed. Sectors like construction are sensitive to economic cycles and energy costs (heating, materials). In the event of reduced investment, there could be fewer construction sites and therefore fewer opportunities for cross-border workers.

Impact on work permits and applications

At the moment, there are no reports from the Ticino (DFE) or federal (SEM) authorities of an increase in layoffs among cross-border workers. However, it's useful to remember that:

  • Type G work permits are linked to maintaining employment
  • In the event of dismissal, the G permit expires after 3 months if no new job is found in Switzerland
  • For cross-border workers with a B permit (residence), the situation is more stable, but finding a new job becomes more urgent

Frequently Asked Questions: what really changes for cross-border workers

Will the slowdown in the Ticino job market directly affect cross-border workers?

At the moment, there are no signs that indicate a direct impact on cross-border workers. However, a more competitive job market could make it more difficult to find a new job in the event of dismissal. It's therefore advisable to act preventively, updating your professional profile and expanding your network of contacts.

Can I lose my G permit if my sector is in difficulty?

The G permit is strictly linked to your employment. If you're dismissed and don't find a new job within 3 months, the permit expires. However, if you change employer within this term, you can maintain the permit. It's essential to act promptly and contact SEM or DFE to verify procedures.

Could salaries in Ticino decrease due to the slowdown?

In a less dynamic job market, companies could be more selective and less inclined to offer salary increases. However, in sectors with personnel shortages (like healthcare and construction), salaries could remain stable or even grow due to competition between companies.

What to monitor in the coming months to understand market trends?

Cross-border workers should keep an eye on:

  • SECO's monthly statistics on the unemployment rate
  • Job offers in their sectors of expertise published on portals like jobs.ch or ticino.jobscout24.ch
  • Communications from Ticino (DFE) and federal (SEM) authorities on possible changes to hiring criteria for cross-border workers
  • Announcements from Ticino companies signaling personnel reductions or production site closures

Concrete actions for frontaliers in Ticino: a step-by-step guide

If the Swiss job market is slowing down, frontaliers in Ticino must act quickly to protect their position. Here's a detailed procedure to best address the situation.

Step 1: Assess your job position

First and foremost, it's essential to understand if your sector is at risk or if it enjoys stability. Here's how:

1. Analyze your sector: Check if your sector is among those reported by the obligation to notify of increasing job vacancies. If so, you may be more exposed 2. Monitor job offers: Search for ads in your sector on sites like ticino.jobscout24.ch, jobs.ch or linkedin.com. Compare the frequency and quality of offers compared to 6-12 months ago 3. Contact colleagues and managers: Send an informal message to colleagues or former colleagues to understand if there are rumors of staff reduction or site closures

Step 2: Update your professional profile

In a more competitive market, an updated profile can make a difference:

1. Update your CV: Include your latest experiences, certifications and acquired skills. If you work in the healthcare sector, add any training updates (e.g., courses on new medical procedures) 2. Optimize your LinkedIn profile: Ensure your profile is complete, with relevant keywords for the Ticino market (e.g., 'nurse', 'construction worker', 'maintenance technician') 3. Create a portfolio: If you work in creative or technical sectors, prepare a collection of work done or completed projects. It can be useful to demonstrate your competence

Step 3: Expand your contact network

Networking is essential to find new opportunities, especially in a less dynamic market:

1. Join frontaliers groups: On Facebook or LinkedIn, there are numerous groups dedicated to transfrontier workers in Ticino. Actively participate in discussions 2. Attend job fairs: Organizations like JobUp in Lugano or the Student Fair in Bellinzona often host events dedicated to frontaliers 3. Contact temporary agencies: Some agencies specialized in frontaliers have offices in Como, Varese or Lugano. Request an interview to be included in their databases 4. Attend sector events: If you work in healthcare, participate in congresses or seminars organized by EOC or SUPSI. If you're in construction, look for events promoted by associations like ATT or FASI

Step 4: Evaluate training paths or certifications

If your sector is at risk, consider acquiring new skills:

1. Professional courses: SUPSI and USI offer evening or online courses for foreign workers. For example, technical language courses (technical German for nurses) or specific certifications (e.g., CFC for construction workers) 2. Regulatory updates: If you work in regulated sectors (healthcare, construction), check if there are new training requirements that you could meet before they become mandatory 3. German language: Improving your German level (B1/B2) can open doors to sectors less exposed to slowdown, such as logistics or services

Step 5: Monitor border crossings and waiting times

If the job market slows down, you may have to look for work in Italy or evaluate alternative paths:

1. Avoid Brogeda: The Brogeda border crossing is often congested. Consider alternative routes like Ponte Tresa or Gaggiolo, especially during peak hours 2. Check traffic updates: Sites like www.traffico.ch or the USTAT app provide real-time updates on waiting times at border crossings 3. Plan your trips: If you have to attend a job interview, plan your departure at least 30 minutes in advance of your usual time

Step 6: Prepare a plan B in case of dismissal

If you work with a G permit, job loss can put your stay in Switzerland at risk:

1. Look for a new job within 3 months: The G permit lapses if you don't find a new job within this term. Start sending applications immediately 2. Contact SEM: If you have doubts about the procedure, contact the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) to verify your rights and deadlines to find a new job 3. Consider moving to another canton: If you can't find a job in Ticino, you might move to another Swiss canton. Remember that to work in another canton, you must obtain a new G permit 4. Check options in Italy: If the Ticino market is saturated, consider working in Italy. However, ensure your health insurance (LAMal) remains valid

Step 7: Keep an eye on official communications

Ticino and federal authorities may issue new guidelines or updates for frontaliers:

1. Follow DFE Ticino: The Ticino Department of Finance and Economy publishes updates on procedures, calls and job opportunities for frontaliers 2. Monitor SECO: The State Secretariat for Economic Affairs regularly reports on the Swiss job market 3. Subscribe to newsletters: Some organizations, like the Ticino Chamber of Commerce, send periodic updates on job opportunities and procedures for frontaliers

Will the slowdown in the labour market in Switzerland directly affect frontier workers in Ticino?

What should I do if I lose my job with a G permit in Ticino?
If you are fired, you have 3 months to find a new job in Switzerland. If you do not find work within this time limit, G permission will expire. You can change your employer within 3 months and keep your permission. Contact SEM or DFE Ticino in time to check the procedures.
Can wages in Ticino decrease due to slowdown?
In a less dynamic labour market, companies could be more selective and less likely to offer wage increases. However, in sectors with shortages of personnel (such as health and construction), wages may remain stable or even grow thanks to competition between companies.
How do I know if my sector is at risk in Ticino?
Monitor job offers in your industry on sites such as ticino. jobscout24.ch or jobs.ch. If you notice a decrease in opportunities or an increase in fixed-time positions, your industry may be at risk. Also, check if your compartment is among those marked by the obligation to report the vacancies in increase, as indicated by SECO.
