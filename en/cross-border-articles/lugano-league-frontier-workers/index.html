In the article published on October 20, 2026, Omar Wicht submits an inquiry to the Lugano City Executive concerning the new regional safeguard clause provided for in the Bilateral Agreements III package. The Lega municipal councillor wants to know whether the city executive intends to ask the Council of State to approach the Confederation to assess its application.

The reference is to the strengthening of the clause approved by the Council of States on September 29, 2026. The inquiry notes that the provision would allow Ticino to request targeted measures if serious economic or social difficulties were to arise.

The situation described by Wicht

The inquiry focuses on Lugano, the canton’s main economic hub, where Wicht points to a particularly large number of cross-border workers employed in the advanced tertiary sector and services. According to the municipal councillor, this concentration would affect traffic and housing costs, as well as place pressure on municipal social services and contribute to wage dumping.

Hence the request to clarify whether the situation in the area justifies intervention and whether the City Executive is willing to formally urge the cantonal government to act. The stated objective is to bring the matter before the Federal Council through the Council of State.

The questions follow four steps. The first asks whether the City Executive is aware of the clause and of Ticino’s explicit right to request its activation. The second asks whether the figures described as disproportionate in the submission constitute the serious difficulty required by law. The third concerns a formal letter to the Council of State requesting an assessment with measures limited to Canton Ticino, as provided for by the FDFA. The fourth asks for the reasons for any failure to intervene to protect Lugano residents.

The source text records the submission of the inquiry and the questions addressed to the City Executive; it does not report a response from the City Executive.

Operational details

From the request to a possible decision

The report distinguishes between a request for assessment and an actual activation. Wicht does not announce the application of the clause; he asks the Municipality whether it is ready to write to the Council of State. The cantonal request, according to the intentions set out in the interpellation, should then be submitted to the Federal Council. The source does not say that the Municipality has already decided to proceed.

This sequence clarifies the scope of the initiative. Wicht does not claim that regional measures have already been introduced for Lugano or the Canton of Ticino. Instead, he asks for verification of whether the strengthening of the clause, approved on September 29, 2026, can be invoked for the territory of Ticino. In the document, the condition cited is the presence of a «serious economic or social difficulty»; the assessment of whether it exists is therefore placed at the center of the institutional debate.

Key points

The source's information limits

The inquiry uses the expression “disproportionate numbers,” but the available text does not report counts, percentages, time series or other numerical values. The reader can therefore understand the criterion invoked, but cannot measure the weight of the situation or compare it with a threshold established by law. The source also does not specify what regional measures would be required: it refers to activation and measures limited to Canton Ticino, without describing their content.

No response from the Municipality of Lugano is documented. It is therefore not possible to say whether the city executive supports the request, rejects it or forwards it to the Council of State. Likewise, the text does not record an examination by the Federal Council, a decision by the Confederation or a timetable for any subsequent steps. These limitations distinguish the political proposal from its concrete effects on residents and workers.

The scope for cross-border workers

For those working between Italy and Canton Ticino, the source remains focused on the territorial dimension. It does not address the G permit, AVS, LPP, LAMal, tax revenue sharing, withholding tax or double taxation. It does not mention INPS or Brogeda and does not refer to the expression “new tax agreement 2026.” Consequently, these topics cannot be used to describe the clause or to attribute changes in taxes, social security, insurance, border crossings or payslips to the request.

Regarding employment, the text is limited to indicating the high concentration of cross-border workers in the advanced tertiary sector and services and reporting Wicht’s assessment of wage dumping. In social and urban terms, it identifies traffic, housing costs and pressure on municipal social services as possible repercussions. The documented fact is therefore the inquiry reported on October 20, 2026; outside the content provided, both the Municipality’s response and any cantonal or federal follow-up remain to be verified.
