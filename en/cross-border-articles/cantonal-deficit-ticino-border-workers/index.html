The Council of State published the interim statement numbers for June 2025, revealing an estimated deficit of -49.7 million francs. This result represents an improvement compared to the estimate of -109 million, thanks to an overall increase of 105.3 million francs in revenue and an increase in expenses of 46 million francs.

The increase in revenue is mainly driven by the Swiss National Bank's share of the profit of 106.1 million francs, which was not included in the initial budget. This revenue was not foreseen in the financial statements and contributed significantly to reducing the deficit.

While revenue has increased, expenses have also gone up. The main increase concerns the complementary AVS/AI benefits, which saw an increase of 12 million francs, and the participation in the reduction of the health insurance premium, an equal amount. Furthermore, healthcare costs increased by 4.7 million francs.

Another important aspect is the increase of 8 million francs in personnel spending, in particular for teaching staff in middle schools, high schools and special education. This increase compared to the estimate could have direct implications on working conditions and wages in the Ticino education sector.

Furthermore, increases were recorded in expenditure for asylum seekers (3.3 million), persons with S status (3 million) and refugees (2.5 million), respectively. These increases reflect greater cantonal intervention towards these vulnerable population groups.

Although the data is provisional, the update reveals greater stability in cantonal finances than initially planned. However, economic volatility and uncertainties remain critical factors to monitor in the remainder of the financial year.

Economic context and forecasts

The cantonal budget is structurally linked to the health of the regional economy and the performance of the Swiss National Bank. The unexpected increase in SNB revenue was a stabilizing factor, but expenditure on staff and social services indicates increasing pressure on public finances. For cross-border workers, the balance of the cantonal budget could influence the quality of social security and health services available in the border region.

For cross-border workers in Ticino, the update to cantonal finances requires specific attention to their benefits and rights. Here is a practical guide on what to do based on the announced news.

Access the UFSP/BAG portal to check your supplementary AVS/AI benefits. The increase of 12 million francs in cantonal spending could translate into improvements to your pension coverage. If you are a cross-border worker, verify that your benefits have been updated based on the new 2024 cross-border workers agreement.

2. Check the LAMal deductible

As a cross-border worker, you are entitled to an adult LAMal deductible of 300-2500 CHF. Check the LAMal website or contact the insurer directly to confirm your entitlement to the deductible. If you are not yet registered, complete the registration form available on the official website.

3. Monitor filing deadlines

The income tax return for cross-border workers is based on the 2020 agreement and the 1976 Convention. Make sure to correctly declare employment income in Switzerland and use the Italian tax credit to avoid double taxation. Use our salary calculator to check your tax withholdings.

4. Update your residence permit

If you are a cross-border worker, check the validity of your G or B permit. The new agreement provides specific rules for the transitional regime under Art. 9 of the Agreement, which applies only to those who meet the conventional definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between December 31, 2018 and July 17, 2023; the covered employment income is taxed exclusively in Switzerland. In other cases, the ordinary regime applies, with Italian taxation and a credit for Swiss tax. The 10.000 euro under Art. 4 L. 83/2023 is only the limit for the income referred to in Art. 1, paragraph 175, L. 147/2013, not a general tax-free allowance.
