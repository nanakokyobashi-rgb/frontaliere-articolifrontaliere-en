From S status to B permit in Ticino

Over the past two years, the Canton of Ticino has experienced a significant shift in how Ukrainian refugees are managed, driven by the introduction of the S statute in March 2022. This temporary measure was designed to facilitate the reception of those fleeing the war, representing an emergency response with immediate local impact. However, its temporary nature has raised questions about long-term sustainability, prompting the Swiss Federal Council to extend it multiple times, with the latest extension until March 2027.

“The extension of the S statute until 2027 poses both administrative and social challenges,” states an industry expert. As this situation persists, it is expected that Ukrainian citizens who arrived at the start of the conflict will consider themselves to have lived in Switzerland for at least five years by that date. Swiss law, notably Article 74 paragraph 2 of the Asylum Law, states that if temporary protection is not revoked within this period, beneficiaries automatically acquire a B permit, which is a long-term residence title.

In practical terms, this legal evolution has clear implications. For example, in the municipality of Lugano, it is estimated that around 1,200 individuals holding S status could transition to a B permit by 2027, representing an approximate 15% increase in the number of residents with stable residence permits in the region. Holders of a B permit enjoy broader rights compared to temporary regime beneficiaries, including access to social benefits, more stable institutional responsibilities, and the ability to work without restrictions.

“Access to a B permit also means increased public resources dedicated to integration, estimated at about 10 million Swiss francs annually for Ticino,” highlights the head of the Migration Office. This transition directly impacts integration services, leading to higher demand for language assistance, vocational training, and housing support. In municipalities like Mendrisio and Bellinzona, a 20% rise in requests for social and employment services is already observed, indicating growing pressure on local resources.

For Ticino businesses, especially in construction and manufacturing, the possibility of hiring workers with a B permit marks a turning point. Currently, 70% of cross-border workers in Mendrisio hold a long-term permit, ensuring stability that encourages investments and long-term planning.

Operational details

From S status to B permit in Ticino

Article 74 paragraph 2 of the Asylum Law is the core of the strategy for the integration and regularization of Ukrainian refugees who arrived in Switzerland with S status. This article establishes that if provisional protection is not revoked, beneficiaries automatically qualify for a B permit after five years of legal residence in Switzerland, through a transition mechanism aimed at promoting social and work integration. Practically, this means that Ukrainian citizens arriving with S status in Ticino between 2022 and 2024 will see their status formalized without additional bureaucratic procedures after 2027, helping to stabilize their legal and social position within the Canton.

For cross-border workers and local businesses, this development can bring benefits and challenges. On one side, greater stability allows professionals and companies to plan more securely for the future, also facilitating access to social benefits and participation in integration policies. On the other, an increase in B permit holders could exert more pressure on public services such as healthcare, schools, and transportation, as well as on the local labor market, already under stress in areas like Lugano, Mendrisio, and Bellinzona. For instance, in 2023, the number of residents with a B permit in Ticino was around 55,000, of whom about 8,000 are Ukrainian nationals—a figure that could rise with this transition.

However, the law does not specify in detail how residence and integration checks should be carried out, leaving room for interpretation and potential disputes. It also states that individuals must have maintained regular residence, complied with legal conditions, and participated in integration activities such as language courses or vocational training.

“Legality of residence and participation in integration programs are key criteria for issuing the B permit,” emphasizes the Department of Finance and Economy.

This change from temporary status to B permit involves not only legal aspects but also social and economic ones. Canton authorities, including the Department of Finance, are already preparing monitoring and support tools, such as employment programs and language learning initiatives, to facilitate this transition. The “Active Integration” project, for example, allocates about 2 million Swiss francs for 2024 to local authorities and associations.

For residents, it’s crucial to stay informed about their rights and obligations, including opportunities for vocational training, such as apprenticeships sponsored by the Ticino Chamber of Commerce, and integration into the local labor market, which in 2023 saw a 3.5% increase in foreign employment compared to the previous year.

Legal deadlines are also clear: permit applications must be completed by 2027, following standard procedures set by SECO and the Federal Migration Office, with an average cost of about 250 Swiss francs per application. The operational checklist includes:

  • verifying legal residence and participation in integration programs,
  • collecting documents proving residence and employment,
  • submitting the application to canton authorities by December 31, 2026.

Practical scenarios for companies wishing to employ Ukrainian nationals involve updating hiring practices to ensure new employees meet the conditions for obtaining a B permit.

This issue is also critical considering future demographic challenges and balancing new residents with the sustainability of public services. The projected growth of about 10,000 B permit residents in Ticino over the next three years calls for careful territorial and social planning.
