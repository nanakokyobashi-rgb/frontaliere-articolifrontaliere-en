From S Status to B Permit in Ticino

By Laura Bianchi (editorial byline of the newsroom, AI-generated text) · Published on 22 March 2026 · Updated on 11 October 2026

After at least five years of continuous residence, people with protection status S are entitled to a linked B permit: requirements, the cantonal role and the federal extension to 4 March 2028.

Context

In brief

  • On 19 August 2026 the Federal Council decided to maintain protection status S for people seeking protection from Ukraine until 4 March 2028.
  • People with protection status S who have lived continuously in Switzerland for at least five years are entitled to a B residence permit linked to status S.
  • The linked B permit is not a permanent residence permit: the cantonal authority issues it, it is granted one year at a time, and it remains tied to temporary protection.
  • The concrete procedure in Ticino must be checked with the cantonal migration authority; the federal sources do not set one universal fee or deadline for every case.

Key facts

  • Federal extension: protection status S and Programme S support continue until 4 March 2028.
  • First group affected: the first people who sought protection after the start of the war reach five years of residence in March 2027; this is an administrative milestone, not the end of status S.
  • Legal basis: Article 74 paragraph 2 of the Asylum Act governs the B permit linked to status S.
  • Cantonal assessment: the competent cantonal authority checks the requirements and issues the B permit; SEM approval is not required for this issuance.
  • Duration: the B permit is issued for one year and can be renewed while the relevant conditions remain satisfied. If status S is revoked, the linked B permit automatically ceases to be valid.
  • Integration: Programme S continues, with the Confederation contributing CHF 3,000 per person per year to cantonal integration efforts, especially language support, education and labour-market access.

From status S to a B permit in Ticino

Status S is temporary protection oriented towards return. In Switzerland, the S permit is valid for up to one year at a time and can be extended. The Canton of Ticino explains that status S is issued centrally by the federal authorities and gives holders access to residence, accommodation, assistance, medical care and schooling for children. Gainful employment can begin without a waiting period, but the start of employment must be notified to the competent authorities.

After at least five years of continuous residence, a person with status S is entitled to the B permit provided for by Article 74 paragraph 2 of the Asylum Act. This permit does not replace temporary protection with an independent right of residence: the document records its link to status S, and its validity follows the protection status. SEM also states that status S continues after the B permit is issued and that renewal follows the ordinary process for a residence permit.

On 19 August 2026 the Federal Council updated the time horizon: status S will not be revoked before 4 March 2028 unless there is a fundamental change in the situation. From 20 August 2026, new restrictions also apply to certain applications, including compliance with Ukrainian military obligations where applicable; these restrictions do not affect people who already hold status S.

Operational details

How the B permit linked to status S works

Reaching five years does not by itself turn status S into an ordinary, independent B permit. The rule concerns people who have lived continuously in Switzerland and provides a B permit linked to temporary protection. The practical procedure runs through the cantonal migration authority, which assesses the person’s situation and issues the document when the requirements are met.

The B permit linked to status S:

  • does not revoke protection status S;
  • states that the holder is recognised as a person in need of protection;
  • is valid for one year at a time and can be renewed if the relevant conditions remain present;
  • loses its validity if the Federal Council revokes temporary protection.

SEM states that holders of a B permit linked to status S have the same employment rights they had under status S. Social assistance, however, is governed by cantonal law and may therefore vary from one canton to another. Moving to another canton follows the rules applicable to ordinary residence permits and depends on the person’s situation, employment and the absence of revocation grounds.

There is also a separate route: a hardship residence permit independent of status S. It is not the same route as the linked B permit. SEM lists conditions including at least five years of continuous residence since applying for status S, authorities always knowing where the person lives, serious personal hardship connected with integration, no revocation grounds and an established identity. The application must be submitted in person to the cantonal migration authority, and this route requires SEM approval.

For people living in Ticino, the canton’s Ukraine information page is the starting point for accommodation, support, work and contact details. The SEM page also points to the responsible cantonal offices. Rules can change when the Federal Council changes temporary protection, so the current official source should be checked before sending documents or moving canton.

Key points

Practical guidance for people in Ticino

1. Check the residence timeline: for the linked B permit, the relevant fact is continuous residence in Switzerland, not a generic forecast tied to 2027. 2. Contact the right authority: status S is federal, while the linked B permit is assessed and issued by the cantonal migration authority. The Ticino page and the cantonal contact list linked by SEM help identify the correct channel. 3. Keep the records: address information, cantonal allocation, S-permit renewals and employment notifications may be needed to document continuous residence. 4. Check employment rules: gainful employment is possible without a waiting period, but the start of employment and changes must be notified as instructed by the authorities. 5. Keep the two permits distinct: the B permit linked to status S is not a settlement permit and does not continue after temporary protection is revoked. An independent permit follows ordinary immigration law or, where relevant, the hardship route. 6. Recheck the official date: on 19 August 2026 the Federal Council set 4 March 2028 as the date before which status S will not be revoked, unless there is a fundamental change. The position remains subject to federal decisions and coordination with the Schengen states.

Cantonal authorities may request different documents depending on the individual situation. In particular, readers should not assume a universal deadline of 31 December 2026, a standard cost of CHF 250, a minimum twelve-month contract or one income threshold for everyone: those claims are not established by the official sources used for this guide.

Frequently Asked Questions
After how long can a person with status S receive a B permit?
After at least five years of continuous residence in Switzerland, the person is entitled to a B permit linked to status S. The cantonal migration authority checks the requirements and issues the document; it is granted for one year at a time and remains linked to temporary protection.
How long will protection status S be maintained for people from Ukraine?
On 19 August 2026 the Federal Council decided to extend status S and Programme S until 4 March 2028, unless there is a fundamental change in the situation. This date does not make the linked B permit permanent.
Is there one deadline or fixed fee for a B-permit application in Ticino?
The federal and cantonal sources consulted do not state one universal deadline or fixed fee for every case. The documents and procedure must be checked with the competent cantonal migration authority.
