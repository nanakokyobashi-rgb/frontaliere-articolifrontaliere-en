Crisis in the Middle East: Impact on Swiss Tourism

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 7 March 2026

The crisis in the Persian Gulf is altering Swiss tourist routes, with flights suspended and fuel prices skyrocketing.

Context

TL;DR

  • Swiss tourism sector hit by Persian Gulf crisis
  • Flights to Dubai and Tel Aviv suspended
  • Fuel prices up 50%, Swiss faces millions in costs
  • Over 5,000 Swiss tourists trapped in the region

Key facts

  • Flights suspended: Dubai until March 10, Tel Aviv until March 22
  • Fuel price increase: 50% since the start of the tensions
  • Additional cost: Three figures in millions of Swiss francs
  • Swiss tourists blocked: Over 5,000
  • Swiss airline: Swiss and Edelweiss affected
  • Tourism flow concern: Swiss Travel Federation (FSV) expressed concern
  • Economic impact: Significant economic losses if situation doesn't stabilize
  • Alternative options: Customers can modify bookings, except for autumn flights

The current situation in the Persian Gulf is causing a wave of changes in the Swiss tourism sector. The crisis in the region, marked by political tensions and conflicts, has led to the suspension of flights to several Arab countries, including Dubai and Tel Aviv. This has forced many Swiss tourists to reconsider their holiday plans, with thousands of fellow countrymen still trapped in the region, awaiting a repatriation flight. The situation is particularly critical for Swiss airline Swiss, which has seen its connections to Dubai suspended until March 10 and those to Tel Aviv until March 22. Heike Birlenbach, Swiss's commercial director, explained that customers can freely modify their bookings, choosing another destination or postponing the trip. However, this option is not available for flights scheduled for autumn. The increase in fuel prices, which has reached 50% since the start of the tensions, represents an additional problem for Swiss. Dennis Weber, the company's financial manager, estimated that if the costs remain at these levels, Swiss would face an additional cost of three figures, expressed in millions of Swiss francs. This could have drastic consequences for the airline's finances. Edelweiss, which connects Zurich to Muscat and Salalah in Oman, declared that it is waiting for further developments before drawing conclusions. A spokesperson for the company indicated that passengers have canceled or postponed flights, while demand for new bookings is lower than in the past. The Swiss Travel Federation (FSV) has expressed concern about a possible reduction in the flow of tourism between Switzerland and the Persian Gulf. This tourist crisis represents a moment of uncertainty for the sector, with potential long-term repercussions. Frequently Asked Questions
What are the flights suspended due to the crisis in the Persian Gulf?
The flights suspended are those of Swiss towards Dubai until March 10 and those towards Tel Aviv until March 22.
What is the impact of the crisis on Swiss tourism towards the Persian Gulf in terms of numbers?
The crisis has led to a suspension of flights, with more than 5,000 Swiss tourists blocked and a 50% fuel price increase, threatening significant economic losses for the sector.
What are the alternatives for Swiss tourists who planned to visit the Persian Gulf?
Swiss tourists can change their reservations for free to choose an alternative destination or postpone the trip. However, this option is not available for scheduled flights in autumn. It is advisable to check offers to other popular destinations or consider holidays in Switzerland.
How is the current situation in the Persian Gulf facing the Edelweiss airline?
Edelweiss is waiting for further developments before making definitive decisions. Passengers canceled or postponed flights, and the demand for new bookings is lower than the past. The airline is monitoring the situation and could adapt its flight plans according to the evolution of the crisis.
What are the potential long-term repercussions for the Swiss tourism sector due to the crisis in the Persian Gulf?
The crisis in the Persian Gulf could lead to a significant reduction in the tourist flow between Switzerland and the region, with negative economic consequences. The increase in operating costs, such as the fuel price, and the loss of confidence of travellers could have long-term repercussions on the Swiss tourist industry, requiring adaptation and diversification strategies.
