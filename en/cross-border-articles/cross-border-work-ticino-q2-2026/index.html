Border Ticino Q2 2026: 79,121 (+0.64%) (cross-border guide)

The Federal Statistical Office (BFS) announced the number of border workers in the Canton of Ticino at the end of the second quarter of 2026: 79,121 people, up compared to the previous three months. The quarter therefore qualifies as an increase: the cyclical variation compared to Q1 2026 is positive by 505 units, equal to +0.64%. On an annual basis, however, the comparison with Q2 2025 shows a slight drop of 457 units (-0.57%), a sign of some volatility in the composition of the Ticino cross-border workforce.

Quarterly change: the rebound from Q1

In the first quarter of 2026, the BFS statistic had recorded 78,616 border crossers. The recovery of 505 units in the following quarter represents a sign of stabilization after the winter months, when traditionally the number of members of the Federal Register of border workers tends to contract. The +0.64% growth, albeit modest, signals a positive trend in the labour market

Operational details

Reading the historical series: seasonal volatility and underlying trend

Looking at the eight quarters available from the BFS, the curve of the Ticino border crossers describes a trend that isfar from linear. In the first quarter of relevance (Q3 2024), the number reached 79,808, representing the maximum peak of the period examined. From there, the flow entered a phase of slight decline, reaching an all-time low in Q4 2025 with 78,698 border crossers. In the following quarter (Q1 2026), the contraction continued to 78,616, marking the point of greatest relative weakness. In Q2 2026 alone, the number rose to 79,121, while still remaining far from the 2024 high.

This trajectory suggests two fundamental considerations. First, seasonal volatility continues to weigh, with winter quarters (Q4, Q1) historically showing a contraction of a few hundred units. Second: net of seasonality, the underlying trend is oscillation around a range of 78,600-79,600 border crossers, without a clear upward or downward direction on multi-year scales. The BFS data therefore indicate an apparent stability, but with underlying dynamics of constant turnover.

Key points

Implications for Payslip and Job Stability

For those who work or intend to look for a job beyond the Ticino border, the FSO figure of 79,121 cross-border workers is, first and foremost, a mirror of the health of the labour market. A stable number, despite slight quarterly fluctuations, signals that the use of cross-border labour remains a consolidated practice in the Ticino economic fabric. This allows those already working in Ticino to maintain reasonable job security, while for those seeking work the employment pool remains considered appealing. At the same time, the figures suggest that competition has not decreased. With the annual decline of -0.57% (457 fewer units compared to Q2 2025), it is possible that some positions previously held by cross-border workers have been reassigned to Swiss residents or citizens of other nationalities, especially in sectors where qualification is high (IT, finance, specialist healthcare). Those working in Ticino as cross-border workers must therefore pay attention to their positioning in the market: a career crystallised in a low-qualification position could prove fragile, while the push towards skills updating (languages, professional certifications, digital skills) remains strategic.

Frequently Asked Questions
How many border workers work in Ticino as of Q2 2026?
According to the Federal Statistical Office (BFS), 79,121 border workers worked in the Canton of Ticino in Q2 2026. The number is up by 505 units (+0.64%) compared to Q1 2026, but is down by 457 units (-0.57%) compared to the same quarter of the previous year (Q2 2025). This figure includes both employees and self-employed persons registered with the Federal Register of Frontier Workers.
What is the gender distribution among border workers in Ticino?
In Q2 2026, 61.6% of Ticino border workers were men (48,700 people), while 38.4% were women (30,420). The imbalance reflects the sectoral composition of cross-border labour demand, concentrated above all in construction, logistics and manufacturing industries, historically predominantly male. Female underrepresentation could also signal greater challenges in reconciling cross-border commuting with family burdens.
Which age group represents the majority of border crossers?
The 50-54 age group is the most numerous, with 11,323 border crossers. Overall, 78.1% of border crossers are between 30 and 59 years old (61,819 people out of 79,121 total). This indicates a mature pool of workers, with consolidated professional experience. On the contrary, young people between 15 and 24 years represent only 3.8% of the total (2,986 people), a sign of a difficult penetration of the cross-border market for Italian recent graduates.
What is the trend of Ticino border crossers in the last two years?
In the last eight quarters (Q3 2024 to Q2 2026), the number of border crossers ranged from a high of 79,808 (Q3 2024) to a low of 78,698 (Q4 2025). Currently, the number remains in the mid-range of this range, at 79,121, with a slight recovery compared to the winter lows. Seasonal volatility remains evident, while the underlying trend shows neither clear growth nor significant contraction.
How does this data affect tax and social security planning?
BFS data photograph the pool of cross-border workers, but do not disaggregate by income. However, knowing that 61.6% are men and that the age group 50-54 is more numerous helps to understand the average profile of those who work in Ticino as an Italian. Each border crossing contributes to AVS, LPP and LAMal in Switzerland, and must manage Italy-Switzerland double taxation.
