Undeclared cash: seizures at Brogeda

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 5 October 2026

Two checks at the Como-Brogeda motorway border crossing led to the seizure of 128.500 and 113.450 euro, amounts exceeding the threshold of 10.000 euro.

More than 240 thousand euros in cash were intercepted in two separate operations at the Como-Brogeda motorway border crossing. The operation was carried out by personnel from the Gruppo Ponte Chiasso of the Guardia di Finanza. The checks involved two different groups of travelers and ended with the seizure of the amounts exceeding the 10.000-euro threshold provided for by current currency regulations.

The first operation

In the first check, a couple of travelers declared that they were carrying a modest amount of cash intended for the purchase of a property in Spain. However, the declaration did not indicate possession of amounts equal to or greater than 10.000 euros. The authorities reported detecting inconsistencies in the statements provided and, for this reason, the officers conducted a more thorough inspection of the vehicle.

The inspection led to the discovery of 138.255,53 euros in cash, in various denominations and currencies. The money was kept in a pouch concealed beneath some luggage. The officers therefore seized 128.500 euros, representing 100% of the amount exceeding the permitted limit.

The subsequent check

A few days later, personnel from the Customs and Monopolies Agency and members of the Guardia di Finanza checked two other travelers. They too declared that they were carrying an amount below the threshold established by law. The inspection was extended to the vehicle and, while the luggage was being checked, 123.431,45 euros in cash were found inside a shopping bag.

In this second incident, the seizure of 100% of the excess, amounting to 113.450 euros, was ordered. The sums seized in the two operations were held as security for the administrative penalties. Their amount is to be determined by the Ministry of Economy and Finance, which is also responsible for imposing them. Operational details

For a cross-border worker crossing Brogeda, it is useful to define the scope. The source does not address the G permit, AVS, LPP, LAMal, INPS or withholding tax: it describes the transport of cash, the currency threshold and vehicle checks. This distinction avoids conflating the incident with cross-border employment taxation or social security.

The declaration does not end the inspection

The two incidents show the same operational step, but in different ways. In both cases, the travelers declared a modest sum or an amount below the prescribed threshold. Subsequent checks, however, led to the discovery of higher amounts. The useful point for anyone passing through the border crossing is therefore the correspondence between what is declared and the cash actually carried in the vehicle.

The first case mentions the purchase of a property in Spain; in the second, the report focuses on the inspection of two other travelers. The first cash was in a small pouch hidden under the luggage, while the second was in a shopping bag. The difference concerns the place where the cash was found, not the outcome: in both interventions, 100% of the amount exceeding the threshold was seized. The first inspection led to the seizure of 128.500 euro out of 138.255,53 euro found; the second to 113.450 euro out of 123.431,45 euro.

The first discovery included various denominations and currencies. For a separate exchange-rate check, the site provides comparatore di cambio, which does not replace the declaration at the border crossing.

Seizure and penalty are two different stages

The sums are not described as an already determined penalty. They were retained as security for the payment of administrative penalties, which will be determined by the Ministry of Economy and Finance, the authority responsible for imposing them. For the cross-border worker, this distinguishes the immediate outcome of the inspection from the subsequent stage indicated in the source. The certain fact is the retention of the seized sums.

Key points

To read the case as a practical sequence, the cross-border worker passing through Como-Brogeda can follow only the steps documented in the two interventions.

Five steps described by the source

1. Verify the amount being carried. The threshold cited is 10.000 euro. The source links it to the current currency regulations and refers to amounts equal to or greater than that limit.

2. Declare the cash consistently with what is present in the vehicle. In the first episode, a modest sum was declared; in the second, an amount below the threshold. In both cases, the inspection subsequently revealed larger sums.

3. Bear in mind that the inspection may extend to the vehicle and luggage. In the first intervention, a small bag beneath some luggage was checked; in the second, a shopping bag. These are the locations indicated in the source.

4. Distinguish the amount found from the amount seized. In the first case, the figures reported are 138.255,53 euro found and 128.500 euro seized; in the second, 123.431,45 euro found and 113.450 euro seized. In both episodes, the seizure amounted to 100% of the excess.

5. Consider the subsequent phase. The seized sums are held as security for payment of the administrative penalties. The source attributes the determination and imposition of the penalties to the Ministry of Economy and Finance.

This sequence keeps the inspection, the seizure of the excess and the penalty phase separate. It is the operational criterion that emerges from the two checks and should not be confused with a guide to taxation for cross-border employment. To organize the crossing, consult mappa dei valichi.
