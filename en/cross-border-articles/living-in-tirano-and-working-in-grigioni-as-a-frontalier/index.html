Living in Tirano and working in Graubünden as a border worker

Ticino is a region bordering Italy, which is home to many border workers working in Switzerland. Among these, some work in Graubünden, one of the three Swiss republics bordering Italy. But what does it mean to be a frontier in this context?

New border agreement 2024

As of 2024, the new border agreement comes into force. This agreement provides that Ticino border workers working in Graubünden are not subject to double taxation. In practice, this means that frontier workers will not have to pay taxes in both countries, i.e. Switzerland and Italy.

Tax credit to avoid double taxation

To avoid double taxation, border crossers can apply for a tax credit. This credit is calculated based on income earned in Switzerland and can range from a minimum of 1,000 to a maximum of 5,000 CHF per year. For example, if a border worker earns CHF 40,000 a year in Switzerland, the tax credit could be CHF 2,000.

Tax at source on earned income

In Switzerland, the income tax at source is 2.2% for frontier workers. This means that the frontier worker pays the tax only in Switzerland, while in Italy he is not subject to any tax. This scheme favours frontier workers working in Switzerland, but it is important to note that the tax could be higher in Italy, depending on the region of residence.
