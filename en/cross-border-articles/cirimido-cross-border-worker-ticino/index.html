Living in Cirimido, working in Ticino: border guide (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 31 August 2026

Find out how to move to Cirimido and work in Ticino as a border worker: G Permit, tax at source, AVS/LPP, cost of living, border crossings.

Context

In brief

  • Cirimido is located just 1–7 km from the border crossings towards Ticino (Brogeda, Gaggiolo)
  • A Permit G is required to work in Ticino, mandatory for cross-border workers
  • Withholding tax is retained in Switzerland; double taxation is avoided through the Convention
  • New Agreement (effective January 1, 2024): €10,000 exemption (new) or €7,500 (existing)

Key Facts

  • What: Living as a cross-border worker in Cirimido and working in Canton Ticino
  • Where: Cirimido (Lombardy, Como) borders Ticino; border crossings Brogeda, Gaggiolo, Chiasso, Ponte Tresa
  • Permit: Permit G (cross-border workers) mandatory, renewable annually
  • Taxation: Withholding tax retained only in Switzerland; tax credit in Italy
  • Agreement: New Cross-Border Workers Agreement signed December 23, 2020, effective January 1, 2024
  • Rates: AVS/AI/IPG 5.3% (employee), LPP 7–18% per age bracket (from age 25), LAMal exemption CHF 300–2500

Cirimido offers an interesting opportunity for those who want to work in Switzerland while maintaining Italian cost of living. Just a few kilometers from the border with Canton Ticino, this Lombard municipality is home to thousands of cross-border workers who cross into Mendrisio, Lugano, Bellinzona, and other Ticino cities every day. The economic advantage is real — housing, fuel, and food cost less than in Ticino — but this choice requires administrative preparation and a clear understanding of tax and social security regulations. This article explains how to live and work as a cross-border worker in Cirimido, what makes sense, what it costs, which documents you need, and how to navigate the Swiss-Italian system.

Strategic location: border crossings

Cirimido is just a few kilometers from Switzerland. The Brogeda crossing is the closest, about 1 kilometer as the crow flies. Gaggiolo, the second main access point, is about 7 kilometers away. For those working in southern Ticino (Mendrisio, Lugano), Brogeda and Gaggiolo are optimal choices. Those with employment further north (Bellinzona, Locarno, Ascona) will use Chiasso (about 20 kilometers) or Ponte Tresa (about 15 kilometers). This variety of crossings allows the cross-border worker to choose based on the employer's location. The advantage is clear: less time behind the wheel, lower mobility costs, less commute stress.

Permit G: the mandatory document

To legally work in Switzerland as an Italian resident, every cross-border worker must have a Permit G (Grenzgängerausweis). It is not optional: without it, it is not possible to carry out regular dependent work in Swiss territory. Permit G is tied to the employment contract and is issued by Canton Ticino. The procedure is straightforward but requires coordination: the Swiss employer submits the application to the Ticino migration authorities (SEM — State Secretariat for Migration, on behalf of the canton). Processing takes approximately 3–4 weeks. Once approved, the permit is valid for 12 months with automatic renewal as long as the employment contract persists and the cross-border worker does not violate Swiss law. Immediate economic advantages

Those who choose Cirimido significantly reduce their cost of living compared to Ticino. Real estate, groceries, fuel, and services (hairdresser, gym, dining) cost less in Lombardy. Furthermore, salary is paid in CHF (Swiss franc), a currency stronger than EUR, which further increases purchasing power in Italy. A cross-border worker earning CHF 50,000 annually enjoys a more favorable economic situation than a Ticino employee with the same amount in CHF spent directly in Ticino, especially considering the cost of living in Switzerland compared to Italy.

Operational details

Taxation: the Italy-Switzerland border worker system

Taxation of border workers is governed by two main agreements: the Convention between Italy and Switzerland, signed on March 9, 1976, which prevents double taxation; and the New Border Worker Agreement, signed on December 23, 2020 and entering into force on January 1, 2024, which has modernized the taxation regime. The cardinal principle is that the tax on the source of labor income is withheld solely in Switzerland, never in Italy. On the border worker's Swiss pay slip, a line item for "Source tax" withheld by the employer will appear. In Italy, double taxation is avoided through a tax credit: in the Italian tax return (model 730 for employees or Redditi for self-employed), the Swiss income is entered in schedule CE, and the Italian tax authority recognizes a credit for taxes paid in Switzerland, normally resulting in a refund.

Exemptions: how tax charges change

The New Agreement established two different regimes depending on the employment start date:

  • Former border workers (already in status before July 17, 2023): exemption of €7,500 on annual gross taxable income, with transitional regime valid until the end of 2033.
  • New border workers (hired from July 17, 2023 onwards): exemption of €10,000 on annual gross taxable income, permanent.

The exemption reduces the amount on which to calculate the Swiss source tax. Example: a new border worker with annual gross income of CHF 60,000 deducts CHF 10,000, leaving CHF 50,000 taxable in Switzerland. The source tax is calculated on CHF 50,000, not CHF 60,000.

Social Security: AHV, IV, BVG, unemployment

Border workers are obligated to contribute to the Swiss social security system. The contribution rates on salaries (employee portion) are:

  • AHV/IV/EPI (pensions/disability/earnings supplement): 5.3%
  • Unemployment insurance (with upper income limit of CHF 148,200): 1.1%
  • Accident insurance (SUVA): 0.7–1.5%
  • BVG (occupational pension fund, second pillar): 7–18% depending on age bracket from age 25 onwards

AHV is mandatory insurance that contributes to an independent Swiss pension annuity separate from the Italian INPS pension. BVG is the occupational pension fund: the employer contributes one portion, the employee another. Those wishing to deepen their understanding of pension building can consult the guide on AHV and BVG.

KVG: Health Insurance

KVG (Krankenversicherung, health insurance) is mandatory in Switzerland. Border workers have an option: they can sign up for Swiss KVG or maintain Italian health insurance. If they choose Swiss KVG, they pay a monthly premium with a deductible ranging from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 annually depending on the chosen coverage level. The decision must be made within 3 months of starting employment. Many border workers maintain Italian coverage to simplify compliance. For a personalized assessment, consult the KVG/CMI section.

Tax Refunds: recovering taxes paid in excess

If the Swiss source tax was higher than what was owed (because the border worker benefited from deductions, tax brackets, or credits not applied at source), it is possible to request a refund from the Swiss Federal Tax Administration (FTA). In Italy, the 730 provides the tax credit automatically. Combined, refunds from Switzerland and Italy can amount to significant sums — up to several thousand CHF for high-income border workers. To better calculate possible refunds, the site provides a detailed guide to refunds.

Double Taxation: how the Italian tax credit works

The Italy-Switzerland convention of March 9, 1976 prevents the payment of labor income tax in both Switzerland and Italy. If the border worker pays tax in Switzerland (via source withholding), in Italy the tax authority recognizes a tax credit in model 730. The credit is normally equal to the foreign tax paid, up to the amount of Italian tax owed. In practice, most border workers receive a net refund in Italy because the Swiss tax is lower than the Italian tax on the same amount.

Key points

Checklist: practical preparation for relocation

Before relocating to Cirimido as a cross-border worker, it is essential to complete a series of formalities. Here is the recommended order:

1. Sign an employment contract in Switzerland The first step is to find an employer in Ticino (or elsewhere in Switzerland) and obtain a written job offer. Without it, Permit G is not issued.

2. Request Permit G through your employer The Swiss employer fills out the application form and sends it to Canton Ticino (SEM). Processing takes 3–4 weeks. Once approved, the cross-border worker receives the document valid for 12 months, automatically renewable.

3. Notify INPS of the start of employment INPS must be informed of the start of employment in Switzerland. This notification is important to avoid contribution penalties and to ensure that Swiss contributions are properly tracked in the Italian social security system.

4. Choose health coverage Within 3 months of hiring, the cross-border worker decides whether to subscribe to Swiss LAMal or maintain Italian health insurance. If choosing LAMal, they request enrollment through their employer. If remaining with Italian insurance, they communicate the new address to their current insurer.

5. Open a bank account (optional but recommended) Many cross-border workers maintain Italian accounts and exchange CHF/EUR through banks or online services (costs and rates vary). Others open Swiss accounts to receive payment directly in CHF. Check exchange commissions and account maintenance fees.

6. Prepare tax documentation Keep all Swiss tax documentation from your employer (tax certificate, Quittung or equivalent). It is essential for completing the Italian 730 tax return with the tax credit in section CE.

Procedure: annual tax declaration

Each year, the cross-border worker must declare Swiss income in Italy using form 730 (employees) or Income Models (self-employed). In the 730 form, gross Swiss income is entered in section CE as "foreign employment income". Italian tax authorities calculate the tax owed and recognize the tax credit for Swiss tax paid at source. In most cases, the cross-border worker receives an overall refund (the amount of Italian tax owed is less than what was already paid in Switzerland). The declaration must be submitted within the required deadlines (usually by May/June for form 730, by November for Income Models).

AVS and LPP: how pension pillars work

AVS contributions (Old-age, Survivors and Disability Insurance) are mandatory in Switzerland and contribute to a Swiss pension annuity independent of the Italian INPS pension. When the cross-border worker reaches age 65 (men) or 64 (women), or meets contribution criteria, they receive a Swiss AVS annuity based on average wages paid and contribution duration. It is not cumulative with Italian INPS — the two systems remain separate. LPP (Occupational Pension Fund, Swiss second pillar) is the occupational pension fund. It becomes mandatory from age 25 if the annual salary exceeds a minimum threshold. The cross-border worker and employer pay contributions proportional to the age bracket: 7–18% of coordinated salary. Upon termination of employment, the accumulated LPP can be withdrawn in full (as capital) or kept tied up in a custody account (Leistungskonto) for possible future use (home purchase, transfer to new employment).

Scenario: how the New Agreement applies to cross-border workers

Let's imagine a new cross-border worker (hired in 2024) residing in Cirimido and working in Ticino. Annual gross salary: CHF 60,000. Exemption: CHF 10,000. Swiss taxable income: CHF 50,000. Tax at source (federal + Ticino cantonal rate, usually 13–16% combined): approximately CHF 6,500–8,000. AVS/AI/IPG (5.3% of CHF 60,000): CHF 3,180. LPP (example 8%, employee portion): CHF 4,000. Approximate net: CHF 45,320 annually (CHF 3,777/month). In Italy, the cross-border worker declares CHF 60,000 (approximately €63,600) on form 730. Italian tax on €63,600 would be approximately €15,000. But the Italian tax credit recognizes approximately €7,900 (CHF 7,500) paid in Switzerland. Italian net refund: approximately €7,100. Total: CHF 45,320 net Swiss + CHF 7,500 Italian refund (tax credit) = substantial overall savings.

Frequently Asked Questions
Can I work in Ticino from Cirimido without the G Permit?
No, the G Permit is mandatory to work legally in Switzerland as a border worker residing in Italy. Without it, the employment relationship is invalid and the border worker incurs administrative and criminal penalties. The Swiss employer submits the request to the Canton of Ticino; the investigation takes 3–4 weeks.
How much tax do I have to pay in Switzerland if I am a border worker?
Source tax is withheld by the Swiss employer. The amount depends on the gross salary, the deductible (€10,000 for new border workers, €7,500 for old ones until 2033), and the federal/cantonal Ticino rate (usually 13–16% combined). In Italy, the model 730 recognizes a tax credit that further reduces the burden, often generating total refunds.
How much does LAMal cost? Do I have to subscribe to it?
LAMal (Swiss health insurance) has variable monthly premiums (excess CHF 300–2500 per year) depending on the policy chosen. It is not mandatory if you keep Italian insurance. You have the right of option: choose within 3 months of hiring. Many frontier workers remain under Italian cover for simplicity.
What is the economic advantage of living in Cirimido rather than Ticino?
Cirimido offers a cost of living 20–30% lower than Ticino (house, groceries, services). In addition, you receive your salary in CHF (stronger than EUR), amplifying purchasing power in Italy. This economic advantage compensates for the daily logistics at border crossings (Brogeda, Gaggiolo).
What happens if I change employers in Switzerland?
Permit G remains valid as long as the new contract is signed and the employer notifies the Canton of Ticino of the change. The annual renewal is automatic. If you are unemployed for more than 30 days, your leave may be suspended. Contact the Ticino authorities immediately if you change jobs.
