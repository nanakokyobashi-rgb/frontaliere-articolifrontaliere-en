Border pension calculation: AVS + INPS

By Laura Bianchi (editorial byline of the newsroom, AI-generated text) · Published on 22 August 2026 · Updated on 31 October 2026

How the pension works for those who work in Switzerland and reside in Italy: total contributions, acquired rights, new 2024 agreement.

Context

TL;DR

  • The cross-border pension combines Swiss AHV + Italian INPS contributions through aggregation
  • The new tax agreement is applied from 1 January 2024
  • Acquired pension rights remain protected by the right of aggregation

Key facts

  • What: Pension totalization between the two social security systems
  • When: Effective January 1, 2024 (new agreement of December 23, 2020)
  • Where: Cross-border workers working in Switzerland and residing in Italy
  • Who: INPS Administration (Italy) and cantonal AHV offices (Switzerland)
  • Entitlement: Cumulation of contributions for the calculation of pension in both countries
  • AHV rate: 5.3% for employees (mandatory contribution)
  • Retirement age: 64 years old women, 65 years old men (in Switzerland)

For the Ticino frontier worker who works in Switzerland and resides in Italy, the pension is not a simple calculation: the system is double, with different rules between Swiss AHV and Italian INPS, but compensated by a totalization mechanism that protects the rights acquired in both countries.

The new tax agreement between Switzerland and Italy, signed on December 23, 2020 and entered into force on January 1, 2024, amended the rules on taxes and duties for those working across the border. But the right to total pension, recognized by the Double Taxation Convention of 9 March 1976, remains the basis on which the pension protection of the frontier worker rests.

How dual affiliation works

Those who work in Switzerland are compulsorily enrolled in the AHV/AI (the Swiss compulsory pension scheme), with contributions equal to 5.3% of the salary for employees. In parallel, if he resides in Italy, he is often also affiliated to the INPS (National Institute of Social Security), unless he is completely exempted by mutual agreement.

Totalization is the mechanism that recognizes contributions paid in Switzerland also for the calculation of the Italian pension, and vice versa. It does not sum the two monthly amounts, but calculates the pension amount in each country in proportion to the contributions paid there.

The new Agreement 2024 and the acquired rights

The transitional regime of Article 9 of the Agreement applies only to those who meet the conventional definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between 31 December 2018 and 17 July 2023; the covered employment income is taxed exclusively in Switzerland. In other cases, the ordinary regime applies, with Italian taxation and credit for Swiss tax. The 10,000 euros of Article 4 L. 83/2023 are only the limit for income referred to in Article 1, paragraph 175, L. 147/2013, not a general deductible. The pension, however, remains covered by the totalization principle, which guarantees the frontier worker not to lose years of contribution even if he has worked in two different countries.

Operational details

Contributions and rates in the Swiss system

In Switzerland, the employee pays 5.3% of the salary for AVS/AI/IPG (old age, disability and lost earnings insurance). Added to this are other contributions: the AD/AC (unemployment) at 1.1%, up to a limit of CHF 148,200, and the LAINF (accident insurance) between 0.7% and 1.5%. The second pillar (LPP) varies according to age: from 25 years and over, it ranges from 7% to 18% depending on the age group, with contributions from both the employee and the employer.

Furthermore, those who reside in Italy are subject to IRPEF (income tax) with progressive rates: 23% up to €28,000, 33% between €28,001 and €50,000, 43% over €50,000. The withholding tax on employee employment is withheld in Switzerland (to avoid double taxation), and the cross-border worker declares the income in Italy via form 730, with tax credit in the EC framework.

How the two systems integrate

The calculation of the pension for cross-border workers is divided into three phases:

  • AVS/INPS part: The number of years of contributions in Switzerland and Italy is counted separately, but the minimum time required in any of the two countries is valid to acquire the right to a pension.
  • Proportional amount: Each system calculates a pension based on the contributions actually paid in its years of affiliation. Then the total is divided between the two countries in proportion to the number of contributory years.
  • Protection for fragmented careers: If the cross-border worker has worked only 5 years in Switzerland and 25 in Italy, he does not lose the 5 Swiss years in the calculation of the Italian pension, and he does not lose the 25 Italian years in the calculation of the Swiss one.

Impact of the new agreement 2024

Since 1 January 2024, the tax regime has changed the withholding tax rates (see the exemptions for old and new cross-border commuters), but has left the right to pension aggregation intact. Cross-border workers who pay less tax in Switzerland can potentially have less deduction in the calculation of the Swiss pension (because the gross amount of the contribution is always based on the salary), but the INPS still recognizes periods of foreign work as insurance periods.

The 1976 Convention remains the pillar: nothing has changed in the aggregation mechanisms.

Key points

Timing of pension request

The request for the AVS pension in Switzerland must be submitted approximately 3-4 months before the expected retirement date (the normal age is 64 for women and 65 for men, unless voluntarily postponed). The form must be completed at the cantonal AVS office (in Ticino, at the taxpayers' office or via the online portal).

At the same time, the cross-border worker must submit an application to INPS for the Italian pension (or for the notional payment of Swiss contributions, via form A027 to be sent to the Italian Revenue Agency or directly to INPS). Processing times vary: the Swiss AVS usually pays the first installment within 2-3 months from the start date of entitlement; INPS in Italy has times that can reach up to 4-6 months.

Documents and operational steps

1. Collection of documents: Italian residence certificate, copy of the work booklet (or Swiss work certificate), certificate of AVS affiliation from the Swiss pay slips. 2. Submission of AVS request: Online or at the Ticino cantonal office. 3. Communication to INPS: Via form SR163 (pension application) and form A027 (totalization of contributions). 4. Verification of position: Request an insurance account statement both in Switzerland (at the AVS office) and in Italy (at the INPS online). 5. Monitoring of disbursement: Follow the status of the application online via the AVS and INPS portals.

Those who have the right to opt for G permit (like many cross-border commuters) must check that their health insurance affiliation (LAMal) in Switzerland does not create conflicts in the declaration of residence during the pension process.

Tools and support

The AVS provides online simulators to calculate the amount of the estimated pension. INPS offers an insurance position verification service via the Mio INPS portal. For cross-border workers with complex situations (long work breaks, changes of residence, periods of unemployment), it is advisable to consult a labor consultant specialized in Ticino border law, or contact the competent cantonal AVS office.

In the tax return form, the cross-border worker must correctly indicate the income from work and the taxes withheld, to avoid errors in the calculation of tax credits in Italy. A payslip calculator can help predict net amounts and withholdings, providing a solid basis for tax reporting.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the pension total for a border worker?
It is the mechanism that recognizes the contribution periods in Switzerland also in the calculation of the Italian pension and vice versa. It does not add the two amounts, but calculates a pension proportional to the contributions paid in each country. It guarantees that those who have worked in two systems do not lose the contribution years already accrued, protection recognized by the Double Taxation Convention of 1976.
At what age can I retire as a border worker?
The ordinary age in Switzerland is 65 years for men and 64 years for women (with the possibility of postponement up to 70 years to increase the amount). In Italy, the age depends on the number of years of contribution. Totalization allows you to count both systems to reach pension entitlement.
Has the new 2024 agreement changed pension rights?
No. The new agreement that applied since 1 January 2024 changed the rules on tax at source (with different deductibles for old and new border workers), but the right to totalisation of the pension remains unchanged. It is protected by the Double Taxation Convention of 1976 and continues to guarantee acquired rights.
Do I have to pay contributions to INPS if I work in Switzerland?
It depends on the residence and the bilateral agreement. If you reside in Italy and work in Switzerland, you generally pay contributions to the Swiss AVS (5.3% as an employee). INPS recognizes its equivalence through totalization. Special cases (e.g. double work) should be verified with a consultant.
How do I verify my AVS contributions?
You can request a statement of your insurance account (Kontoauszug) at the cantonal AVS office in Ticino. The service is free and you can access it online if you have an SPID or a Swiss authentication certificate. Check the number of years and contribution amounts to calculate the estimated amount of your future pension.
