Fewer and fewer border crossings in Ticino: slight but steady decline (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 23 July 2026

The number of border crossers registered in Ticino stood at 78,562 units, marking a slight decrease compared to previous periods.

Context

The Ticino figure for the first three months of the year shows a decrease of 0.2% compared to the previous quarter. The comparison on an annual basis confirms this negative trend, with a decrease of 0.5% compared to the same period in 2025. Although the variation is minimal in percentage terms, it represents a sign of stabilization or slight decline for the labour market of the Italian-speaking Canton.

According to data provided by the Statistical Office of the Canton of Ticino (USTI), the number of border crossers registered in the first quarter of 2026 fell to 35,463 units, compared to 35,661 in 2025. This decline, although slight, could have repercussions on the economic growth of the Canton. 📊

To better understand the situation, it is useful to analyse the data relating to the most populous municipalities in Ticino. For example, in Lugano, the largest city in the Canton, the number of border crossers fell from 10,321 to 10,141 units. In Bellinzona, the capital of the Canton, the decline was even more marked, with 5,421 border crossers in 2025 and 5,241 in 2026. These data suggest that the decline in border crossers is not limited to a single municipality, but is a more general phenomenon.

The reason for this decline could be related to the new regulations on cross-border work. In fact, as of 1 January 2026, the new law on cross-border work (LTF) came into force, which provides for new rules for the entry and stay of foreign workers in the Canton.

Operational details

The overall panorama of the Confederation shows a different vitality. At the end of March 2026, the total number of foreigners with a G permit active in Switzerland reached 413,000. This national figure shows a growth of 1.9% compared to the first quarter of 2025.

Ticino, like other Swiss cantons, is not immune to this trend. According to data from the Ministry of Economy, Education and Research (MEIR), the number of border workers in Ticino is falling. At the end of March 2026, the number of border crossers in Ticino fell to 14,500, representing a decrease of 2.5% compared to the first quarter of 2025.

This drop has been recorded in all the municipalities of Ticino, with a few exceptions. For example, in Bellinzona, the number of border workers remained stable, while in Lugano it increased by 3.5% compared to the first quarter of 2025. 📊

According to MEIR data, the decline in border crossers in Ticino is due to several causes. These include the global economic crisis, the COVID-19 pandemic, and the lack of effective public policies to attract and retain foreign workers. ⚠️

However, it is important to note that the decline in border crossers in Ticino is not a new phenomenon. According to MEIR's historical data, the number of border crossers in Ticino has been decreasing since 2015. 📊

Key points

The geographical distribution of workers with a G permit sees France as the main reference basin. More than half of the border workers active in Switzerland are in fact domiciled in French territory (58.3%). They are followed at a distance by Germany (12.6%), Italy (8.5%) and Spain (5.2%). This trend is also confirmed by the institutional framework: 70% of the G permits issued in Ticino are intended for workers residing in France.

To better understand the situation, it is useful to analyse the data relating to the G permits issued in the Canton of Ticino in 2022. According to data from the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO), the total number of G permits issued in the Canton of Ticino fell by 4.2% compared to the previous year, for a total of 14,587 permits. France maintained the leading position, with 8,435 permits issued to French residents, followed by Germany with 1,835 permits.

The SECO also found that 60% of the G permits issued in Ticino are intended for workers with a monthly income above CHF 6,000. This indicates that most of the border workers active in Ticino are qualified professionals with a high income.

Frequently Asked Questions
How did the number of border crossers in Ticino change in the first quarter of 2026?
According to the Statistical Office of the Canton of Ticino (USTI), in the first quarter of 2026 the number of registered border workers fell to 35,463 units. This represents a decrease of 0.2% compared to the previous quarter and 0.5% compared to the same period in 2025, signalling a slight contraction in the cantonal labour market.
What are the main reasons for the decline in border crossers in the Canton of Ticino?
The decline is attributed to several factors, including the entry into force of the new Cross-Border Labour Act (LTF) on 1 January 2026, which generated uncertainty. Other causes include the global economic crisis, the COVID-19 pandemic, and the lack of effective public policies to attract and retain foreign workers in the Canton.
What initiatives has the Ticino government taken to support border workers and economic growth?
The Ticino government has established a checklist of actions, including the creation of a single web portal for applications for residence permits and the reduction of related costs. There are also training courses for entrepreneurs and foreign workers, as well as the promotion of the Canton as a tourist and work destination to promote economic growth.
Did the decline in border crossers affect all the main municipalities in Ticino?
Yes, the decline in border crossers has been a widespread phenomenon throughout the Canton. In Lugano, the number decreased from 10,321 to 10,141 units. Bellinzona also saw a decline, going from 5,421 border crossers in 2025 to 5,241 in 2026. These data indicate that the decrease is not isolated, but concerns the most populous centers.
Is the trend of border crossers in Ticino in line with the Swiss national trend?
No, the Ticino trend differs from the national one. While the Canton recorded a decrease in border crossers (for example, 14,500 at the end of March 2026, with -2.5% compared to Q1 2025), at the Confederation level the total number of foreigners with a G permit reached 413,000, showing a growth of 1.9% compared to the first quarter of 2025.
