Swiss glaciers lose protective shield: cross-border worker impact (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 9 May 2026

Dry winter and Swiss glaciers at risk. Discover the implications for Ticino cross-border workers.

Context

In brief

  • Swiss glaciers with low winter snow cover
  • Average deficit of 25% compared to the 2010-2020 period
  • Similar situation to record years 2022 and 2023

Key facts

  • What: Low snow cover on Swiss glaciers
  • When: Winter 2025-2026
  • Where: Upper Valais, Ticino, Grisons
  • Who: Swiss glacier monitoring network (GLAMOS)
  • Amount: Average deficit of 25% compared to the 2010-2020 period

Swiss glaciers are at risk after a dry winter. The low winter snow cover makes them vulnerable to intense melting during the summer. This phenomenon is particularly evident for the glaciers of Upper Valais, Ticino, and Grisons. The warm and dry month of April contributed to this situation, as highlighted by the Swiss glacier monitoring network (GLAMOS) in a report published on Friday.

GLAMOS measured the thickness of the snow accumulated during the winter on 25 Swiss glaciers, comparing them with previous years. The average deficit of the winter 2025-2026 reaches 25% compared to the layers measured in the 2010-2020 period. 'The starting situation for the summer is therefore unfavorable,' glaciologist Matthias Huss told the Keystone-ATS agency.

Implications for cross-border workers

Snow acts as a protective layer, preserving the underlying ice from direct solar radiation. If this layer melts early, the glacier is exposed to melting earlier and for longer. This is compounded by the so-called albedo effect: dark ice absorbs more solar energy than light snow and therefore heats up more quickly. This further accelerates glacier melting.

For cross-border workers living in Ticino and working in Switzerland, this phenomenon can have significant implications. For example, it could influence tourist and recreational activities related to glaciers, such as summer skiing and high mountain excursions. Furthermore, it could affect the natural landscape that characterizes the region, an important factor for the quality of life and well-being of residents.

Regional differences

There are significant regional differences in snow cover. The lack of snow was more marked in southeastern Switzerland. On the Pers and Murtèl glaciers in Engadin, experts recorded new negative records for the winter mass balance. Even on the glaciers of southern and eastern Valais, such as Findel, Giétro, or the large Aletsch glacier, in central Switzerland, and in Ticino, the snow in April was significantly below average, with a reduction of between 15% and 40%.

Only in the more western Bernese Oberland and western Valais, for example on the Plaine Morte or Trient glaciers, did the amounts of snow approach average values. The start of summer for the glaciers is therefore 'practically identical' to that of the extreme years 2022 and 2023, according to Huss. Then, the combination of low winter snow and summer heatwaves led to record losses for the glaciers. In those two years, the glaciers in Switzerland lost 10% of their volume.

'This is a cause for concern,' Huss continued, adding however that ultimately it is the summer climate that determines melting, and it is not yet possible to determine whether the summer will again be scorching. 'However, in recent times almost all summers have been unusually hot. Therefore, there is a fear that the situation will repeat itself this year and that the glaciers will once again be under strong pressure.'
