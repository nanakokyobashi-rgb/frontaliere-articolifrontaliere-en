On Thursday 24 September 2026, the Swiss National Bank (SNB) decided to maintain the reference rate at 0%, the level at which it has stood since June 2025. The reason given is inflation in the Confederation which remains under control.

In the statement, the central bank added that, if necessary, it is willing to intervene in the foreign exchange market to ensure adequate monetary conditions. The warning does not announce an intervention: it does however signal that the exchange rate remains an observed variable. For a Ticino-Italy cross-border worker, the concrete link in the news therefore lies in the relationship between the franc and the currency used across the border, without the source quantifying a change. Unlike the US Federal Reserve (Fed) and the European Central Bank (ECB), which recently increased their reference rates, the Swiss central bank has chosen the monetary status quo.

The SNB notes a slight acceleration in inflation, due to the surge in oil prices. Despite this movement, he believes monetary policy is adequate to keep inflation within the price stability range and support economic activity. The goal is to keep inflation between 0% and 2%.

The new forecasts for Switzerland

On Thursday, during the quarterly analysis of the country's economic and monetary situation, the SNB significantly revised its gross domestic product (GDP) forecasts for the current year upwards. It now indicates a range between 1.5% and 2.0%, compared to around 1% estimated in mid-June. The growth of the Swiss economy should then amount to more or less 1.5% in 2027.

Inflation projections have been revised upwards by approximately ten basis points: 0.7% for the current year and 0.8% for the following year. The institute attributes the correction to the stronger-than-expected increase in prices of petroleum products. For 2028 it expects stabilization at this level.

The estimates, however, remain conditional on maintaining a reference rate of zero for the entire period considered. Finally, the central bank reports that the context of commercial policy and the trend of exchange rates remain factors of uncertainty.
