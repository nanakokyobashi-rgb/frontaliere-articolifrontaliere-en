If you are a border crane operator working in Ticino, it is important to understand the requirements and conditions for working in Switzerland. In this article, we will explore the details about G Leave and Tax at Source.

# G Leave Requirements

To work as a crane operator in Ticino, you must have the G Permit, which is a working document for border workers. The G Permit is issued by the Immigration Office of the Canton of Ticino and requires the presentation of certain documents, including:

  • An employment contract with a Ticino employer
  • A professional qualification certificate issued by an accredited institution
  • A statement that you have no criminal record

Here is a concrete example: if you are a border crane operator with work experience in Italy, you could present an employment contract with a Ticino employer and a professional qualification certification issued by an accredited institution in Switzerland.

Tax at source

Tax at source is a tax that is withheld by the employer from the wages of frontier workers. In Switzerland, withholding tax is only withheld for frontier workers and not for Swiss workers. The tax rate is 5% on gross salary.

Operational details

Tax at source is only withheld in Switzerland for frontier workers, according to the EC Framework of 730. This means that Ticino border workers must declare their working income in Switzerland to the Tax Administration of the Canton of Ticino, following the current regulations on cross-border work.

As for the requirements to work in Switzerland as a border worker, you must have a B (C) residence card attesting to the possibility of working in Switzerland. In addition, it is mandatory to be enrolled in the insurance for border workers and to pay the social insurance contribution.

The employment income of Ticino border workers is subject to the Tax at source, which is withheld by the employer in Switzerland. The tax rate at source is 20% for frontier workers earning up to CHF 120,000 per year. Key points

The cross-border worker who wishes to work as a crane operator in Ticino must have the G Permit, which is a work document that allows cross-border workers to carry out work activities in Switzerland. To obtain this permit, the cross-border worker must meet certain requirements.

G Permit Requirements

To work as a crane operator in Ticino, the cross-border worker must be at least 18 years of age and be in possession of a professional qualification certificate issued by an authorized institution. Furthermore, the cross-border worker must be able to demonstrate his work experience in the field of maintenance and repair of machines and systems.

Salary and working conditions

The cross-border crane operator in Ticino can expect an average salary of around 6,500 Swiss francs per month, which can vary depending on experience and professional qualification. Furthermore, the cross-border worker must be able to cover their living costs in Switzerland, which can be higher than those in Italy.

Concrete examples

For example, a cross-border worker with 5 years of work experience as a crane operator in a maintenance company in Italy can expect a salary of around 7,000 Swiss francs per month in Ticino. However, if the cross-border worker has no work experience in the sector, his salary could be lower, around 5,500 Swiss francs per month.

Regulations and dates

The G Permit is regulated by the Federal Law on Migration (LAMIF) of 16 December 2005 and by the Labor Law (LSt) of 13 March 1943. Furthermore, the cross-border worker must be in possession of an employment contract that satisfies the conditions established by the Federal Law on the Protection of Workers (LPP) of 20 March 1981.
