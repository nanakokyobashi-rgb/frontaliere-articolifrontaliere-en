Physiotherapist in Ticino: Diploma Recognition, Requirements, and Procedures (cross-border guide)

Di Valerie Linc · Published on 11 July 2026

The requirements to work as a physiotherapist in Ticino, for border workers, are described below.

Context

In short, working as a physiotherapist in Ticino can be obtained through the recognition of the Italian physiotherapy diploma by the competent Cantonal Authority. To proceed with the professional qualification in Switzerland, it is necessary to follow the procedure managed by the competent Cantonal Authority.

In Ticino, in 2021, the average rates for a physiotherapy session are around 60-70 francs, while for a complete treatment, which can also include home care, it can reach 250-300 francs. Regarding the requirements, a bachelor's degree in physiotherapy or a bachelor's degree in motor science, with a specialization in physiotherapy, is required. In addition, it is necessary to have a recognized international certification, such as the Specialization Diploma in Physiotherapy (D.S.F.) or the Specialization Diploma in Joint Manipulation and Osteopathy (D.S.M.A.O.).

With regard to the procedures, it is necessary to submit an application to the competent Cantonal Authority for the qualification to the profession of physiotherapist. The application must be accompanied by all the necessary documents, including the degree, specialization, international certification and regional medical practice. Subsequently, the competent Cantonal Authority will evaluate the application and, if the training objectives have been achieved, will issue the certificate of qualification.

Professional 📝 qualification:

The process of professional qualification in Switzerland is managed by the competent Cantonal Authority, in the case of Ticino, by the Cantonal Health and Training Authority. Border workers, as physiotherapists, must apply for professional qualification to the Canton of Ticino, following the procedures established by the Cantonal Authority.

General 🗓️ requirements:

In order to work legally in Switzerland, applicants must possess one of the following credentials:

  • Degree in physiotherapy awarded by an Italian university recognised by the European Commission (CRE) or by a professional school accredited by ANVUR.
  • Bachelor's degree in physiotherapy awarded by a Swiss university recognised by the European Commission (CRE) or by a vocational school accredited by the Ministry of Education, Culture and Research.

📊 Rules and amounts:

According to Swiss legislation, border workers can only practice as physiotherapists at health facilities accredited by the Cantonality of Ticino. The average annual salary for physiotherapists in Switzerland is about CHF 80,000.

Key points

To work as a physiotherapist in Ticino, border workers must apply for professional qualification in the Canton of Ticino. Here is a detailed overview of diploma recognition, requirements and procedures:

1. Recognition of the diploma: Bachelor's degrees in Physiotherapy recognized in other regions of Italy can be accepted in Ticino. However, the Ticino Ministry of Health requires that the curriculum vitae and cover letter be submitted in Italian. In addition, the Ticino athlete must take a state exam, which is a written and oral exam that assesses skills in terms of theoretical and practical knowledge.

2. Requirements: Requirements to work as a physiotherapist in Ticino include:

  • be a regular resident of Switzerland
  • have a degree in Physiotherapy recognised in Switzerland or Italy
  • be registered with the Order of Physiotherapists of Ticino (OFTI)
  • be in compliance with the training and updating requirements
  • have a good knowledge of German and Italian (the Ticino athlete speaks Italian)

3. Procedure: The procedure to obtain professional qualification in Ticino is as follows:

  • submit the application with the required documents (diploma, curriculum vitae, cover letter, declaration of having no criminal record)
  • take the State examination
  • wait for the approval of the curriculum vitae and cover letter
  • pay the licence fee
