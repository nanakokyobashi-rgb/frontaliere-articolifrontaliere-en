AVS-INPS totalisation for border workers: how it works (cross-border guide)

Di Laura Bianchi · Published on 27 August 2026

Cumulate the contribution periods between Switzerland and Italy for retirement. 1976 Convention, New Agreement 2024: practical guide, documentation and calculation of annuity.

Context

In brief

  • Totalization allows for the aggregation of contribution periods between Swiss AVS and Italian INPS for pension calculation.
  • It applies to cross-border commuters with a work history in both countries, according to the Convention of December 9, 1976.
  • The New Cross-Border Commuter Agreement (effective January 1, 2024) modernizes the coordination regulatory framework.

Key Facts

  • What: Totalization of contribution periods between AVS (Switzerland) and INPS (Italy)
  • When: Convention signed December 9, 1976; New Cross-Border Commuter Agreement entered into force January 1, 2024
  • Where: Canton Ticino (Switzerland) and border regions (Italy)
  • Who: Cross-border commuters who have worked in both countries; INPS, FSIO (Federal Social Insurance Office)
  • Rates: AVS 5.3% for employees; Italian IRPEF 23%–43% depending on income
  • Legislation: Convention of December 9, 1976; Italian Law no. 83/2023 (ratification of New Agreement)

The totalization of social security contributions between Switzerland and Italy represents one of the most important mechanisms for cross-border commuters who accumulate contribution history in both systems. This institution allows those who have worked for both Swiss and Italian employers to aggregate contribution periods, preventing periods from 'getting lost' when transitioning from one system to another.

For Ticino cross-border commuters, this topic is particularly relevant. Many started their careers in Italy, subsequently moved to Switzerland, or oscillate between the two systems during their working lives. Totalization ensures that contribution periods paid in one country are recognized when calculating pension entitlement and its overall amount.

Operational details

How the totalization mechanism works

Totalization is not an automatic operation, but a procedure that requires awareness and action from the worker. Here's how it concretely works.

When a cross-border commuter ceases working and reaches retirement age, they have the right to request recognition of the contributory periods accumulated in both countries. The competent authorities — the FSIO (Federal Social Insurance Office) for Switzerland and INPS for Italy — coordinate through a bilateral administrative procedure. The cross-border commuter who has worked in both Switzerland and Italy must submit a request specifying all contribution periods in both systems. This documentation is crucial: it serves to demonstrate that the worker has effectively paid contributions or has been covered by the respective mandatory insurance schemes.

Once the periods are accumulated, the pension calculation follows a 'pro-rata' formula: each system calculates its pension amount based on the contributions paid in its own territory, but recognizes the totalized periods to determine if the person is entitled to a pension and according to what amount.

Practical scenario: Ticino cross-border commuter with a mixed history

Consider the generic case of someone with 8 years of INPS contributions in Italy (accumulated before transferring to Switzerland) and subsequently 17 years of OASI contributions in Switzerland. Without totalization, the cross-border commuter might not reach the minimum contribution required by one of the two systems, thus losing pension rights. With totalization, the total 25 years are recognized and combined.

Key points

How to apply for aggregation: procedure and documentation

The application process is a delicate stage requiring coordination between the two social security systems. There is no fixed deadline to start the application, but it is advisable to act in advance to avoid delays.

Required documentation

The cross-border worker must collect a series of documents that demonstrate the continuity and completeness of their contribution history:

  • INPS contribution statements (available from the INPS online portal)
  • OASI-DI-IC (AVS-AI-IPG) contribution statements from Switzerland (available from the registers of the insured at cantonal offices or directly from the FSIO)
  • Employment certificates for any missing periods or documentary gaps
  • Identification documents (passport, identity card)
  • Certificate of residence

These documents are the foundation of the application: they allow authorities to verify that the worker has indeed paid contributions in both countries.

Where to submit the application

In Switzerland, cross-border workers residing in Canton Ticino can contact the cantonal OASI/DI office (Social Solidarity and Social Security Division of the Ticino DFE) or the FSIO in Bern directly. In Italy, the request for recognition of aggregation must be submitted to INPS, preferably via the online portal or at the competent local office of the place of residence.

The procedure is not linear: the time required for recognition depends on the number and type of periods accumulated, any career interruptions, and the type of insurance (compulsory, voluntary, provisional).

Frequently Asked Questions
Does the totalization increase the total amount of my pension?
The totalisation does not increase the amount of the benefit, but guarantees full recognition. Without aggregation, the frontier worker may not reach the minimum contribution requirements required by either system, thus losing the right to a pension in that country. With totalization, periods from both systems are cumulated to determine access and annuity amount. Do I have to submit the application for totalization during the work activity?
No, it is not mandatory to submit the request during work. Generally, the border worker submits the application for totalisation when he/she is preparing to cease activity and access the pension. However, it is advisable to contact the competent offices (FOPS in Switzerland, INPS in Italy) at least 12–18 months before the expected retirement age, in order to promptly collect all the necessary documentation and verify the completeness of the declared contribution periods.
If I worked in Italy first and then in Switzerland, do the two periods add up?
Yes, the contribution periods in Italy (INPS) and Switzerland (AVS) are added together for the purposes of determining access to the pension and its amount. The contributions themselves (which remain distinct in the two systems) are not added, but rather the payment periods — the years and months of contribution. This accumulation allows you to reach the minimum contribution required by each system and calculate its total income without losses.
What documents do I need to collect to request totalization?
You need: extracts of INPS payments (from the INPS portal), AVS-AI-IPG extracts from Switzerland (from cantonal registers or directly from the FOPH), work certificates for any missing periods, passport or identity card, and certificate of residence. It is essential to collect these documents on time, because INPS and the FOPH use them to verify the correctness and continuity of the declared contribution periods.
Does the New Frontier Agreement (2024) change the way totalization is calculated?
The New Frontier Agreement, which entered into force on 1 January 2024 (ratified in Italy by Law no. 83/2023), modernises the overall regulatory framework and introduces additional provisions on tax and social security aspects.
