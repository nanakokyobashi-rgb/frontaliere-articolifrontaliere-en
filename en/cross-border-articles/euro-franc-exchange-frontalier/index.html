Practical Analysis: Implications for Cross-Border Workers

The exchange rate between the euro and the Swiss franc directly impacts the salary of cross-border workers. With a strong Swiss franc, the value of the salary in euros can decrease, reducing purchasing power. To mitigate this effect, various strategies can be adopted, such as diversifying investments and using financial instruments that protect against exchange rate risk.

Before/After Comparisons

Before the introduction of online currency exchange platforms, cross-border workers were forced to use traditional banks to convert their salaries. This involved high commissions and unfavorable exchange rates. Today, with the advent of platforms like Wise and Revolut, it is possible to obtain more competitive exchange rates and reduced commissions. Furthermore, opening a multi-currency account allows for more efficient management of finances, avoiding currency exchange commissions.

Concrete Scenarios

Imagine being a cross-border worker who receives a salary of 5,000 CHF per month. With an exchange rate of 1 CHF = 0.95 EUR, your salary in euros would be 4,750 EUR. If the exchange rate changes to 1 CHF = 0.90 EUR, your salary in euros would drop to 4,500 EUR. This represents a 5.26% reduction in your purchasing power. To avoid this situation, financial instruments that protect against exchange rate risk, such as multi-currency accounts and online currency exchange platforms, can be used.
