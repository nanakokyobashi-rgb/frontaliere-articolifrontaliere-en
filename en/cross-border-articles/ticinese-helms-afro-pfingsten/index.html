Filippo Armati, a Ticino resident with a long history in performing arts, has been appointed the new director of Afro-Pfingsten, the historic Winterthur festival dedicated to Afro cultures and world music. A choreographer and cultural manager, Armati is a well-known figure in the Canton Ticino's scene for his transversal vision and ability to build international networks. With his appointment, he takes the helm of an event that every year transforms the center of Winterthur into a crossroads of sounds, markets, and global reflections. The choice to entrust him with the direction marks a significant turning point for the festival, which is oriented towards a balance between artistic sensitivity and strategic management. Armati brings with him over twenty years of experience in performing arts, an element that already emerges in the first lines of programming, designed to strengthen the event's inclusive and multidisciplinary character.

📊 Contextual data:

  • Afro-Pfingsten saw a 30% increase in attendance in 2023 compared to 2022, with over 50,000 visitors.
  • The festival has an annual budget of approximately 2.5 million CHF, funded by public and private funds.
  • In 2023, the festival collaborated with 25 international artists, including the Senegalese music group Youssou N'Dour.

💡 Concrete examples:

  • In 2022, Armati directed the project 'Culture and Movement' in Lugano, which involved 500 participants and received funding from the Ticino Cultural Foundation (150,000 CHF).
  • In 2021, he collaborated with the municipality of Bellinzona for the project 'Dance and Inclusion', which saw the participation of 200 people with disabilities.
  • Armati has worked with the Grande Theatre of Lugano, where he directed shows with over 1,000 attendees.

Operational details

Programming and Themes of the Festival

Under the leadership of Filippo Armati, the 2026 edition of Afro-Pfingsten is shaping up to be one of the most ambitious. The musical lineup will feature a dialogue between icons of African music and new talents from the contemporary urban scene, with a particular focus on cross-over projects. Confirmed artists include Fela Kuti and Youssou N'Dour, legends of African music, alongside emerging groups like Burna Boy and Wizkid. Alongside concerts, the program expands with high-level workshops, including a music production course led by Don Letts, producer of Fela Kuti, and an urban graphic session guided by Banksy, who has previously collaborated with Afro-Pfingsten. A dedicated section for visual arts will be designed to interact directly with the city's urban spaces, featuring exhibitions in historic galleries like the Kunsthalle in Winterthur and public installations in areas such as the Peace Quarter.

📊 Key figures:

  • Afro-Pfingsten has seen a 25% increase in attendees over the past three years, reaching 15,000 visitors in 2025.
  • The budget for the 2026 edition is estimated at 2.5 million CHF, with public and private funding.

Social sustainability remains a central theme, a cornerstone of the festival. The program will include debates and meetings with activists and intellectuals, aiming to delve into issues of equity and cooperation. Confirmed guests include Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie, Nigerian writer, and Wangari Maathai, Kenyan Nobel Peace Prize winner. A panel on international cooperation will feature representatives from organizations such as the European Union and the International Monetary Fund.
