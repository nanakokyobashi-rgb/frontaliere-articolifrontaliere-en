Why Pusiano for the Ticino border crossing

Pusiano is a small town in the province of Como (Lombardy), located a short distance from the Ticino crossings Chiasso and Brogeda. For those who work in Ticino, living in Pusiano means benefiting from a lower cost of living than in Switzerland, while maintaining proximity to the workplace. The border worker who chooses this geographical location has access to consolidated Italian services and reasonable commuting times to the Canton of Ticino.

Operational details

Social security and coverage: AVS, LPP, KVG

AVS/AI contributions and unemployment insurance

The cross-border worker who works in Ticino pays the AVS/AI/IPG contributions to the competent Swiss body, not to the Italian INPS. The years of contributions in Switzerland count towards the AVS pension, not towards the Italian pension. It is a crucial aspect: those who accumulate a career in Switzerland benefit from the Swiss calculation rules, which are generally more favorable than the Italian ones for those with mixed contributions.

AVS/AI/IPG employee contribution: 5.3% of the gross salary Unemployment insurance contribution (AD) and continuous allowance (AC): 1.1% (with a cap of CHF 148'200 of taxable income) Accident insurance (LAINF): 0.7–1.5% (generally paid by the employer)

These contributions are automatically deducted from the pay slip. The cross-border worker receives a bank statement documenting the contributions paid. It is essential to keep these documents for the duration of employment and beyond.

Second pillar: BVG (Pension Fund)

The LPP is the second Swiss pillar and is mandatory for employees with an annual gross salary exceeding CHF 21'570 (figure 2024, subject to annual variation). The employer and employee pay variable contributions based on age:

  • Ages 25–34: ~7% of salary
  • Ages 35–44: ~10%
  • Ages 45–54: ~15%
  • Ages 55–64: ~18% (maximum range)

The LPP accumulates paid-in capital as an annuity upon reaching the Swiss retirement age (65 for men, 64 for women). If the cross-border worker changes jobs, the funds are transferred to a new fund or an intermediate solution.

Right of option and KVG (health insurance)

Cross-border commuters have the right to opt for health insurance (KVG). It is not mandatory to stay in Italy: it is a choice.

If you choose Swiss KVG: sign up at a Ticino fund (Visana, CSS, Assura, Helsana). Premiums vary by age, gender, canton of residence and choice of deductible (CHF 300–2,500 for adults; higher deductibles = lower premiums). Advantages: more generous coverage than in Italy, simplified access to Ticino hospitals, quick administrative procedures.

If you remain insured in Italy: maintain the Italian INPS/CMI coverage. Advantages: continuity with the Italian system. Cons: Less generous coverage, possible complications if you need urgent care in Switzerland. Check first whether the employer accepts this choice.

Practical advice: check with the employer which option is accepted before signing the contract.

Third pillar (3a) and pension planning

The cross-border commuter can open a private pension account (pillar 3a) with a Swiss bank, paying up to CHF 7,056 per year (2024), deductible from Swiss taxable income. It is a complementary pension savings tool, useful if your salary is sufficient and you want to accumulate additional capital for retirement.

The Ticino banks (Banca Cantonale del Ticino, UBS, Raiffeisen) offer specific pillar 3a solutions for cross-border commuters. It is advisable to compare the offers of at least 2-3 institutions before subscribing.

Practical procedure: step-by-step for the transfer

Step 1: Find job offer and verify G Permit

Before moving to Pusiano, it is essential to have a written job offer from a Swiss employer in the Canton of Ticino. The G Permit cannot be obtained independently: the employer submits the application to the canton (SEM office - Secretariat of State for Migration) following a binding offer.

In the application, the employer specifies:

  • Cross-border worker data (name, date of birth, nationality)
  • Professional qualification and RAL (gross annual salary)
  • Duration of the contract (at least 1 year, preferably indefinite)
  • Description of the position

The G permit is valid for five years with a contract lasting more than one year; with a shorter contract it lasts as long as the contract. Once received, the cross-border worker takes it to the municipality of Pusiano (registry office) to register as a Swiss resident in Italy. The procedure is free.

Step 2: Opening a Swiss bank account and managing the currency

Once hired, the employer will indicate an affiliated bank for the salary. If the cross-border commuter wants to choose the institution, the main Ticino options are Banca Cantonale del Ticino, UBS, and Raiffeisen. The account must allow salary crediting in CHF and withdrawal in EUR with minimal exchange commission. Many cross-border commuters open two accounts: one in CHF at the Swiss bank (for CH payments) and one in EUR at an Italian bank or an online provider for fast transfers to Italy.

Average annual exchange rate: essential for the Italian tax return. CHF income must be converted to EUR using the official average rate of the Revenue Agency.

Step 3: Registration with Swiss insurance funds (AVS, LPP, LAINF)

All cross-border workers are automatically registered with:

  • AVS/AI/IPG: NISS number (Individual Swiss Social Security Number)
  • LPP: employer's pension fund (or external fund if SMEs)
  • LAINF: accident insurance (generally paid by the employer)

There is no need to fill out forms: the employer communicates the data to the central cash register. The cross-border worker will receive notification of registration. Keep AVS account statements, LPP affiliation document and LAINF certificate: they are essential for any changes of employer or future checks.

Step 4: Choice of KVG and subscription within 30 days

Within 30 days of starting work (or transferring), the cross-border worker must communicate the choice of health coverage.

If you choose Swiss KVG: 1. Request quotes from 2-3 Ticino offices 2. Compare age, deductible, model (GP vs HMO) 3. Subscribe online or at a branch; coverage generally starts the following month 4. Notify the employer of the new coverage

If you stay in Italy: 1. Contact the Italian INPS/CMI and communicate that you work in Switzerland 2. Request a certificate of continuity of contributions 3. Check with the employer if this option is accepted 4. Keep documentation for future requests

Step 5: Italian tax return (Form 730/Unique)

By May 31st of the year following the first year of work in Switzerland, complete the Italian declaration. Required forms:

  • Income from employment: Swiss gross income converted to EUR at the average annual rate
  • EC framework: "Credits for taxes paid abroad". Indicates Switzerland, type of income (employment), tax paid in CHF (to be converted to EUR)
  • The Agency applies the credit automatically

If necessary, fill out the Single Form (for those with diversified incomes). Use the Revenue Agency website (www.agenziaentrate.gov.it) to download updated forms and instructions.

Step 6: Monitoring of the tax and contribution situation

Every year you will receive:

  • AVS/AI account statements and contribution certificate
  • CUD form or IRPEF certification from the Swiss employer (for Italian declaration)
  • Communication from the KVG fund (if chosen)
  • Notifications from the Italian Revenue Agency

Keep all documents for at least 5 years. If you change employer, immediately communicate the new details to the new employer to avoid interruptions in contributions.

Frequently Asked Questions
If I live in Pusiano and work in Ticino, in which country do I pay taxes?
Employee income tax at source is paid directly in Switzerland by the employer according to the Ticino federal and cantonal rates. Italy applies a tax credit in declaration 730 (EC framework) to avoid double taxation. You generally do not pay additional taxes in Italy if the average Swiss rate is higher than the Italian rate, as is the case for most border crossers in the income bracket up to EUR 50,000.
Which income does the €10,000 limit apply to?
Article 9’s transitional regime applies only to workers who meet the Agreement’s definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between 31 December 2018 and 17 July 2023; covered employment income is taxable exclusively in Switzerland. The ordinary regime applies in other cases, with Italian taxation and credit for Swiss tax where applicable. The €10,000 in Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is only the limit for income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law 147/2013, not a general allowance. FTA factsheet · Agreement, Article 9 · Normattiva, Article 4.
Do I have to register with INPS or AVS if I work in Ticino as a border worker?
Swiss AVS, not INPS. If you work in Ticino as an employee, the AVS/AI/IPG contributions (5.3% + unemployment insurance 1.1%) are paid by the employer in Switzerland. INPS does not intervene on the Swiss share. However, if you have years of work in Italy, INPS can recognize them at the time of retirement (contributory reunification). Keep your AVS statements: they are essential for the recognition of the Swiss annuity.
Can I stay insured at the Italian health fund or do I have to choose the LAMal?
You have the right of option: you can stay in Italy or subscribe to the Swiss LAMal. Many border workers choose the LAMal for more generous coverage, simplified access to Ticino hospitals and no problem with reimbursements. Check before signing the contract if the employer accepts Italian coverage (some require LAMal). If you stay in Italy, make sure that INPS coverage is recognized in Switzerland for emergencies.
What is the LPP and do I have to pay contributions as a border worker?
The LPP (pension fund) is the second pillar of Swiss pensions and is mandatory if your gross annual salary exceeds CHF 21,570. You and your employer pay age proportional contributions (7-18%). It accumulates capital paid as annuity upon reaching the Swiss retirement age (65 years for men, 64 for women). It is complementary to the AVS and guarantees adequate social security protection for your future retirement.
