This episode is just one of the many cases of work in extreme temperatures that are emerging in Ticino.

According to Swiss legislation, the maximum temperature allowed inside a workplace is 28 degrees. This limit is established by article 45 of the federal decree of 26 June 2015 (DF 2015-507) relating to the safety and health of workers. Frequently Asked Questions
What is the maximum temperature allowed at work in Switzerland?
Swiss legislation establishes a maximum temperature of 28 degrees in a working environment, as indicated by article 45 of the federal decree of 26 June 2015 (DF 2015-507). Employers are required to ensure an environment with a temperature between 18 and 25 degrees, unless there is a specific rule for particular activities.
Are there exceptions to the temperature limit at work in Switzerland for some sectors?
Yes, in specific sectors such as the food industry, the temperature can exceed 28 degrees. However, it is mandatory that mitigation measures are taken to protect workers from adverse effects. The article mentions a case in Bellinzona where 32 degrees was considered acceptable thanks to such measures.
What are the obligations of Swiss employers regarding thermal safety?
According to the Federal Labour Act (LFL) of 1943, section 13, employers must ensure a safe and healthy working environment, with temperatures between 18 and 25 degrees. They must take measures such as installing cooling systems, creating rest areas, and training employees to manage extreme heat conditions.
Does heat affect accidents and deaths at work in Ticino?
Yes, the statistical data indicates a significant impact. In 2020, 60% of the victims of accidents at work in the Canton of Ticino were due to heat-related causes. In 2019, 30% of work-related deaths in Ticino were heat-related, highlighting a real problem and the need for interventions.
What can workers or trade unions do in the event of excessive temperatures in the workplace?
In the event of extreme temperatures, workers or trade unions can report the situation to the competent authorities, as happened in Laveno (37 degrees) and Lugano (40 degrees). It is essential that companies take mitigating measures and that unions are involved to ensure compliance with regulations and employee safety.
