Key points

How to read this data

Wait times come from real-time queue monitoring, sampled every 15 minutes during peak hours. The ranking uses a weighted average over the last 7 days — hours with more readings count more, so a single isolated spike doesn't skew the picture. Crossings with too few readings are excluded for the same reason. The "Δ 7d" column compares this week's average with the previous week's (same 7-day window, shifted back): a positive value (▲) means the crossing got slower, a negative one (▼) means it improved. For live traffic at any single crossing, see the border-wait hub.

What to actually do with this data

A weekly ranking is only useful if you act on it before you leave, not after. Three practical ways to use it: pick the fastest crossing in your area when your schedule is flexible; if your usual crossing got a lot worse this week (see above), try an alternative in the same area for a few days; and if you can choose your departure time, remember this average is built from peak hours — a small time shift often matters more than which crossing you pick.
