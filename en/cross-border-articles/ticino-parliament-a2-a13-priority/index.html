Ticino Parliament: A2-A13 to become a USTRA priority again

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 6 October 2026

Ticino Parliament votes on a resolution not to postpone the A2-A13 connection beyond 2050. Locarnese is the only major region without direct motorway access.

On Tuesday, the Ticino Parliament supported, almost unanimously, a resolution addressed to the Federal Council. The request is precise: the A2-A13 motorway link must not be postponed further and must return to USTRA's priority projects, USTRA being the Federal Roads Office.

In a hypothetical scenario, a cross-border commuter whose destination is in the Locarnese would be faced with a planning decision concerning direct access to the motorway. The resolution, however, does not describe a new opening: it records the political request concerning the future of the link. calcolatore fiscale remains outside this account: the issue here is infrastructure priority, not the individual's situation.

The vote met with no opposition. There were no votes against, while the seven abstentions came from the Greens and MPS. It was recalled in the chamber that the Locarnese remains the only major Swiss region without direct motorway access. This is the territorial factor that supported the request not to postpone the project again.

The position of the Ticino Government

The concern expressed in the chamber stems from the Confederation's plan to postpone construction of the link beyond 2050, perhaps not before 2055. Speaking on behalf of the Government, State Councillor Raffaele De Rosa said: «This is a postponement that cannot be proposed».

In recent days, the Ticino Government had stated its position on the Confederation's Trasporti '45 programme. The assessment given was that it was a step in the right direction, but insufficient to meet the Canton’s needs. The same statement expressed concern about the postponement of the Bellinzona-Locarno link.

The resolution thus brings the issue to the federal level. The Ticino Parliament is asking the Federal Council not to postpone A2-A13 any further and to return it to USTRA's priorities. The source links this request to the Locarnese's lack of direct access and the Government's opposition to a postponement beyond the stated horizon.

Operational details

The issue for those traveling through the Locarnese

For cross-border commuters, the news conveys a practical distinction: the debate concerns the planning of a connection, not an immediate change to the route. For anyone who, in a hypothetical scenario, has the Locarnese as their destination, the discernible point is the uncertainty of the timeframe, not a new timetable, a new construction site or an alternative road indicated by the source.

The useful comparison is between the objective expressed by Parliament and the scenario challenged in the chamber. On the one hand, A2-A13 should avoid further delays and return to USTRA's priority projects. On the other, construction has been placed in a timeframe beyond 2050, perhaps no earlier than 2055. The two perspectives do not produce the same timeframe for a region that Parliament defines as Switzerland's only large region without direct access to the motorway.

Politics is not yet a timetable

The resolution is not equivalent to the construction of the project. It is a request addressed to the Federal Council and, at the same time, a political signal about the project's place among USTRA's priorities. For this reason, no opening date can be inferred from the vote: the available text does not indicate one. Nor is the Trasporti '45 program presented by the Ticino Government as a sufficient response to the Canton’s needs; the assessment is more nuanced, because the program is considered a step in the right direction but insufficient.

The reference to Bellinzona-Locarno adds the cantonal dimension to the discussion. The Ticino Government had expressed concern precisely about the postponement of that connection, while Parliament focused its resolution on A2-A13. For the cross-border commuter reader, this means distinguishing between the project’s name, the venue of the request and its possible future construction.

For this reason, a reliable update must remain anchored to the Ticino Parliament, the Federal Council, USTRA and the Ticino Government, the actors cited in the source.
