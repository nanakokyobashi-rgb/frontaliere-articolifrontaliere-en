Seizure of protected parrots at the Gandria crossing (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 18 September 2026

A Swiss citizen was stopped at the Gandria crossing with two ash parrots without Cites authorization. Intervention by UDSC and USAV.

Context

In brief

  • Swiss citizen stopped at the Gandria border crossing
  • Transporting two African grey parrots in the car
  • The animals belong to a protected species
  • Cites import authorization was missing

Key facts

  • Date: Thursday, September 10
  • Location: Gandria border crossing
  • Authorities: FOCBS and FSVO officers
  • Species: African grey parrots (Appendix I)
  • Vehicle: Car with Swiss license plates

Officers of the Federal Office for Customs and Border Security (FOCBS) recently intercepted and seized two birds during a routine check at a border crossing. The incident took place on Thursday, September 10, when a Swiss citizen was stopped at the Gandria border crossing while traveling in a car with Swiss license plates. Inside the vehicle, customs officers discovered the presence of two African grey parrots. The driver was in no way in possession of the authorization required by law to import protected specimens. The timely intervention of the border authorities made it possible to block the illegal transport of these particular exotic animals, which fall fully into categories protected at the international level.

The intervention of customs and veterinary authorities

Immediately after the initial check carried out by FOCBS officers, the birds were immediately impounded. The operation took place on the specific order of the Federal Food Safety and Veterinary Office (FSVO). Given the total absence of the documentation required for customs clearance, the two animals were seized with an absolute ban on the owner being able to dispose of them in any way. The competent authorities continue to monitor compliance with customs regulations regarding the transit of protected wildlife species, reminding everyone that every cross-border movement of animals covered by protection agreements must be accompanied by the relevant official permits issued by the designated bodies before travel.

Operational details

The owner's statements and the origins of the birds

During the checks carried out at the Gandria border crossing, the owner of the two African grey parrots provided his version of the facts to the intervening customs officers. The man stated that he had purchased the birds directly from a private breeder. According to what was reported by the Swiss motorist himself, the seller did not provide him with any Cites certificate at the time of the transfer of the animals. This lack of primary documentation immediately triggered in-depth checks by the FOCBS, as current legislation does not allow exceptions based on the buyer's good faith or the failure to deliver documents by the private seller.

The species' protection level

The owner's situation worsened further due to the particular zoological classification of the transported birds. The African grey parrots in question actually belong to a species listed in Appendix I of protection of the Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species (Cites). This is the highest level of protection provided by the international convention, reserved exclusively for species that are concretely at risk of extinction and whose international trade or transit is strictly regulated and restricted to exceptional and rigorously authorized cases. The total absence of the Cites attestation associated with such a high level of protection explains the firmness with which the FOCBS and the FSVO proceeded with the immediate seizure of the parrots at the border crossing.

Key points

Procedures for the transit of protected animals

Anyone who needs to transport animals or specimens of protected species across borders must comply with a series of very specific formal procedures established by international conventions and adopted by the federal authorities. Before planning any movement involving customs clearance, it is essential to check the status of the species by consulting the official Cites lists. For animals falling under Annex I, such as the African grey parrots involved in the check at Gandria, it is mandatory to obtain in advance the import and export permits issued by the competent offices. Purchasing from private breeders in no way exempts the buyer from the burden of demanding and verifying the presence of all original documentation before traveling in their own vehicle.

Tools for customs verification and in-depth analysis

To avoid administrative or criminal penalties and the consequent seizure of the animals, it is necessary to regularly consult the directives published by the Federal Food Safety and Veterinary Office (OSAV) and the FOCBS regarding tourist and commercial traffic of goods and protected species. Travelers and commuters who regularly cross customs checkpoints must pay the utmost attention to regulations on the protection of fauna and flora.
