Since the outbreak of conflict in Iran, Ticino has become an increasingly sought-after destination for millionaires fleeing the Persian Gulf. According to Simon Incir, owner of real estate agency Engel & Völkers Ticino, requests to purchase properties south of the Alps have increased by 15% compared to previous months. 'While no transactions have been finalized yet, initial viewings have already been scheduled,' Incir told the SonntagsBlick. The demand is not limited to Swiss buyers: among the purchasers are primarily Europeans living in the United Arab Emirates, Qatar, and Iran, often holding British, French, Italian, or Swiss nationality.

A thriving real estate market between Ascona and Lugano

This phenomenon is no coincidence. Ticino offers a wide availability of luxury properties, particularly between Ascona and Lugano. According to Engel & Völkers Ticino’s database, 261 properties are currently for sale, including villas and apartments with swimming pools, lake views, or vistas over Monte San Salvatore. 'A second home in Switzerland represents an opportunity to assess whether to move permanently,' explains Incir. This mechanism allows foreign buyers to test the quality of life in Ticino without immediate commitments.

Tax benefits and exclusive services

In addition to flexible housing options, the canton offers lump-sum tax benefits for foreign residents who earn income abroad. This system enables them to pay a fixed tax rate, often more advantageous than traditional tax systems. An element that makes Ticino particularly attractive for those seeking economic stability in an unstable geopolitical context.

Families interested in a permanent move can also rely on high-quality international schools and proximity to Milan-Malpensa Airport, less than an hour away. The combination of these factors is transforming Ticino into a kind of 'safe haven' for those looking to protect their assets and provide their children with a quality education.

Before vs after: what changes for cross-border workers

Before the war in Iran broke out, demand for luxury properties in Ticino was already high but limited to a specific audience: Europeans with tax residency abroad. Since the conflict, the pool of buyers has expanded to include professionals and entrepreneurs seeking alternatives in their home countries. For Ticino’s cross-border workers, this phenomenon could have indirect implications:

  • Increased demand for services: real estate agencies, law firms, and tax consultants are seeing a rise in inquiries.
  • Property revaluation: pressure on certain market segments could drive prices up, making purchases less accessible to local residents.
  • Greater competition: sellers may prioritize offers from foreign buyers, leaving less room for Ticino-based purchasers.

The role of real estate agencies

Ticino’s real estate agencies are already riding the wave. Engel & Völkers Ticino, for example, has launched targeted advertising campaigns aimed at millionaires fleeing the Gulf. 'We are receiving requests from clients who want to evaluate the possibility of purchasing a property in Ticino by the end of the year,' explains Incir. The goal is to attract both those seeking a second home and those looking to relocate permanently.

What happens if the transaction isn’t completed?

Not every visit will lead to a purchase. However, the mere fact of having scheduled a visit demonstrates the growing interest in Ticino. For those who already own property, this trend could represent an opportunity to consider additional investments or to rent out the property to an affluent clientele.

Operational details

Why does Ticino attract so many millionaires fleeing the Gulf?

The phenomenon is not only linked to the war in Iran. Ticino offers a unique combination of advantages that makes it a point of reference for those seeking stability and security. Housing flexibility is one of the main drivers: buying a property in Ticino allows you to settle down without having to immediately obtain a Swiss residence permit. This is possible thanks to the presence of holiday homes that can be purchased without particular restrictions.

The mechanism of flat-rate tax breaks

The flat-rate tax regime for foreign residents is one of the main reasons for attraction. According to Ticino legislation, those who transfer their tax residence to Ticino and receive income abroad can opt for preferential taxation. This means paying a fixed tax, often lower than traditional systems, without having to declare every single source of income. A non-negligible advantage for those who want to protect their assets.

Proximity to Milan: a plus for families

For families with children, Ticino offers another strategic advantage: its proximity to the metropolitan area of Milan. Malpensa airport, less than an hour away, guarantees direct international connections, making it easy to travel for business or pleasure. In addition, the international schools in Ticino, such as the International School of Lugano, represent an excellent choice for the education of children.

Possible scenarios for cross-border commuters

For cross-border commuters who work in Ticino but reside in Italy, the increase in demand for real estate could have mixed effects:

  • Rising prices: If the demand for luxury real estate grows, prices could rise, making buying it less accessible to local residents. - Increased competition: sellers could favour foreign buyers, offering more favourable conditions, leaving less room for cross-border commuters. - Investment opportunities: those who already own a property may consider renting it out to wealthy customers, obtaining attractive returns.

The procedures for buying a property in Ticino

Buying a property in Ticino as a foreigner is relatively simple, but it requires some attention:

1. Check availability: Not all properties can be purchased without restrictions. Holiday homes are accessible without a residence permit, but cantonal requirements must be met for permanent residence.

2. Contact with a real estate agency: Agencies such as Engel & Völkers Ticino offer dedicated services to foreign buyers, including tax and legal advice.

3. Tax assessment: it is essential to check your tax situation and assess whether the flat-rate regime is convenient. In some cases, it may be necessary to consult an accountant who specializes in international tax law.

4. Viewing and negotiation: Once the property has been identified, the viewing and price negotiation are carried out. At this stage, the presence of a real estate agent with experience in the Ticino market is essential.

5. Conclusion of the purchase: the transaction requires the signing of a notarial contract and the payment of registration fees. The notary will take care of all the paperwork.

What happens if you decide to move permanently?

Those who decide to move to Ticino must face some administrative procedures:

  • Registration with the municipality of residence: it is necessary to register with the municipality of reference and obtain a residence permit. - Change of tax residence: You must report the transfer to the tax authorities of your home country and in Switzerland. - Opening a bank account: to manage finances locally, it is advisable to open an account in a Ticino bank.

Comparison with other Swiss cantons

Ticino is not the only canton to attract foreign millionaires. Vaud and Geneva also offer tax breaks and a high-level real estate market. However, Ticino stands out for: - The proximity to Lombardy: Milan can be reached in less than an hour, a significant advantage for those who travel often for work. - The availability of luxury properties: between Ascona and Lugano, the market offers villas with lake view, swimming pool and exclusive services. - The flat-rate tax regime: in Ticino, the benefits are particularly advantageous for those who receive income abroad.

Risks to consider

Despite the benefits, there are some risks to be assessed:

  • Rising prices: Growing demand could push up the costs of luxury real estate. - Competition with local buyers: Cross-border commuters may find it more difficult to purchase a property due to foreign competition. - Change of residence: moving to Ticino requires careful planning, especially with regard to taxation and administrative aspects.

The war in Iran has accelerated a trend that was already underway." Experts point out that Ticino is becoming a sort of "safe haven" for those seeking economic stability and quality of life.

For cross-border commuters, this could mean both new opportunities and challenges, especially in terms of property accessibility.

The implications for the cost of living

The increase in demand for luxury real estate could also have repercussions on the cost of living. Rental prices could rise, especially in the most sought-after areas such as Lugano and Ascona. For cross-border commuters, this means having to carefully consider their budget.

Everything suggests that the phenomenon is destined to consolidate. The war in Iran has accelerated a trend that was already underway, making Ticino an increasingly popular destination. For those seeking stability and security, the canton represents a strategic choice.

Step by step: how to buy a property in Ticino for foreigners

If you are among the millionaires fleeing the Gulf interested in a residence in Ticino, here is the detailed procedure to buy a property. Follow these steps to avoid mistakes and optimize time.

1. Identify your goal

Decide if you want to buy a property as a second home (without the need for a residence permit) or if you intend to move permanently to Ticino. This will influence the choice of property and subsequent procedures. - Second home: you can buy properties such as villas or apartments without any particular restrictions, as long as it is not your main residence. - Permanent residence: You will need to comply with cantonal requirements, including proof of sufficient income and registration with your municipality of residence.

2. Contact a specialist real estate agency

Agencies such as Engel & Völkers Ticino offer dedicated services to foreign buyers. An agent with experience in the Ticino market will guide you in finding the property that best suits your needs. You can consult their online database, which currently lists 261 properties between Ascona and Lugano.

3. Evaluate the tax convenience

Ticino offers flat-rate tax breaks for foreign residents. To find out if this option is advantageous for you, consult an accountant who specializes in international tax law. Some key points to consider: - The flat-rate regime allows you to pay a fixed tax, which is often lower than traditional systems. - You must prove that you receive income abroad. - Taxation varies depending on the municipality of residence.

4. Schedule a property viewing

Once you have identified some properties of interest, it is time to visit them. Engel & Völkers Ticino has already scheduled the first visits for interested customers. During the visit, you will be able to evaluate: - The quality of the construction and furnishings. - The location (proximity to schools, services, transport). - The potential revaluation of the property.

5. Negotiation and purchase proposal

If you are convinced by a property, you can submit an offer. At this stage, it is essential to have a real estate agent by your side who knows the Ticino market well. Negotiation can be competitive, especially for luxury properties.

⚠️ Attention: Real estate prices in Ticino can be high, especially in the most sought-after areas such as Lugano and Ascona. Carefully evaluate your budget.

6. Conclusion of the purchase and notary procedures

Once your offer has been accepted, you will have to proceed with the conclusion of the purchase. Notary practices in Ticino are streamlined, but they require a few steps: - Signing of the purchase contract at a Ticino notary. - Payment of registration fees (variable depending on the municipality). - Registration of the property with the cantonal land registry.

7. Transfer of residence (if applicable)

If you have decided to move permanently to Ticino, you must: - Register with the municipality of residence within 14 days of arrival. - Obtain a residence permit (B or C, depending on the length of stay). - Update your tax residency with the relevant authorities.

📌 Documents needed: Passport, employment contract (if applicable), certificate of residence, tax documentation.

8. Opening a bank account

To manage your finances in Ticino, it is advisable to open an account with a local bank. The main Ticino banks, such as UBS and Credit Suisse, offer services dedicated to foreigners.

9. Rental or use of the property

If you have purchased a second home, you can choose to: - Rent it: the luxury rental market in Ticino is growing, with attractive yields. - Use it personally: enjoy the property during your stays in Switzerland.

10. Monitoring and maintenance

Once you have purchased the property, it is important to keep it in top condition. You can rely on Ticino real estate management agencies for ordinary and extraordinary maintenance.
