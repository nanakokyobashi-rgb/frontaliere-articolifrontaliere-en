Health tax chaos for cross-border workers in Luino and Verbania (cross-border guide)

Di Laura Bianchi · Published on 13 May 2026

The Pd denounces disparities between cross-border workers in Luino and Verbania, with potential union appeals and constitutional doubts.

Context

In brief

  • Health tax for cross-border workers creates disparity between Luino and Verbania
  • Pd accuses the center-right of normative confusion
  • Possible union appeals for constitutional doubts

Key facts

  • What: Disparity in health tax for cross-border workers between Luino and Verbania
  • When: May 2026
  • Where: Luino and Verbania
  • Who: Democratic Party, Mayor Enrico Bianchi, Senator Alessandro Alfieri
  • Amount: Not yet specified

In the same factory, a worker from Luino might pay the health tax, while one from Verbania might not. This is the image chosen by regional councilor Samuele Astuti to describe the chaos that, according to the Democratic Party, is arising around the so-called health tax for cross-border workers. In front of Palazzo Verbania in Luino, the Pd gathered Senator Alessandro Alfieri, Astuti himself, and Mayor Enrico Bianchi for a press conference dedicated to cross-border workers, between health tax and returns to border municipalities.

Astuti spoke of a measure 'born overnight' within the budget law, without consultation with territories, unions, and workers. 'When the new fiscal agreement on cross-border workers was created,' he recalled, 'there were assemblies, discussions, and a great deal of shared work. Here, instead, we found ourselves faced with a norm imposed from above.' For the regional councilor, the problem does not concern only cross-border workers but the entire balance of border areas: 'This tax risks weakening already fragile territories.'

Normative confusion

Today, according to Astuti, a paradoxical situation has been reached: 'Piedmont has decided not to apply it, while the Lombardy Region continues to push for its introduction as soon as possible.' Hence the example of the worker from Luino and the one from Verbania, employed in the same company but subject to different rules. 'It's a snapshot of the confusion they are creating,' said Astuti, also highlighting the constitutional doubts about the norm and the possible appeals announced by the unions.

Senator Alessandro Alfieri, on the other hand, focused on the issue of returns to cross-border municipalities, accusing the government of wanting to withdraw resources from the territories. 'We had built a precise balance: no more euros asked from the old cross-border workers and no fewer euros for the municipalities,' he explained. According to Alfieri, the government is instead modifying that structure: 'First, they tried to make these resources end up in general taxation, then they decided to move them towards the Regions. But this money belongs to the border municipalities.'

Impact on border municipalities

The senator also criticized the expansion of the beneficiaries, which risks reducing the quotas destined for historically cross-border territories: 'Municipalities like Luino will receive fewer resources, and that is a serious mistake.' Words shared by Mayor Enrico Bianchi, who defended the role of local administrations in managing resources. 'There is a lack of trust in municipalities that I find censurable. We have shown that we know how to manage public funds well,' he said, recalling the path taken by the Luino administration, which went from a situation of deficit to a free surplus of about two and a half million euros.

Bianchi also indirectly replied to the words of regional councilor Massimo Sertori, who in recent weeks, precisely from Luino, had defended the health contribution, arguing that it would serve to increase the salaries of doctors and nurses in border areas. 'It was necessary to clarify some things,' explained the mayor, 'because that narrative was proposed without contradiction. This tax is born in a messy way and when things are born badly, they produce results that do not work.' For Bianchi, the risk is to offload onto border territories problems that should be addressed in other ways.

Operational details

Practical Implications for Cross-Border Workers

The health tax for cross-border workers represents a significant change that could have a direct impact on workers who daily cross the border between Italy and Switzerland. In particular, the disparity between Luino and Verbania raises important questions about equal treatment and regulatory coherence.

Comparison Between Luino and Verbania

Workers from Luino and Verbania operating in the same company might find themselves in different situations. While cross-border workers from Luino might be subject to the health tax, those from Verbania might not be. This disparity not only creates confusion but could also lead to economic inequalities among workers.

Constitutional Doubts

The constitutional doubts raised by the Democratic Party and the unions could lead to legal challenges. If the regulation is declared unconstitutional, a review of the health tax might be necessary to ensure fair treatment for all cross-border workers.

Impact on Border Towns

The reduction of returns to border towns represents another critical aspect of the new regulation. Towns like Luino, which have historically benefited from these funds, might see a decrease in available resources. This could have a significant impact on local administrations and the services offered to residents.

What Cross-Border Workers Should Do

Cross-border workers residing in Luino or Verbania should closely monitor regulatory developments. It is advisable to consult a tax advisor to better understand the implications of the health tax and prepare for any changes. Additionally, workers might consider joining unions or associations that represent their interests to advocate for fairer regulations.
