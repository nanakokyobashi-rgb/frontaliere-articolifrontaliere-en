The new speed bump installed at the Italian-Swiss border crossing at Gaggiolo is a safety measure. Following reports of delays and traffic disruption recorded in recent days, the UDSC explained the reasons for the decision. The agency specified that the work concerns speed reduction and the reorganization of the customs area.

The reason for the measure

The measure was adopted after several critical situations had been identified in the customs area. Heavy vehicles, drivers and operational staff move simultaneously near the traffic lanes. Their presence together exposed pedestrians, including workers and drivers involved in customs operations, to risks.

The speed bump was installed and preceded by warning signs. The objective indicated by the UDSC is to reduce the risk of accidents and protect people working or travelling through the area, with particular attention to nighttime hours. The statement therefore links the measure to the daily presence of workers, drivers, operators and pedestrians in the immediate vicinity of the roadway.

The position of Swiss Customs

The UDSC maintains that the speed bump is appropriate to the characteristics of the road and does not constitute an unjustified obstacle to the flow of vehicles. The reference indicated by the office is the rule already in force at that location: by law, vehicles must in any case proceed at walking pace. For this reason, the structure’s main function is safety and speed reduction, without any substantial change to transit conditions.

The measure is not isolated. The parking spaces intended for heavy vehicles have been redesigned to make vehicle management more orderly and safer. The lighting has also been replaced to ensure better visibility, especially at night. The UDSC presents the package as an overall reorganization of operational spaces and services, with the priority of protecting operators and road users.

The UDSC’s clarification brings together two aspects of the measure: reports concerning traffic and the need to protect those working or travelling through the customs area. The official statement identifies ensuring the greatest possible safety in work activities and movement for all the people who use the border crossing every day as its objective.

Operational details

For those crossing Gaggiolo, the UDSC's communication calls for distinguishing between two aspects: the perception of a slowdown and the traffic rule already in force. Reports from recent days concern traffic disruptions; the authority nevertheless maintains that the new speed bump does not add significant slowdowns compared with the conditions already prescribed. The practical interpretation is therefore that this is a point where speed must already be very low and where slowing down serves to make the coexistence of people and vehicles safer.

An operational area, not just a lane

The description by Swiss Customs shifts attention from the carriageway alone to the entire border area. Those passing through do not merely encounter a feature on the road: heavy vehicles, drivers, customs personnel and pedestrians operate in the same space. For staff and drivers involved in customs operations, the risk identified by the source arises precisely from the need to move near the lanes.

For those working at the crossing, safety also depends on coexistence between commercial flows, customs operations and pedestrian movements. That is why the UDSC presents the speed bump, parking spaces and lighting as parts of the same intervention.

Four steps at the border crossing

1. Read the signage in advance. The speed hump is presignaled by warning signs. The signage prepares traffic before the point concerned, without reaching the structure as if it were unexpected.

2. Proceed at walking pace. At the indicated point, the law requires vehicles to proceed at walking pace. This is the most concrete instruction for drivers: the speed hump does not replace the rule, but serves to moderate speed and ensure safety.

3. Consider the entire area. There are not only traffic lanes. The source points to the simultaneous presence of heavy vehicles, drivers, customs operators and pedestrians, with people who may move near the carriageway during customs operations. Anyone crossing must therefore pay attention to these users being present together.

4. Take nighttime conditions into account. The lighting was replaced to ensure better visibility, especially at night. During that period, the work was designed to protect those working or passing through the area; caution remains necessary because people and vehicles share the same space.

The organization of heavy vehicles
