Border health costs: reimbursements between Switzerland and Italy (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 27 August 2026

Health rights for border workers Ticino-Italy: LAMal and INPS in comparison, reimbursement procedures and how to choose the most convenient insurance.

Context

In a nutshell

  • LAMal: Swiss health insurance with option right for border workers G
  • 2020 Agreement: CH-IT health coordination from 1 January 2024
  • LAMal Deductible: CHF 300-2500 per year for adults
  • Italian INPS: manages refunds for those residing in Italy

Key facts

  • What: Right of option for border workers with Permit G on the insurance choice
  • When: Agreement in force from 1 January 2024 (signed 23 December 2020)
  • Where: Canton of Ticino and neighbouring Italian provinces (CH-IT coordination)
  • Who: Frontier workers with G Permit; INPS, BAG/UFSP, LAMal insurers
  • Coverage: LAMal: deductible CHF 300-2500 adults; copayment 10%
  • Regulations: Double taxation agreement 9 December 1976; Frontier Agreement 2020

The Ticino-Italy border health system operates according to a principle of coordination between Switzerland and Italy, enshrined in the New Frontier Agreement that entered into force on 1 January 2024. Those who work in Switzerland with a G Permit and reside in Italy have the right of option: they can choose to cover themselves with LAMal (Swiss health insurance) or maintain the coverage of the Italian INPS system.

This choice has direct implications on the reimbursement of healthcare expenses. LAMal is the main insurer in Switzerland, with deductibles ranging from CHF 300 to CHF 2500 for adults, plus a 10% co-payment up to a maximum. The expenses covered depend on the contract chosen and the services provided in the territory

Operational details

For the border worker, the choice between LAMal and INPS is not trivial. LAMal offers direct access to Swiss doctors and hospitals, generally quick advice and short waiting lists. However, out-of-pocket costs (deductible and copayments) can be significant: CHF 300-2500 deductible per year, plus 10% copayment up to a maximum. For those with high healthcare needs, the calculation of total costs requires attention.

On the contrary, those who choose to remain in the Italian INPS system benefit from a wide coverage of basic medicine, with hospitalizations and surgeries normally borne by the public service. However, Italian public border hospitals often have longer waiting lists. Accessing Swiss services as an INPS insured requires a coordination procedure and is not always immediate. Coordination between Switzerland and Italy

The Double Taxation Convention (signed 9 December 1976) and the 2020 Frontier Agreement provide that the country of primary insurance is responsible for reimbursements. If you are LAMal insured and undergo a hospital visit in Como (Italy), your claim should normally be forwarded to your LAMal insurer, who will assess whether to recognise the benefit according to Swiss criteria.

Key points

The choice between LAMal and INPS must be made at the time of issuing Permit G. It is not a decision you can postpone: the responsible insurer is designated from the beginning, and changing it requires formal procedures with the competent authority.

First step: ask for information from the competent office. If you are a new cross-border worker with Permit G, contact:

  • SEM (State Secretariat for Migration) Switzerland: provides information on insurance schemes and LAMal options
  • Visa office of your Italian province: clarifies whether INPS coordination is already established in your case
  • Tax Agency / local INPS: if you choose the Italian system, register and ask for a complete overview of your rights

Second step: choose the insurer (if LAMal). If you opt for LAMal, contact directly an accredited Swiss insurance company operating in Ticino. Compare contracts, deductibles, co-payments. Explicitly ask which services are covered in Italy (not all insurers cover them in the same way). Also verify if the insurer has simplified reimbursement agreements with hospitals or border Italian doctors.

Third step: keep complete documentation. For each medical visit, hospitalization or prescribed medication, ask for a detailed receipt, written diagnosis (if available) and signed medical prescription. These documents are essential for requesting future reimbursements. Keep them for at least 5-7 years, also in digital format (photos/scans).

Fourth step: request reimbursements promptly. Do not wait months: send your reimbursement request within the timeframe established by your insurance (usually 30-90 days from the date of care). Attach all original documents or certified copies. If you receive an initial denial, ask in writing for the reason and consider filing an appeal.

Frequently Asked Questions
Who has the right of option between LAMal and INPS?
Border workers with G Permit from 1 January 2024 (date of entry into force of the 2020 Border Agreement). This right allows you to choose between Swiss LAMal insurance and the Italian INPS system. The choice must be made when issuing the permit. Borderers with Permit B have different rules and do not automatically have the right of option.
What is the difference between LAMal and INPS for a border crossing?
LAMal is the Swiss private insurer with a CHF 300 to CHF 2500 deductible for adults and a 10% co-payment, offering fast access to Swiss doctors and hospitals. INPS is the Italian public system with benefits covered by the State, but longer waiting lists and coordination required for care in Switzerland. The choice depends on personal health needs and overall costs.
How do I request reimbursement if I paid for a medical examination in Switzerland?
Keep complete receipt, diagnosis and medical prescription. If LAMal insured, send the documents to your insurer within 30-90 days of treatment. If INPS insured, contact the local INPS office to check if the Swiss service is refundable according to Italian criteria (may require pre-authorization). Response times are 2-4 weeks for LAMal, 4-8 weeks for INPS.
Can I change insurance during the year if I am a border worker with a G Permit?
No, the choice of insurance is designated upon issuance of the permit and is normally binding for 12 months. Changes require a formal procedure at the insurance institution (LAMal or SEM/INPS), justified by documented exceptional circumstances. It is advisable to evaluate the choice well before signing with the help of a consultant.
Which Convention regulates double taxation between Switzerland and Italy?
It is the Convention signed on 9 December 1976 between Italy and Switzerland. It establishes tax coordination between the two countries and, together with the 2020 Frontier Agreement (in force since 1 January 2024), regulates the coordination of health and social security rights for border workers. Switzerland is not an EU/EEA member.
