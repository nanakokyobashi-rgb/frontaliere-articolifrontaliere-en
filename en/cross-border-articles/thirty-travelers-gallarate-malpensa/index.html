Thirty travelers in Gallarate after the train to Malpensa

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 27 September 2026

About thirty travelers were stranded at Gallarate station on the evening of 22 September, waiting for train 2989 at 23.24, which was replaced by buses due to nighttime works.

TL;DR

  • About thirty people were waiting for train 2989 in Gallarate
  • The 11.24pm service was replaced by a bus
  • Screens and announcements instead indicated the train to Malpensa
  • The error arose from a systems update on September 21st

In the late evening of Tuesday 22 September, around thirty travelers remained at Gallarate station after waiting for the last direct connection to Malpensa. Train 2989, indicated as leaving at 11.24pm, was in fact present on the screens and in the announcements. The route, however, had been scheduled for days to be replaced by buses due to night-time work on the line.

The reconstruction arises from the story of a passenger who arrived around 11pm on a train from Milan. On platform 2 he found workers heading to the airport, young people heading to Saronno and numerous foreign tourists. Some had already been waiting for a long time, because even the previous trips had not left from Gallarate.

What happened on platform 2

The work program had been communicated. The official notices from RFI and Trenord indicated that, on weekday nights from 14 September to 30 October, the scheduled maintenance between Saronno and Castellanza on the Ferrovienord network would have resulted in the cancellation, between Gallarate and Saronno, of several trips on the Milan Centrale-Malpensa-Gallarate connection. Affected trains included 2985, 2987 and 2989, with replacement by buses.

The difficulty therefore did not concern the decision to change the service, which had already been prepared, but the way in which that change was shown to passengers. A train arriving from Malpensa was stopped on the platform, with the lights on and the doors closed. The staff had let the passengers off and had not allowed those waiting in Gallarate to get on. According to the story, he had been told to wait for the next train, even though the 11.24pm train was the last one of the evening.

At the scheduled time, the announcements continued to indicate departure. The convoy then moved without passengers, while those who had to reach Malpensa or Saronno remained on the platform.

The night without assistance

The station was now closed and there was no employee to ask for directions. A traveler contacted Trenord customer service, but the operator also said 2989 was initially in circulation. After midnight, some present organized themselves. A group made up of the author of the report, a Malpensa worker and some tourists called a car with driver, arriving at the airport around 2am.

Operational details

The case compares the scheduled service and what the traveler sees on site. Before 22 September, the change was communicated via notices and hourly engine: 2989 would not travel between Gallarate and Saronno, and the route would be covered by buses up to Saronno. On the night of the 22nd, however, the systems update had removed that change. The result was an operational contradiction: the commercial service was organized differently, while the station continued to offer the train.

For those who have to continue their journey in the evening or reach the airport, the difference is not just formal. The presence of the convoy, the lights on and the announcements had created a coherent indication only in appearance. Even the hourly engine, consulted by customer service at least initially, returned 2989 as an active run. In these conditions, the passenger was not faced with simply incomplete information, but with conflicting indications.

The infrastructure node

The explanation provided by Trenord recalls the structure of the line. Gallarate is on the RFI network. The connection towards Malpensa and Saronno instead involves the Ferrovienord network up to Milano Bovisa, then the route returns to the RFI tracks up to Milano Centrale. The works indicated in the notices concerned the Saronno-Castellanza section of the Ferrovienord network.

According to the company, the September 21 update was the responsibility of infrastructure managers and had made the change disappear. The reconstruction was carried out with RFI and FerrovieNord, indicated as competent for the Malpensa-Bovisa route. The passage explains why a suppression scheduled days ago remained correct in the original alerts, but not in the systems used that evening.

To read the case in a practical way, it is best to separate three moments: the decision on the works, the publication of the replacement with buses and the updating of the information at the station. The first and second had been arranged; the third deleted the decisive data. For those moving between the affected stations, the risk arises precisely when these three sources do not coincide. Key points

How to orient yourself during the construction period

Anyone who must leave Gallarate on weeknights between 14 September and 30 October can follow the sequence indicated by the official notices:

1. Check if the trip concerns one of the routes 2985, 2987 or 2989. The change concerned the Gallarate-Saronno route, with replacement buses. 2. For 2989, check the scheduled departure at 11.24pm from Gallarate and the destination of the bus to Saronno. Trenord also reported an additional extraordinary bus ride, departing at the same time, directed to Malpensa. 3. Compare the RFI or Trenord notice with what appears on the timetable engine and on the displayboards. On September 22nd these channels did not show the same information: therefore the check serves to identify any misalignments. 4. If the train still appears but the doors remain closed, ask the staff for directions when available and contact Trenord customer service. On the evening reported, customer service also initially saw the 2989 in circulation. 5. If the station is closed and there are no staff, the source documents that some travellers, after midnight, made their own arrangements. A group called a car with a driver and reached Malpensa around 2. The source describes this as an autonomous organization of passengers and, separately, reports the extraordinary bus ride organized by Trenord.

What changes after verification

Trenord announced that, once the anomaly was identified, the infrastructure manager modified the station announcements. The company then indicated that the problem is not expected to happen again. The same note specified that the extraordinary bus ride had been arranged, even if it was not reported on site that evening.

The data to keep, for those who have to organize a trip, is the distinction between the bus to Saronno and the additional direct trip to Malpensa: these are two indications reported in the Trenord reconstruction and must be read together with the starting point, Gallarate, and the time of 11.24pm.
