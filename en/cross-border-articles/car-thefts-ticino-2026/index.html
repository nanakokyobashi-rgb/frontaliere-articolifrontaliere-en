Car thefts in Ticino: Land Rover, Alfa Romeo, and Porsche most targeted (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 5 May 2026

Car thefts are increasing in Switzerland, with Ticino above the national average. Here's what you need to know and how to protect yourself.

Context

In brief

  • Car thefts on the rise in Ticino
  • Land Rover, Alfa Romeo, and Porsche most targeted
  • AXA paid out 5 million francs in 2025

Key facts

  • What: Increase in car thefts
  • When: 2025
  • Where: Ticino, Geneva, Vaud, Basel City
  • Who: AXA
  • Amount: 5 million francs

Car thefts in Switzerland are increasing, and Ticino is no exception. The insurance company AXA recorded a higher number of claims in 2025 compared to previous years, with a peak in the cantons of Geneva, Vaud, and Basel City. Ticino also stands above the Swiss average. According to AXA, car thefts have nearly doubled compared to the pre-pandemic period, with a total of approximately 400 cases per year. Luxury cars are the most targeted, with an upward trend.

Michael Villiger, head of motor vehicle claims at AXA, observed that 'on Swiss roads, vehicles are becoming increasingly modern and better equipped. Thieves seem to be targeting these models more often, and apparently, they are increasingly successful in stealing high-value vehicles.'

Most affected models

The most stolen cars are Land Rover SUVs, with a theft probability over four times higher than the average. They are followed by Alfa Romeo and Porsche. Also targeted are Audi, Jeep, Fiat, BMW, and Mercedes-Benz. Less exposed are Tesla, Suzuki, Subaru, Seat, Skoda, Mazda, Ford, Opel, Hyundai, Nissan, and Mitsubishi.

Regional differences

Regional differences may be linked to proximity to the border. In these regions, organized gangs seem to operate more frequently, transporting vehicles abroad. Cars registered in Appenzell Innerrhoden also show a higher frequency of claims, but in this case, they are rental vehicles that tend to circulate throughout Switzerland.

Operational details

Practical Analysis

The increase in car thefts in Ticino and other Swiss regions has a significant impact on cross-border workers who commute to Switzerland and reside in Italy. These workers not only face the risk of losing their vehicle but also complications related to insurance and reporting procedures.

Comparison with the Previous Situation

Before the pandemic, car thefts were less frequent, and the number of incidents was lower. Today, with the increase in luxury vehicles and the presence of organized gangs, the situation has changed. Cross-border workers must be particularly attentive and adopt preventive measures to avoid becoming victims of theft.

Concrete Scenarios

1. Scenario 1: A cross-border worker parks their car in an unmonitored area and it gets stolen. Without an alarm system or a GPS tracking device, the chances of recovering the vehicle are reduced. 2. Scenario 2: A cross-border worker uses an advanced alarm system and a GPS tracking device. In case of theft, the authorities can quickly locate the vehicle and increase the chances of recovery. 3. Scenario 3: A cross-border worker reports any suspicious activity to the authorities. This can help prevent theft and identify the organized gangs operating in the area.

Implications for Cross-Border Workers

Cross-border workers who commute to Switzerland and reside in Italy must be aware of the risk of theft and take necessary precautions. Parking the car in secure areas, using advanced security devices, and constantly monitoring their vehicle can make a difference.
