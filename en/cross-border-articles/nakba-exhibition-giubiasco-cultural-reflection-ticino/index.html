Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 5 September 2026

From January 25 to February 19, 2026, Galleria Job hosts a collective exhibition on the Israeli-Palestinian reality. A significant cultural event for cross-border workers.

Operational details

The curatorial approach, led by Marino Cattaneo, is explicit in its intent: to provide "materials to listen, reflect, try to understand, rediscover a political conscience, imagine a different world." The exhibition doesn't limit itself to presenting photographs of a walled Jerusalem but aims to construct a more "tortuous" and complex reading path that escapes media oversimplification. The intellectual core of the exhibition is well represented by a quote from the Israeli historian Ilan Pappé, from his essay "The End of Israel":

"The centuries-old attempt by the West, led by the United Kingdom, to impose a Jewish state on an Arab country seems to have come to an end. [...] Their only hope for the future will be a willingness to live as citizens with equal rights in a liberated and de-colonized Palestine."

This clear stance defines the critical perimeter of the event, positioning it as a cultural and political act. For visitors, it's an opportunity to delve into a perspective often marginalized in public debate.

Practical Information

  • Where: Galleria Job, Via Borghetto 8, 6512 Giubiasco
  • When: From Sunday, January 25 to Thursday, February 19, 2026
  • Hours: Wednesday and Thursday from 14:00 to 18:00, or by appointment by calling +41 79 621 37 38.

Key points

For a cross-border worker, life in Ticino goes beyond the home-to-work commute. Events like the "Collettiva Nakba" represent a precious opportunity to integrate into the social and cultural fabric of the canton, transforming the time spent across the border into a richer and more complete experience. Dedicating a few hours to such an exhibition, easily reachable in Giubiasco, means allowing for a space of intellectual decompression, a way to process the world's complexity outside of daily work pressures. It's an invitation to see Ticino not only as a place of economic opportunity but also as a center for debate and critical thinking.
