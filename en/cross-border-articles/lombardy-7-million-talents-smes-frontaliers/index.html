Lombardy allocates 7 million euros for PhD researchers in SMEs: an opportunity for Ticino cross-border workers (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 14 April 2026

The Lombardy Region is investing 7 million euros in the 'Talenti – Knowledge Transfer' call to hire PhD researchers in SMEs. Grants of up to 56,000 euros per year for 24 months are available for permanent employment contracts.

Context

TL;DR

  • Lombardy allocates 7 million euros to hire PhDs in SMEs.
  • Grant covers 70% of costs for permanent hires, 60% for fixed-term.
  • Measure aims to bridge labs and factories, boost innovation.
  • Cross-border workers from Ticino may find new career prospects.

Key facts

  • Amount allocated: 7 million euros
  • Target: PhD holders in Lombardy SMEs
  • Permanent hire coverage: 70% of costs, max 56,000 euros/year for 24 months
  • Fixed-term hire coverage: 60% of costs, max 48,000 euros for 12 months
  • Eligibility: SMEs in Lombardy with recent research collaboration
  • Application method: Online via 'Bandi e Servizi' platform
  • Cross-border implications: Work in Italy, taxed in Italy, social security contributions follow Italian regulations
  • Sectors benefiting: Advanced technologies, biotech, energy, ICT

The Lombardy Regional Government has approved the ‘Talenti – Trasferimento delle conoscenze’ call for proposals, a measure allocating 7 million euros to encourage the hiring of PhD holders in small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) with operational headquarters in Lombardy. The declared aim is to create a stable bridge between laboratories and factories, accelerating the transfer of prototypes and innovative technologies to the market.

The measure, proposed by the Economic Development Counsellor Guido Guidesi and Research Counsellor Alessandro Fermi, seeks to retain talent within Lombardy and enhance its competitiveness. Fermi emphasised the need to provide qualified professional outlets beyond the academic sphere: ‘This is a strategic investment in human capital.’

Lombard SMEs and eligibility requirements

The call for proposals is open exclusively to SMEs based in Lombardy that intend to hire individuals with a PhD and have completed a collaboration with a research body within the past year. The grant cannot be combined with other regional incentives for the same position.

Support: how the grant works

The regional support consists of a non-repayable contribution calculated on the gross annual cost of the hired personnel:

  • Permanent employment: coverage of 70% of costs, with a maximum of 56,000 euros per year for up to 24 months.
  • Fixed-term employment: coverage of 60% of costs, up to a maximum of 48,000 euros for a period not exceeding 12 months.

Each company may benefit from the grant for only one hire, which must be formalised after the official entry into force of the measure. Applications will be submitted exclusively online via the regional platform ‘Bandi e Servizi’, with operational procedures and deadlines to be announced shortly.

‘Bringing advanced university training directly into the heart of production companies’ is the declared goal of the Lombardy Region. For cross-border workers, this could mean new career prospects in high-tech innovation sectors, with contracts featuring significant non-repayable contributions from the public entity.

Operational details

For cross-border workers from Ticino, the Lombardy tender represents a concrete opportunity, albeit with several critical aspects to consider carefully. The measure is designed for companies based in Lombardy, meaning that employees hired will primarily work on Italian soil, with potential implications for taxation and social security contributions.

Comparing past and present: before and after the tender

Before this measure, small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) in Lombardy already had access to tools for hiring qualified staff, though with lower contributions and stricter constraints. For example, previous regional tenders for PhD holders often covered less than 50% of costs or imposed lower spending limits. With this new measure, however, the 70% coverage for permanent hires marks a significant increase, particularly for SMEs looking to invest in innovation without excessively burdening payroll costs.

Sectors most likely to benefit

Given the goal of knowledge transfer, the sectors that stand to gain the most from the tender include:

  • Advanced technologies and Industry 4.0: automation, robotics, artificial intelligence.
  • Biotech and life sciences: pharmaceuticals, medical devices, clinical research.
  • Energy and sustainability: green tech, energy efficiency, circular economy.
  • ICT and digitalisation: software, cybersecurity, cloud computing.

For cross-border workers, these fields offer fertile ground for high-value careers, especially when combined with language skills (Italian and German) or expertise in cross-cutting areas such as data management or regulatory compliance.

Practical scenarios for cross-border workers

A Ticino cross-border worker interested in applying for one of these positions will need to weigh several factors:

1. Contract and workplace: The hiring is with an SME in Lombardy, so the primary workplace will be in Italy. However, some companies may offer partial remote working or trips to Ticino, particularly if they operate in cross-border sectors.

2. Taxation and social contributions: Income will be taxed in Italy, but workers will need to check how this affects their tax position in Ticino, especially in relation to the Italo-Swiss agreements to avoid double taxation.

3. Social security: Social security contributions will follow Italian regulations (INPS), but workers must ensure that their time working in Lombardy is recognised for pension purposes in Ticino, in line with the Italo-Swiss Convention of 9 March 1976.

4. Language and integration: While the tender is open to international candidates, proficiency in Italian is often an implicit requirement for working in Lombardy SMEs. For cross-border workers, this could be a competitive advantage, particularly if paired with German or English skills.

Opportunities for Ticino SMEs

Although the tender is aimed at Lombardy SMEs, even Ticino-based companies operating in innovative sectors could indirectly benefit from this measure. For example, some SMEs in Ticino could forge partnerships with Lombardy SMEs for joint research projects, leveraging regional funding. In such cases, cross-border workers might take on transboundary coordination roles, with mixed contracts or secondment agreements.

Risks and limitations

Despite the incentives, there are several limitations to consider:

  • Duration of the benefit: The subsidy is valid for a maximum of 24 months for permanent hires. After this period, the company will have to bear the full cost of the employee.
  • Single-use incentive: Each company can benefit from the tender only once, limiting hiring opportunities for firms looking to recruit multiple PhD holders.
  • Competition: Lombardy SMEs may prefer candidates already residing in Lombardy to simplify contract and tax management, reducing opportunities for cross-border workers.

How to prepare

Cross-border workers interested in applying should:

  • Update their CV to highlight transferable skills (project management, digitalisation, compliance) and applied research experience.
  • Verify their Italian language proficiency (at least B2 level) and, where possible, German.
  • Monitor publications from Lombardy SMEs in their sectors of interest, often available on company websites or platforms like LinkedIn.

Key points

To participate in the 'Talents – Knowledge Transfer' call, Lombardy SMEs must follow a rigorous procedure that involves several steps. Interested cross-border workers, on the other hand, will have to prepare to apply for open positions. Here is the step-by-step guide for both subjects, with attention to the implications for those who reside in Ticino.

For Lombardy SMEs: how to apply

1. Verification of requirements: Make sure that the company is an SME with operational headquarters in Lombardy and that the candidate has a PhD with a collaboration concluded no more than a year ago with a research organization.

2. Waiting for the implementation notice: The operating procedures and deadlines for the submission of applications are not yet specified. The Lombardy Region will soon publish an official notice on the 'Calls and Services' platform (bandi.regione.lombardia.it). It is crucial to monitor this page so as not to miss the terms.

3. Preparation of documentation: SMEs will have to prepare: - Employment contract (permanent or fixed-term, depending on the choice). - Documentation attesting to the candidate's PhD and collaboration with a research organisation. - Economic-financial plan that demonstrates the sustainability of the project.

4. Online submission: Applications must be sent exclusively through the regional platform 'Calls and Services', after registration. No applications will be accepted on paper or through other channels.

5. Evaluation and granting of the contribution: The Lombardy Region will evaluate the applications according to criteria of innovation, sustainability and consistency with the objectives of the call. The evaluation times are not yet specified, but the Regional Council has undertaken to make the outcome known as soon as possible.

For Ticino cross-border commuters: how to apply

1. Search for open positions: Lombardy SMEs will publish job offers on: - Individual company websites ('Work with us' section). - Platforms such as LinkedIn, Indeed, or InfoJobs. - Regional portals such as Talent Lombardia (talent.regione.lombardia.it).

2. Preparing your CV and cover letter: Your CV should be short and results-oriented, with a focus on: - Applied research experience or innovative projects. - Skills in digitization, data management, regulatory compliance (increasingly required). - Languages: Italian (B2/C1), German (useful for cross-border sectors), English (mandatory for many roles).

3. Apply for positions: Send your CV and motivation letter directly to the company, following the instructions in the advertisement. Some SMEs may also require a videoconference or face-to-face interview.

4. Contract verification: If you receive an offer, it is essential to check: - Place of work (it could be in Italy, with possible trips to Ticino). - Contract: checks whether it is permanent or fixed-term, and the duration of the regional benefit. - Taxation: Income will be taxed in Italy, but it is necessary to check how this impacts on the tax return in Ticino (for example, through the calcolatore fiscale frontalieri).

5. Social Security Management: Make sure that the company correctly applies the Italian social security contributions (INPS). Upon returning to Ticino, check with the SUVA or INPS the recognition of the periods worked for pension purposes.

What happens after hiring?

Once hired, cross-border workers must:

1. Register as a resident in Italy (if the place of work is in Lombardy). This could involve registering with the Italian Revenue Agency and opening an Italian tax number.

2. Open a current account in Italy (if you don't already have one), especially if the company disburses the salary through an Italian bank.

3. Check health coverage: In Italy, registration with the National Health Service (SSN) is mandatory for residents. However, cross-border commuters in Ticino may already be covered by Swiss health insurance. It is advisable to consult your health insurer to avoid duplication or gaps in coverage.

4. Manage mobility: If the place of work is in Milan or other cities in Lombardy, consider the option of living in Ticino with daily or weekly trips. In this case, it is useful to consult the mappa dei valichi frontalieri to optimize travel.

Beware of scams and fake offers

In recent months, cases of fraud related to job offers for cross-border commuters have increased, especially in sectors such as ICT and life sciences. To avoid falling into the trap:

  • Verify the company: Check that the SME is really registered in Lombardy (you can do this through the Italian Chamber of Commerce). - Be wary of offers that are too generous: If a salary seems too high compared to the market, it could be a scam. - Never pay to be hired: Serious SMEs do not ask for money for hiring.

Recommended Next Steps

2. Prepare the documentation: Update your CV and motivation letter, with particular attention to the skills required for the call.

3. Consult the experts: If you have any doubts about taxation or social security, contact an accountant specializing in cross-border commuters or an employment consultant.

4. Attend networking events: Some SMEs participate in cross-border trade shows or recruitment events. For example, the Research and Innovation Exhibition in Milan could be an opportunity to meet the recruiters directly.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the minimum requirements for a PhD holder to apply for the call for applications?
The candidate must hold a PhD and have completed, within the last year, a collaboration with a research body. No further formal requirements are specified, but the SME will assess the candidate's skills based on the project's needs.
Can I apply if I live in Ticino but already work in Lombardy?
Yes, the call does not impose residency restrictions on candidates, but the position must be with an SME operating in Lombardy. However, you should verify the tax and social security implications of any potential change in residency or tax regime.
How is the regional contribution calculated?
The contribution is calculated based on the gross annual cost of the hired personnel. For permanent contracts, the Lombardy Region covers 70% of costs up to a maximum of €56,000 per year for 24 months. For fixed-term contracts, the coverage is 60% up to €48,000 for 12 months.
When does the application deadline for the call end?
The operational procedures and deadlines for submitting applications have not yet been specified. The Lombardy Region will soon publish an implementing notice on the 'Bandi e Servizi' platform. It is essential to monitor the official page to avoid missing the deadlines.
Do I need to move to Italy to work for a Lombardy-based SME?
Not necessarily. Some SMEs may offer smart working or cross-border commuting to Ticino, especially if they operate in cross-border sectors. However, the contractual seat will be in Lombardy, which may require registering as a resident in Italy for tax and administrative purposes.
How does this call impact the taxes of Ticino cross-border workers?
Income will be taxed in Italy, but cross-border workers must also declare it in Ticino. The Italy-Switzerland Convention of 9 March 1976 provides mechanisms to avoid double taxation. For a precise estimate, you can use the [cross-border worker tax calculator](nav:tax-return).
Can I combine this contribution with other regional incentives?
No, the benefit cannot be combined with other regional incentives for the same position. Each company can benefit from the call only once, regardless of the number of hires.
