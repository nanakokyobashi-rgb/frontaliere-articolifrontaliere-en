Anticipating the pension for border workers Ticino-Italy involves complex choices on AVS, LPP and double taxation. Discover the mistakes to avoid with the New 2024 Agreement.

Early retirement for a Ticino-Italy cross-border worker is not a trivial choice. It involves simultaneous decisions on three fronts: the Swiss AVS system, corporate pension provision via LPP, and tax management in a context of Italy-Switzerland double taxation. With the entry into force of the New Cross-border Agreement on 1 January 2024, the taxation rules have changed for those who work in Italy and reside in Ticino (G permit) or for Ticino residents who work across the border.

The greatest risk is advancing your pension without an integrated vision of how this impacts your overall income, the social security contributions still due, and the taxes to be paid in both countries.

Why timing matters for AVS and LPP

The Swiss AVS calculates the pension based on the contributions paid during your working life. An early retirement entails: fewer years of contributions (lower basic pension), possible additional percentage reduction, need to pay supplementary contributions for "empty" periods.

The second pillar (LPP) operates according to different logics: the accumulated capital is invested until retirement, when it is converted into an annuity according to age-related conversion factors. Early retirement reduces the accumulation time and changes the conversion factor, lowering the monthly income.

A cross-border worker who withdraws early will have contributed to both AVS and LPP for a shorter period, with cumulative impacts on the total pension. There is no automatic compensation between the two pillars: both suffer the reduction.

The New Cross-Border Agreement and taxation

The threshold of 10,000 euros is not a general exemption: art. 4 of Law 83/2023 limits it to the income referred to in the art. 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013.

The Swiss withholding tax is withheld in Switzerland: Italy does not apply a double levy thanks to the tax credit in the EC framework of the 730 declaration. However, at retirement, the flows change. The income from AVS and LPP coming from Switzerland is subject to Italian taxation according to the double taxation agreement of 9 March 1976.

A cross-border worker who anticipates his pension must foresee how this fiscal transition will take place. Those who consult the salary calculator can simulate the impact of the transition from earned income to retirement income on their net paycheck.

Operational details

Five common mistakes in early retirement

Cross-border planners often stumble upon the same repeated errors, especially when they confuse employment taxation with social security situation.

Mistake 1: Confusing the New Frontier Workers Agreement with the pension

The New Cross-Border Agreement (effective 1 January 2024) regulates the taxation of cross-border WORKING INCOME, not pension income. The threshold of 10,000 euros is not a general exemption: art. 4 of Law 83/2023 limits it to the income referred to in the art. 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013. It's not like that. The income from AVS and LPP is taxed according to different rules, based on the Italy-Switzerland double taxation agreement of 1976. Those who retire early lose the benefit of the exemption in the first year of withdrawal.

Mistake 2: Underestimating the impact on the AVS income

The AVS calculates the average pension over the entire contributory career. Early withdrawal means fewer years of contributions, therefore a lower calculation base. The AVS algorithm also provides a percentage reduction for those who take their pension before the normal age, but the technical details vary from case to case. Anyone who does not consult SECO (Central Compensation Office) or the AVS office of their canton risks discovering too late that the anticipated pension is lower than expected.

Mistake 3: Ignoring the second pillar (LPP)

The LPP, fueled by contributions that vary from 7% to 18% depending on age (25 years and over), is often the "silent pillar" for cross-border commuters. Many know that the Italian employer pays to the INPS, but they do not realize that in Switzerland the LPP operates in parallel. Early retirement limits the growth of this fund, reducing the capital to be converted into income.

Mistake 4: Not planning for the tax transition

With the G permit (domicile Ticino, work Italy), the right to opt for KVG health insurance is a strategic choice. Upon retirement, the context changes: the cross-border worker could choose to transfer his residence to Italy (waiving the G permit) or keep it in Ticino (D permit). Each option has different tax and social security implications. Those who don't plan this step find themselves with a complicated Italian tax return.

Mistake 5: Forgetting AVS supplementary contributions

A cross-border worker who has "empty" periods in his career (unemployment, leave) can pay supplementary AVS contributions to avoid losing years of cotization. Before anticipating your pension, it is advisable to check whether these periods can be "covered" retroactively, thus increasing your future income.

How these errors combine in practice

Imagine a scenario: a cross-border worker decides to retire before the normal age without consulting either SECO or INPS. The threshold of 10,000 euros is not a general exemption: art. 4 of Law 83/2023 limits it to the income referred to in the art. 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013.

These errors combine to result in a reduction in overall income that could have been avoided with 12-18 month advance planning. The difference between a well-managed transition and an improvised one can amount to thousands of francs in difference in annual income.

Key points

Operational checklist for early retirement

If you are considering early retirement as a cross-border worker, here is a concrete checklist to avoid the mistakes described above.

Step 1: Contact SECO and check your AVS situation

The AVS Central Compensation Office (SECO) has a section dedicated to cross-border commuters. Request an extract of your contribution history and a preliminary calculation of the annuity for different retirement scenarios. Don't wait until the month of the official application: having the data 12-18 months in advance allows you to plan.

Step 2: Analyze your second pillar (LPP)

If you have an LPP account with a Swiss institution or with your Italian employer (if the Swiss regime applies), request a management report and an income projection at the time of withdrawal. Compare the scenarios: anticipating retirement by 1-2 years leads to measurable differences in income, often between 5% and 15% depending on the institution.

Step 3: Fill out a Ticino-Italy tax statement

With the help of a Ticino or Italian tax consultant specialized in cross-border workers: estimate your pension income (AVS + LPP + any income from parallel work), identify whether you remain in the regime of the New Cross-border Workers Agreement or if your situation changes, plan how to avoid double taxation using the tax credit (CE framework of the Italian 730 declaration). This step is crucial because it determines whether the tax savings from the lower annuity offset the regime change.

Step 4: Evaluate your KVG option right

If you have permit G, check whether you intend to keep it when you retire or transfer your residence to Italy. This choice impacts: health insurance coverage (Swiss KVG vs Italian system), local taxation (Ticino vs border Italy), access to public services (healthcare, local contributions). Many cross-border workers keep their G permit even in retirement to maintain KVG coverage.

Step 5: Plan your AVS supplementary contributions

If you have "empty" periods in your career, evaluate with SECO whether it is worth making voluntary supplementary contributions before applying for a pension. This can increase the pension and make up for missing years of contributions. The cost of these contributions is tax deductible in Switzerland and often generates a positive return on your annuity.

Step 6: Document everything for your tax return

Keep: communications from SECO and your LPP institution, pay slips for the last 12 months (to certify last-day income), pension certificates from INPS (if applicable), G/residence permit documentation. Switzerland and Italy exchange tax data: there is no need to hide anything, on the contrary, the ordered documentation speeds up the procedure and reduces the risk of investigations.

Frequently Asked Questions
Which income does the €10,000 limit apply to?
Article 9’s transitional regime applies only to workers who meet the Agreement’s definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between 31 December 2018 and 17 July 2023; covered employment income is taxable exclusively in Switzerland. The ordinary regime applies in other cases, with Italian taxation and credit for Swiss tax where applicable. The €10,000 in Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is only the limit for income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law 147/2013, not a general allowance. FTA factsheet · Agreement, Article 9 · Normattiva, Article 4.
How much does the advance of the pension affect the AVS income?
The AVS calculates the pension on the basis of the average income and years of contribution. Anticipating retirement means: (1) fewer years of contributions paid = lower basic income, (2) possible additional percentage reduction for those who retire before ordinary age. There is no fixed figure, but it depends on your specific career and the length of the advance. For an accurate estimate, please contact SECO (AVS Central Clearing Office) with a request for preliminary calculation.
Do I keep the LAMal (health insurance) option right if I have allowed G and retire?
Yes, the right of option for LAMal is a benefit linked to the G permit (border worker domiciled in Ticino, working in Italy). He also remains retired, provided that you retain the G permit. If you move your residence to Italy (and give up the G permit), you lose access to LAMal and are covered by the Italian healthcare system. Evaluate this decision carefully, because it impacts rewards, costs, and access to health services.
How do I handle double taxation when I retire?
The Italy-Switzerland double taxation agreement of 9 March 1976 establishes that pension annuities (AVS, LPP) from Switzerland are taxed in Italy according to declaration 730. In any case, Switzerland applies a federal/cantonal tax on the income received. However, the Swiss payment generates an Italian tax credit in the EC framework, which reduces the overall tax burden. Consult a border agent accountant to fill in the 730 correctly and avoid double payments.
Should AVS supplementary contributions be paid before retiring?
If you have 'empty' periods in your career (unemployment, leave, years abroad without payments), you can make voluntary supplementary contributions to AVS to fill these gaps. Each additional contribution year increases the basis for calculating the annuity. Convenience depends on how many years are missing, your job longevity projections, and the amount of additional income. SECO can calculate whether these contributions are worth paying in your specific case.
