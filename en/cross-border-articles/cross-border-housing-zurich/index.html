One out of ten new immigrants chooses Zurich, but finds only dilapidated apartments or exorbitant rents. What changes for those working in Ticino.

In 2022, around 12,000 immigrant people chose Zurich, equal to 10% of arrivals in Switzerland. A tempting figure but which clashes with a daily reality: those looking for a home in the Swiss economic capital find only "leftovers from the real estate market". This was revealed by the analysis of the Zurich Kantonalbank (ZKB) published on 23 March 2026, based on federal statistics. To give you an idea, around one person in twenty of the entire Swiss population lives in Zurich, but the market is so saturated that new arrivals end up in "junk" apartments or with out-of-market rents.

The paradoxical situation also emerged in Bellinzona during the annual meeting of the Italian-Swiss border associations. The president of Frontaliere Ticino, Marco Bernasconi, says: "Many of our members work in Zurich but sleep in Lugano or even across the border because north of the Gotthard they can't find anything decent under CHF 2,500 a month for a three-room apartment." A cross-border worker who crosses the Brogeda border crossing every morning can save up to CHF 800 on rent, even taking into account the cost of transport and time.

The Zurich UDC has launched a cantonal initiative to give priority in housing to those who already reside in Switzerland, but the proposal is dividing. According to the cantonal construction secretary, it is not just a question of rules but of numbers: "Zurich has less than 0.2% of vacant apartments, the lowest in Western Europe". The result is that Italian border workers, who already have to demonstrate to the Department of Institutions (DFE) that they have a valid rental contract to obtain the G permit, find themselves signing contracts for damp rooms or rooms without thermal insulation.

A concrete example comes from an engineer from Como who has been working for a bank on Paradeplatz since 2024: he pays 2,800 CHF for a 35m2 studio apartment in a 1960s building without a lift in Wiedikon. "In Italy I could rent a four-room apartment with a lake view for the same amount," he says. The story is common: cross-border workers end up in a spiral in which 35-40% of the gross salary ends up only in rent, when the common sense rule would not exceed 25%.

Operational details

From a regulatory point of view, Zurich law does not explicitly prohibit discriminating against newcomers, but the Swiss Civil Code (art. 261) requires that the selection criteria be "objective and verifiable". In practice, the owner can ask for three months' deposit, bank references and work certificates, but cannot refuse a candidate just because he does not yet have a residence permit. The problem is that the evaluation takes place in 48 hours and those who have no credit history in Switzerland lose their job.

For cross-border commuters the typical route is as follows: 1. Signing of the employment contract in Zurich → 2. Searching for an apartment within 30 days → 3. Submission of the G permit application to the DFE in Bellinzona → 4. Check of the rental contract by the migration office → 5. Issuance of the permit valid for 5 years, renewable only if you can prove that you still have the accommodation.

If the property does not meet the minimum requirements (20 m2 living area for a single person, 12 m2 for each additional cohabitant, opening window, independent heating), the DFE can reject the application. In 2025, 47 cross-border commuter applications were rejected for this reason, 12% more than in 2024.

One way out is the cooperative market: in Zurich there are 130 non-profit real estate companies offering rents 20-30% below the market. To access, however, you need to become a member with a minimum investment of CHF 2,000 and remain on the waiting list for 3-5 years. Commuting cross-border commuters can get around the problem by asking the Municipality of Lugano or Mendrisio for a certificate of need, which gives additional points in the rankings. You need to bring: pay slip, employment contract, proof of residence in Ticino and declaration that you travel at least 60 km a day for work reasons.

Key points

First steps for those who need to find a home before starting to work in Zurich:

1. Realistic budget: Calculate your rent to be no more than 25% of your gross salary. If you take 6,000 CHF, look below 1,500 CHF. 2. Documents ready: employment contract, three more pay slips, criminal record extract and accident insurance certificate. The complete package makes you close the contact in 24 hours. 3. Less popular platforms: in addition to homegate.ch and immoscout24.ch, try woko.ch (student shared houses), tutti-ch.ch (cooperatives) and the Facebook groups "Ticino-Zurich Cross-Border Rentals". 4. Tricks for cross-border workers: when you call, immediately specify that you have G permit and permanent employment. Many owners fear that you will return to Italy after a few months. 5. Beware of trap ads: if they ask you for money before the visit or do not provide the precise address, run away. They are almost always scams.

FAQ – Questions we receive at the Bellinzona office:

Q: Can I apply for a G permit if I have a temporary lease? A: Yes, but it must have a minimum duration of one year and the property must pass the health department inspection. If it's a temporary contract, they only give you 6 months leave.

Q: Can my employer force me to move to Zurich? A: No. The employment contract may indicate the location, but living in Ticino is your right. The only case is if you have a role with night availability, then they can ask for residence in the district.

Q: If I lose my house, do I also lose my permit? A: You have 30 days to find an alternative. If you present the new attestation to the DFE within the deadline, the permit remains valid. Otherwise you fall into the "homeless" category and renewal becomes difficult.

Q: Is it really worth living in Como and working in Zurich? A: It depends on your salary. If you earn more than CHF 90,000 a year, the savings of CHF 1,500 per month on rent will cover the cost of transport (approximately CHF 420 for a GA travelcard). But count 3 hours of travel per day: in a year that is 720 hours, equivalent to a working month.

Frequently Asked Questions
How does the real estate market in Zurich work for Italian cross-border commuters?
The real estate market in Zurich is saturated and newcomers end up in 'junk' apartments or with out-of-market rents. Italian cross-border commuters must prove that they have a valid lease to obtain the G permit, but landlords can ask for difficult conditions such as three months' deposit and bank references.
How much can an Italian cross-border commuter who chooses to live in Lugano instead of Zurich save for a three-room apartment?
A cross-border commuter can save up to CHF 800 per month (considering rent and transport) by choosing Lugano instead of Zurico for a three-room apartment, with an annual saving of about CHF 9,600.
What are the minimum requirements for an apartment to obtain a G permit in Switzerland?
To obtain the G permit, the accommodation must meet minimum requirements: at least 20 square meters for one person, an additional 12 square meters for each roommate, an opening window and an independent heating system. Failure to comply with these standards may lead to the rejection of the application.
How can I get accommodation in Zurich if I am a cross-border commuter on a budget?
Consider housing cooperatives in Zurich, which offer rents that are 20-30% lower than the market. Access requires the subscription of a membership fee (minimum 2,000 CHF) and a waiting list of 3-5 years. Alternatively, search on less conventional platforms or dedicated Facebook groups.
What documents do I need to speed up the search for an apartment in Zurich as a cross-border commuter?
To conclude a rental contract quickly, prepare a complete package: employment contract, the last three pay slips, an extract from the criminal record and the accident insurance certificate. Having these documents ready can allow you to close the deal within 24 hours.
