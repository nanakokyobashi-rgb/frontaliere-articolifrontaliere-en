Interreg Italy-Switzerland to renaturalize the Lura stream between Ticino and Varese. Cultural festival in Saronno 4-6 September and travelling exhibition in schools

It is called Riverlhab and is the Interreg project that brings together Italy and Switzerland to address the renaturalization of the Lura stream, with the aim of tackling the impacts of climate change on river habitats in the border territory between the two countries. The lead is the Consorzio Parco del Lura, based in Como, while the partners include ERSAF, the Department of Territory of the Canton Ticino, and the Scuola Universitaria Professionale della Svizzera Italiana (SUPSI).

Operational details

Comparison tables and sharing of best practices

The project involves the organization of comparison tables between the different stakeholders involved: Municipalities, provinces, Lombardy Region, ERSAF, local Civil Protection groups, water service managers, as well as the Canton of Ticino with the Office of Watercourses and the Environment Division. The objective is to study and share best practices in the management of river areas already experienced in both territories. This cross-border approach allows for a direct comparison between Italian and Swiss experiences, with the possibility of adopting innovative strategies tested in similar contexts.

For local technicians and administrators, the project offers a specific training path focused on integrated design through Nature Based Solutions and adaptation strategies to climate change. The training will include introductory meetings, technical-scientific and managerial modules, in-person and remote workshops, site visits, and the creation of an educational manual. Four public meetings on climate change and active citizenship are also planned. This training approach involves both professionals in the hydraulic and environmental sectors and local administrations in Ticino and Lombardy.

Territorial experimentation and the role of SUPSI

The most concrete part of Riverlhab concerns the experimentation of specific interventions for the renaturalization of riverbanks. Six project areas have been identified along the course of the Lura stream, where widespread territorial interventions will be carried out. Each will have specific local objectives but with significant impacts on the entire river valley.

Key points

The Ri.Ver Festival: first appointment in Saronno

The Ri.Ver Festival, the first edition of the cross-border cultural festival, will take place in Saronno from September 4 to 6. This event represents the ideal opportunity for citizens, administrators, and environmental sector operators to delve into the project's themes and discover the cross-border territory from a new perspective. The festival will connect the two territories and bring to the attention of the communities the themes of river regeneration and shared environmental management.

For those who cannot reach Saronno, the itinerant exhibition represents an alternative to discover the project. Curated by the SUPSI Institute of Design, the interactive and multimedia exhibition will be hosted in schools, civic centers, and libraries of both territories. This itinerant format allows reaching a wider audience, from schools to local communities, to raise awareness on the themes of territorial decay and river dynamics. The portability of the exhibition ensures that even the smallest centers of the cross-border territory can benefit from this educational resource.

Active participation and training opportunities

In addition to the exhibition, days of cleaning the stream and other activities to activate citizenship are planned, as well as a 'narrative game' to bring citizens closer to the knowledge of their territory and good practices of river management. These initiatives also allow non-specialists to contribute concretely to environmental care and feel protagonists of the regeneration of their territory.

Frequently Asked Questions
What exactly is the Riverlhab project?
Riverlhab is an Interreg project funded by the European Union involving Italy and Switzerland for the renaturation of the Lura stream. Led by the Lura Park Consortium, with Italian (ERSAF) and Swiss partners (Canton of Ticino Territory Department, SUPSI), the project aims to define common methodologies to intervene in complex river basins and address the impacts of climate change on river habitats in transboundary territory.
How do I participate in Riverlhab activities?
You can participate in different ways: by visiting the travelling exhibition in schools and civic centres, by participating in the Ri.Ver Festival in Saronno from 4 to 6 September, by registering for citizen science activities for the participatory monitoring of waterways and wetlands, or by participating in the stream cleaning days. If you are an administrator or technician, you can follow specific training courses on Nature Based Solutions and climate change adaptation.
Who are the main partners of the project?
The main partners are: Consorzio Parco del Lura (leader, Como), ERSAF, Department of the territory of the Canton of Ticino, and the Professional University School of Italian Switzerland (SUPSI). The project also involves municipalities, provinces, the Lombardy Region, Civil Protection groups, water service managers, the Ticino Water Courses Office and the Environment Division of the Ticino Canton.
What is Riverlhab's overall budget?
The total budget of the project is 1,476,620.34 euros, to which is added a Swiss contribution of 101,200 francs. The funds are intended to finance renaturalization, training, research, citizen science and public events such as the Ri.Ver Festival, as well as the creation of a traveling exhibition and participatory design with SUPSI students.
What will happen to the Lura stream during the project?
The project provides for specific renaturalization interventions in six areas identified along the auction of the Lura stream. Interventions will be carried out throughout the territory with specific objectives at the local level. Students from SUPSI's Institute of Design will contribute to the design of stations and points overlooking the river. The ultimate goal is to mitigate the impacts of climate change on river habitats even beyond the end of the project.
