Pentecost 2026: traffic returning to Ticino

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 25 May 2026

After the Pentecost long weekend, the return to Ticino promises fewer traffic jams than in previous years. Here's what to expect.

Context

In brief

  • Pentecost 2026 return: fewer queues expected
  • Monday, May 25th peak traffic day
  • Maximum 2 km of queue at Airolo

Key facts

  • What: Pentecost return
  • When: May 25th, 2026
  • Where: A2 motorway, San Gottardo south portal
  • Who: Federal Office for Roads (FEDRO)
  • Amount: 21 km of queue in 2025

After the long Pentecost weekend, the return to Ticino is expected to be less problematic compared to previous years. According to FEDRO forecasts, Monday, May 25th will be the last day with high traffic. The south portal of the San Gottardo, particularly at Airolo, will be the critical point, but no critical situations like those recorded between Friday and Saturday are expected, with a peak of 21 kilometers of queue and waiting times that exceeded four hours.

Pentecost 2025: the comparison

Last year, during the Pentecost holiday, traffic at Airolo reached a maximum wait time of one hour and 20 minutes. Traffic remained intense from 10 AM to 8 PM, then gradually decreased during the night. In previous years, both in 2023 and 2024, delays never exceeded 40 minutes. This Sunday, some returns were also recorded, with a queue that fluctuated between one and two kilometers throughout the day. Traffic heading north was sustained but within the norm, with delays that never exceeded 30 minutes.

Implications for cross-border workers

For cross-border workers who commute to Ticino and reside in Italy, the Pentecost return is a crucial moment. This year's forecasts indicate a less critical situation compared to the past, but it is always advisable to plan the trip in advance. Those who need to cross the Alps heading south can expect shorter waiting times compared to previous years, but it is important to monitor traffic conditions in real-time. FEDRO provides constant updates on its website, which can be useful to avoid excessive queues.

What to do in case of queues

If you find yourself in a situation of heavy traffic, there are some precautions you can take to make the journey more comfortable. Make sure you have water, snacks, and a portable phone charger available. If possible, try to avoid peak hours, which usually occur between 10 AM and 8 PM. Use navigation apps that provide real-time traffic updates, such as Google Maps or Waze, to find alternative routes.

Conclusion

In summary, the Pentecost 2026 return to Ticino should be less problematic compared to previous years. However, it is always advisable to plan the trip in advance and monitor traffic conditions. For cross-border workers, this means organizing to avoid peak hours and using navigation tools to find alternative routes. With a bit of preparation, the return journey can be more comfortable and less stressful.
