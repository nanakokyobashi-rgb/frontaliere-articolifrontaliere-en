Pedemontana warns: no toll payment via SMS or email

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 7 October 2026

## TL;DR - Pedemontana does not request toll payments via SMS or email - Scammers threaten to block or seize the vehicle - Links lead to fake domains - Verify

The alert from Varese

In Varese, the scam involving fake tolls, already reported in recent days, has become more aggressive. Autostrada Pedemontana Lombarda, the company that manages the Lombardy motorway section, has reported a new development in the fraudulent messages spread via email, SMS and WhatsApp.

The new development concerns the pressure exerted on the recipient. To push them to pay, scammers evoke the risk of the vehicle being blocked or administratively impounded if the payment is not made immediately. Pedemontana denies this possibility and clarifies that it never requests toll payments through SMS or email with links intended for payment.

According to the company's recent findings, compared with the initial attempts, the texts are designed to appear more credible. The addresses also change continually: the links lead to fake domains not attributable to Pedemontana, with the aim of bypassing security filters and computer blocks.

The messages may also reproduce official logos and trademarks. The aim is not only to obtain payment: the communications also seek to collect personal data, banking information or PIN codes for credit cards. For a cross-border commuter who receives a similar request, the reference to the vehicle or the urgency does not replace verification of the toll. Any tolls still to be paid must be checked exclusively on the official portal pedemontana.com.

Calculating travel expenses is a different matter: to organize the budget, one can consult costo auto pendolare, while verification of the request remains entrusted to the channels indicated by the company.

The company reiterates that, to protect one's data, one must never click on suspicious links and that payment requests must always be verified directly through the official channels of Autostrada Pedemontana Lombarda.

Operational details

When pressure replaces verification

Before and after in fraudulent messages

The most insidious aspect, according to Pedemontana, is the shift from a fake request to a request crafted to appear plausible. In the first attempts, the subject was the toll; in more recent communications, the text adds an immediate and serious consequence: the blocking or administrative immobilization of the vehicle. The company describes the threat as false, but it is intended to push the recipient into making a payment without verification.

For users of the Lombardy motorway section, and especially for a cross-border commuter who recognizes the company's name in the message, the visual design is not a sufficient criterion. An official logo can be replicated, and an address can change from one message to the next. The fact that the text appears better written does not change the rule stated by Pedemontana: payments are not requested via SMS or e-mail containing links.

The risk is not limited to the payment

The message may also target personal data, banking information, and credit card PINs. For this reason, the request should not be assessed solely based on whether a toll is involved. The channel, link, and pressure exerted on the recipient are part of the pattern described in the notice.

The source does not provide routes, schedules, amounts, or specific categories of users. It therefore does not make it possible to determine, based on the text received alone, whether an individual recipient actually needs to pay. For a cross-border reader, the practical distinction is clear-cut: the plausibility of the message does not constitute confirmation, and verification must be carried out through the official channel. Key points

What to do when faced with a suspicious message

A simple sequence

1. Don't click. If the request arrives via SMS, email or WhatsApp and contains a payment link, it should be treated as suspicious. Pedemontana urges people not to open those links. 2. Don't give in to the urgency. The threat of a block or administrative hold is the element scammers use to push people into making an immediate payment. It is not confirmation that a toll exists. 3. Verify through the official channel. Open the pedemontana.com portal and check there whether there are any tolls still to be paid. Do not use the address contained in the message, because domains change and may not be attributable to the company. 4. Call if in doubt. The call center indicated by Pedemontana answers at the toll-free number 800 936 360. This is the contact to use when the request is unclear. 5. Protect your data. Messages may aim to collect personal data, banking information or PIN codes for credit cards. The presence of an official logo or brand is not enough to make the communication authentic.

These steps also apply to a cross-border commuter who receives the notice while organizing their journey: verification remains separate from the rush of the trip. The company indicates the official portal and call center as references for checking any tolls and clarifying doubts.

One must not turn a received message into an automatic decision. First identify the channel, then avoid the link, and finally check your status on the official website. If a doubt remains, call the toll-free number. The process is short and does not require relying on the domain written in the communication.

Frequently Asked Questions
How can I verify whether I really need to pay a Pedemontana toll?
Pedemontana Lombarda never requires payment of tolls via SMS, e‑mail or WhatsApp containing links. The only safe way to check for any outstanding debts is to access the official portal pedemontana.com or call the toll-free number 800 936 360. Any message inviting you to pay through these channels should be considered suspicious.
What are the signs that indicate a fraudulent toll message?
The signs include the presence of a link to make the payment, the threat of blocking or administrative seizure of the vehicle if you do not pay immediately, the use of official logos but with domains other than pedemontana.com, and the request for personal data, banking information or credit card PIN codes. These elements are typical of the scam scheme described by the company.
What should I do if I receive a suspicious SMS about a toll?
Never click on the link contained in the message. Do not give in to the urgency of the alleged vehicle block. Open the pedemontana.com website to check for any tolls to pay or call the call center at the toll-free number 800 936 360. Never provide personal or banking information or PINs, even if the message seems authentic.
