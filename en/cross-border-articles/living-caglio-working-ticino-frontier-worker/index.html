For Article 9 of the Agreement, “old frontier workers” are people who met the tax requirements and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais on 17 July 2023 or during the period from 31 December 2018 through 17 July 2023. Article 9 keeps covered employment income taxable exclusively at source in Switzerland; other workers follow the ordinary regime, with ordinary taxation in Italy, a credit for Swiss tax and Swiss withholding at 80% of the ordinary rates. Operational details

Cost of living and financial planning

Those who reside in Caglio benefit on average from a lower cost of living than those who already live in Ticino. Housing rentals, groceries, household utilities and local services in Lombardy are usually less expensive. This advantage is significant on an annual basis and represents one of the main reasons for the choice of transfer for many cross-border commuters. However, commuting costs reduce part of the benefit: fuel for the car, vehicle maintenance, Swiss vignettes for access to cantonal roads, parking in the main Ticino centers (Lugano, Bellinzona) and wear and tear on the vehicle due to daily journeys weigh on the personal budget. An honest analysis must include these factors to evaluate the net benefit of moving.

In addition to regular commuting, occasional changes in expenditure must be considered: international driving licence, car insurance adequate to Swiss regulations, potential fines for traffic violations, and vehicle reliability costs (inspections, tyres) in mountain and winter climates.

Healthcare: KVG and coverage options

All cross-border commuters with G Permit have the right to opt for Swiss health insurance (LAMal, Law on Health Insurance). The KVG is not a 'health tax', but a compulsory insurance covering medical, hospital, pharmaceutical and rehabilitation services. Deductibles for adults vary from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 per year, depending on the plan chosen; Monthly premiums vary significantly between different insurance carriers.

A cross-border worker can choose to remain registered with the Italian system (INPS/SSN) - maintaining Italian national coverage - or opt for the Swiss KVG. This decision has concrete implications on coverage (which hospital, which doctor, which services covered), on costs (monthly premium, deductible, reimbursement percentages) and on times to access treatment (medical emergencies in Switzerland are generally rapid, but the wait for specialist visits varies). The choice must be evaluated individually based on the personal health profile, family needs, and frequency of use of medical services.

Crossings, connections and daily commuting

Caglio is located a few kilometers from several border crossings that connect the province of Bergamo to Ticino. The main ones are Chiasso and Brogeda. Depending on which part of Ticino you are working in - Lugano in the south, Bellinzona in the centre, Locarno in the north - the choice of the optimal pass and road conditions varies considerably. Crossing times, road congestion and crossing infrastructure vary seasonally (tourist summer, tourist influxes) and by time slots (morning and evening rush hours). Planning the route means considering not only the distance in kilometres, but also the commuter flows typical of your specific work area.

New Cross-border Workers Agreement: 'old' and 'new' cross-border workers

The New Cross-border Workers Agreement of 23 December 2020 (applied since 1 January 2024, ratified in Italy with Law 83 of 13 June 2023) distinguishes two categories of workers with different regimes. Old cross-border commuters remain taxed only in Switzerland (art. 9 of the 2020 Agreement). The "old cross-border workers" fall under the art. 9 only if they satisfied the tax requirements and had worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais in the period indicated; for covered incomes they are taxed exclusively in Switzerland. This distinction directly affects the calculation of the taxable amount in Italy and the amounts of annual tax refunds.

Pension pillars and supplementary pensions

As a cross-border worker, the worker accumulates contributory seniority in the Swiss social security system (AVS, first pillar). The old-age pension is calculated on the basis of the contributions paid in Switzerland during the years of cross-border work. Any years of contributions in Italy (before the transfer) can be integrated with the INPS, provided that the transfer is correctly notified. The second pillar (LPP, occupational pension) is mandatory in Switzerland from the age of 25 and the contribution percentage varies from 7% to 18% depending on the age of the member. The third pillar (3a) allows for additional, tax-deductible private savings to supplement your future pension. These pillars interact: a Ticino pay slip already contains deductions for AVS and LPP, and the net amount is significantly lower than an equivalent Italian salary.

Transfer Procedure and Permit G Request

The first step is to ensure you have a valid employment contract with a Swiss employer (in Ticino or another canton). Without a contract, it's impossible to apply for Permit G. Once the contract is signed, the employer must submit the request to the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM). In parallel, register with the municipal registry of Caglio to prove stable residence in Italy; this document is essential for the permit application. SEM will communicate processing times; usually, Permit G is issued within 2-4 weeks. During this waiting period, you can already work in Switzerland with the contract and application receipt in hand. Essential documents: valid ID (Italian ID card or passport), signed employment contract, declaration of residence in Caglio issued by the Italian municipality, and receipt of registration with Swiss local authorities (if required by the canton). We recommend being assisted by a specialized accountant for cross-border work to verify that the documentation is complete and compliant with SEM's current procedures.

Taxation: Declaration, Model 730, and Refunds As a cross-border worker, your Italian income declaration (model 730 or Unico for more complex situations) must include the foreign income earned in Switzerland. The CE section of the declaration is specific for income produced in countries with a tax convention: Switzerland is listed in the convention of March 9, 1976. In this section, declare the gross amount of Swiss income and the tax paid in Switzerland for withholding; the Revenue Agency automatically calculates the tax credit, avoiding double taxation regulated by the convention. Many cross-border workers are entitled to refunds (rebates of overpaid taxes). The refund is calculated by comparing the tax paid in Switzerland (withholding at source) and that due in Italy, calculated according to progressive IRPEF rates (23% up to €28,000, 33% from €28,001 to €50,000, 43% over €50,000). Entitlement to a refund depends on the net salary, allowed Italian deductions, and applied fiscal progressivity. A specialized cross-border work accountant analyzes the individual case and requests the refund from the Revenue Agency using the correct procedure. Refunds are usually processed within 6-12 months from the official request.

Health Planning: LAMal versus INPS Within the first month of starting work in Switzerland, you must formally decide whether to join LAMal or remain in the Italian system (SSN via INPS). The choice must be informed and based on your real needs. LAMal offers direct access to Swiss hospitals, clinics, and doctors, with generally short waiting lists for emergencies and specialist visits; however, monthly premiums (around CHF 300-500 for healthy adults) and franchises (CHF 300-2,500) can be significant. The Italian SSN offers coverage at a lower cost (through INPS contributions already paid in the pay slip), but longer waiting times for certain specialist services and the need to return to Italy for complex hospitalizations. We recommend evaluation based on: frequency of medical service use, presence of chronic pathologies, number of dependents, and facilitated access to medical facilities near your residence and workplace.

Article 1(237–238) of Italian Law No. 213/2023 does not apply indiscriminately to all cross-border workers. It covers: (a) Italian residents who work and stay in Switzerland and use the Italian SSN; (b) frontier workers under Article 9(1) of the Italy–Switzerland Agreement whose income is taxable only in Switzerland and who exercised the legal right to choose health insurance; and (c) dependants of those two categories. Paragraph 238 sets a progressive 3–6% share of net Swiss salary, with a €30 minimum and an ordinary €200 maximum for each month worked from 2024. Frequently Asked Questions
What is the G Permit and how is it required for those who live in Caglio?
The G Permit is the residence permit that entitles a worker residing in Italy (as in Caglio) to work daily in Switzerland (Ticino). It is different from Permit B, which is reserved for those who already reside in Switzerland. To apply for the G Permit you need a valid employment contract with a Swiss employer; the employer submits the application to the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM). Usually the permit is issued within 2-4 weeks. In parallel, register with the Municipality of Caglio to prove permanent residence.
How does the taxation of a border worker resident in Caglio and working in Ticino work?
Withholding tax on income from work takes place exclusively in Switzerland (5.3% AVS/AI/IPG, plus AD/AC 1.1%, LAINF 0.7–1.5%, LPP 7–18%). Italy avoids double taxation by means of the tax credit, managed in the EC framework of the declaration (form 730). Many frontier workers are entitled to refunds if the tax paid in Switzerland exceeds that due in Italy according to the IRPEF rates (23% up to € 28,000, 33% up to € 50,000, 43% more).
