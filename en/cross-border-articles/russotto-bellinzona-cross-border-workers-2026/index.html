Russotto on Bellinzona: 'A choice I might not repeat' (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 4 May 2026

Andrea Russotto recalls his years in Ticino, marked by family sacrifices and the pressure of being labeled as the new Del Piero.

Andrea Russotto, born in 1988, is a name that made many football fans dream. Growing up in Lazio's youth academy, he was hailed as the 'new Del Piero' and arrived in Bellinzona, under the shadow of the Castles, at just 16 years old. The media pressure was overwhelming, but the choice to move to Ticino was courageous. With AC Bellinzona, between loans and returns, he collected 9 appearances and 1 goal in Challenge League, 8 appearances in Super League with 1 assist, and 5 matches in the Swiss Cup adorned with 3 goals.

A heavy comparison

The comparison with Del Piero was enormous, perhaps too big for a boy who was still searching for himself. Russotto chose Bellinzona also to distance himself from overwhelming media pressure. 'Well, oh, maybe the comparison was a bit too big. Surely, though, at that time I was at a crucial moment in my growth and had important qualities. Comparisons in football come quickly, but being associated with sacred monsters like Del Piero was perhaps premature. I was still too young.'

The impact of leaving home

At first, it was almost traumatic. I grew up in a small town near Rome, between the parish football pitch and the friends from football school. Finding myself so far from home at such a young age was not easy. My family made enormous sacrifices: my mom and my sister left practically everything to follow me to Switzerland, while my father went back and forth every weekend.

Implications for young cross-border footballers

The story of Andrea Russotto is an example of how young talents can find opportunities beyond national borders. Ticino, with its proximity to Italy, offers an ideal environment for young footballers seeking to make the big leap. However, media pressure and expectations can be a significant burden.

Comparison with other experiences

Russotto had a unique experience, but he is not the only Italian footballer to have played in Switzerland. Many others have found in Ticino a springboard for their careers. However, not everyone has had the same success. The difference often lies in the ability to manage pressure and adapt to a new environment.

Possible scenarios

For young footballers considering moving to Switzerland, it is important to carefully evaluate the situation. Proximity to family and the support of a local community can make a big difference. Moreover, it is crucial to have a well-defined career plan and adequate support to face the challenges that may arise.

The role of families

Families play a crucial role in the success of a young footballer. Emotional and practical support can make the difference between success and failure. Russotto was fortunate to have a family that made great sacrifices for him, and this contributed to his success.

Conclusion

The story of Andrea Russotto is an example of how football can offer unique opportunities, but also of how it can be difficult to manage expectations and pressure. For young footballers considering moving to Switzerland, it is important to have a well-defined plan and adequate support to face the challenges that may arise.

What to do if you're a young footballer looking to move to Switzerland

If you're a young footballer considering moving to Switzerland, there are a few things you should do to ensure the best possible start. First, it's important to find an agent who can help you navigate the complex world of Swiss football. A good agent can help you find the right team and negotiate a contract that's fair for you.

Step-by-step for the move

1. Find an agent: An experienced agent can help you find the right team and negotiate a contract. 2. Evaluate your options: Consider the different teams and opportunities they offer. Look for a team that has a good development program for young footballers. 3. Prepare your documentation: Make sure you have all the necessary documents for the move, including your passport, visa, and medical certificate. 4. Find accommodation: Look for accommodation near the team and the local community. This will help you integrate more easily. 5. Plan your future: Make sure you have a plan for your future, both in football and outside of it. This will help you manage pressure and expectations better.

Conclusion

Moving to Switzerland can be a unique opportunity for young footballers, but it's important to be prepared. With the right support and a well-defined plan, you can succeed and achieve your goals.
