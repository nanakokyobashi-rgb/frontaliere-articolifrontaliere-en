Mission Emergency: Chiasso Becomes 'Capital' of Rescue and Safety

On April 26, Chiasso will be the scene of a unique event: Mission Emergency. This event represents a unique opportunity for citizens to inform themselves directly about the rescue and safety services offered by the ten first-responder entities present in the area.

During the event, the first-responder entities will present their daily work with stands in direct contact with citizens. Visitors will be able to discover how rescue services work, how interventions are organized, and what strategies are adopted to ensure citizen safety.

The Municipality of Chiasso and the Chiasso Police are the initiators of this initiative, which aims to create greater awareness of the importance of rescue and safety in everyday life. The event will be a testament to how collaboration between local entities can lead to concrete and positive results for the community.

During the event, visitors will be able to discover how rescue services are organized in case of emergency. For example, the Chiasso Police has implemented an alarm system that allows citizens to be alerted in real-time in case of danger. This system was tested during an exercise in July 2022 and has demonstrated its effectiveness in ensuring citizen safety.

In addition, visitors will be able to discover how rescuers are trained to face common emergencies. For example, the Ticino Red Cross has trained its operators to face medical emergencies, such as COVID-19 infections.

The event will be organized with stands set up throughout the city, making it easily accessible to all citizens of Chiasso and neighboring municipalities, such as Lugano, Mendrisio, and Como. Visitors will be able to discover how rescue services are organized in case of emergency and how they can contribute to ensuring citizen safety.

Mission Emergency: Chiasso Becomes 'Capital' of Rescue and Safety

Chiasso has transformed into a reality acceleration for a day, where law enforcement and rescue services have come together to present their activities and services to citizens. Among the participants are the Chiasso Municipal Police, the Cantonal Police, the TPO Transport Police, the Federal Customs Administration and Border Security (UDSC), and the Canton P Rescue Centre. These entities have set up stands throughout the city, where citizens can inform themselves and get a clearer idea of how law enforcement works and how it interacts with citizens.

"Security is a fundamental public good, and as law enforcement, it is our duty to protect it," says the head of the Chiasso Municipal Police. "This event is a unique opportunity to make our services known and to listen to the concerns of the community."

One concrete example of how law enforcement works together is the 'Border Security' project, launched in 2020 with a CHF 500,000.00 grant. This project has allowed for increased police presence along the Italian border and improved cooperation between law enforcement agencies. According to data provided by the authorities, in 2022, 12,500 border controls were carried out, with a 20% increase compared to 2021.

The Cantonal Police presented its 'Road Safety' project, which involves the installation of 20 new cameras in critical points of the Ticino road network. This project was funded with CHF 1,200,000.00 from the Ticino Road Safety Fund. ⚠️

Citizens can also inform themselves about the 'Medical Emergency' project, launched in 2019 with a CHF 750,000.00 grant. This project has allowed for increased presence of rescuers in case of medical emergency and improved cooperation between rescue services. According to data provided by the Canton P Rescue Centre, in 2022, 5,000 rescue interventions were carried out, with a 15% increase compared to 2021.

The TPO Transport Police presented its 'Transport Safety' project, which involves the installation of 10 new cameras in critical points of the Ticino road network. This project was funded with CHF 900,000.00 from the Ticino Transport Safety Fund. 📊

Citizens can also inform themselves about the 'Digital Security' project, launched in 2020 with a CHF 1,000,000.00 grant. This project has allowed for increased security of data and improved cooperation between law enforcement agencies and rescue services. According to data provided by the Ticino Cybersecurity Centre, in 2022, 2,500 cybersecurity interventions were carried out, with a 25% increase compared to 2021.

The event was organized with the support of the Chiasso Prefecture and the Lugano Province. "Security is a fundamental public good, and as authorities, it is our duty to protect it," says the Chiasso Prefect. "This event is a unique opportunity to make our services known and to listen to the concerns of the community."

The event was a success, with over 2,000 citizens participating in workshops and seminars. "It was a very important event for our community, which allowed us to make our services known and to listen to the concerns of the people," says the Chiasso Mayor. "We are already looking forward to organizing another similar event, to continue making our services known and improving public safety in our community."

This historical event is still a symbol of hope and rebirth for the population of the Ticino Canton, and the concept of rescue and safety has always been fundamental for the local community.The Work of First-Responder Entities

The first-responder entities of the Ticino Canton are responsible for managing emergencies and public safety. They are composed of various units, including the Cantonal Police, the Forest Corps, the Alpine Rescue Service, and the Medical Emergency Service.

Every year, the first-responder entities of the Ticino Canton respond to over 10,000 emergency calls, including road accidents, forest fires, domestic violence cases, and other crisis situations. According to the latest activity report, in 2022, the first-responder entities responded to 11,432 emergency calls, with an average response time of 3 minutes and 45 seconds.

The Importance of Rescue and Safety

Rescue and safety are fundamental for the local community. According to a research conducted by the Department of Police and Security of the Ticino Canton, 90% of the inhabitants of the Canton consider safety as one of the most important priorities for the community.

Emergency management is a complex process that requires a large amount of human and material resources. The first-responder entities of the Ticino Canton have an annual budget of CHF 120 million, which is used to purchase new vehicles and equipment, train personnel, and finance rescue and safety activities.Concrete Examples

Here are some concrete examples of how the first-responder entities of the Ticino Canton have responded to emergency situations:

  • On August 15, 2022, a violent storm hit the Bellinzona area, causing extensive damage and interrupting the supply of potable water for several hours. The first-responder entities of the Ticino Canton responded quickly, providing assistance to citizens and restoring the supply of potable water within 24 hours.
  • On October 20, 2022, a forest fire hit the Lugano area, causing extensive damage and putting the lives of several civilians at risk. The first-responder entities of the Ticino Canton responded immediately, providing assistance to civilians and extinguishing the fire within 4 hours.
