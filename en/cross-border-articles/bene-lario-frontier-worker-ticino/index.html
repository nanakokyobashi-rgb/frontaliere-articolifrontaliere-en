Bene Lario is among the preferred destinations for those who decide to work in Ticino while retaining their residence in Lombardy. The decision to move as a cross-border worker entails a series of economic and bureaucratic considerations revolving around the tax regime in force. The main development in recent years concerns the application of the New Cross-Border Workers Agreement, signed on December 23, 2020, and effective as of January 1, 2024, following Italian ratification through Law 83 of June 13, 2023.

The regulatory change represents a turning point for those considering moving to this area of the province of Como. The agreement redefines how tax is calculated on the pay of workers who reside in Italy but work in Switzerland. As for the transitional regime under Article 9 of the Agreement, it applies only to those who meet the treaty definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between December 31, 2018, and July 17, 2023; the covered employment income is taxed exclusively in Switzerland. In other cases, the ordinary regime applies, with taxation in Italy and a credit for Swiss tax. The 10.000 euro amount under Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is only the limit for the income referred to in Article 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013, not a general tax-free allowance. Withholding tax and double taxation

A crucial aspect that directly affects the payslip: withholding tax is deducted in Switzerland. Italy does not impose additional taxation; rather, it grants a credit for the taxes paid to the canton where one works. Operational details

Moving to Bene Lario: advantages and disadvantages

The choice to reside in Bene Lario while working in Ticino involves a series of aspects that go beyond pure fiscal convenience. The cross-border community already established in this area highlights some practical advantages related to geography and quality of life, along with challenges that need to be considered realistically.

From a housing perspective, Bene Lario offers a lower cost of living than the Canton of Ticino in various sectors: rent, purchase of properties, catering services and commerce. Those who choose to live in Italy and work in Switzerland capitalize on this difference, reinvesting the savings margin in the family budget or in long-term project capital (AVS/LPP pension savings, third pillar 3a).

Cross-border connections to Ticino are a crucial element. Bene Lario is located in a position that allows you to reach various passes and work destinations: the area can be reached from the main road routes towards the Italian border (A9, Lario state road). Travel times to the main Ticino work centers vary depending on the specific destination, the vehicle used and the traffic conditions at the crossings (Chiasso, Brogeda, Gaggiolo, Ponte Tresa). In the summer season and during peak periods, it is advisable to budget for an increase in transit times.

Considerations on commuting mobility

A cross-border worker in Bene Lario must choose whether to travel by private car or use public transport that connects the Como side to the Swiss border crossings. Both options have economic implications: the costs of petrol, vehicle wear, motorway tolls (A2, A9) and any parking in Ticino add up; on the other hand, public transport passes are a recurring expense but offer predictability and reduce the stress of daily driving.

Another element of evaluation is access to public and health services. Bene Lario has local medical infrastructures (GP, pharmacy, basic services) with Italian standards; for specialist services or significant emergencies, many cross-border commuters resort to the services of the Cantonal Hospital Authority (EOC) in Ticino, covered by KVG (the compulsory health insurance in Switzerland). Those who reside in Italy and work in Switzerland have the right to choose: they can choose to insure with an Italian provider (INPS + private coverage) or opt for the Swiss KVG, with different implications on premiums, deductibles and provider network.

Children's schooling introduces further variables. Many cross-border families manage school attendance in Italy but take advantage of some complementary services (nursery, after-school activities) that they find in the Ticino municipalities. The language (Italian in Italy, Italian/German in Ticino depending on the canton and municipality) is usually managed naturally, but requires a certain awareness in managing dual residency.

Key points

Procedure: how to start commuting from Bene Lario to Ticino

Anyone who decides to move to Italy as a resident and accept a job offer in Ticino must follow a sequence of administrative requirements. The critical points concern the work permit, Swiss tax registration, registration with social security bodies, health insurance.

To work in Switzerland, an Italian citizen needs a G Permit (border) or, alternatively, B Permit (residence with right to work). The G Permit is issued by the Canton where the work is carried out (e.g. Lugano, Mendrisio, Bellinzona depending on the company). The procedure requires a job offer signed by the company, the forwarding of the application via the employer to the cantonal migration office (SEM), and the issuing of the permit within an indicative period of a few weeks. The G Permit authorizes cross-border work while maintaining residence in Italy.

Tax registration and contributions

Once hired and in possession of the permit, the cross-border worker is automatically registered with the Swiss social security bodies. AVS (old age and survivors insurance) and AI (disability insurance) are mandatory: the deduction is equal to 5.3% of the employee's gross salary. AD (unemployment insurance) covers the risk of job loss with an additional deduction of 1.1% up to the annual capacity (CHF 148,200). LAINF (accident insurance) is paid by the employer and covers professional accidents. Finally, the LPP (professional pension insurance / second pillar) varies between 7% and 18% of the salary depending on the age group (obligatory from the age of 25) and the company pension plan.

The threshold of 10,000 euros of the art. 4 of Law 83/2023 concerns exclusively income from employment carried out abroad, in a border area or in a neighboring country, continuously and exclusively, by people resident in Italy, referred to in the art. 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013; it is not a general exemption nor an exemption from Swiss withholding tax. INPS and the Revenue Agency respectively manage the verification of Italian social security contributions (minimum integration) and the correct application of the 2024 Cross-Border Agreement.

Choice of health insurance

Health insurance is a decisive step. In Switzerland, enrollment in a KVG (Federal Law on Health Insurance) is mandatory: it is coverage with a deductible (from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 for adults) and monthly premiums that vary according to the company and the risk. Cross-border commuters have the right to choose: they can choose to remain registered with the Italian healthcare system (SSN + private supplementary insurance) or switch to the Swiss KVG. The choice affects access to services, delivery times, direct costs and the network of usable facilities.

To proceed effectively, it is advisable:

  • Request from the Swiss employer all the documentation necessary for registration with the federal bodies (declaration of employment, company data, name of the SUVA intermediary if present).
  • Open a current account with a bank that operates in both countries (CHF and EUR), to simplify the management of your pay slip and recurring payments.
  • Evaluate the convenience between Swiss KVG and Italian supplementary insurance with the support of an intermediary who knows both systems.
  • Check with the Municipality of residence in Italy for registration as a foreign resident and communication to the Guardia di Finanza to comply with the declaration obligations.

Moving as a cross-border worker from Bene Lario to Ticino is an economically advantageous choice for many, but requires careful planning regarding taxes, permits, social security and healthcare. Having clear documentation from the start and following the expected administrative timescales significantly reduces stress and costs during the transition.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the difference between Permit G and Permit B for those who work in Ticino?
The G Permit (border) allows work in Switzerland while maintaining residence in Italy, without the obligation to move. Permit B requires actual residence in Switzerland. Those who choose Bene Lario as a residence almost always opt for the G Permit, which has a faster procedure (a few weeks) and less housing bureaucracy.
Who qualifies for the transitional regime in Article 9?
Article 9’s transitional regime applies only to workers who meet the Agreement’s definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between 31 December 2018 and 17 July 2023; covered employment income is taxable exclusively in Switzerland. The ordinary regime applies in other cases, with Italian taxation and credit for Swiss tax where applicable. The €10,000 in Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is only the limit for income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law 147/2013, not a general allowance. Should you choose the Swiss LAMal or stay in the Italian SSN?
It depends on health habits and budget estimates. The Swiss LAMal offers fast performance and high quality but has fixed monthly costs and deductibles. The Italian NHS is free but requires private supplementary insurance for non-covered benefits. Many border workers choose LAMal for direct access to the Ticino EoC, especially if they work close to the border.
What are the fixed monthly costs of a border crossing in Bene Lario?
They vary according to the means of transport (private car vs. public transit), the LAMal chosen, and the living expenses in Italy. What happens if you change jobs or move back to Switzerland?
If you stop work in Ticino, Permit G automatically ceases; you have 14 days to leave the Canton or convert it to Permit B if you find new work. If you decide to move to Switzerland, apply for Permit B at your new canton of residence. The accumulated AVS/LPP contributions remain credited and flow into your social security pillar.
