Como Swimming, 750k euros in court: minority report (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 5 May 2026

Minority councilors of the Como municipality have filed a report with the Prosecutor's Office for the management of 750,000 euros for the Geno Avenue swimming pool.

Context

Briefly

  • 750,000 euro allocation for Como Nuoto
  • Minority exposes to the Prosecutor's Office
  • Dispute over maintenance and funding

Key Facts

  • What: Municipal allocation for swimming pool renovation
  • When: May 2021 (delivery), December 2022 (resolution)
  • Where: Como, Italy
  • Who: Como Municipality, ASD Como Nuoto
  • Amount: 750,000 euros (250,000 regional, 500,000 municipal)

The political battle over the swimming pool in viale Geno in Como has reached the courts. Minority councilors of the Municipality have filed a detailed expose with the Prosecutor's Office, asking for clarifications on the management of the concession to ASD Como Nuoto. At the center of the report is a public funding of 750,000 euros for renovation work, which the signatories believe the Municipality should not have allocated. The amount is divided into 250,000 euros of regional contributions and 500,000 euros of municipal funds.

The signing councilors

The councilors involved are Lorenzo Cantaluppi (FdI), Alessandro Falanga (FI, then resigned), Stefano Fanetti (Pd), Eleonora Galli (Pd), Stefano Legnani (Pd), Patrizia Lissi (Pd), Barbara Minghetti (Svolta Civica), Giordano Molteni (Fi), Elena Negretti (Lega), Vittorio Nessi (Svolta Civica), Paola Tocchetti (Fi), Antonio Tufano (FdI), Luca Vozella (Svolta Civica). The signatures of the current councilors Davide Gervasoni (Fi) and Paola Ceriello, who joined Forza Italia after the drafting of the expose, do not appear.

Operational details

Analysis of the Implications

The case of the viale Geno swimming pool in Como has several implications for cross-border workers living and working in the area. First, the management of public funds and the transparency of administrative procedures are crucial aspects that concern all citizens, including cross-border workers. If the funds allocated for the maintenance of municipal facilities are used controversially, there could be a negative impact on the quality and availability of sports services.

Comparison with the Previous Situation

Before the concession to Como Nuoto, the viale Geno swimming pool was managed directly by the Municipality. The new private management was supposed to guarantee greater efficiency and better maintenance of the facilities. However, the report of 'serious deterioration' after just fifteen months from the signing of the contract raises doubts about the association's ability to adequately manage the structure.

Future Scenarios

If the Prosecutor's Office were to accept the complaint from the minorities, it could open an investigation into the management of public funds by the Municipality of Como. This could lead to changes in the management of municipal sports facilities and greater attention to the transparency of administrative procedures. For cross-border workers, this could mean greater security in the quality of the services offered and greater trust in local institutions.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the details of the 750,000 euro allocation?
The allocation consists of 250,000 euros in regional contributions and 500,000 euros in municipal funds, destined for the renovation of the Geno Avenue swimming pool in Como.
Who filed the report with the Prosecutor's Office?
The report was filed by the minority councilors of the Municipality of Como, including Lorenzo Cantaluppi, Stefano Fanetti, Eleonora Galli, Stefano Legnani, Patrizia Lissi, Barbara Minghetti, Giordano Molteni, Elena Negretti, Vittorio Nessi, Paola Tocchetti, Antonio Tufano, and Luca Vozella.
What are the main criticisms raised in the report?
The main criticisms concern the management of public funds and the failure to fulfill tender obligations by Como Nuoto, which was supposed to carry out the maintenance work at its own expense.
