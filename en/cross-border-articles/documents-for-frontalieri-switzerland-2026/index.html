Documents needed to start working in Switzerland: full checklist (cross-border guide)

Discover all the documents you need to start working in Switzerland as a cross-border worker: G permit, contract, insurance and much more.

To start working in Switzerland as a cross-border worker, it is essential to have all the documents in order. The first step is to obtain the G permit, which allows you to work in Canton Ticino. This permit is essential to sign a work contract with a Swiss company. In addition to the G permit, you need a signed work contract that specifies the employment conditions and salary. Another crucial document is health insurance, mandatory for all workers in Switzerland. It is important to choose an insurance that covers medical expenses and is recognized by the Swiss authorities. Furthermore, cross-border workers must register with the AVS, the Swiss pension system, and open a bank account in Switzerland to receive their salary. These steps are fundamental to ensure a smooth and legal transition into the Swiss job market.

Main documents

Among the main documents required are the G permit, the work contract, health insurance, and AVS registration. The G permit is issued by the Swiss authorities and allows you to work in Canton Ticino. The work contract must be signed by both parties and must include all employment conditions, such as working hours, salary, and benefits. Health insurance is mandatory and must be taken out before starting work. AVS registration is necessary to ensure pension coverage and must be done within one month of starting work.

Practical Analysis

Working in Switzerland as a cross-border worker involves several practical implications that are important to know. For example, the G permit allows you to work only in the Canton of Ticino, so it is important to ensure that you have the correct permit for the workplace. Furthermore, the employment contract must be signed by both parties and must include all terms of employment. It is important to read the contract carefully and ensure that you understand all the clauses before signing it.

Health Insurance

Health insurance is mandatory for all workers in Switzerland and must be taken out within one month of starting work. It is important to choose an insurance that covers medical expenses and is recognized by the Swiss authorities. There are several insurance options available, so it is advisable to compare the offers and choose the one that best suits your needs.

AVS Registration

Registration with the AVS is necessary to ensure pension coverage and must be done within one month of starting work. The AVS is the Swiss pension system and guarantees an old-age, disability, and survivors' pension. It is important to register with the AVS to ensure pension coverage and to avoid legal problems.

Opening a Bank Account

Opening a bank account in Switzerland is advisable to facilitate the payment of the salary. It is possible to open a bank account at any time, but it is advisable to do so before starting work. There are several banks in Switzerland that offer accounts for cross-border workers, so it is advisable to compare the offers and choose the one that best suits your needs.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the main documents required to start working in Switzerland?
The main documents required to start working in Switzerland as a cross-border worker are the G permit, the work contract, health insurance, and registration with AHV. These documents are essential to ensure a smooth and legal transition into the Swiss job market.
How do you obtain the G permit?
The G permit can be requested at the competent office of the Canton of Ticino. It is important to submit all the required documents and follow the procedures indicated by the Swiss authorities. Once the G permit is obtained, it is possible to sign a work contract with a Swiss company.
What are the tax implications for cross-border workers in Switzerland?
Cross-border workers in Switzerland are subject to withholding tax, which is a form of direct taxation. It is important to know the tax rates and the deductions available to reduce the tax burden. Additionally, it is important to know the rules for filing tax returns and ensure that the deadlines are respected.
