The Meloni Government has passed a budget law that modifies the cost-sharing contribution paid by cross-border workers to the National Health Service, the health tax they are required to pay. The text is now set for parliamentary debate. The change concerns the payment intended to ensure greater funding for healthcare in border areas.

The change in the budget text

Article 70 of the new Italian budget law provides, starting in 2027, for a switch to a fixed monthly amount between 30 and 150 euros. The amount will not be the same for everyone: the text indicates that the contribution will be determined each year by the region of residence. The source also refers to the individual regions concerned as the entities responsible for deciding the specific amount.

In the official paragraph concerning the salaries of former cross-border workers, prior to 17 July 2023, the effective date indicated is therefore 2027. For dependents, an additional maximum amount of 50 euros is provided. This concerns the new cost-sharing contribution to the SSN, not a payment described as a percentage of salary.

The proposal thus changes the criterion used to present the healthcare contribution for cross-border workers. The source does not indicate a single nationwide amount: it leaves the actual amount to the region of residence and sets only the minimum and maximum monthly limits.

Operational details

From proportional calculation to fixed fee

The comparison between the mechanism described so far and the one included in the budget text concerns both the calculation basis and the monthly cap. Before the change, the payment was linked to the monthly salary: the source indicates a percentage between 3% and 6%, with a minimum of 30 euro and a maximum of 200 euro for each month. The 3% and 6% are therefore the two endpoints of the specified percentage, while 30 and 200 euro are the monetary limits.

Key points

Effective date and penalties in the draft

The OCST, identified by the source as the most representative Swiss trade union, pointed out a positive element in the text: the provision would no longer be retroactive to 2024. According to this interpretation, collection of the contribution would begin in 2027, the same effective date reported in the paragraph on the fixed amount. The conditional verb reflects the status of the measure, which is still scheduled for parliamentary discussion.

In the event of non-payment, the draft provides for an administrative penalty equal to 100% of the unpaid contribution. The source does not indicate any other penalties, interest, calculation methods or recovery procedures; these elements cannot be inferred from the text provided.

The AFC information sheet, listed among the official references for cross-border workers' taxation, concerns withholding tax on employment income, which is withheld in Switzerland. For new cross-border workers, Switzerland levies up to 80% and Italy taxes the income, with a tax credit to eliminate double taxation; former cross-border workers are taxed only in Switzerland. This tax information does not add any figures to the contribution to the SSN described by the source.

What the text does not quantify

It does not address rebates or the New Cross-Border Workers Agreement either. For official documentation on the SSN contribution, the reference indicated is the Ministry of Health Decree 11/14/2025.
