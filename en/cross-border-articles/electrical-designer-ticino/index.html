The new Cross-border Workers Agreement was signed on 23 December 2020, ratified in Italy with Law 83 of 13 June 2023 and is in force from 1 January 2024. For a cross-border worker who resides in Italy and works as an electrical designer in the Canton of Ticino, the sequence distinguishes three moments: signature, ratification and application. The operational date indicated by the source is January 1, 2024, not 2026.

The basic tax rule concerns withholding tax: the withholding of the income from work of cross-border workers occurs only in Switzerland. Italy avoids double taxation with the tax credit reported in the EC framework of 730. To read the pay slip, therefore, the Swiss withholding tax and the Italian credit are two elements to be kept separate; the source does not describe a full levy on the same income in both countries.

The dates that affect the deductible

The distinction that changes the threshold is temporal. Those who were already such before 17 July 2023 are old cross-border workers: they are entitled to an exemption of €7,500, with a transitional regime from 2024 to 2033. New cross-border workers instead have an exemption of €10,000. The source links the threshold to the temporal category of the worker, not to the profession of electrical designer.

In the cross-border case, permit G also comes in. The geographical reference is the Canton of Ticino; Brogeda is among the crossings mentioned for the connection with Italy, but the source does not associate traffic data or different tax rules with the crossing. Permit, place of work and taxation remain distinct profiles: the first is administrative, the second follows the Swiss withholding and the Italian credit.

The Italy-Switzerland Convention against double taxation was signed on 9 March 1976. Switzerland is not a member of the EU or the EEA. This reference delimits the comparison between the Swiss and Italian systems without attributing to the profession a tax discipline different from that documented.

Swiss contributions and LAMal

AVS/AI/IPG is listed at 5,3% for the employee. AD/AC is 1,1% with a CHF 148'200 cap. LAINF is 0,7–1,5%; LPP ranges from 7% to 18% by age bracket from age 25. The source does not present these items as a single tax rate.

LAMal is health insurance, not a tax on health. G cross-border workers have the right to choose; for adults, the deductible ranges from CHF 300 to CHF 2'500. It should not be confused with the €10'000 tax allowance for new cross-border workers: the former is in francs and concerns LAMal, while the latter is in euros and concerns the tax treatment of the category.

Entities that should not be confused

Withholding tax rates and federal and cantonal tax rates are established by federal or cantonal laws. At federal level, administration is the responsibility of AFC/ESTV, the Federal Tax Administration; at cantonal level, the cantonal tax administrations are involved. By contrast, the source excludes UFAS and BFS from setting the rates: UFAS concerns social security, including AVS/AI, while BFS performs a statistical function and collects data.

The distinction is useful when looking for a reference for the payslip. A social security institution should not be presented as a tax authority, and a statistical office should not be used as a source of tax rates. The role of AFC/ESTV concerns federal tax administration; the cantonal side remains linked to the canton’s tax administrations.

In the context of Ticino-Italy relations, tax rebates are also mentioned, but the source does not provide amounts, percentages or criteria for including them in the individual calculation of income. Similarly, the G permit is mentioned as a reference for cross-border work, without a detailed procedure in the available material. These limitations prevent the addition of undocumented administrative steps or salary figures.
