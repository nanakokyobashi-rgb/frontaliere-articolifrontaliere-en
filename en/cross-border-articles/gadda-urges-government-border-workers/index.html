The issue of Italian cross-border workers in Switzerland has returned to the center of political attention. The Honorable Maria Chiara Gadda, vice-president of the deputies of Italia Viva, presented a parliamentary question to the ministers of Economy, Foreign Affairs and Regional Affairs to ask for the immediate convening of the inter-ministerial table on cross-border workers. This table, established a year ago, has no longer met after the inaugural session and Gadda believes that it is essential to resume its work to address the various critical issues affecting Italian workers in Switzerland.

According to what was reported by the trade unions and confirmed by numerous reports from workers, the tax authority of the Canton of Ticino is apparently disregarding the application of the rules. Gadda stresses that urgent action is needed to resolve these issues and ensure that cross-border workers are protected.

The problem of cross-border workers is not limited only to taxation. Health tax and refunds are also crucial issues that need to be addressed. Cross-border workers often find themselves in complex situations, working in Switzerland and living in Italy, which brings with it a number of bureaucratic and fiscal challenges.

The so-called health tax is the share of participation in the National Health Service provided for by law 213/2023 (art. 1, paragraphs 237-239): from 3% to 6% of the net salary received in Switzerland, with a minimum of 30 and a maximum of 200 euros for each month worked, defined each year by the Region of residence.

Law 213/2023, art. 1, paragraphs 237–238, does not subject all cross-border workers without distinction. This includes: (a) residents of Italy who work and stay in Switzerland and use the SSN; (b) the cross-border workers referred to in art. 9, par. 1, of the Italy-Switzerland Agreement, with taxable incomes only in Switzerland, who have exercised the right of option for health insurance; and (c) dependent family members of the two categories. Paragraph 238 provides for a progressive quota of 3–6% of the Swiss net salary, with a minimum of €30 and an ordinary maximum of €200 for each month worked from 2024. The ceiling can be doubled in cases of non-payment or non-communication provided for by the art. 1, paragraph 100, Law 207/2024. The Region of residence determines the rates and methods every year.
