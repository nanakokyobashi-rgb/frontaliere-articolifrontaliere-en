Chiasso-Pasture asylum centers: 40 places at risk in 2027 (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 26 September 2026

From 1° gennaio 2027, the federal mandate passes from AOZ to ORS Service; the VPOD and OCST unions warn of up to 40 layoffs in Chiasso and Pasture among 120 AOZ Ticino employees.

Forty layoffs are the worst-case scenario indicated by the VPOD union for the reorganization of the Federal Asylum Center in Pasture and Chiasso. The change already defined concerns the federal mandate: as of 1° gennaio 2027, it will move from AOZ to ORS Service.

The meeting with the staff

The prospect worries the unions, which organized an extraordinary meeting with AOZ Ticino staff. There are 120 employees. The meeting is intended to discuss the changes within the facilities with them and the possible consequences for employment.

The co-secretary of VPOD Ticino, Edoardo Cappelletti, links the possibility of the 40 layoffs to the Chiasso Center not reopening. According to the union, ORS will not be able to absorb all of AOZ's employees, and a reduction in employment has already been suggested. If Chiasso were not to reopen, VPOD's estimate is that around forty employees would be left without work. Cappelletti describes the prospect as burdensome, serious and irresponsible, especially for the Mendrisio and Chiasso region.

«It has already been suggested that ORS will not be able to absorb all of AOZ's employees», says Cappelletti.

OCST is also supporting the cause. Regional deputy secretary Nenad Jovanovic explains that the consultation aims first and foremost to minimize the number of people affected by a possible loss of employment: the proposed solution is redeployment with the new employer or in other areas through the procedure. If layoffs were to occur, OCST is calling for a serious social plan to support people in their search for new employment.

The discussion also concerns the contract. AOZ employees are currently covered by a collective labor agreement. With the change at the top, this certainty disappears: according to Cappelletti, ORS does not intend to take over the agreement signed by VPOD. The union therefore fears a deterioration in working conditions and the dismantling of the rights obtained through the staff commission.

Operational details

For those following cross-border work, the directly documented point concerns employment. The news concerns jobs at two facilities in Ticino and a transition of the mandate from AOZ to ORS Service. For a cross-border worker who was among the personnel involved, the issue would therefore be continuity of employment and working conditions, not effects on other areas that the source does not mention.

The transition therefore has two levels. The first is quantitative: how many jobs remain after the change of operator. The second is qualitative: under which contract and working conditions the work continues. Even redeployment, in the logic set out by OCST, does not eliminate the question of the conditions applied by the new employer. Likewise, job loss is not presented as an outcome already decided: it is the scenario that the unions want to contain through consultation, redeployment and a social plan.

Chiasso remains the turning point. VPOD links the failure to reopen to the possibility of around forty people being left without work. The source does not, however, present a definitive outcome regarding the reopening; therefore, the estimate cannot be turned into a dismissal that has already been decided. For those who have to assess their own position, the difference between a hypothesis, consultation and a decision is essential.

There is also the issue of service quality, mentioned alongside the defense of employment and working conditions. Cappelletti challenges the criterion by which the Federal Council awarded the public contract: according to the union, the lowest price was not accompanied by clear and binding criteria on employment continuity and working conditions. The debate therefore concerns both the continuity of jobs and the way in which the service will be organized.

Those seeking new employment can meanwhile consult the annunci di lavoro, without considering this tool as an outcome already imposed by the reorganization.

Key points

The operational phase indicated by the source is staff consultation. The tools mentioned are the extraordinary assembly, the dialogue with trade unions, and the resolution that the Staff Assembly is about to deliberate. For those involved, the sequence can be read as follows.

The steps indicated by the consultation

1. Follow the extraordinary assembly organized with the AOZ Ticino staff. This is the venue designated for discussing the reorganization within the facilities.

2. Bring the issue of reassignment into the consultation, either to the new employer or in other sectors, as indicated by OCST. The stated objective is to minimize the number of people affected by job loss.

3. If dismissal is put on the table, request that a serious social plan be evaluated. OCST identifies this as a support tool for those who will have to seek new employment.

4. Follow the approval of the Staff Assembly's resolution. The topics indicated are the defense of employment, working conditions, and service quality.

5. Verify the addressees to whom the resolution will be directed: the AOZ Board of Directors, the State Secretariat for Migration, the State Council, and the Ticino delegation to the Federal Assembly.

For a cross-border worker who may be involved, these are the steps directly linked to the source: participating in the on-site dialogue, following any reassignment, and verifying how the collective agreement is being addressed. It is not possible to derive from the text an individual deadline other than January 1, 2027, nor to attribute an outcome to the 40 dismissals at this stage.

Monitoring should therefore focus on the decisions emerging from the consultation and the resolution, not on scenarios presented as concluded. If job loss becomes a reality, reassignment and the social plan are the two points indicated by the source to support the search for new employment. Frequently Asked Questions
What is the planned change in the federal mandate of the Chiasso and Pasture centers?
The federal mandate will transition from AOZ to ORS Service as of January 1, 2027, as stated in the source. This change of manager underlies the employment concerns expressed by the unions.
How many jobs are at risk according to the unions and on how many employees is this estimate based?
The VPOD and OCST unions report the worst-case scenario of 40 layoffs. The estimate refers to the 120 employees of AOZ Ticino employed in the Chiasso and Pasture centers.
What actions do unions ask for to avoid or manage any job losses?
The unions ask to follow the extraordinary meeting with the staff, to promote relocation to the new employer or other areas, and, if necessary, to obtain a serious social plan to support the search for new employment.
