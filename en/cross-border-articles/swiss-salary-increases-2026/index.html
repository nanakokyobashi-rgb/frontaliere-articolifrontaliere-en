Impact on cross-border workers

For cross-border workers who live in Italy and work in Switzerland, these increases represent an opportunity to improve their purchasing power. However, it is important to consider the tax and pension implications as well.

Cross-border workers must take into account the rules on double taxation and the specific rules for withholding tax payments. For example, a worker residing in Varese who earns 70,000 CHF annually in Switzerland could see a 5% increase in 2026, bringing their salary to 73,500 CHF. However, they will have to pay a withholding tax rate that can vary from 15% to 25% depending on the municipality of residence in Ticino, such as Lugano or Mendrisio.

Before these increases, salaries in Switzerland were already among the highest in Europe, but the difference with Italian wages remained significant. With the latest increases, the disparity is further reduced, offering cross-border workers more opportunities for savings and investment.

However, it is crucial to monitor the conditions of the new 2026 tax agreement, which could introduce changes in rates and tax methods. Cross-border workers must be ready to adapt to these new rules to maximize the benefits of salary increases.

What to Do Concretely

For cross-border workers who want to take advantage of salary increases, it is essential to follow some concrete steps, taking into account the specifics of Ticino and Swiss regulations.

Step 1: Review Your Tax Return

Cross-border workers should review their tax return to optimize taxation. For example, a cross-border worker residing in Mendrisio with an annual salary of 80,000 CHF could reduce the tax burden by taking advantage of deductions for medical expenses, pension contributions, and transportation costs. Deductions for medical expenses can amount to up to 1,500 CHF, while those for pension contributions can be fully deducted.

Step 2: Consult a Financial Advisor

It is advisable to consult a financial advisor to best plan your financial future. An expert can help identify the best investment and savings opportunities. For example, an advisor may suggest investing in third-pillar pension funds (3a) that offer tax advantages. For a cross-border worker with a salary of 90,000 CHF, contributing to the third pillar can reduce the taxable income by approximately 6,883 CHF annually.

Step 3: Monitor New Tax Rules

Cross-border workers must monitor the new tax rules introduced by the new 2026 tax agreement. It is important to be ready to adapt to the new tax rates and methods to maximize the benefits of salary increases. For example, the 2026 tax agreement could introduce new progressive rates that could significantly influence the tax burden. A cross-border worker residing in Lugano with a salary of 100,000 CHF could see a reduction in the tax burden of 5-10% thanks to these new regulations.

Step 4: Plan Pension Contributions

Cross-border workers must ensure they pay the correct AVS and LPP contributions to guarantee an adequate pension. It is possible to consult an expert to best plan pension contributions. For example, a cross-border worker with a salary of 75,000 CHF must pay AVS contributions equal to 8.4% of the annual salary, with a maximum cap of 39,060 CHF. For the second pillar (LPP), the contribution depends on the employer, but it is on average around 10-15% of the salary.

Step 5: Take Advantage of Investment Opportunities

With higher salaries, cross-border workers can consider new investment and savings opportunities. It is advisable to diversify investments to reduce risks and maximize returns. For example, a cross-border worker with a salary of 85,000 CHF could consider investing in real estate in Ticino, where the average price for an apartment is around 600,000 CHF, offering potential returns of 3-4% annually.
