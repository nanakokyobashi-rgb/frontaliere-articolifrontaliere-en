Mountain rescue, exercise at Sasso Malascarpa

Twenty-three rescuers from the Lariano Triangle Station of the Alpine and Speleological Rescue, XIX Lariana Delegation, participated in a technical-health exercise in recent days. The indicated composition included three technical and three medical instructors, including one instructor. The report thus presents, in the same activity, a technical and a health component.

The program was divided into two technical-health scenarios. The work was dedicated to the use of extrication devices, including the NEXT device, intended to recover and transfer injured people in harsh environments and complex operational contexts. The activity also included the in-depth analysis of immobilization systems.

I work on the channel

Part of the exercise took place on the ground. The technicians traveled along an impervious canal at Sasso Malascarpa using the sedan stretcher technique. It is the step that connects the use of the equipment to the practice of the rescue maneuvers described in the report.

The source indicates periodic exercises as the tool for maintaining and updating the technical and health skills of rescuers. The technicians who have recently joined the Corps can thus gain new experience in using the equipment and carrying out rescue manoeuvres, working alongside more experienced colleagues. This collaboration is part of the training described in the news.

Skills maintained in the field

The exercise sequence

Four steps described by the source

To reconstruct the activity without adding external elements, the sequence can be read as follows:

1. Stazione Triangolo Lariano organizes an exercise consisting of two technical-medical scenarios. 2. Rescuers work on using extrication equipment, including the NEXT device. 3. The activity also explores immobilization systems. 4. At Sasso Malascarpa, technicians cross a rugged channel using the rescue stretcher. 5. Technicians who have recently joined the Corps are paired with more experienced colleagues.

These steps bring together the operational information contained in the source. The NEXT device is used to recover and transfer injured people in rugged environments and complex circumstances; the rescue stretcher, on the other hand, is the technique used during the route through the channel. The exercise therefore includes both an in-depth review of the equipment and practice in the field.

The final step concerns the transmission of experience. New technicians have the opportunity to learn how to use the equipment and practice rescue maneuvers alongside more experienced colleagues. The source connects this dynamic to periodic exercises, which make it possible to maintain and update technical and medical skills.
