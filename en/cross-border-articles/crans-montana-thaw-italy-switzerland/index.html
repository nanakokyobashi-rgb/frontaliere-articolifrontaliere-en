Crans-Montana, thaw between Italy and Switzerland (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 5 May 2026

After months of tension, Ambassador Cornado softens the tone. Gianini: some statements have been counterproductive.

Context

In brief

  • Italian ambassador tones down rhetoric after Crans-Montana tragedy
  • Cornado: 'Among friends, it's right to say everything, with loyalty'
  • Gianini criticizes some Italian positions

Key facts

  • What: Dialogue between Italy and Switzerland after Crans-Montana tragedy
  • When: 4 May 2026
  • Where: Crans-Montana, Switzerland
  • Who: Ambassador Gian Lorenzo Cornado, National Councillor Simone Gianini
  • Toll: 41 victims, over 100 injured

On Monday evening, during the RSI's 60 Minuti show, the Italian ambassador to Berna, Gian Lorenzo Cornado, toned down the recent tensions between Italy and Switzerland following the Crans-Montana tragedy. Among the guests of the show hosted by Reto Ceschi, Cornado recalled how institutional dialogue has never ceased, even after months marked by polemics, misunderstandings, and very harsh positions taken by Italy. At the center, once again, the consequences of the fire that occurred at the bar Le Constellation, with 41 people losing their lives and more than a hundred injured.

According to Cornado, the meeting between the Confederation's president Guy Parmelin and the Council's president Giorgia Meloni represented a turning point compared to the polemics of the past weeks. A political step that, in the words of the diplomat, could mark the beginning of a different phase in the relations between Rome and Berna, beyond the dossiers and bilateral agreements that have continued their course, as he himself explained, without interruptions.

Cornado's statements

The Italian diplomat first toned down the rhetoric regarding the possible investigation into the rescue operations during the night of the incident. 'These were exceptional circumstances,' he said, recalling the magnitude of the drama. It is right, he added, 'to investigate and clarify,' also because there are injured people whose conditions would have worsened due to the wait before hospitalization. But he specified that 'no one will ever make polemics on this point.' The same attitude was maintained on the judicial front. Italy has requested to be able to act as a private accuser in the Vaud penal proceedings, but the ambassador ruled out that this initiative should be read as an act of distrust towards the Confederation.

Gianini's criticism

Of a completely different tone, although welcoming the more conciliatory words of the ambassador, was the intervention of the Ticinese national councillor of the PLR Simone Gianini. In his opinion, some Italian positions have had counterproductive effects. Gianini denounced the risk of fueling anti-Swiss sentiments in Italy and, in reaction, anti-Italian sentiments in Switzerland, particularly in the border regions. The parliamentarian criticized above all the climate created by some Italian talk shows, in which — he sustained — the responsibility for the tragedy would have been attributed to Switzerland as a whole, almost involving the population as well.

The medical bills

Another sensitive point is that of the bills sent to Italian patients hospitalized in Switzerland. Parmelin, recalled Cornado, reiterated that no more bills will be sent to the Italian patients involved in the tragedy and assured that no family will be asked to pay. The president of the Confederation will propose a solution to the Federal Council within the framework of the current legislation. A position that, according to the ambassador, fully satisfied the president Meloni.
