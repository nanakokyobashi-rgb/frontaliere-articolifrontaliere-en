  • What: Early AVS retirement possible up to 2 years before the reference age
  • When: AVS 21 reform in force since 2024, reference age 65 years
  • Where: Canton Ticino, for cross-border workers with a G permit
  • Who: AVS (Swiss first pillar), LPP (second pillar), INPS (Italy)
  • How it works: Withholding tax deducted in Switzerland; Italy applies a tax credit

  • Legal reference: New Agreement signed on December 23, 2020, ratified by Law 83/2023

The reference age for the AVS pension is set at 65 years for men and women from 2024, following the AVS 21 reform. Those who wish to retire early may do so up to two years earlier, accepting a permanent reduction in the pension. For a cross-border worker with a family and children, the decision to retire early has direct effects on the LPP second pillar and coordination with the Italian INPS.

The AVS 21 reform also introduced a flexibility mechanism: the pension can be deferred beyond age 65, obtaining a supplement calculated for each year of deferral. Early retirement, by contrast, entails a penalty calculated based on the reduction rate provided for by federal law. For households with children, planning becomes central: the timing of retirement determines replacement income and the remaining family burden.

The legal framework for cross-border workers

The New Agreement between Switzerland and Italy on cross-border workers, signed on December 23, 2020 and in force since January 1, 2024 following ratification by Italy under Law 83 of June 13, 2023, governs the taxation of workers with a G permit. Withholding tax is deducted in Switzerland, while Italy avoids double taxation through the tax credit mechanism in Section CE of Form 730. The transitional regime under Article 9 of the Agreement applies only to those who meet the treaty definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between December 31, 2018 and July 17, 2023; covered employment income is taxed exclusively in Switzerland. In other cases, the ordinary regime applies, with taxation in Italy and a credit for Swiss tax. The 10.000 euro amount under Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is merely the threshold for the income referred to in Article 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013, not a general allowance. Operational details

Bringing your AVS pension forward to 63 or 64 means accepting a permanent reduction in your first pillar pension. The penalty is more pronounced the further away you are from the reference age of 65, and is also reflected in the amount of the LPP pension, which is calculated on the retirement assets accumulated at the time of leaving. For a family with children still dependent or undergoing university studies, the economic picture changes significantly: early retirement reduces gross income but frees up time to dedicate to the family.

Scenario comparison: bring forward or postpone

Considering the current Italian IRPEF rates — 23% up to €28,000, 33% from €28,001 to €50,000, 43% over €50,000 — a cross-border worker who pays his pension early could find himself in a lower bracket, with a theoretical tax saving. However, the advance annuity is lower by law, and the replacement income (Swiss AVS annuity) will be taxed at source in Switzerland with subsequent tax credit in Italy. A comparison between scenarios, even hypothetical, shows that postponing one or two years can significantly increase the overall AVS plus LPP income, useful for covering family expenses in the long term.

Coordination between the Swiss AVS and the Italian INPS depends on the worker's contribution history. Those who have contributed in both countries can accumulate the insurance periods, while the amount of the benefit is calculated pro rata. For children, the Swiss AVS pension does not provide supplementary family allowances other than the LPP ones; any Italian family benefits follow INPS regulations. The tax burden, between Swiss withholding tax and Italian tax credit, must be carefully evaluated on a case-by-case basis, because the pension advance modifies both the taxable income and the deductions due for dependent family members.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the AVS reference age for frontier workers from 2024?
Since 2024, with the AVS 21 reform, the reference age has been unified to 65 years for men and women. You can advance your annuity up to two years in advance by accepting a permanent reduction calculated at federal rates. Border workers with a G permit who have also contributed in Italy can request a pro rata benefit from INPS.
How does the tax regime for retired frontier workers work?
Tax at source is withheld exclusively in Switzerland on labour income and AVS income. Italy avoids double taxation through the EC 730 tax credit. What Swiss contributions affect future income?
The employee pays AVS/AI/IPG at 5.3%, AD/AC at 1.1% up to the cap of CHF 148'200, LAINF between 0.7% and 1.5%, and LPP between 7% and 18% by age group from 25 years. These contributions feed into the first and second pillars and determine the amount of the retirement pension.
Is it convenient to advance the pension with dependent children?
It depends on the family situation. Advancing the pension reduces the AVS and LPP annuity but can lower the Italian personal income tax bracket (23% up to €28,000). For households with children still in training, the theoretical tax savings must be compared with the permanent loss of income. A case-by-case simulation is recommended.
