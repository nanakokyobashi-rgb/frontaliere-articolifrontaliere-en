Switzerland, temporary work restarts: what effects for border workers (cross-border guide)

The Swiss labour market registers a sign of recovery in the temporary work sector, with an increase in temporary working hours of 1.5% in the first half of 2026.

Key facts

  • What: Trend in the temporary labour market.
  • When: First semester 2026.
  • Where: Swiss Confederation.
  • Who: Swissstaffing.
  • Amount: +1.5% temporary working hours.

In the first half of 2026, the Swiss labour market registered a recovery signal in the temporary work sector, interrupting a contraction phase that had lasted for three years. According to data released by Swissstaffing, the association that represents the main employment agencies of the Confederation, the hours worked through the interim channel grew by 1.5% compared to the same period of the previous year. This figure marks a significant turnaround after a long period of stagnation, although companies are approaching new hires with marked caution, avoiding excessive momentum.

Economic uncertainty holds back steady hiring

The general economic framework remains characterised by numerous unknowns. International geopolitical tensions, together with the tariff policies adopted by the US administration and the slowdown in economic growth recorded in recent years, continue to negatively influence business decisions. Operational details

The manufacturing sector remains the main driver for the demand for temporary workers. In the first months of 2026, industrial production showed signs of improvement, with an increase in the demand for temporary workers that is directly reflected in the increase in hours worked in the sector. This positive trend also represents an important variable for the cross-border workforce, historically integrated into sectors such as the mechanical industry, logistics, construction and manufacturing.

Digitization and recruitment of personnel

The recruitment process is undergoing a radical technology-driven transformation. A survey conducted by Swissstaffing reveals that 79% of employment agencies now integrate artificial intelligence tools to manage selection processes. The adoption of these technologies responds to a contingent operational need: due to a slower labour market and a slightly higher unemployment rate than in the past, the volume of applications for each vacant position has grown significantly, reaching more than one hundred applications per single position. Artificial intelligence allows agencies to analyze resumes more efficiently, speeding up profile screening and optimizing available resources. Key points

For those who live or work as border workers, the return to growth in temporary work constitutes an encouraging signal, although it does not yet represent a definitive turnaround for the entire labor market. The temporary work agencies operating in the Canton of Ticino and the rest of the Confederation have historically been one of the main entry points for workers residing in Italy. Their importance is crucial in sectors such as logistics, transportation, and business services, where the flexibility required by companies matches the availability of qualified manpower from border provinces. The ability to intercept these opportunities requires a proactive approach, keeping in mind that the demand for temporary personnel can translate into new opportunities for integration for those who know how to present themselves with the right technical skills.

Frequently Asked Questions
How is the temporary labour market in Switzerland doing in 2026?
In the first half of 2026, the Swiss temporary labour market showed a recovery, with a 1.5% increase in temporary hours worked compared to the previous year. This data, provided by Swissstaffing, interrupts a contraction lasting three years, indicating a cautious signal to restart after a period of stasis.
Why has open-ended recruitment declined in Switzerland?
Permanent recruitment through agencies fell by 19.4% in the first half of 2026 due to economic uncertainty. Swiss companies prefer the flexibility of temporary work to manage costs and quickly adjust staff, postponing commitments related to fixed contracts in a climate of geopolitical tensions and slowing growth.
How does artificial intelligence affect recruitment in Switzerland?
79% of Swiss employment agencies use artificial intelligence for candidate screening. This is due to the increase in applications (over one hundred per seat) in a slower market. AI speeds up the analysis of CVs, making a careful presentation of the documentation crucial to pass the first phase of automated skimming.
What sectors are driving the demand for temporary workers in Switzerland?
The manufacturing sector is the main driver for the demand for temporary workers in Switzerland, with signs of improvement in industrial production in 2026. Other important sectors for the cross-border workforce include the mechanical industry, logistics, construction and business services, especially in the Canton of Ticino.
