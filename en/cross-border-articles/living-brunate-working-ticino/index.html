For those considering living in Brunate and working in Ticino as a cross-border worker, these two dates are the starting point for understanding the applicable tax treatment. The Italian ratification is Law 83 of June 13, 2023.

The distinction that changes the tax framework

The date that separates the two regimes is July 17, 2023. Existing cross-border workers, already in that status before that date, have an exemption of €7'500 and fall under the 2024–2033 transitional regime. New cross-border workers, on the other hand, have a €10'000 tax-free allowance. The stated criterion is the worker's status in relation to July 17, 2023, not merely the place of residence.

The rule concerning the payslip is equally clear: for cross-border workers, withholding tax on employment income is withheld only in Switzerland. Italy avoids double taxation through the tax credit reported in section CE of the 730 form. Therefore, when assessing the move to Brunate, the comparison should not be constructed by automatically adding a Swiss tax and an Italian tax on the same income. The source links the Italian mechanism to the tax credit, not to a second withholding.

The bilateral framework also includes the Convention for the avoidance of double taxation between Italy and Switzerland, signed on March 9, 1976. Switzerland is not a member of the European Union or the European Economic Area: this element too forms part of the framework within which the G permit, taxation and cross-border employment should be understood. The point is not to assign a special rule to Brunate, but to apply the cross-border-worker regime correctly to the individual situation.

Read in sequence, the chronology distinguishes three stages: signing on December 23, 2020, Italian ratification through Law 83 of June 13, 2023, and application from January 1, 2024. The date of July 17, 2023, on the other hand, is used to identify existing and new cross-border workers.

2026 is not the date of the agreement

The USTAT figure for 2026 and the date of the new Cross-Border Commuters Agreement should not be conflated. The agreement entered into force on January 1, 2024, while the first quarter of 2026 is the statistical period. What are the key dates of the new Cross-Border Workers Agreement?
The new Frontier Agreement was signed on 23 December 2020 and entered into force on 1 January 2024. The Italian ratification took place with Law 83 of 13 June 2023. In addition, the watershed date of 17 July 2023 distinguishes old frontier workers from new frontier workers for the application of tax regimes and related deductibles.
How does the exemption work for old and new cross-border workers?
Old border workers, i.e. those who were already border workers before 17 July 2023, benefit from an exemption of 7,500 euros and fall under the transitional regime 2024-2033. New frontier workers, on the other hand, are entitled to a deductible of 10,000 euros. The income tax at source is only withheld in Switzerland, while Italy avoids double taxation through the tax credit in the EC framework of model 730.
