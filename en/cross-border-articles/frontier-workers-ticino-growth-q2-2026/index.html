The Federal Statistical Office (BFS) has published the official data relating to border guards of the Canton of Ticino for the second quarter of 2026. The statistic shows 79,121 border workers resident in Italy and employed in Switzerland, with a growing trend compared to the immediately preceding period. The comparison with the first quarter of 2026 shows an increase of 505 units, corresponding to a positive change of +0.64%. However, the data shows a different picture when compared to the same quarter of the previous year: in Q2 2025 there were 79,578 border crossers, which results in a drop of 457 units, with a negative change of -0.57% on an annual basis.

Reading the historical series: eight quarters of fluctuations

The BFS data provides a historical series covering the last two years, from the third quarter 2024 to the second quarter 2026. The analysis of the indicative values shows oscillating trends, without a linear trajectory, neither expansive nor contractile. The maximum peak of the series was recorded in the third quarter of 2025, when border crossers reached 79,608 units. The minimum point corresponds to the fourth quarter of 2025 with 78,698 units, resulting in an amplitude of variation of 910 units over eight quarters — a fluctuation that represents about 1.1% of the average total. The series does not reveal a dominant trend, but seasonal cycles and micro-fluctuations probably related to the recruitment, resignation and production cycles of Ticino companies. The rebound observed between Q1 and Q2 2026 falls within this normal volatility rather than signaling a lasting cycle reversal.

How to read the data for those working at the border

The BFS Q2 2026 data provides an official snapshot of the Ticino cross-border labor market, useful for those evaluating or planning an occupational transition from Italy to Switzerland, or for those already residing in Italy and working across the border. The QoQ growth of +0.64% signals short-term stability, suggesting that the employment cycle continues to create new job opportunities even though the annual trend remains weak. For job seekers, this slight quarterly uptick is a positive signal of persistent demand; for those already working, the numbers confirm that the cross-border pool remains robust. Consulting the site's statistics dashboard provides historical charts for reference to monitor quarterly performance: a useful resource to verify whether personal numbers align with the general trend. Every employed cross-border worker must declare income annually to the Italian Revenue Agency; tax refunds (reimbursements from excess withheld tax) are typically issued by May-June.

What does the BFS data indicate about Ticino Q2 2026 border crossers?
The Federal Statistical Office (BFS) registers 79,121 border workers resident in Italy and employed in Switzerland in the Canton of Ticino for the second quarter of 2026. Which age group is most represented among border workers in Ticino?
The 50-54 year old class is the most numerous with 11,323 border guards. Overall, workers between 40 and 59 years represent about 65% of the border basin (about 52,000 units), indicating that the Ticino market mainly absorbs profiles with consolidated experience. The youngest groups (15-24 years) are marginalised, with only 2,986 total border crossers (about 3.8% of the catchment area).
What is the gender distribution among Ticino border crossers according to BFS data?
According to BFS Q2 2026 data, men make up 61.6% of the total (48,700 units), while women represent 38.4% (30,420 units). This imbalance reflects occupational segregation in sectors traditionally absorbed by the border basin, where sectors such as construction, industry and logistics predominate among cross-border workers.
