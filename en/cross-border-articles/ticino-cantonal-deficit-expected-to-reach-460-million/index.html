The financial picture

The Canton’s 2027 Budget projects an operating deficit of 98,5 million francs.

It is this trajectory that forms the backdrop to the interpellation submitted by Amalia Mirante together with Giovanni Albertini and Evaristo Roncelli, of Avanti con Ticino&Lavoro. In the text, the signatories urge the Council of State to clarify which measures have already been developed and why they have not yet reached Parliament.

“Why, in the face of these prospects, is the Government postponing the multi-year rebalancing plan until the beginning of the next legislative term?”

According to the interpellants, a substantial share of the deterioration could be attributed to factors already known. Among these, they cite the launch of EFAS, initiatives related to health insurance premiums, Confederation savings, the abolition of imputed rental value and the future introduction of individual taxation. In their view, this entire set of factors should have called for advance planning.

The 2027 Budget sets out measures amounting to approximately 237 million francs. Some of them, however, are based on extraordinary or temporary operations: the sale of assets, recourse to funds and reserves, and increased contributions from companies with government holdings. The interpellation argues that such solutions cannot replace a structural review of public spending.

The spending review remains central

The spending review also features among the interpellation’s topics. The time elapsed since the November 2023 motion is now close to three years, while the time since the Grand Council approved the legislative decree exceeds two; according to the signatories, an overall plan, accompanied by financial targets and defined deadlines, is still not available. Presented in December 2025, the BAK Economics study had identified a cost gap of approximately 96,7 million compared with comparable Cantons, but that figure does not automatically translate into achievable savings.

The final questions also concern the effects the postponement could have on indebtedness, public services and the finances of municipalities. As for revenues, the signatories ask whether measures such as raising the cantonal multiplier, changing exemption thresholds or adjusting the rates applied to married couples and families have been examined. The document finally mentions two motions from November 2025 that have not yet received a response, concerning the financing of initiatives on health insurance premiums and the concrete implementation of the initiative for 10%.

What remains open for those who work in Ticino

The hypotheses are not decisions

For the cross-border worker working in Ticino, the news has a clearly defined scope: the source describes a political debate over the cantonal accounts and possible rebalancing choices. It does not present a measure already applied to cross-border taxation, permits or social benefits.

The text does not mention changes to the G permit, withholding tax, AVS, LPP or LAMal. Nor does it address tax rebates, double taxation, Brogeda, INPS or a new 2026 tax agreement. For this reason, it is not possible to derive from the source an amount for a cross-border worker's paycheck, a new deadline or a procedure to follow.

The practical point is different: the interpellation asks the Council of State to clarify what consequences the postponement could have for indebtedness, public services and municipalities' finances. These are areas the reader can follow, but without turning the signatories' questions into decisions already adopted.

How to follow the dossier

A four-step review

First: start from the two financial references cited in the source, the 2027 Budget and the 2028-2030 Financial Plan. The comparison must separate the operating deficit from subsequent projections and keep self-financing distinct, which is indicated as set to become negative from 2028.

Second: examine the composition of the measures envisaged in the 2027 Budget. The useful check consists in distinguishing what derives from the sale of capital assets, funds and reserves, or increased contributions from companies with public holdings, from structural spending review measures. It is precisely this difference on which the signatories base part of their criticism.

Third: verify whether the State Council will present the requested document before the end of the legislative term. The political request is that the document clarify which consolidation measures are feasible, what effects they would have on the accounts and when they could be implemented. Without a complete picture, a series of questions will remain open.

Fourth: follow the spending review and the two motions of November 2025 separately. For the first, the source refers to the motion of November 2023, the legislative decree approved by the Grand Council and the BAK Economics study of December 2025. For the latter, it will be necessary to check when the answers will arrive on how to finance the initiatives for health-insurance premiums and how to implement the initiative for 10%.

This sequence allows the cross-border worker not to automatically attribute effects on the G permit, withholding tax, AVS, LPP, LAMal, revenue-sharing payments or double taxation to the cantonal accounts: the source does not formulate decisions in these areas.
