Pension Switzerland: Record 725-point Raiffeisen Barometer (cross-border guide)

Di Laura Bianchi · Published on 18 September 2026

The Raiffeisen and ZHAW barometer reaches the record of 725 points. Pillar 3a grows to 79%, but uncertainty remains over the financing of the AVS.

Context

In brief

  • Raiffeisen and ZHAW barometer at 725 points
  • First pillar financing and Old Age and Survivors Insurance (OASI) among concerns
  • 39% fear that OASI will not cover expenses
  • Pillar 3a reaches a new high of 79%

Key facts

  • Organization: Raiffeisen and ZHAW
  • Barometer value: 725 points out of 1000
  • Increase: plus 28 points compared to the previous year
  • Initial value 2018: 587 points
  • Survey sample: over 1000 people aged between 18 and 65
  • OASI concern: 39% of respondents
  • Pillar 3a share: 79% of the population
  • Awareness of the new Pillar 3a rule: 63% of the sample
  • Supplementary contribution: 5% of the sample

The situation of the Swiss pension system is showing further improvement, according to the latest Raiffeisen barometer, produced in collaboration with ZHAW, the Zurich University of Applied Sciences. Driving this progress are primarily the better financial situation of pension funds and a higher savings rate among the population, elements that have directly impacted overall economic conditions. The Risk and Insurance Institute of ZHAW highlighted how economic factors have generated a favorable context. Compared to the previous year, the barometer value rose by twenty-eight points, reaching seven hundred and twenty-five points out of a maximum of one thousand. In the first survey in 2018, the recorded points were five hundred and eighty-seven. This is the highest level since the beginning of the research conducted by the sponsoring entities, signaling a positive dynamic for the Swiss pension system. The overall improvement is primarily due to the good financial conditions of all three pension pillars, but the survey also combines economic indicators with data from a survey conducted on over one thousand people aged between eighteen and sixty-five. This sample provides a picture that highlights not only positive aspects but also persistent elements of uncertainty regarding the future sustainability of the system. The major concern detected among the population remains the long-term financing of the first pillar, with 39% of respondents fearing that OASI will no longer be able to cover its expenses in the future. This data reflects a widespread climate of apprehension among workers regarding the stability of basic public pension benefits in the coming years. In-depth data analysis shows how private pension savings continue to play a central role for resident citizens and for those planning their professional and financial future in Swiss territory, dealing with current rules and regulatory developments concerning occupational pensions and individual provident pillars. Those who wish to delve deeper into retirement planning and understand how contributions work can regularly consult the tools dedicated to pension planning to navigate the different pillars of the Swiss system.

Operational details

Alongside fears regarding the financial sustainability of the first pillar and the relevant public institution, data collected by the survey show a growing trend toward voluntary individual pension savings. Increasingly more people are setting aside sums in anticipation of old age, and the share of respondents who own or contribute to a pillar 3a has reached a new high of seventy-nine percent. Further impetus to this form of private savings may have come from the possibility, introduced at the beginning of twenty-twenty-six, to retroactively fill contribution gaps in the third pillar segment. The potential in this regard nevertheless remains broad within the active population. Sixty-three percent are aware of the new rule introduced, but only five percent have actually made a supplementary payment to cover uncovered periods. This discrepancy between the theoretical knowledge of the rule and its concrete application highlights how individual financial planning requires resources and accurate assessments by individual taxpayers. Another aspiration frequently mentioned but particularly difficult to achieve for many workers is early retirement, which often remains a wish rather than a concrete reality. More than half of people aged between fifty-one and sixty-five would like to leave the workforce before the ordinary retirement age, but at the same time almost a third believe they cannot realize this dream due to economic conditions and the requirements necessary to sustain the annuity over time. Financing early retirement will be even more challenging for future generations due to falling conversion rates, higher life expectancy, and increasingly non-linear working careers, as warned by the research institute's experts. Consequently, a potential longer stay in the Swiss professional world also gains greater importance. For about two-thirds of the interviewed sample, working longer would in principle be conceivable, but for most people this option is only viable on a targeted basis or part-time, balancing income needs with the sustainability of work commitment at an advanced age.
