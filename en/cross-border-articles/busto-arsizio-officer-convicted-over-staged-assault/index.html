In July, the Court of Appeal sentenced G.F., an officer with the Busto Arsizio Local Police, to two years in prison, after the Busto Arsizio Court had acquitted him at first instance. The filed grounds set out a different reconstruction of the morning of February 1, 2023, when the man had reported an assault on via Travelli.

According to his account, the officer was carrying out a duty alone in the restricted traffic zone. He reported being pushed from behind by two strangers and described two young men wearing different jackets, one orange and one light-colored. The report led to the deployment of several Local Police patrols, also involving the State Police. The officer was taken to hospital and discharged with a prognosis of ten days.

The incident attracted the city’s attention and also involved the Municipality of Busto Arsizio, which joined the proceedings as a civil party. In the Court’s view, G.F. was responsible for several offenses: simulation of a crime, causing public alarm, ideological falsification and fraud. The administration was awarded 5mila euro in provisional compensation, plus more than 9mila euro in legal costs.

The images at the center of the ruling

The Court gave particular weight to private recordings collected along the officer’s route, between via Carlo, via Sant'Ambrogio and via Travelli. The footage came from a couple of shops and a bank: some systems recorded continuously, while others were activated by movement.

None of the images showed the two alleged attackers. Instead, the recordings deemed decisive showed the officer alone: at one point he approached a wall and deliberately struck it with a knee; other sequences showed him rubbing his cheek and other parts of his body against a wall or a column.

At first instance, the Court had described those gestures as “bizarre,” but considered them insufficient to prove that the man had deliberately inflicted the injuries on himself. The same decision also considered the presence of a blind spot plausible. After reexamining the footage and renewing part of the evidentiary proceedings, the Court of Appeal instead found the uncovered area too small: the arrival, push and fall could not have occurred without at least part of the action or body appearing in the images. The theory involving the gates was also ruled out, because the bank’s continuous recording showed them always closed during the interval in question.

The most relevant point is not merely that no camera showed the two men. The Court based its reasoning on all the recordings, the size of the uncovered area and the continuity of the bank's system. The conclusion stems from comparing what should have appeared with what the images actually show: in the judges' view, the reported sequence of events could not have remained entirely out of frame.

The second level concerns causing a false alarm. The call to the Control Center had focused patrols on the search for the two men described and had also involved the State Police. The searches also extended to other areas of Busto Arsizio and Saronno station, but produced no results and involved a considerable number of working hours. For the Court, resources intended for public services were thus directed toward people who had not committed any assault.

The third level concerns contradictions in the defendant's version. G.F. had said that he could not identify the precise location of the assault and that he had associated two people seen running afterward with the incident, without being certain that they were the perpetrators. He had also denied injuring himself against the wall, explaining those movements as simply bracing himself after a shock. The Court deemed this explanation incompatible with the behavior captured on video.
