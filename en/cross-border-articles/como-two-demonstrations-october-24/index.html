Two separate calls

On Saturday, October 24, Como could face two distinct events, organized through online channels and described in opposing terms. Among social media pages and profiles, as well as in specific WhatsApp groups, two events would in fact be in the process of being organized, perhaps not exclusively involving students.

The first call is explicitly student-oriented. The available content describes it as an initiative with all permits in order and entirely peaceful aims, intended to protest against the various problems affecting schools in the city and province.

The second, by contrast, would have objectives much more similar to the vandalism already observed, especially in Milan in recent hours. The report attributes the destruction of shop windows, cars and stores to groups of vandals and hooligans, distinguishing these actions from the peaceful demands expressed in squares and marches.

The material circulated about the second event is limited to just a few messages that were viewed. In one of them, the demonstration is presented as a sort of civil war, with the intention of smashing everything and repeating what happened in Milan. This is the passage referring to the event identified as posing the greatest security risk.

The initial picture is therefore of two events that would have the same date but not the same profile. On the one hand, there is a school protest described as orderly and peaceful; on the other, an event described through messages evoking disorder and damage. The available information does not place the two initiatives on the same footing.

Operational details

Fiamma Tricolore's statement

The attention surrounding the potentially riskier demonstration has not remained confined to social media pages. In recent hours, a statement from Marco Dalle, regional secretary of Fiamma Tricolore, reached newsrooms, explicitly mentioning the event scheduled in Como for Saturday, October 24.

The statement opens with events unfolding in Milan and several European cities. Dalle directly addresses the students and young people who will take part in the initiative, reminding them that demonstrating is a fundamental right. According to the statement, however, the right to demonstrate cannot be used as a justification for ravaging, looting, or damaging what people have built through years of sacrifice and work.

Dalle reiterates the message with these words:

Expressing one's dissent remains legitimate, but damage is unacceptable: behind a commercial establishment, a shop window, or any affected property are people, families, and workers who deserve respect.

The appeal therefore concerns not only shop windows or stores, but also the people and families behind the businesses and assets that may be damaged. The position expressed in the statement distinguishes the freedom to protest from acts of destruction and looting.

Dalle then adds an invitation to participants: to bring the Tricolore with them, described as a symbol of national identity and belonging. The message looks to the country's future and emphasizes the need for younger generations to bring Italy, its citizens, everyday difficulties, and their legitimate aspirations back to the forefront.

The statement also addresses solidarity with the people of other countries. For Fiamma Tricolore, this solidarity should not translate into indifference toward the problems experienced daily in Italy. The statement thus adds a public stance to the alert over possible unrest, while the available official confirmations remain few.

The calendar dilemma

The Sinigaglia and the deployment of law enforcement

The most delicate part of what follows concerns the schedule already set for Saturday, October 24. At 15 on the same day, Como will host Sassuolo at the Sinigaglia stadium. The match obviously requires a large deployment of law enforcement, an element that makes managing other events in the city over the course of a few hours more complex.

The source specifically points to this overlap of demands. If, in addition to the sporting event, several similar demonstrations were confirmed, it would be difficult to accommodate all the events within the same time period. The difficulty would increase in the case of events scheduled close together or even overlapping.

The sports calendar does not clarify what the outcome of the calls will be, but it introduces a concrete constraint on October 24. On the one hand, there is a home game already listed with its time and stadium; on the other, two possible events are being discussed, with very different characteristics and an uneven level of risk.

At the moment, there is little corroborating information and few official details about the second initiative. The available reconstruction is based mainly on the messages reviewed and on Marco Dalle’s statement, which explicitly referred to fears of new unrest in Como.

The coming hours should provide further information. The main point to clarify concerns the actual scale of the two calls and their compatibility with the event at the Sinigaglia. For now, the coincidence of the match and the possible staging of several demonstrations on the same day leave the mystery surrounding Saturday unresolved.
