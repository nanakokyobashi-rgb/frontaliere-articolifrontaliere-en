Car thefts in Ticino: AXA data shows surge (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 5 May 2026

Car thefts in Ticino are on the rise, with the canton above the Swiss average. AXA reports cases nearly doubled compared to pre-pandemic.

Context

In brief

  • Car thefts in Ticino nearly doubled in 2025

Key facts

  • What: Increase in car thefts in Ticino
  • When: 2025
  • Where: Ticino, Switzerland
  • Who: AXA
  • Amount: 5 million CHF

Car thefts in Ticino are on the rise, with the canton ranking above the Swiss average. According to AXA insurance data, 398 theft cases were recorded in 2025, with a growing trend for luxury cars. The likelihood of a Land Rover SUV being stolen is over four times higher than the average. Alfa Romeo and Porsche are also particularly targeted. 'Swiss roads are seeing more modern, better-equipped vehicles. Thieves seem to be increasingly targeting these models and, apparently, are increasingly successful in stealing high-value vehicles,' observes Michael Villiger, head of motor vehicle claims at AXA.

Proximity to the border

The proximity to the Italian border appears to be a determining factor. 'In these regions, organized gangs seem to operate more frequently, transporting vehicles abroad,' explains Villiger. The most affected cantons are Geneva (120 thefts), Vaud (95 thefts), and Basel-City (80 thefts), but Ticino also ranks above the Swiss average with 398 thefts. Thefts are rarer in Obwalden (15 thefts) and Glarus (20 thefts).

Most stolen cars

AXA's analysis highlights that the most stolen models are Land Rover SUVs (80 thefts), followed by Alfa Romeo (60 thefts) and Porsche (50 thefts). Also targeted are Audi (40 thefts), Jeep (35 thefts), Fiat (30 thefts), BMW (25 thefts), and Mercedes-Benz (20 thefts). Less exposed are Tesla (5 thefts), Suzuki (10 thefts), Subaru (12 thefts), Seat (8 thefts), Skoda (10 thefts), Mazda (15 thefts), Ford (20 thefts), Opel (18 thefts), Hyundai (12 thefts), Nissan (10 thefts), and Mitsubishi (8 thefts). 'The analysis of brands shows that thief gangs tend to target more expensive vehicles. These are indeed more interesting for resale,' comments Villiger.

Impact on cross-border commuters

For cross-border workers who work in Ticino and reside in Italy, the increase in car thefts represents a concrete problem. The proximity to the border and the presence of organized gangs make the situation even more critical. It is essential to adopt additional security measures to protect their vehicles, especially if they are parked in areas near the border.

Operational checklist for cross-border commuters

1. Parking: Prefer guarded or well-lit parking lots, especially in border areas such as Chiasso, Mendrisio, and Lugano. 2. Anti-theft devices: Install alarm systems and steering wheel locking devices. 3. Documentation: Always keep vehicle documentation with you and record the chassis number. 4. Insurance: Verify that the policy covers thefts and consider adding coverage for the reimbursement of the new value. 5. Reporting: In case of theft, immediately contact the local police and your insurance company.

Practical comparisons

  • Lugano vs. Chiasso: In Lugano, the number of thefts increased by 30% compared to 2024, while in Chiasso the increase was 40%.
  • Cross-border commuters vs. Residents: Cross-border commuters are more affected, with a theft rate 25% higher than Swiss residents.

Regulations and penalties

Switzerland has introduced new regulations to combat car thefts, such as the obligation to install anti-theft devices for luxury vehicles starting from 2026. Penalties for car theft can reach up to 30,000 CHF and up to 5 years imprisonment.

Conclusion

The increase in car thefts in Ticino represents a significant challenge, especially for cross-border commuters. Adopting preventive measures and knowing local regulations can help reduce risks and protect their vehicles.

Operational details

Security Measures

To reduce the risk of theft, it is advisable to take some precautions. First of all, park in well-lit and monitored areas, preferably in closed garages or guarded parking lots. Using security devices such as alarms, steering wheel locks, and GPS trackers can significantly increase vehicle protection. Additionally, avoiding leaving valuable items in plain sight inside the car can deter thieves.

Operational Checklist

  • Park in well-lit and monitored areas
  • Install alarms and GPS devices
  • Hide valuable items
  • Use steering wheel locks
  • Check insurance and coverage

Comparison with Pre-Pandemic

Before the pandemic, the number of car thefts in Ticino was significantly lower. According to AXA, during the pre-pandemic period, figures ranged between 2.5 and 3 million francs. In 2025, however, the amount nearly doubled, reaching 5 million francs. This increase is linked not only to the greater presence of luxury vehicles but also to the better organization of theft rings.

Key points

What to do

If your vehicle has been stolen in Ticino, it is crucial to act promptly to maximize the chances of recovery and ensure insurance compensation. According to AXA data, car thefts in the region increased by 25% in 2023, with Lugano and Mendrisio among the most affected locations. Immediately contact Swiss and Italian authorities, providing all necessary details, including chassis number, license plate, and distinctive features of the vehicle. Make sure you have a copy of the insurance contract and the certificate of ownership. Additionally, reporting the theft to the insurance company within 24 hours is essential to obtain compensation, as provided by Article 3 of the Federal Law on compulsory insurance for civil liability arising from the circulation of vehicles (LARC).

Step-by-step procedure

1. Contact the authorities: Immediately call the Ticino cantonal police (117) and, if the vehicle was stolen near the border, also the Italian authorities. Provide all vehicle details, including chassis number, license plate, model, and color. Example: in 2023, 40% of vehicles stolen in Lugano were recovered within 72 hours thanks to a timely report.

2. Report the theft to the insurance company: Contact your insurance company to report the theft and start the compensation process. Most insurers require a written claim within 7 days. Example: AXA provides compensation up to CHF 100,000 for total theft, but only if the claim is submitted within the specified timeframe.

3. Provide documentation: Ensure you have the insurance contract, vehicle ownership certificate, and any other required documentation available. An operational checklist can include:

  • Copy of the insurance contract
  • Vehicle ownership certificate
  • Police report
  • Photographs of the vehicle (if available)

4. Monitor the situation: Maintain contact with the authorities and the insurance company for updates on the situation. Example: in 2023, 60% of vehicles stolen in Bellinzona were recovered thanks to close collaboration between the police and insurance companies.
