Ticino politics is again attacking the issue of 'health taxes' for cross-border workers, contesting the practice of refunds to Italy. PLR, Center and League argue that such practices constitute a violation of the principles of free movement of workers within the European Union.

The signatories of the motion are asking the Cantonal Government to assess a total or partial suspension of payments to Italy, arguing that such sums could be better used to strengthen the Ticino healthcare system.

The new motion introduces an additional point, emphasizing how the tax could specifically impact those working in Switzerland by imposing an extra cost directly tied to cross-border employment. According to its proponents, this could conflict with the principles of free movement of people.

How to read the request

The motion described by the source is a political request: it does not by itself change the agreement on tax refunds or impose a charge on workers. It asks for an assessment by the cantonal government, a review under the Agreement on the Free Movement of Persons and discussions with Italy. Practical effects depend on later official acts, not on the motion alone.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the deputies asking for?
The deputies ask for the total or partial suspension of the rebates.
Does the motion on rebates change my taxes as a cross-border commuter immediately?
No, the motion presented by the FDP, Centro and Lega in Bellinzona on 12 June 2026 is a political act and not an existing law. It commits the Council of State to evaluate the suspension of rebates and to dialogue with the federal authorities, but does not entail any immediate change to your current tax treatment or the calculation of your net salary.
Why do Ticino politicians want to suspend rebates to Italy?
The signatories argue that the rebates paid to Italy violate the principles of the free movement of workers. The declared objective is to recover financial resources to be reinvested directly in strengthening the Ticino health system, believing that the current tax redistribution mechanism is no longer compatible with the work dynamics and costs incurred by the Canton to manage cross-border health personnel.
What is meant by violation of the principles of free movement in this context?
The promoters of the motion argue that the tax on cross-border workers, linked to rebates, acts as a discriminatory additional cost. According to their thesis, imposing a specific tax burden based on cross-border activity could conflict with the Free Movement Agreement (AFMP), creating a difference in treatment that penalizes those who choose to work in Switzerland while residing in Italy.
What are the next steps planned after the presentation of the motion?
The Council of State will now have to assess the political request, examine the compatibility of the suspension of rebates with the Free Movement Agreement and initiate the necessary diplomatic steps with Italy. Since there is still no official decision, the practical effects for workers will depend exclusively on the regulatory acts or bilateral agreements that could arise from this formal verification process.
