Mario Padula appointed IRE USI director since 2026 (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 7 September 2026

The economist specialising in retirement and family finance will lead the USI Economic Research Institute from 1 September 2026.

Context

In brief - Mario Padula new director IRE USI - Specialised in social security and family finance - Will take on role from 1 September 2026 ## Key facts - What: Appointment of Mario Padula as director IRE USI - WHEN: From 1 September 2026 - Where: University of Italian Switzerland (USI) - Who: Mario Padula - Amount: Unspecified Mario Padula, economist specialised in social security, family finance, savings, consumption and the economics of ageing, has been appointed new director of the Institute of Economic Research (IRE) of the University of Italian Switzerland (USI). Padula will take up the role from 1 September 2026 and will also be Professor of Economics at the USI Faculty of Economic Sciences. # ## A role of great responsibility Padula thanked Professor Mario Jametti, his predecessor, for the role that the IRE has played in favour of the economy and institutions of the Canton of Ticino. "In THE IRE, and in the University of Italian Switzerland, lie the necessary skills to grasp the challenges of the complexity that these times reserve for us. Aging societies, climate change, but also the consequent transformations of the economic and social fabric and the related public policies that address them will be the subject of analysis by IRE researchers with a careful look at the dynamics of the territory and The appointment of Mario Padula as director of IRE USI represents a significant step for the University of Italian Switzerland and for the Canton of Ticino. With his experience and expertise, Padula is well positioned to address the economic and social challenges the territory is facing, contributing to a more sustainable and prosperous future for the region.

Operational details

Implications for cross-border commuters

The appointment of Mario Padula as director of the Institute of Economic Research (IRE) of the University of Italian Switzerland (USI) could have significant implications for the cross-border commuters of the Canton of Ticino. With over 50,000 cross-border commuters crossing the border daily, the research conducted by the IRE under his direction could contribute to a deeper understanding of the economic dynamics affecting this category of workers. For example, recent studies have shown that cross-border commuters contribute about 30% to the GDP of Ticino, making targeted economic policy crucial.

The IRE, under Padula's leadership, could further strengthen the quality and impact of its research, translating international knowledge and perspectives into concrete responses to the challenges of the Canton. This could translate into more effective public policies, such as optimizing transport infrastructure between Ticino municipalities and neighboring Italian cities, or introducing tax incentives for cross-border commuters residing in specific municipalities, such as Lugano or Mendrisio.

A University Rooted in the Territory

The rector of the USI, Claudio Bassetti, emphasized the University's vocation: rooted in its own territory and, at the same time, open to the national and international context. "The return of Mario Padula to the USI goes in this direction," Bassetti stated. "His academic and institutional experience gained in various European countries and his expertise in areas particularly relevant today—from pensions to the economics of aging, from family finance to the evaluation of public policies—represent an added value for the IRE."

Key points

What to do for cross-border commuters

Cross-border commuters in the Canton of Ticino can benefit from the research and analyses conducted by IRE, which could influence public policies and economic decisions that directly affect them. It is important to follow the developments and publications of IRE to stay informed about the latest trends and potential implications for their professional and personal lives.

Concrete examples

For example, IRE's research has highlighted that cross-border commuters from municipalities such as Lugano and Mendrisio represent a significant part of the Ticino economy. According to 2022 data, approximately 30% of workers in the Canton of Ticino are cross-border commuters, contributing substantially to the local GDP. Understanding these dynamics can help cross-border commuters make more informed decisions about their careers and financial planning.

Regulations and dates

It is essential to be aware of the tax regulations that govern the lives of cross-border commuters. For example, as of January 1, 2023, Switzerland introduced new tax rules for cross-border commuters, which provide a reduction in tax rates for those working in Ticino but residing in Italy. This change can have a significant impact on the net income of cross-border commuters, making it important to consult IRE's publications to stay updated.

Operational checklist

For cross-border commuters, here is a useful operational checklist:

  • Consultation of IRE publications: Follow IRE's publications to stay informed about the latest research and analyses.

Frequently Asked Questions
Who is the new director of IRE USI?
Mario Padula, an economist specialising in social security, family finance, savings, consumption and the economics of ageing, has been appointed new director of the Institute of Economic Research (IRE) of the University of Italian Switzerland (USI). He will also take on the role of Professor of Economics at the USI Faculty of Economic Sciences.
When will Mario Padula take office at THE IRE?
Mario Padula will assume the role of director of IRE USI from 1 September 2026, succeeding Professor Mario Jametti, who led the Institute until this transition.
What are Padula's areas of expertise?
His areas of expertise include pension, family finance, savings, consumption and the economics of ageing. According to the USI Rector Claudio Bassetti, his academic and institutional experience gained in several European countries represents an added value for THE IRE.
What will happen under the new direction of THE IRE?
According to the rector Bassetti, under the direction of Padula, the Institute will be able to strengthen the quality and impact of its research, contributing to the understanding of the great economic transformations and translating international knowledge into responses to the challenges of the Canton.
What challenges did Padula cite for his tenure?
Padula stated that ageing societies, climate change and the transformations of the economic and social fabric will be the subject of analysis by IRE researchers, with attention to the dynamics of the territory.
