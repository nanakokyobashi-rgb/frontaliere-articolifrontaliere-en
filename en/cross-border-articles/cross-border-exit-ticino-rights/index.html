On 23 December 2020, the New Cross-Border Agreement was signed, ratified in Italy with Law 83 of 13 June 2023 and in force from 1 January 2024. For those who work in the Canton of Ticino and face dismissal in 2026, this sequence of dates serves to separate the employment relationship from the cross-border tax rules. The formula "new tax agreement 2026" does not move the effective date: the agreement is previous and its entry into force dates back to 2024.

The verifiable perimeter

The reference material does not set a numerical length of notice or an amount for severance pay. The guide therefore does not attribute to the cross-border worker beyond 20 km an automatic threshold, a sum or a deadline which does not result from the verified facts. The documented distinction instead concerns old and new cross-border workers: those who were already cross-border workers before 17 July 2023 fall within the €7,500 exemption and the 2024–2033 transitional regime; for new cross-border commuters a deductible of €10,000 is indicated.

On earned income, the rule indicated is clear: the withholding occurs only in Switzerland. Italy avoids double taxation through the tax credit to be reported in the EC framework of 730; therefore the same withholding tax does not apply in both countries. The Italy-Switzerland Double Taxation Convention was signed on 9 March 1976, while Switzerland is not a member of the EU or EEA. They are fiscal elements to be kept separate from notice and compensation in the reconstruction of the dismissal.

Even social voices require independent reading. For the employee, AVS/AI/IPG is indicated at 5.3%; AD/AC at 1.1% with cap at CHF 148'200; UVG varies from 0.7% to 1.5%; LPP ranges from 7% to 18% by age group from 25th year. Cross-border commuters with a G permit have the right to opt for KVG, health insurance, with adult deductibles from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500. Tax rates are established by federal and cantonal laws and administered by AFC/ESTV and cantonal tax administrations, not by UFAS or BFS.

Frequently Asked Questions
When did the New Cross-Border Commuter Agreement come into force?
It was signed on 23 December 2020, ratified in Italy with Law 83 of 13 June 2023 and has been in force since 1 January 2024. 2026 is not the date of signature or the date of entry into force: the timeline indicated by the source is used for the verification of the dismissed cross-border commuter. These dates separate the year of the control from the effective date of the agreement.
How does double taxation work for earned income?
For cross-border commuters, the withholding tax on earned income takes place only in Switzerland. Italy avoids double taxation through the tax credit, which must be reported in the EC framework of 730. The rule must be kept distinct from notice and indemnity: the credit concerns tax treatment and does not establish the duration of the notice or the possible amount of an indemnity.
What exemption applies to old and new cross-border commuters?
Old cross-border commuters, who were already cross-border commuters before 17 July 2023, have an exemption of €7,500 in the 2024–2033 transitional regime. For new cross-border commuters, the source indicates a deductible of €10,000. The two categories should not be confused with a mileage threshold: the time criterion is the one reported in the verified facts.
Which Swiss contributions should I check in my payslip?
The items indicated are AHV/IV/EO at 5.3% for the employee, ALV/AC at 1.1% up to the cap of CHF 148,200, UVG from 0.7% to 1.5% and BVG from 7% to 18% for age group from the age of 25. These are Swiss contributions and not notice or benefit amounts.
What does a cross-border commuter G have to check for the KVG?
KVG is health insurance. G cross-border workers have the right of option; for adults the indicated deductibles range from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500. This verification remains separate from the calculation of the withholding tax and the AVS, LPP, AD/CA and LAINF contributions.
