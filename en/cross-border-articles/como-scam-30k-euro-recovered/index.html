In Como, a cyber scam stole 30mila euro from an employee of a foundation that assigns homes at subsidized rents to people in need. The timely report and intervention by the State Police made it possible to recover the entire sum.

A phone call from an individual claiming to be an employee of the bank where the foundation holds its current account prompted the woman to contact the Cybersecurity Operations Section of Como. The purported operator led her to believe that fraudulent activity was underway against the organization and that she had to follow certain instructions to safeguard the money.

The contact and the transfer

The scam appeared more credible thanks to spoofing: the caller’s number was altered, so the bank’s number appeared on the display. Convinced that she had to stop a fraudulent transaction, the employee authorized an immediate bank transfer of 30mila euro to a Poste Italiane current account used by the scammers.

To complete the transfer, both the available funds and the foundation’s bank overdraft facility were used. The money, therefore, did not come only from the liquidity already present in the organization’s account: the sum also included the funds made available through the banking relationship. Postal Police investigators immediately recognized the scheme and moved to block the money. The entire current account used by those responsible was seized, making it possible to recover the entire sum.

Once the judicial authority was informed without delay, it ordered the return of the 30mila euro to the foundation from Como, which fully recovered its financial loss. The result was made possible by the promptness of the report and the rapid action of the investigators.

Operational details

The case in Como shows a scam designed to replace verification with urgency. The victim was not contacted by just any stranger, but by someone who presented themselves as an employee of the foundation's bank. The message was alarming: fraudulent activity had allegedly involved the organization, and instructions had to be followed to protect the funds.

The social engineering technique shifted attention to the danger claimed by the scammers. Rather than appearing to be a request to send money, the bank transfer was presented as the necessary step to block an illicit transaction. Spoofing completed the charade: the bank's actual number appeared on the display. In this specific case, the number displayed therefore lent support to the identity claimed over the phone.
