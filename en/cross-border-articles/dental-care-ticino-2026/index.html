Dental care in Ticino: 150 million more, but no improvements (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 6 May 2026

Voting on June 14, 2026 on the dental care initiative. A committee denounces a double blow: more deductions and taxes

Context

In brief

  • Voting on the dental care initiative on June 14, 2026
  • A committee denounces a double blow: more deductions and taxes
  • 150 million paid by workers and companies

Key facts

  • What: Initiative for dental care
  • When: June 14, 2026
  • Where: Canton Ticino
  • Who: Committee against the dental care initiative
  • Amount: 150 million francs

The committee against the dental care initiative has raised an alarm: the initiative up for vote on June 14, 2026 in Canton Ticino represents a double blow for workers and companies. According to the committee, the initiative provides for an increase in deductions and taxes without guaranteeing concrete improvements in the service. The initiative, which aims to finance dental care, would result in an expenditure of 150 million francs paid by workers and companies.

The committee denounces that, despite the increase in contributions, there will be no significant improvements in the services offered. 'This is a deceptive initiative that hits workers and companies without bringing real benefits,' committee members say. The initiative was presented as a solution to improve access to dental care, but according to the committee, the funds raised will not be used effectively.

Implications for cross-border workers

Cross-border workers employed in Ticino will be directly affected by the increase in deductions and taxes. According to the committee, cross-border workers could see an increase in deductions from their paychecks without seeing improvements in dental care. 'This is an additional burden for those who already significantly contribute to the economy of Canton Ticino,' committee members emphasize.

Operational details

  • ## Practical Analysis

The initiative for dental care in the Canton of Ticino represents an emblematic case of how healthcare policies can have a significant impact on workers and businesses. The increase in deductions and taxes envisaged by the initiative could have direct repercussions on workers' paychecks, including cross-border commuters working in Ticino.

Before/After Comparison

Before the initiative, contributions for dental care were already provided, but the increase envisaged by the initiative represents an additional burden for workers. According to the no committee, the additional 150 million francs will not be used to improve services, but simply to cover existing expenses.

Concrete Scenarios

If the initiative is approved, cross-border workers could see an increase in deductions from their paychecks. This could have a significant impact on their purchasing power, especially in an already complex economic context. The no committee invites everyone to carefully consider the consequences of this initiative before going to the polls.

Implications for Businesses

Companies operating in Ticino could see an increase in labor costs due to the initiative. This could impact the competitiveness of local companies and their ability to attract and retain talent. The no committee emphasizes the importance of a sustainable healthcare policy that does not excessively burden workers and businesses.

Key points

Action

For cross-border workers who wish to participate in the vote on June 14, 2026, it is important to follow some concrete procedures. First, it is necessary to verify your right to vote and ensure that you are registered in the electoral rolls. Cross-border workers can consult the electoral calendar website for updated information on the voting procedure.

Step-by-step procedure

1. Verification of voting rights: Cross-border workers must ensure they are registered in the electoral rolls of the Canton Ticino. This can be done by consulting the website of the municipality of residence or contacting the electoral office. 2. Receiving voting materials: Citizens and cross-border workers registered in the electoral rolls will receive the voting materials at home. This material includes the ballot papers and instructions for voting. 3. Filling out the ballots: The ballot papers must be filled out following the instructions provided. It is important to express your vote clearly and legibly. 4. Sending the ballots: The completed ballots must be sent to the electoral office by the deadline. The ballots can be sent by post or delivered in person to the polling station. 5. Participation in the vote: Cross-border workers can also participate in the vote at the polling stations. It is important to present yourself with a valid identity document.

Deadlines

The date of the vote is June 14, 2026. The ballot papers must be sent to the electoral office by this date. Cross-border workers can consult the electoral calendar website for updated information on deadlines and voting procedures.
