The ceremony in Masnago

The square in front of Franco Ossola Stadium, in Masnago, now bears the name of Pietro Anastasi. The tribute took place in front of what had been his home as an athlete and celebrated one of the most representative figures in Varese sports and Varese Calcio. The dedication honors not only the champion but also the man, remembered as generous, humble and unforgettable.

The red-and-white roses were in Mrs. Anna's hands. Emotion could be heard in Gianluca's voice, while those who, over the course of his life and career, had known, loved and cheered for Pietro Anastasi gathered in the square. The moment brought together the memory of the goalscorer and that of the man appreciated by those who had come to Masnago for the occasion.

The decision to dedicate the area to him was described as right, deserved and appreciated, as well as approved unanimously. The Municipality installed the plaque bearing a dedication to Anastasi. The image depicts him in the red-and-white jersey as he looks toward the entrance to the Franco Ossola. The marker placed in front of the stadium thus makes the square's naming and its connection with the facility that was his home as an athlete visible.

Anastasi was born far away from here, in Catania. Yet it was precisely in Varese that he grew up, made his name and returned to live after appearing on the major stages of Italian and international football. His journey therefore connects his Catanian origins with the relationship he built with Varese, without separating his sporting career from the life he lived after returning.

For this reason, the name on the square brings together in one place the stadium, Varese Calcio and the memory of Pietro Anastasi. The ceremony gathered the affection of those who were present in person or in spirit and gave the recognition a dimension tied to the city as well as to sport. Anastasi's name is thus placed right in front of the stadium entrance, identified as the place of his experience as an athlete, within a ceremony designed to remember the champion and the man together.

Operational details

The memories of those who knew him

A symbol for Varese

Ambrogio Borghi, who was Anastasi's teammate, summed up in the shortest and most incisive phrase of the day the meaning of the bond between the footballer and Varese.

“If Naples has Maradona, Varese has Anastasi”

For Borghi, Anastasi's time with the red-and-white side was relatively brief, but it marked an era and linked him to the finest and strongest Varese team of all time. The phrase sums up the weight the player had in the city's sporting history, even beyond the length of his time wearing the red-and-white shirt.

Gedeone Carmignani focused above all on the striker's qualities. In his assessment, Anastasi knew how to move into space, had a nose for goal and was willing to press. With these qualities, he would be useful to every coach today. The assessment portrays a player capable of combining a presence in the box with a willingness to work even without the ball, as recalled during the meeting in Masnago.

The group of guests also used words tied to the personal dimension of the remembrance. Generous, always present and modest were the adjectives used to describe a man who was always there when someone sought him out. In this image, Pietro is not only the goalscorer celebrated in sporting memory, but a person to turn to and from whom to receive help. It is the human quality that accompanied the remembrance of his career.

Roberto Bettega came from Turin, linked to Anastasi by having played for Varese, Juventus and the national team. Bettega recalled the period when, upon returning to Juventus, the two were roommates. He scored immediately, then went scoreless for many matches; Anastasi stood by him, helped him, and his teammate began scoring again. The testimony brings a specific episode of personal support into the ceremony, distinct from the account of goals and jerseys worn.

The words heard in Masnago thus set the technical assessment of the striker alongside the memory of a person who was present in the lives of others. The celebration of the athlete belongs to the history of football; that of the man is conveyed instead through his relationships with teammates and those who sought him out. It is this dual reading, sporting and human, that explains the participation of the guests who came for Pietro Anastasi.

Key points

The story told by the plaque

From the national team to the provincial side

The Municipality has installed a plaque commemorating Anastasi's success in Varese and the memory Italy has preserved of him. The image of the footballer in a red-and-white shirt faces the entrance to the Franco Ossola. The message goes beyond Varese's history and also evokes what Anastasi represented for those who followed the national team and for those who had seen him star in the Italian championships.

For national-team supporters, the memory is tied to the 1968 European Championship final, played in Rome. Anastasi and Gigi Riva scored the two goals of the match. For those who had followed Varese, on the other hand, his name evoked the season of the provincial side capable of upending the league's balance, with the 5-0 victory over Juventus. Inter came after Juventus, but the bond with the red-and-white shirt remained the focal point of the dedication in front of the stadium.

The decisive move to Varese

The plaque also speaks to those who, having emigrated from the South, looked to a footballer as an example, an idol and a reason for redemption. Anastasi, born in Catania and later raised in Varese, became a symbol for different audiences: for those who supported Italy, for those who recognized the strength of the provincial side and for those who supported the player at the big clubs.

The origins of his relationship with Varese are conveyed through Guido Borghi's account and an incident involving Alfredo Casati, a club executive. Casati was supposed to board a flight in Catania, but gave his seat on the plane to a woman who urgently needed to return to Milan. He therefore stayed in the city and took the opportunity to attend a match. Massiminiana were playing: Casati saw Anastasi and decided to bring him to Varese.

From that decision developed Pietro's deep bond with the new city, with his Anna and with so many people. The account of the transfer adds a concrete element to the ceremony: before the champion celebrated by the national team and the big clubs, there was the player seen on the pitch in Catania. Fate, as the account heard in Masnago described it, brought that match, Casati and his future experience in Varese together.

It is this sequence that completes the meaning of the square's new name. In front of the Franco Ossola, the athlete remembered for his goals, the man described as generous and the person who had built a relationship with Anna and the city came together. The applause directed at Anastasi, whether in person or in thought, accompanied a dedication born of his sporting history and his life in Varese.
