On October 6, 2026, SECO published the latest data on the Swiss labor market, painting a picture that combines higher unemployment than in the previous year with job growth.

In September, 140.480 people were registered as unemployed, with an unemployment rate of 3%. Compared with August, the number fell by 1.064, equivalent to 0,8%. The comparison with September 2025, however, shows an increase of 7.247 people, equal to 5,4%. Once adjusted for seasonal effects, the rate reaches 3,1%.

SECO also considers a broader indicator: in September, 237.244 people were registered with the regional employment offices as unemployed or as other registered jobseekers, adjusted for seasonal variations, with a rate of 5%. This total exceeds that of unemployed people alone because it also includes those seeking a new job without being counted as unemployed according to administrative criteria.

The market that continues to create jobs

The UST statistics published on August 27, 2026, describe a different trajectory on the employment front. In the second quarter of the year, jobs in Switzerland reached 5,698 million, with 111.800 more jobs than in the same quarter of 2025. The increase amounted to 2% year on year. Services accounted for the main share of the growth, with 98.700 additional jobs; industry and construction added another 13.100.

The combination of these figures prevents a one-sided reading. Companies continue to hire and report difficulties finding qualified personnel, but the number of people without work is higher than a year earlier. For some groups, the market is therefore more competitive, even though total employment has grown.

For Italian workers, interest in Switzerland remains linked to average salaries that are higher than in Italy, the strength of the Swiss economy, and the possibility of living across the border while earning a salary in Swiss francs. However, the latest data suggest that access is not necessarily as straightforward as in the past. In the Canton of Ticino, the number of cross-border workers is showing signs of slowing. This local factor accompanies national job growth and makes it necessary to distinguish between the general trend and the one affecting those seeking work across the border.

Operational details

The national labor market is not the same as the Ticino figure

The question of whether working in Switzerland still pays off does not receive an automatic yes or no from the figures. The source confirms the reasons that make the market attractive to many Italians: average salaries higher than in Italy, the strength of the Swiss economy, and the possibility of living across the border while retaining a salary in Swiss francs. But the same picture shows tougher selection for some categories. For a cross-border worker, economic advantage and the likelihood of finding a job are not the same piece of information.

The increase in employment indicates that companies continue to hire, while the rise in the number of unemployed indicates that job hunting may be more competitive for some people. Both things can happen at the same time, and this very overlap is what should be borne in mind when assessing a cross-border career move. The source does not describe a general employment crisis: it does, however, make clear that the market has become more competitive for some categories and that companies report difficulties finding qualified staff. For Italian readers, the useful comparison is therefore between the skills in demand and the opportunities actually available, not between a single national figure.

Key points

A five-step method

The source makes it possible to build a practical assessment without turning statistics into promises. For someone who is a cross-border worker or is considering a job in Canton Ticino, the first check concerns the date to which each number refers.

Separating dates, territory and skills

1. Separate the sources and dates. Use the SECO release of October 6, 2026, for unemployment and the UST statistics of August 27, 2026, for employment. They are different snapshots: September for the unemployed, second quarter for jobs.

2. Distinguish the national level from the local level. The 5,698 million jobs describe Switzerland. They should not be read as available positions in Canton Ticino. For the canton, the source only reports a slowdown in the number of cross-border workers.

3. Look at the structure of growth. Services added 98.700 jobs, while industry and construction added 13.100. The figure helps show where the increase is concentrated, but the source does not indicate specific professions or requirements.

4. Compare your profile with the demand for qualifications. Companies continue to hire and report difficulties finding qualified personnel. The question to ask is whether the skills required by companies coincide with those available, as the same data suggests.

5. Keep the signals separate. The increase in unemployed people and the growth in jobs do not cancel each other out. The former describes a greater presence of people looking for work; the latter shows expansion of staff. The choice should therefore be based on actual opportunities, not on a single percentage.

This method avoids two opposite conclusions: thinking that Switzerland no longer offers opportunities or that every job search is easy. The source supports neither conclusion. For the cross-border worker, the practical point is to use the statistics as an initial filter: check the period, territory, sector and skills. The available material does not allow details to be added about individual opportunities in Canton Ticino beyond those indicated.
