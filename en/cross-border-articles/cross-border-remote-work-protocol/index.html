Italy and Switzerland sign the protocol on teleworking

By Marco Ferrari (editorial byline of the newsroom, AI-generated text) · Published on November 27, 2026 at 08:45 GMT+1

The Protocol was signed on June 6, 2024. Teleworking from home up to 25% since January 1, 2024.

Key facts

  • Signatories: Giancarlo Giorgetti and Karin Keller-Sutter
  • Locations: Rome and Bern
  • Subject: amendment to the Agreement on cross-border workers
  • Quota: up to 25% of working time
  • Expected effective date: retroactive to January 1, 2024

In June 2024, the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) announced the signing of the Protocol amending the Agreement on cross-border workers then in force. The document was signed on June 6, in Rome and Bern, by the Minister of Economy and Finance Giancarlo Giorgetti and Federal Councillor Karin Keller-Sutter. The aim of the agreement was to make the tax treatment of telework performed by cross-border workers permanent.

From January 1, 2024, according to the press release, cross-border workers may work from home for a share not exceeding 25 percent of their working hours. The note clarifies that, within this threshold, remote work changes neither the country entitled to tax employment income nor cross-border worker status: these are the two aspects that the MEF indicates remain unchanged.

From the previous agreement to the signing

The tax regulations were already based on a declaration of intent signed by Italy and Switzerland in November 2023. The Protocol signed in June 2024 was intended to replace it definitively, with retroactive effect from January 1, 2024. The MEF also specified that the main points of the existing regulations were not being changed. The reported development was therefore the formalization of a permanent arrangement through the Protocol, not the announcement of a different telework threshold.

Operational details

How to interpret the 25 percent limit

The stated share should be read as a maximum for the working time spent working from home through teleworking. In arithmetic terms, 25 out of 100 is equivalent to one quarter of the total time considered. The announcement does not convert the percentage into hours, days or working days per week: in fact, it does not provide a work schedule or a distribution of the week to use as a basis for a calculation. Translating the limit into a fixed number of days would therefore require details that the note does not provide.

Two aspects that teleworking does not change

The MEF links the possibility of working from home within the threshold to two distinct statements. The first concerns the State entitled to tax income from employment; the second concerns frontier worker status. The note states that teleworking has no impact on either. The documented point is limited: within the limit described, working from home does not change these aspects under the regulation presented.

The sequence of acts can also be verified: the declaration of intent dated back to November 2023, while the Protocol signed in June 2024 replaced it with retroactive effect from January 1, 2024. The text says that the main points of the existing regulation remained unchanged, but it offers no previous values for comparison and does not quantify savings, new taxes or other amounts. The comparison therefore concerns the form of the agreement and its effective date, not a numerical change in taxation.

Key points

Signing and entry into force do not coincide

The press release distinguished between signing and entry into force. The former had taken place on June 6, 2024; the latter was expected as soon as both countries had completed their respective internal approval procedures. The note did not specify a deadline for those procedures and did not report any subsequent update. Therefore, the document published in June 2024 alone does not make it possible to establish whether, or on what date, the Protocol subsequently formally entered into force. The retroactivity indicated as of January 1, 2024 described the intended effect, but did not eliminate the approval step mentioned in the same text.

The press release does not specify how to calculate the 25 percent, nor does it detail operating procedures or additional conditions for applying the quota. It also does not quantify the economic impact for an individual worker or describe different rules for higher percentages. The source makes it possible to establish the threshold, the two aspects declared unchanged and the transition from the statement of intent to the Protocol; it does not make it possible to add details beyond these points.

The scope of the note is limited to the taxation of teleworking. It does not detail the mechanism of withholding tax or double taxation. Moreover, it does not name the Canton of Ticino, the Brogeda border crossing or INPS. For a separate official overview of taxes for cross-border workers, see the AFC information sheet.

Frequently Asked Questions
Who signed the Protocol and when?
According to the MEF press release, the Protocol was signed on June 6, 2024, by the Minister of Economy and Finance Giancarlo Giorgetti and Federal Councillor Karin Keller-Sutter. The signing took place in Rome and Bern. The act amended the current Agreement on frontier workers and permanently governed the taxation of remote work for frontier workers.
What proportion of teleworking was envisaged?
The communiqué indicated the possibility, from January 1, 2024, of working from one's own home in a teleworking arrangement for up to 25 percent of working time. The note did not translate this percentage into a number of hours or days per week. Instead, it specified that teleworking changed neither the State entitled to tax employment income nor cross-border worker status.
Had the Protocol already entered into force on the date of signing?
The text distinguished the signing from the entry into force: the latter was expected after both countries had completed their respective internal approval procedures. The communiqué did not indicate when they would be completed, nor did it provide a subsequent update. The source, therefore, does not allow confirmation of the date of the actual entry into force.
