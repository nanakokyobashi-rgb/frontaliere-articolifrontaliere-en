The G permit is an essential document for cross-border workers in Switzerland. It is necessary to work and reside in the country. The work contract is another important document, as it establishes the terms of employment and salary.

Required documents

Required documents for cross-border workers include:

  • G permit
  • Work contract
  • Identity document
  • Certificate of residence

G permit

The G permit is issued to cross-border workers who work in Switzerland but reside in a bordering municipality. For example, a resident of Como working in Lugano must obtain this permit. The application process may vary depending on the municipality of residence, but it generally includes submitting a signed work contract and a certificate of residence.

Work contract

The work contract must be signed by both parties and must include details such as salary, working hours, and employment conditions. For example, a contract for full-time work in Bellinzona might specify a minimum salary of CHF 4,312 per month (minimum wage in Ticino in 2023).

Opening a bank account

Opening a bank account in Switzerland is often necessary to receive the salary. Swiss banks generally require a valid work permit and an identity document. For example, Banca Stato del Cantone Ticino offers specific accounts for cross-border workers with advantageous conditions.

Operational details

Practical Analysis

The practical analysis of the documents required for cross-border workers reveals that the G permit is the most important document. It is necessary to work and reside in Switzerland. The employment contract is another crucial document, as it sets the working conditions and salary. Additionally, it is essential to have Swiss health insurance, which must be taken out within three months of starting the working activity.

Required Documents

  • G Permit: This permit is issued by the Canton Ticino and allows working and residing in Switzerland. It is valid for one year and can be renewed.
  • Employment Contract: It must be drawn up in one of the official Swiss languages (Italian, French, or German) and must include details such as salary, working hours, and holidays.
  • Health Insurance: It is mandatory and must be taken out within three months of starting the working activity. The average annual cost for an adult is approximately 3,000 CHF.
  • Identity Document: Valid passport or identity card.
  • Residence Certificate: Issued by the municipality of residence in Italy, for example, for residents of Como or Varese.

Application Procedure

The application procedure for the G permit is as follows:

1. Submit the Application: The application must be submitted to the immigration service of the Canton Ticino. It can be done online or in person. 2. Provide Required Documents: Besides the documents listed above, a recent photograph and the application form must be provided. 3. Wait for Decision: The immigration service has 30 days to make a decision. If approved, the G permit is issued.

Key points

Action

To start working in Switzerland as a cross-border worker in the Canton of Ticino, you need to follow the procedure for applying for the G permit. This permit allows residents of neighboring countries to work in Switzerland without having to transfer their residence. It is essential to have all the necessary documents and follow the procedure correctly to avoid delays or rejections.

Required Documents

The documents required for the G permit application include:

  • Valid passport or identity card
  • Work contract signed by a Swiss employer
  • Certificate of residence issued by the municipality of residence in Italy (e.g., Lugano, Mendrisio, Chiasso)
  • Criminal record certificate issued by Italian authorities
  • Completed and signed application form

Procedure

The procedure to obtain the G permit may vary slightly depending on the municipality of residence. For example, in Lugano, the process may take about 4-6 weeks, while in Chiasso it might be faster, with processing times of 2-4 weeks. It is important to verify the specific requirements of your municipality of residence.

Operational Checklist

Here is an operational checklist to facilitate the process: 1. Verify requirements: Make sure you have all the necessary documents. 2. Fill out the form: Fill out the application form carefully. 3. Submit the application: Submit the application at the immigration office of your municipality of residence. 4. Wait for the response: Wait for the response from the Swiss authorities, which can take several weeks. 5. Pick up the permit: Once the application is approved, pick up the G permit at the competent office.
