The new cross-border agreement, signed on 23 December 2020 and in force since 1 January 2024, introduces new rules for the purchase of real estate in Switzerland by cross-border commuters. This agreement, ratified in Italy with Law 83 of 13 June 2023, has significant implications for those wishing to invest in real estate in the Canton of Ticino.

Constraints for non-residents

The purchase of real estate in Switzerland by non-residents is subject to specific constraints. Italian cross-border commuters who wish to purchase property in Switzerland must comply with local regulations, which may vary from canton to canton. In the canton of Ticino, for example, a special permit must be obtained for the purchase of real estate by non-residents.

Taxation and differences with the purchase of the first home

The taxation of buying real estate in Switzerland is a crucial aspect to consider. Italian cross-border commuters are subject to withholding tax on earned income, which is only withheld in Switzerland. Italy avoids double taxation with the tax credit (EC framework of 730).

Swiss rates and contributions include the AHV/IV/EO at 5.3% for employees, the ALV/AC at 1.1% with a ceiling of CHF 148,200, the UVG between 0.7% and 1.5%, and the BVG between 7% and 18% for age groups 25 and over. In Italy, personal income tax ranges from 23% up to €28,000, to 33% between €28,001 and €50,000, and to 43% over €50,000.

Procedure and Necessary Documents

The procedure for buying a property in Switzerland by Italian cross-border commuters involves the submission of a series of documents, including the residence permit, the employment contract, and the tax return. It is also necessary to obtain authorization from the competent local authorities, such as the Federal Tax Office (FTA/ESTV) and the cantonal tax administrations.

What-if scenarios

A cross-border commuter in a similar situation may face several challenges, including the need to obtain authorization to purchase a property and the management of taxation in both Switzerland and Italy. For example, a cross-border commuter who wants to buy a property in the Canton of Ticino will need to submit an application for authorization to the local authorities and prove that they are eligible for the purchase.

Comparisons with the previous situation

Before the new cross-border agreement came into force, the rules for the purchase of real estate by non-residents were less clear and often more restrictive. With the new agreement, procedures have been simplified and permits are more easily obtained, as long as the requirements set by the local authorities are met.
