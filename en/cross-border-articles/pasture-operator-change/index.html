At Pasture, the operator changes: jobs at risk (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 25 September 2026

Starting in January, the federal mandate for the asylum center will pass from AOZ to ORS: in Ticino, 120 employees are currently working, but not all of them will be rehired.

Context

In brief

  • The federal mandate will pass from AOZ to ORS in January
  • ORS will not rehire all employees currently in service
  • In Ticino, there are currently 120 employees in service

Key facts

  • What: change of operator for assistance
  • Where: Pasture
  • When: in January
  • Current operator: AOZ
  • New operator: ORS
  • Personnel indicated for Ticino: 120 employees
  • Announced outcome: not all will be rehired

At Pasture, the federal mandate for the asylum centre will pass from AOZ to ORS in January. The news brings together two elements: the change of operator for assistance and the possible effect on staffing. ORS, in fact, will not rehire all employees currently in service.

The available data concerns Ticino: today there are 120 employees in service. The source does not specify how many work at Pasture, how many are employed in assisting the asylum centre and how many will be rehired by the new operator. For this reason, the precise number of jobs at risk is not indicated. The title points to employment risk, but the text does not allow it to be converted into a figure.

What changes in January

Before the changeover, the federal mandate for the asylum centre is entrusted to AOZ; from January it will pass to ORS. The sequence is clear, while the exact date of the change, the reasons for the replacement and the criteria by which ORS will determine which employees to rehire are not included in the news. Nor are any individual communications, contractual conditions or timelines for personnel indicated.

For those who work in the assistance sector at Pasture, the concrete issue is therefore continuity with the new operator. The source confirms that it will not concern all employees currently in service, but does not say how many will remain, how many will leave or whether the overall number will change in a clearly defined way.

The figure not to confuse

The 120 employees mentioned is the figure referring to Ticino today. They are not presented as the number of positions available at ORS and are not described as the number of people who will lose their jobs. This distinction is essential for reading the news correctly and for not attributing to the source information that it does not contain.

The possible connection with cross-border work is limited: the decision concerns employment in Ticino. The source, however, does not indicate how many cross-border commuters are among the 120 employees or whether the change involves people with a G permit. Any individual assessment therefore remains tied to information about the individual employment relationship, which is not detailed in the article.

Operational details

Per chi lavora nel Canton Ticino, il passaggio ha una conseguenza pratica precisa: il riferimento operativo indicato dalla notizia cambia da AOZ a ORS, ma la continuità non è garantita per l'intero personale. Non è però possibile leggere il cambio come una modifica generale del mercato del lavoro o come una nuova regola per tutti i lavoratori. La fonte riguarda il mandato federale per il Centro d'asilo di Pasture e i dipendenti oggi in servizio in Ticino.

Il confronto aiuta a separare ciò che è già definito da ciò che non lo è. Prima, AOZ è l'operatore collegato al mandato. Dopo, ORS subentra a gennaio e non riassume tutti i dipendenti in servizio. La notizia non dice se il personale escluso sarà sostituito, se il servizio avrà una dimensione diversa o se ci saranno altre posizioni. Non si possono quindi costruire scenari numerici.

Per un frontaliere che lavorasse nell'attività interessata, il punto sarebbe verificare se il proprio posto rientra tra quelli riassunti. Ma il testo non pubblica un elenco, non distingue tra residenti e frontalieri e non indica quanti dei 120 siano lavoratori italiani. Il totale del Ticino non può essere trasformato in una stima dell'impatto sulla mobilità transfrontaliera.

Il perimetro informativo è altrettanto importante. Nel testo non compaiono riferimenti a permesso G/B, AVS, LPP, LAMal, ristorni Ticino-Italia o imposta alla fonte. Per questo la notizia non consente di ricavare scadenze, diritti, trattenute o procedure su questi temi. Sono elementi estranei al contenuto disponibile, non spiegazioni del cambio di operatore.

Nessun calcolo automatico dei posti

Quando l'articolo dice che non tutti saranno riassunti, comunica una riduzione rispetto all'intero gruppo, ma non una percentuale. L'unico numero certo resta 120, riferito ai dipendenti in Ticino oggi; manca il dato sulla sede di Pasture e manca il numero dei riassunti. Anche per un frontaliere, quindi, non è corretto dedurre un rischio individuale dal solo fatto che il mandato cambi. La fonte non afferma che ORS stia assumendo e non indica posizioni alternative.

Key points

For those directly concerned, the possible action is above all an orderly verification of the information, without attributing to the text documents or deadlines it does not contain.

Four possible checks

1. Delimit the case. The news concerns the federal mandate for the Pasture Asylum Center. Working in Ticino, by itself, is not enough to say that one’s position is affected.

2. Compare the two operators. AOZ is the name linked to the mandate before the change; ORS is the new operator indicated for January. The month is the only time reference provided.

3. Separate the general data from the personal data. The 120 employees refer to Ticino today. They are not 120 positions declared at risk, nor are they the number of employees ORS will rehire.

4. Verify the reabsorption. The source makes clear that ORS will not take on all the employees, but it does not say who will be rehired, within what timeframe, or according to which criteria. Without these elements, it is not possible to complete the verification of the individual case.

This procedure does not replace individual communication; it serves to avoid confusing the change of operator with an already quantified dismissal. The text does not indicate offices, forms, documents, contacts, number of positions, the exact date in January, or contractual conditions. Consequently, it is not possible to invent administrative steps or indicate an application to submit.

For the cross-border reader, the same caution applies to topics that are not in the news. Possession of a G permit, calculation of withholding tax, AVS, LPP, or LAMal are not linked to the change. The source does not even say whether the worker concerned lives across the border. Any further investigation into these instruments must remain separate from the Pasture report.

If the goal is to monitor possible alternatives, the annunci di lavoro can be consulted, without presenting results not cited as ORS offers. If, instead, one wants to estimate the economic effect of a personal scenario, the news provides no information on salary, working hours, allowances, or deductions. Frequently Asked Questions
When will the Pasture Asylum Centre operator change?
The federal mandate for the Pasture Asylum Center will shift from AOZ to ORs in January. The source does not indicate the exact day of the changeover and does not explain the reasons for the replacement. The certain figure is therefore the month indicated and the change between the two operators.
How many employees are indicated for Ticino?
The source indicates 120 employees currently in service in Ticino. It does not specify how many work in Pasture or how many belong to the assistance of the Asylum Center. The number therefore does not automatically coincide with the seats that will be maintained by ORs or with those at risk.
Will ORs resume all staff?
No. The text specifies that ORs will not summarize all employees on duty. However, it is not indicated how many will be summarized, how many will be excluded or how decisions will be made. For this reason, the news reports an occupational risk, but does not quantify job losses.
Does the source indicate how many cross-border commuters are involved?
No. The news concerns a work activity in Ticino, but does not indicate how many border workers are among the 120 employees and does not specify who holds a G permit. It does not even provide data on taxation, social security or individual procedures: these elements cannot be derived from the text.
