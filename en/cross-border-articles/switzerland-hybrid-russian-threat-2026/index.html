Switzerland under pressure: rising Russian hybrid threat (cross-border guide)

Di Valerie Linc · Published on 7 May 2026

The Federal Council warns: Russian espionage, cyberattacks, and sabotage threaten Swiss security. Here's what changes for cross-border workers.

Context

In brief

  • The Russian threat is the main one for Swiss security
  • Moscow uses sabotage, disinformation, and cyberattacks
  • Switzerland is hit by espionage and attacks on critical infrastructures

Key facts

  • What: Annual report of the Federal Council on security
  • When: Approved on March 15, 2026
  • Where: Switzerland, with impact on Canton Ticino and cross-border workers
  • Who: Swiss Federal Council
  • Amount: 30 mentions of Russia in the report

The security situation in Switzerland has worsened significantly in recent years, with a worrying acceleration in 2025. This is what emerges from the annual report of the Federal Council on the state of the threat approved yesterday, Wednesday, which outlines an increasingly unstable international scenario and a Confederation directly exposed to the hybrid war conducted by Russia. Geopolitical developments also affect internal security, which is confronted with the terrorist threat, violent extremism of the right and left, the threat of espionage, and attacks on critical infrastructures.

The Russian threat

Russia remains the main threat to security, stability, and peace in Europe, states the Federal Council's assessment. Not surprisingly, Moscow is explicitly mentioned 30 times in the document, more than any other state actor, testifying to the centrality of the Russian threat to Swiss and European security. The Kremlin aims to restore its sphere of influence in Eastern Europe through a global and lasting conflict against the West, using hybrid means: sabotage, disinformation campaigns, cyberattacks, and espionage.

Practical Implications for Cross-Border Workers

The Russian hybrid threat and the strategic uncertainties of the United States could have a significant impact on the daily lives of cross-border workers in Ticino. It is important to be aware of the risks and to adopt adequate security measures to protect personal and professional information.

Cybersecurity

Cyberattacks represent a growing threat to critical infrastructures and Swiss companies. Cross-border workers should adopt advanced cybersecurity measures to protect their data and that of the companies they work for. This includes the use of complex passwords, regular software updates, and the adoption of advanced security solutions.

Industrial Espionage

The threat of industrial espionage is very high. Major powers operate in Switzerland through networks of undercover officers, targeting government institutions, international organizations, universities, and innovative companies. Cross-border workers should be aware of this threat and adopt adequate security measures to protect the sensitive information of the companies they work for.

Economic Impact

The strategic uncertainties of the United States and the Russian hybrid threat could have a significant impact on the Swiss economy and job opportunities for cross-border workers. It is important to closely monitor the situation and adopt adequate economic security measures to protect investments and career opportunities.
