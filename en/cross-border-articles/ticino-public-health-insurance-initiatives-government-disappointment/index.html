Health insurance initiatives: Lega and PS disappointed after meeting with Ticino government (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 13 April 2026

The two popular initiatives approved on 28 September 2025 face opposition from the State Council. Disappointed expectations for the promoters: no direct implementation announced by the government

Context

TL;DR

  • Ticino government met with initiative promoters on April 13, 2026.
  • Two initiatives aim to cap premiums at 10% of income or make them fully tax-deductible.
  • Government plans phased implementation, but promoters demand immediate action.

Key facts

  • Meeting Date: April 13, 2026
  • Initiatives Approval: September 28, 2025
  • Premium Cap: 10% of disposable income
  • Tax Deductibility: Full deductibility of premiums
  • Estimated Cost: 400 million Swiss francs
  • Official Announcement: April 15, 2026
  • Cross-Border Workers: 60% earn below 60,000 francs annually
  • Current Premium Increase: 5-7% annually

In Bellinzona, the Ticino government met on Monday, April 13, 2026, with representatives from the Ticino League and the Socialist Party, the two groups behind the initiatives on mitigating health insurance premiums approved by voters last September 28, 2025. The meeting, held at Palazzo delle Orsoline, consisted of two separate sessions of about forty minutes each in the afternoon. The State Council, led by the Ticino president, provided the initiative promoters with some early insights into its proposed measures to implement the two initiatives, without delving into specific details.

The initiatives’ requests

The two initiatives, approved by popular vote, propose different measures: the one backed by the Socialist Party caps health insurance premiums at 10% of disposable income, while the Ticino League’s initiative calls for the full tax deductibility of premiums. To date, the Ticino government has not yet presented a detailed strategy for implementation, only outlining general guidelines in the weeks prior. The initiative promoters have expressed concerns from the outset, but after Monday’s meeting, their disappointment has grown.

«If the initiative is not implemented directly and in full, the League will not be satisfied» — Daniele Piccaluga, coordinator of the Ticino League

The Socialist Party’s reaction

The Socialist Party has also expressed dissatisfaction. Laura Riget, co-president of the party, stated: «We are not satisfied, and what we can say at this point is what we have been forced to reiterate for months: you cannot toy with the will of the people.» Riget emphasized the need for swift and concrete implementation with an immediate impact on citizens hardest hit by rising health insurance premiums. According to the Socialist leader, it is unacceptable to delay the implementation of these measures while waiting for financial coverage.

«It must have an effect on the wallets of citizens who can no longer cope with the explosion of health insurance premiums» — Laura Riget, co-president of the Ticino Socialist Party

📊 The cost issue and phased implementation

A critical point is the State Council’s intention to tie the implementation of the initiatives to the availability of necessary financial resources. Estimates suggest that the long-term costs could amount to around 400 million francs. The Ticino government has already indicated it will proceed with a phased implementation, though the timeline and modalities have not yet been defined. Laura Riget stressed that, in the Socialist Party’s view, implementation should not be contingent on funding.

«This is a clear red line for us. In our opinion, implementation should not be legally tied to financing» — Laura Riget

Waiting for the official announcement

All parties involved agreed to maintain the utmost discretion until the official presentation of the measures, scheduled for Wednesday, April 15, 2026. On Tuesday, April 14, the details previewed today to the Ticino League and Socialist Party will also be shared with the other political parties in Ticino. Meanwhile, the initiative promoters are preparing to assess the State Council’s message, which will contain the concrete plan to implement the two initiatives. Only then will it be possible to determine whether the Ticino government intends to proceed with direct implementation or, instead, opt for a gradual approach tied to available resources.

💡 Key takeaway

The government’s cautious stance on the financial feasibility of the initiatives has raised concerns among initiative backers, who insist on immediate and unconditional action to address the burden of rising health insurance premiums on citizens.

Operational details

For cross-border workers and Ticinese residents who commute daily between Italy and Switzerland, the wait for the implementation of health insurance initiatives is a source of uncertainty. The two proposals, approved by popular vote, aim to ease the financial burden of insurance premiums, but their implementation risks being delayed due to disagreements between the Ticinese Government and the initiative promoters. Let’s explore the possible scenarios and their implications for those who live and work between the two countries.

Scenario 1: Direct and immediate implementation

If the Ticino State Council were to decide to implement the initiatives without budget constraints, cross-border workers and Ticinese residents could benefit from an immediate reduction in health insurance premiums as early as next year. According to estimates by the Socialist Party, limiting premiums to 10% of disposable income could ease the financial load for middle- to low-income households. For example, a family household with an annual income of 60,000 Swiss francs could save up to 6,000 francs per year on insurance premiums, depending on the chosen health insurance provider.

However, this scenario assumes that the Ticino Government will find an alternative financial cover, such as reallocating existing resources or introducing additional taxation. At the moment, neither option has been officially confirmed.

Scenario 2: Phased and conditional implementation

The gradual approach would introduce a transition phase during which measures would be rolled out progressively. According to insights shared by Laura Riget, the Ticino State Council seems inclined toward this solution, though without providing details on timing. In this case, cross-border workers might need to wait at least two or three years before seeing any concrete benefits. For instance, in the first year, only a partial deductibility of premiums from taxes might be applied, while the limit to 10% of disposable income would come into effect only later.

For those who commute daily between Italy and Switzerland, this scenario would add further uncertainty when planning family expenses. Cross-border workers must already cope with high costs such as the minimum deductible for health insurance (currently set at 300 Swiss francs), which could undergo unannounced changes.

With the rise of premiums, many cross-border workers have already opted for less expensive but less comprehensive insurance solutions, or have skipped preventive care to contain expenses.

Current challenges for cross-border workers

Cross-border workers face several ongoing challenges related to health insurance in Ticino:

  • High annual premium increases (5-7%).
  • Additional costs like the minimum deductible (300 Swiss francs) and co-payments.
  • Limited options for less expensive insurance, often resulting in reduced coverage.
  • Financial planning uncertainty due to potential changes in premium structures.

The two initiatives, if implemented as proposed, could address some of these challenges by introducing cost-containment measures and income-based premium limits. However, the timeline and scope of implementation remain subject to political negotiations and financial constraints.

Frontier workers employed in Ticino but residing in Italy are particularly vulnerable to rising health insurance premiums, as they must bear Swiss insurance costs while often earning less than their locally based colleagues. According to estimates from the Ticino Department of Finance and Economy (DFE), around 60% of frontier workers have an annual income below 60,000 francs, making them highly exposed to premium fluctuations.

If the proposed measures are implemented, frontier workers could:

  • Benefit from an immediate reduction in premiums, if the measures are rolled out directly.
  • See the benefits delayed due to a gradual approach.
  • Face new tax rules if the Lega succeeds in making premiums fully tax-deductible.

At present, neither of these scenarios is certain. The only official data available comes from Monday’s meeting on April 13, which raised more questions than it answered.

Key points

The official presentation of the measures by the Ticino State Council, scheduled for Wednesday, 15 April 2026, marks a crucial moment for cross-border workers and residents. Below is a practical guide to navigating the next steps and understanding potential changes to your health insurance situation.

Step 1: Prepare for the official presentation

On Wednesday, 15 April at 3:00 PM, the State Council will unveil its proposal outlining measures to implement the two health insurance initiatives. The event will take place at Palazzo delle Orsline in Bellinzona and will be broadcast live on the official Canton of Ticino website. It is essential to follow the announcement to determine whether the Government plans to implement the measures directly or adopt a gradual approach.

⚠️ Warning: It is currently unclear whether the measures will be applied retroactively or only from 2027 onwards. The State Council has not provided precise indications, so it is advisable to closely monitor the presentation.

Step 2: Assess the impact on your health insurance

Depending on the State Council’s decisions, cross-border workers may need to adjust their health insurance policies. Here’s what to do:

  • If a 10% limit on disposable income is introduced: Check whether your current insurance already complies with this threshold. If not, compare offers from different health insurance providers to find a more affordable solution.
  • If full deductibility of premiums is approved: Review whether your tax situation in Italy allows you to benefit from this relief. In this case, consulting a tax advisor specialising in Swiss-Italian taxation could be helpful.

To simplify the evaluation, the website Frontaliere Ticino offers an insurance cost calculator to compare options based on your income and household composition.

Step 3: Monitor official communications

The Ticino State Council will publish the official proposal and related annexes on its institutional website. It is important to subscribe to the DFE Ticino newsletter or follow the Canton’s social media accounts to receive real-time updates. Additionally, representatives from the League and the Socialist Party have already announced they will assess the Government’s proposals and may organise protest initiatives if the measures are deemed unsatisfactory.

Step 4: Plan for healthcare expenses

While awaiting further clarification, consider the following: 1. Review your household budget to assess the impact of health insurance premiums on your monthly expenses. 2. Contact your health insurance provider to inquire about any potential changes to policies that may be introduced. 3. Evaluate alternative solutions, such as increasing your deductible or switching to a more affordable health insurance provider, if necessary.

Step 5: Engage in the political debate

The League and the Socialist Party have already announced they will assess the Government’s proposals and may collect signatures for a new initiative or promote a parliamentary motion. Cross-border workers interested in participating in the debate can:

  • Subscribe to party newsletters to receive updates.
  • Attend public meetings organised in Ticino’s municipalities.
  • Contact their political representatives to share their views.

What happens if the initiatives are not implemented?

If the State Council decides to postpone or scale back the measures, cross-border workers should prepare for further premium increases. In this scenario, it may be worth exploring alternative solutions, such as:

  • Switching to a more affordable health insurance provider, even if it offers less comprehensive coverage.
  • Taking out a supplementary insurance policy to cover expenses not included in the basic plan.
  • Evaluating the possibility of transferring your residence to Italy, if financially feasible.

In any case, staying informed and acting promptly will be key to minimising the financial impact of the new regulations on your budget.
