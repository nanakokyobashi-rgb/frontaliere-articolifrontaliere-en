New episode in Lissago

During the night between Friday, October 16, and Saturday, October 17, another door in the «large courtyard» in Lissago was broken down. The same couple, already flagged, reported and removed, entered the apartment. On the morning of Saturday, October 17, State Police officers identified the two occupants and reported them again.

The new report comes as the issue is being addressed on several fronts. Municipal council committees are meeting, the issue is being discussed, local police and State Police are deploying officers and filing reports. Social services are also taking action to try to reach those responsible. Despite this sequence of interventions, the occupations in the courtyard are not stopping.

The «large courtyard» comprises 18 apartments used as public housing. The text describes a vicious circle: a door is forced open, occupants enter, identification and a report follow, but the problem resurfaces. The repetition of the occupations keeps the issue open for those living in the area and for the administrations called upon to manage it.

The Municipality has identified two courses of action and a third, more complex one, for which it nevertheless intends to try every possible avenue.

Evictions, renovation and assistance

The first course of action concerns evictions and sealing. Some occupied apartments have already been subjected to them; the same measures should also be extended to the new homes where the «squatters» have broken down the doors.

The second involves renovating the courtyard. The aim is to obtain funding from a regional grant program to make the 15 apartments currently uninhabitable available, the ones that are systematically occupied. Repopulating the courtyard, according to the approach indicated by the Municipality, would help prevent new occupations and the public-order and hygiene problems connected to the situation.

The third course of action combines the reports, which have now been repeated for weeks, with an attempt to reach the two occupants and bring them back into the network of people assisted by social services. This path is made difficult by the occupants’ own opposition, as they have been known for years to those who provide assistance in the city.

Meanwhile, Lissago residents continue to keep watch and report the new occupations. Their expectation is that a definitive solution will be found, one capable of restoring peace to the nights and dignity to the neighborhood.
