Freight traffic in the Alps: the train goes backwards and the trucks grow (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 12 September 2026

The Federal Office of Transport records a growth in road transport equal to twice that of rail transport along the Alps.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Growth of rail freight transport stopped at 3.1%
  • 7% increase for road transport by lorries
  • Rail market share fell to 68% of the total
  • Expected to exceed one million trucks by the end of the year

Key facts

  • What: Trend in transalpine freight traffic
  • When: Recent half-yearly data released by the UFT
  • Where: North-south transit axis of the Alps
  • Who: Federal Office of Transport UFT
  • Amount: 511,000 trucks already transited in the period

More and more goods cross the Swiss Alps, but road transport grows more than twice as fast as rail transport. This is reported by the Federal Office of Transport in its semi-annual reports. Rail freight traffic grew by 3.1%, while truck transport increased by 7%. As a result, the rail sector saw its market share decrease by 0.9 percentage points to 68%. Meanwhile, 511,000 trucks have already been transited and the UFT predicts that, by the end of the year and for the first time in a long time, the overall share will once again exceed the threshold of one million heavy vehicles along the crossings. This evolution of transit flows directly affects the overall mobility and infrastructure also used daily by frontier workers and commuters along the main road routes.

Logistical factors and construction sites

Operational details

The repercussions on mobility and cross-border flows

The strong growth of road transport along the alpine passes inevitably affects the overall management of the road and highway network connecting Italy to Switzerland. Cross-border workers who cross the border every day to go to work in Canton Ticino find themselves sharing the same communication arteries with a growing flow of heavy vehicles and trucks. When rail transport experiences slowdowns or disruptions related to international construction sites, the pressure inevitably shifts to the road network, influencing travel times and the punctuality of commuters. The increase in the volume of transiting trucks requires constant attention to traffic conditions and the management of strategic customs crossings. For those who travel regularly for professional reasons, monitoring the state of traffic becomes essential to organize daily commutes and avoid long waits at the most frequented crossing points such as Brogeda or Gaggiolo.

Data analysis and modal comparison

The gap recorded between the +3.1% for trains and +7% for road transport highlights an opposing trend compared to the historical objectives of transferring freight traffic from road to rail. The reduction in the rail market share, which dropped to 68%, demonstrates how external factors, including the construction sites open in Germany expected until 2030, heavily impact the competitiveness of the rail carrier. This dynamic also affects supply methods and regional logistics, altering the balance of goods flows that feed the Swiss and Ticino economies. Frequently Asked Questions
What is the percentage growth of road freight transport compared to rail?
Truck transport grew by 7%, while rail transport stopped at an increase of 3.1%, according to data released by the Federal Office of Transport.
What are the main causes of disruption to rail freight?
The decrease in the importance of rail transport is linked to the inconvenience on the north-south axis caused by construction sites in Germany, a situation that is expected to persist until 2030.
How many trucks have already been transited and what are the forecasts for the end of the year?
In the reference period, 511,000 trucks have already transited. The Federal Office of Transport expects that by the end of the year the total share will once again exceed the threshold of one million heavy goods vehicles for the first time in a long time.
