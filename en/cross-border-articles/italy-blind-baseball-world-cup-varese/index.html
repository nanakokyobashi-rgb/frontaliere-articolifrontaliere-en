Italy are world champions in blind baseball

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 4 October 2026

Italy triumphs in Castiglione della Pescaia: Cuba defeated 10-4. Varese players Oliveri (MVP) and Trombini (best young player) were the protagonists.

Context

In brief

  • Italy world champions in Castiglione della Pescaia
  • Cuba defeated 10-4 in the sixth inning
  • Danny Oliveri MVP, Fabio Trombini best young player
  • Vuolo and Boscardi on the Italian staff

Key facts

  • Event → Blind Baseball International Cup
  • Location → Castiglione della Pescaia
  • Final → Italy-Cuba 10-4
  • Manager → Marco Corazza
  • MVP → Danny Oliveri
  • Best young player → Fabio Trombini
  • Coaches → Francesco Vuolo and Matteo Boscardi
  • Batting → Danny Oliveri 5 for 7

Castiglione della Pescaia hosted the final of the Blind Baseball International Cup, which ended with the world title for Italy. The Azzurri, led by manager Marco Corazza, defeated Cuba 10-4, reaching the final result in the sixth inning. The game concluded a tournament characterized by outstanding performances and strong representation from the Varese area.

A victory with roots in Varese

The Italian success indeed puts several figures linked to the province of Varese in the spotlight. Among the coaches of the Italian team were Francesco Vuolo and Matteo Boscardi, identified as coaches from Varese and as pillars of the local and national movement. The area’s presence did not stop at the bench, but also involved the protagonists on the field.

Fabio Trombini, a player for Hurricane Cisv Saronno, was among the most important performers in the competition. Alongside him, Danny Oliveri, a Varese resident by adoption and former Hurricane player, stood out. Oliveri finished the tournament 5 for 7 at bat and was named MVP of the event.

The recognition awarded to Trombini was that of best young player of the entire World Championship. The Italian team thus collected results and individual awards, with the contribution from Varese distributed between the technical leadership and the field. The title won against Cuba represents the culmination of this journey, but the story of the victory also includes the roles held by the protagonists linked to Varese and Saronno.

The performance in the first part took the Azzurri into the knockout matches. Here the team had to confirm what it had shown in the previous games, facing opponents at a stage with no room for mistakes. In the semifinal, Italy overcame Great Britain 11-4, securing access to the final against Cuba.

The sequence of the final

The title game began uphill. In the second inning, Cuba took a 1-0 lead after a defensive mistake by Italy. In the third inning, however, the Azzurri turned the situation around by scoring three runs with two outs. The reaction did not end the game: in the fourth inning, the Cuban team regained the lead, moving ahead 4-3.

The turning point came in the fifth inning. Italy took advantage of four hits and two errors by the opponents to put together a five-run inning. The score thus moved to 8-4, creating the decisive margin in the closing part of the game.

In the sixth inning, the Azzurri added two more runs, reaching the final score of 10-4. A defensive double play closed out the game. The progression of the game shows the shift from the initial deficit to the comeback, and finally the breakaway that gave Italy the world blind baseball title.

Key points

The Varese-area contribution to the title

Bench and field

The local character of the victory emerges from the makeup of the Italian contingent. The Varese area is represented both in the coaching staff and among the players who received the tournament's main individual awards. The two Varese coaches, Francesco Vuolo and Matteo Boscardi, worked from the bench, described as fundamental guides for the team and pillars of the local and national movement.

On the field, the key figure is Danny Oliveri. The player, who made Varese his home and is a former Hurricane, was one of the competition's standout performers. His performance is summed up by his 5-for-7 at the plate, while the MVP award named him the best player in the tournament.

Individual awards

Alongside Oliveri, the event also honored Fabio Trombini. The Hurricane Cisv Saronno player was recognized as the best young player of the entire World Championship. The two awards highlight two different aspects of Italy's performance: on the one hand, the impact of a player named MVP; on the other, the emergence of a young player from the Saronno team.

The Varese-area tally therefore includes two coaches, an MVP and the competition's best young player. This is not merely a numerical presence in the contingent, but roles and results explicitly highlighted in the account of the tournament. The bench led by Vuolo and Boscardi, Oliveri's performance and the recognition earned by Trombini together form the local dimension of the success.

With the title won in Castiglione della Pescaia, Italy concludes a tournament in which the Varese contribution was visible in every decisive area: in the team's leadership, on-field performance and the awards presented at the end of the competition.
