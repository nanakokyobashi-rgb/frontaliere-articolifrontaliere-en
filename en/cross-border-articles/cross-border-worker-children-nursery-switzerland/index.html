Guide for cross-border workers: Ticino childcare (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 14 June 2026

Everything you need to know about costs, waiting lists, subsidies, and alternatives for cross-border workers with preschool-age children in Ticino

Context

In brief

  • Cross-border workers can enroll their children in Ticino's nurseries
  • There are waiting lists managed by the municipalities
  • Subsidies are available for low-income families
  • Alternative Italian options exist for children

Key facts

  • What: Nursery enrollment for cross-border workers
  • When: Annual enrollments, with waiting lists
  • Where: Municipalities of Ticino
  • Who: Cross-border workers with preschool-age children
  • Cost: Variable costs, subsidies for families

The Canton of Ticino offers nurseries for resident children, including those of cross-border worker parents. Enrollment takes place annually by the end of February for the following year, and waiting lists are managed at the municipal level. For example, in Lugano, parents must enroll by February 28 for the next school year. Costs vary based on family income: for a family with an income of 60,000 francs, the monthly cost can be around 800 francs. Subsidies are available for low-income families, as established by the Cantonal Law on the allocation of subsidies for extrafamilial childcare of July 1, 2015. For instance, a family with an income of 40,000 francs can receive a subsidy of 400 francs per month. > 'Subsidies are crucial to make nurseries accessible to low-income cross-border worker families,' states a representative of the Ticino Education Office. For cross-border workers, it's essential to plan ahead and inquire with specific Ticino municipalities to learn about waiting list details and available subsidies.

Operational details

Waiting lists and registration

Enrollment in Ticino nurseries generally takes place between January and March for the following year. Cross-border workers must submit their application to the municipalities where they reside in Italy, providing the required documentation, including their residence permit and Swiss payslip. For example, the Municipality of Chiasso requires applications to be submitted by March 31st for the following year, with a registration fee of CHF 50.

"It is crucial to respect deadlines to avoid the risk of losing your place," says the head of nurseries for the Municipality of Lugano.

Costs and subsidies

Nursery costs vary depending on the municipality and family income. For example, in Lugano, the monthly cost can range from CHF 150 to CHF 1,200 depending on income. Low-income families can benefit from cantonal subsidies, as provided for by the 2013 Cantonal Law on the allocation of subsidies for day care of children (LAsCD), which provides subsidies of up to CHF 1,050 per month. Financial documentation must be submitted to determine the subsidy amount.

  • Checklist for documentation:
  • Swiss payslip
  • Residence permit
  • Italian and Swiss tax documents

Italian alternatives

For families who cannot find a place in Ticino nurseries, there are Italian alternatives in the border municipalities. For example, in Como, rates start from € 150 per month. These facilities offer competitive rates and can be a viable option for cross-border workers, especially when considering savings on registration fees and additional costs.

Key points

Step-by-step enrollment procedure

1. Check the enrollment requirements on the website of your municipality of residence, for example in Lugano or Bellinzona, as the terms may vary. 2. Prepare the required documentation, such as G permit, payslip, and birth certificate of the child. 3. Submit the enrollment application within the established deadline, usually by the end of March for the following year. 4. Wait for the response and check your position on the waiting list; some municipalities like Locarno regularly publish updates. 5. If you don't get a spot, consider Italian alternatives, taking into account the differences in costs and services offered.

FAQ:

Frequently Asked Questions
Can I enroll my child in a Ticino daycare if I'm a cross-border worker?
Yes, cross-border workers can enroll their children in Ticino daycare centers, provided they reside in Ticino.
How are the waiting lists for daycare centers managed?
Waiting lists are managed at the municipal level. It's necessary to submit an application within the established deadlines.
Are subsidies available for cross-border worker families?
Yes, low-income families can benefit from cantonal subsidies. It's necessary to submit financial documentation.
