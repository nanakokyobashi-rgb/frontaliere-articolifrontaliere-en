Stabio-Gaggiolo speed bump: queues and anger from cross-border workers (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 12 September 2026

Reports about the speed bump just beyond Gaggiolo customs describe more than ten extra minutes every day. Official counts show a crossing already under pressure.

Context

The pinch point just beyond customs

Our newsroom has been collecting reports from cross-border workers travelling through Gaggiolo towards Stabio. The point they identify is the speed bump immediately beyond the crossing: during peak periods, the slowing effect adds to checks and merging traffic, turning a few seconds lost by each car into a queue that spreads along the road.

According to the reports shared by drivers, the journey now takes more than 10 minutes longer than usual. Queues are said to start around 05:00 on weekdays, and the problem is also reported on public holidays. These are local reports, not certified measurements: without a before-and-after comparison, we cannot attribute the entire phenomenon to the bump alone.

The anger among cross-border workers is understandable. People leaving before daylight to arrive at work on time see their safety margin disappear every morning; those crossing on holidays face the same bottleneck. Residents are affected too, as recurring queues bring noise, braking and diversions onto nearby roads.

Operational details

The figures confirm that the crossing is under pressure

The official 2025 count by the Osservatorio Ambientale della Svizzera Italiana at station N24 Stabio-Gaggiolo records 1,083 vehicles per hour between 05:00 and 06:00 on weekdays and 1,152 between 06:00 and 07:00. The daily average reaches 18,991 vehicles on weekdays, 15,836 on Saturday and 12,489 on Sunday. The figures do not prove that the speed bump causes the queues, but they show why a capacity reduction at a sensitive point could become visible very early in the day.

Corriere del Ticino described via Gaggiolo as one of the most congested routes and reported flows of around 1,000–1,100 vehicles per hour between 05:00 and 20:00. The public discussion is not starting from zero: the Municipality of Stabio has called traffic on via Gaggiolo and via Cantonale unsatisfactory, linking it to safety and liveability, while a local petition in 2015 collected around 1,200 requests for safer crossings.

The choice is not between safety and flow. It is about checking whether the current solution is proportionate to the real traffic and whether alternatives — a lower bump, a different geometry or effective signs — can protect pedestrians and residents without adding ten minutes to thousands of journeys every day.

Key points

The request: public data and a proportionate solution

We ask the competent authorities to publish the technical reason, installation date and speed limits associated with the bump. We also ask for comparable measurements of journey times and queues before and after the intervention, on weekdays and public holidays. Only then can it be decided whether the bump should be removed or lowered, or whether its configuration should change, without questioning road safety.
