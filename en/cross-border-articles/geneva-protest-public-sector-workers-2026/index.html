3,000 officials protest austerity measures in Geneva (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 8 May 2026

Between 2,500 and 3,000 people protested in Geneva against the austerity measures planned by the Canton, including wage freezes and indexation freezes.

Context

In brief

  • 3,000 officials demonstrate in Geneva against austerity measures
  • Wage increases frozen for 4 years and indexation frozen
  • Cuts of 533 million francs without union consultation

Key facts

  • What: Protest against austerity measures
  • When: May 7, 2026
  • Where: Geneva, starting from Parc des Cropettes
  • Who: Inter-union committee of state employees and subsidized sector
  • Amount: Cuts of 533 million francs

Between 2,500 and 3,000 people demonstrated today in Geneva, initiated by the Inter-union committee of state employees and the subsidized sector, to protest against the austerity measures planned by the Canton. Starting from Parc des Cropettes, the procession crossed the Monte Bianco bridge before reaching the old town, where the Grand Council is located. 'Salaries under attack, services at risk,' chanted the protesters, who have taken to the streets for the fifth time since November. 'Our work is essential, our salary is existential,' read the signs. The procession followed a strike. The unions and state employees remain mobilized against the austerity measures planned in the 2026 bis budget project. They particularly denounce the freeze on wage increases for 2026 and the three following years, as well as the freezing of indexation. The Inter-union committee estimates wage losses of up to 5.1%.

Operational details

Analysis of Savings Measures

The savings measures announced by the Canton of Geneva will have a significant impact on public sector workers and, indirectly, on cross-border workers employed in Switzerland. The freeze on salary increments for four years and the suspension of indexation represent an estimated wage loss of up to 5.1%, according to the Interunion Committee. This means that workers will see a decrease in their purchasing power, especially in a context of inflation.

Comparison with the Previous Situation

Before the austerity measures, public sector workers benefited from annual salary increases and indexation that took inflation into account. With the new budget project, not only are the increases frozen, but indexation is also suspended, meaning that salaries will not keep up with rising prices. This could lead to a decrease in the standard of living for many workers, especially those with lower salaries.

Scenarios for Cross-Border Workers

For cross-border workers employed in the public sector in Geneva, these measures could mean a reduction in disposable income. With the freeze on salaries and the increase in the cost of living, many may need to review their budget and consider alternative options. Some might decide to seek employment in other Swiss cantons or in Italy, where economic conditions might be more favorable. However, it is important to note that the savings measures are not yet final and may be subject to changes following negotiations with the unions.

Key points

What to do if you are a cross-border worker

If you are a cross-border worker employed in the public sector in Geneva, it is crucial that you are aware of the planned austerity measures and their potential impact on your income and lifestyle. Here are some steps you can take to prepare:

1. Stay informed: Keep up-to-date with the latest news regarding austerity measures and negotiations between the government and unions. You can find updated information on the Cartello intersindacale website or on the websites of local unions.

2. Review your budget: With wage freezes and rising living costs, it might be helpful to review your budget and identify areas where you can cut expenses. Also consider increasing your savings to tackle any potential future financial difficulties.

3. Explore alternative options: If the austerity measures become definitive, you might want to explore alternative options to increase your income. This could include looking for a new job in another Swiss canton or in Italy, or seeking opportunities for training or professional development that could enhance your career prospects.

4. Participate in consultations: If you are a union member, participate in the consultations and assemblies organized by your union to discuss the austerity measures and possible responses. Your voice is important and can make a difference.

5. Use available tools: If you need assistance managing your finances or exploring alternative options, you can use the tools available on the Frontaliere Ticino website. For example, the salary calculator can help you estimate your net income after any potential pay cuts, while the permit comparator can help you evaluate job options in other Swiss cantons.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the savings measures planned by the Canton of Geneva?
The savings measures planned by the Canton of Geneva include freezing salary increases for 2026 and the three following years, freezing indexation, reducing social aid, increasing the number of students per class, raising the occupancy rate of beds in nursing homes, and ending the free public transport in the canton. These measures are planned in the 2026 bis budget project and in the catalog of additional savings measures presented by a group of experts commissioned by the State Council.
How can I prepare for potential salary reductions?
To prepare for potential salary reductions, it's important to stay informed about the latest news regarding the savings measures and the negotiations between the government and the unions. You can find updated information on the website of the inter-union committee or on the websites of local unions.
