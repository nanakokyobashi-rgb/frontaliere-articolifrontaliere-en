LAMal or CMI 2026: The key choice for cross-border workers (cross-border guide)

Di Laura Bianchi · Published on 23 February 2026 · Updated on 24 September 2026

With 2026 LAMal premiums of CHF 279 to 487 for residents of Italy and CMI keeping you in the SSN with no premium, the decision for cross-border workers in Ticino becomes crucial. Analysis of costs, coverage, and practical cases.

Context

TL;DR

  • Cross-border workers must choose between LAMal or CMI within 3 months of starting work in Switzerland.
  • LAMal offers full Swiss coverage and care in Italy; CMI keeps you in the Italian National Health Service (SSN) with no insurance premium.
  • Choice is irreversible unless you change employers or status.

Key facts

  • Deadline: 3 months from the start of employment in Switzerland.
  • LAMal premium 2026: CHF 279.00 to CHF 487.20 per month for an adult living in Italy (FOPH premiums for Italy, without accident cover).
  • CMI cost: No insurance premium; "old" cross-border workers pay the SSN a contribution of 3% to 6% of net salary (€30 to €200 per month).
  • LAMal coverage: Full in Switzerland and in Italy (country of residence), emergencies in the rest of the EU.
  • CMI coverage: Comprehensive in Italy, emergencies and planned treatments in Switzerland with prior authorization.
  • Family unit: LAMal requires individual premiums per adult, with CMI the whole dependent family stays in the SSN.
  • Irreversibility: Choice for CMI is binding, can only be changed with employer change or loss of status.

For every new cross-border worker who starts working in Switzerland, the question is inevitable and the deadline is strict: 3 months from the start of employment to choose their health insurance scheme. Those who choose Italy must file the request for exemption from LAMal with the Social Insurance Institute (IAS), Contributions Office, of the Canton of Ticino in Bellinzona; the choice revolves around two acronyms: LAMal or CMI. It's not a trivial choice. On one hand, there's the solidity of the Swiss healthcare system (LAMal), and on the other, the affordability and familiarity of the Italian one (staying in the National Health Service through the LAMal exemption, referred to here as CMI). With 2026 LAMal premiums for an adult living in Italy ranging from CHF 279.00 to CHF 487.20 per month, and an SSN that charges no insurance premium, the economic gap seems clear. But price isn't everything. The choice, known as the "right of option," is a crossroads that determines access to care, out-of-pocket costs, and coverage for the entire family. And it's almost always an irreversible choice: once you opt for the Italian system, you can't go back, unless you change employers or lose your cross-border worker status.

Operational details

Let's delve into the technical details of the comparison, analyzing the substantial differences that every cross-border worker must consider before signing.

LAMal: The Swiss System

The Swiss model is based on a per-capita premium, fixed by age group and health insurer, completely unrelated to income. Residents of Italy do not pay the Ticino premium but the rate each insurer sets for Italy: in 2026 it ranges from CHF 279.00 to CHF 487.20 per month for an adult, without accident cover.

  • Coverage: Full and direct for all healthcare services in Switzerland. A cross-border worker insured under LAMal can also be treated in Italy, the country of residence, under Italian rules; in the rest of the EU, coverage is guaranteed for medical emergencies through the TEAM card (the back of the Swiss health card).
  • Out-of-pocket costs: In addition to the premium, the insured person contributes to the costs with a minimum annual deductible of CHF 300 and a 10% co-payment on subsequent expenses, up to a maximum of CHF 700 per year.
  • Family Unit: Each adult family member must take out their own policy and pay an individual premium. This can make LAMal very expensive for families.

CMI: The Italian Choice

Choosing CMI does not mean buying a private policy: with the LAMal exemption the cross-border worker stays insured with the Italian National Health Service (SSN), with no insurance premium.

  • Coverage: Comprehensive in Italy, under SSN rules. In Switzerland, it covers emergencies and planned treatments, but the latter require prior authorization from the relevant local health authority (ASL).
  • Out-of-pocket costs: No deductible and no reimbursement cap: SSN co-payments (ticket) apply. However, Italy's 2024 budget law (Law 213/2023, paragraphs 237-238) requires "old" cross-border workers who opted for the SSN to pay a contribution of 3% to 6% of net salary, with a minimum of €30 and a maximum of €200 per month; the rate is set every year by the region of residence (Ministry of Health decree of 14 November 2025, Official Journal no. 293 of 18 December 2025).
  • Family Unit: The real strength. If only the cross-border worker is employed, the whole family follows that choice and stays insured with the SSN, without individual premiums.

⚠️ Warning: The choice for CMI is binding. The so-called "right of option" can be exercised only once. Choosing Italy means permanently giving up the basic Swiss system for the entire duration of the employment relationship as a cross-border worker.

Key points

The final question remains: which system is more convenient in 2026? The answer depends strictly on your personal and family profile.

Who benefits from what? Practical cases

  • Single cross-border worker, under 35, in good health: CMI is almost always the most advantageous choice from an economic point of view. The annual savings can exceed 2,000 euros compared to LAMal.
  • Family with two adults and children: In this scenario, CMI becomes even more convenient. The sum of two LAMal premiums for adults living in Italy (roughly CHF 560 to CHF 975/month in 2026) is almost unsustainable compared to the SSN, which covers the whole household without insurance premiums.
  • Worker with pre-existing conditions or who expects to use specialized care in Switzerland: LAMal offers an unparalleled advantage: direct access, without prior authorizations, to the entire Swiss healthcare network. For those with specific medical needs, this peace of mind can be worth the higher cost.

💡 Journalist's advice: Don't just look at the monthly premium. Thoroughly analyze your health needs, your family composition, and the expected frequency of access to Swiss healthcare facilities. A decision based solely on immediate savings could prove costly in the long run. Frequently Asked Questions
What happens if I choose the Italian health insurance system (CMI) and then lose my cross-border commuter status?
If you lose your cross-border commuter status, you stay insured with the Italian National Health Service as a resident of Italy: CMI is not a policy and has no premium to pay. In addition, if you decide to return to work in Switzerland, you may have to choose the Swiss health insurance system (KVG) again.
Can I freely use health services in both countries with both systems, KVG and CMI?
Only with the KVG (LAMal). A cross-border worker insured under the KVG can be treated both in Switzerland and in Italy, the country of residence; the CMI covers fully in Italy and requires ASL authorisation for planned treatment in Switzerland.
When do I have to communicate the choice of insurance scheme at the Chiasso border?
Not at the border: the request for exemption to stay in the Italian system must be filed within 3 months of the start of employment in Switzerland with the Social Insurance Institute (IAS), Contributions Office, of the Canton of Ticino in Bellinzona. Without a timely request you remain subject to compulsory LAMal insurance. After the deadline, it is no longer possible to change the regime, unless you lose the status of cross-border commuter or change employer.
How do you calculate the annual MIF reimbursement ceilings for a household?
The CMI has no reimbursement ceilings: it is not a private policy but enrolment in the Italian National Health Service, which also covers dependent family members and applies the usual SSN co-payments. "Old" cross-border workers additionally pay the Law 213/2023 contribution of 3% to 6% of net salary, with a minimum of €30 and a maximum of €200 per month, set every year by the region of residence.
What are the differences between the KVG deductible and the additional costs for specialist care?
With KVG, the minimum deductible is CHF 300 per year; beyond this threshold, you pay 10% of the costs up to a maximum of CHF 700 in annual co-pay. Specialist care follows the same deductible and the same percentage; hospital stays add a contribution of CHF 15 per day.
