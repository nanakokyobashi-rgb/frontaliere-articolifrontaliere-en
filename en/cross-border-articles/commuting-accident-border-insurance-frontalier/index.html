Occupational safety management and health protection are a fundamental element for those who cross the border on a daily basis to reach their place of work in the Canton of Ticino. When it comes to protection against accidents, Swiss legislation provides specific protections that accompany the employee not only within the company boundaries, but also during the trips necessary to go to work and to return to their home in Italian territory. Swiss legislation stipulates that coverage against occupational and non-occupational accidents is compulsory for all employees active in the Swiss territory, including frontier workers.

Operational details

When a worker crosses the Italian-Swiss border, the distinction between the usual commute and an occasional work trip is of fundamental importance for the correct qualification of the accident event. The onward journey includes the normal route followed by the worker from their residence in Italy to the company headquarters in Canton Ticino. If during this journey a road accident or an unforeseen event occurs that causes personal injury, the event is analyzed in light of the criteria established by the LAINF and managed through Swiss insurance channels.

Differences between usual route and transfers

The business trip, on the other hand, differs from the simple commuting route since it takes place at the direct disposal of the employer for the performance of specific tasks outside the usual location. In both cases, LAINF insurance protection intervenes to cover medical expenses, hospital care and any daily allowances for temporary incapacity for work. However, the methods of verification and the documentation required may vary depending on whether the event occurred along the customs crossing route or during an external professional mission.

Key points

The procedure for reporting an accident while on duty requires timeliness and accuracy from the border worker. As soon as the health conditions allow, the employee must immediately inform their Swiss employer, who will proceed to submit the official notification to SUVA or the competent LAINF insurer. Omitting or delaying this communication may result in delays in the payment of daily indemnities and reimbursement of medical expenses incurred both in Switzerland and in Italy.

Frequently Asked Questions
What exactly does LAINF insurance cover in the event of a commuting accident?
LAINF coverage guarantees medical care, hospitalization and the payment of daily allowances in the event of an accident that occurred during the normal journey between home and work in Switzerland, provided that the route is direct and without unjustified deviations.
How should a border worker who suffers an accident along the way proceed?
The worker must promptly inform the Swiss employer, collect the medical documentation issued by the emergency room and keep any minutes of the intervening authorities, allowing the company to start the practice with SUVA.
What are the differences between commuting and business travel?
The home-work journey concerns regular cross-border commuting, while the transfer takes place on the specific order of the employer for off-site tasks. In both cases the LAINF offers protection, but with specific checks on the dynamics.
