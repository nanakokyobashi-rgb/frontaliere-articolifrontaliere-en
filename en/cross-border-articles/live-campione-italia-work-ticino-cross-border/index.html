Champion of Italy: living in the enclave and working in Ticino (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 27 August 2026

Time, crossings, taxation and costs for those who live in Campione d 'Italia and work as a border worker in Canton Ticino under the new 2024 Agreement.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Campione d 'Italia is an Italian enclave in Swiss territory, on Lake Lugano
  • From 1 January 2024, the New Italy-Switzerland Border Agreement is in force
  • Old border guards: exemption of €7,500; new: excess of €10,000
  • Double Taxation Convention signed on 9 December 1976

Key facts

  • What: New border agreement between Italy and Switzerland
  • When: Signed on 23 December 2020, effective from 1 January 2024
  • Where: Canton Ticino (border crossings) and Champion of Italy
  • Who: Italy and Switzerland (Italian ratification with Law 83 of 13 June 2023)
  • Old frontier workers: exemption up to €7,500, transitional regime 2024–2033
  • New frontier workers: deductible of €10,000
  • Tax at source: withheld exclusively in Switzerland
  • Double taxation agreement: 9 December 1976

Campione d 'Italia is an Italian enclave located on the eastern shore of Lake Lugano, completely surrounded by the Canton of Ticino. This geographical peculiarity makes the country a unique case for frontier workers: it does not take long journeys to reach Lugano, which is about 15 kilometres away. For those who want to live in the enclave and work in Ticino, the daily logistics are different from those who live beyond the land border.

Operational details

Realistic costs and journey times

Those who live in Campione d 'Italia and work in Ticino have limited journey times. The distance between Campione and Lugano is about 15 km, which can be covered in 20-30 minutes by car depending on traffic, passing through the Bissone pass. The connection by lake, with the boats of the Società Navigazione del Lago di Lugano, offers a panoramic but slower alternative, about 40-50 minutes. For destinations in Sottoceneri (Lugano, Mendrisio, Chiasso) and Sopraceneri (Bellinzona, Locarno), the times increase proportionally.

# Costs to budget

Life in Campione d 'Italia has generally lower costs than in Ticino, but commuting involves recurring expenses. Petrol in Italy costs less than in Switzerland, but the short journey doesn't matter too much. The highest cost concerns compulsory health insurance (LAMal): border workers with a G permit have the right of option between Swiss and Italian insurance. Annual deductibles for adults range from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500, depending on the chosen sickness fund. For those who travel a few kilometers, the cost of the car (Swiss vignette CHF 50, insurance, maintenance) is the main item. The CHF/EUR exchange rate affects purchases and money transfers; it is convenient to compare the rates before each operation.

Key points

What to do before moving: operational checklist

Before settling in Campione d'Italia to work as a cross-border commuter in Ticino, some essential requirements must be verified. The first step is to secure a job in Switzerland, since the G permit is tied to the employer. Subsequently, maintain official residence in Campione d'Italia: the Italian municipality issues the documentation required for the Swiss authorities.

G permit procedure

The Swiss employer starts the procedure at the cantonal population office. The documents typically required are: employment contract, Italian residence certificate, valid identity document, criminal record certificate (for some categories), proof of housing. After approval, the cross-border commuter receives the G permit, which allows daily entry into Switzerland.

Tax management and income declaration

In Switzerland, the employer applies withholding tax directly on the payslip. In Italy, the cross-border commuter must file an income tax return (form 730 or Redditi PF), indicating foreign income in section CE and requesting the tax credit to avoid double taxation. For new cross-border commuters, income exceeding the €10,000 allowance is taxable in Italy; for old cross-border commuters, the transitional regime applies with an exemption up to €7,500. Documentation to keep includes: Swiss salary certificate, proof of withholding tax paid, employment contract.

Timelines and deadlines to remember

The Italian income tax return must be filed by 30 September (form 730) or 30 November (form Redditi PF). Registration with LAMal must be completed within three months of starting work. The G permit must be renewed periodically, generally every five years or when changing employer.

Frequently Asked Questions
What documents are needed to obtain the G permit from Campione d 'Italia?
You need: a Swiss employment contract, a valid identity document, a certificate of residence in the Municipality of Campione d 'Italia, and in some cases a criminal certificate. The employer initiates the procedure at the cantonal office of the population in Ticino. The G permit allows daily entry into Switzerland without the right of domicile.
How does taxation work for a border crossing resident in Campione d 'Italia?
Tax at source is withheld only in Switzerland. In Italy, the frontier worker submits the tax return (730 or PF Income) indicating the income in the CE framework. Italy recognizes a tax credit for taxes already paid in Switzerland, avoiding double taxation. The old border guards have an exemption of €7,500; the new ones have a deductible of €10,000.
How much does the LAMal cost for a border crossing with a G permit?
Border workers with a G permit have the right of option between Swiss LAMal and Italian insurance. Annual deductibles for adults range from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500, depending on the chosen sickness fund. The monthly premium depends on age, working canton and insurance model.
Should you live in Campione d 'Italia and work in Ticino?
It depends on your personal priorities. Benefits include lower living costs, Swiss wages in CHF, and short journeys to Lugano. The disadvantages concern the limited commercial offer in the enclave, the management of two tax and insurance systems, and the need to return weekly to Italy to maintain border status.
What are the Swiss social contributions on the paycheck?
AVS/AI/IPG: 5.3% to be paid by the employee. AD/AC: 1.1% (up to CHF 148'200 salary). LAINF: between 0.7% and 1.5%. LPP: between 7% and 18% by age group, mandatory from the 25th year. These deductions reduce the net salary but finance retirement, accidents and unemployment.
