Living in Brienno and working in Ticino is a practical choice for Italians looking for a competitive salary and the stability of Swiss employment. Brienno is a municipality in the province of Como in Lombardy, in a favourable geographical position on the border with the Canton of Ticino. Commuting to Chiasso, Mendrisio, Lugano and Bellinzona is therefore practical for people seeking cross-border worker status.

However, moving is not only a geographical choice. It has important tax, regulatory and social security implications. Since the New Frontier Agreement entered into force on 1 January 2024, the rules have changed significantly. Anyone moving to Brienno today must understand how tax at source works for cross-border workers, the rights and duties attached to a G permit, how to avoid double taxation under the Italy-Switzerland Convention of 9 March 1976, and the benefits of the 2024 tax regime.

Brienno as a residence base for cross-border workers in Ticino

Brienno is a mountain municipality in the province of Como, overlooking Lake Como. Its location creates a natural link between the Canton of Ticino and the main Lombardy labour markets. This makes it suitable for people who want to reside in Italy while working in Switzerland.

Living in Brienno offers practical advantages: a lower cost of living than Swiss Ticino, a local community, proximity to border crossings such as Chiasso, Gaggiolo and Ponte Tresa, and proximity to the main employment centres in Ticino, including Lugano, Mendrisio and Bellinzona.

G permit and 2024 tax regime

A cross-border worker moving to Brienno is subject to the G permit, which is issued by the Swiss border authorities (SEM) in coordination with the Italian police headquarters and consulates. The G permit is issued for 15 years and allows the worker to reside in Italy and work in Switzerland.

A key change introduced by the New Frontier Agreement, signed on 23 December 2020 and in force since 1 January 2024 through Law 83 of 13 June 2023, concerns employment income taxation. The basic principle is that tax at source is withheld in Switzerland, not in Italy. This avoids double taxation. Italy recognises the Swiss tax credit through the Italy-Switzerland double-taxation convention signed on 9 March 1976 and box CE of the Italian Form 730.

For new cross-border workers, meaning those who begin cross-border employment after 17 July 2023, the New Agreement introduces an EUR 10,000 allowance on Swiss employment income. Old cross-border workers remain taxed only in Switzerland (Art. 9 of the 2020 Agreement).

Operational details

Tax at source and the New Agreement of 2024

Tax at source in Switzerland is calculated on gross income and varies at federal and cantonal level. The Canton of Ticino applies specific rates combined with mandatory contributions: AVS/AI 5.3% paid by the employee, AD/AC 1.1% up to CHF 148,200, and LAINF from 0.7% to 1.5%.

The key point is that, for a cross-border worker resident in Brienno, tax at source is paid only to the Swiss authorities: AFC/ESTV at federal level and the cantonal tax administration in Ticino. Italy does NOT withhold tax on employment income earned in Switzerland.

This results from the Italy-Switzerland Convention of 9 March 1976, which primarily assigns taxing rights to the country where the income originates. To avoid double taxation of worldwide income in Italy, the system uses a tax credit. When the cross-border worker files the Italian tax return using box CE of Form 730, Swiss tax paid at source can be deducted from Italian IRPEF up to the applicable Italian tax. Italian IRPEF uses the following brackets: 23% up to EUR 28,000, 33% from EUR 28,001 to EUR 50,000, and 43% above EUR 50,000.

Hypothetical scenario: new cross-border worker with CHF 60,000 gross annual income

A cross-border worker who starts working in Ticino in 2024, lives in Brienno and has gross annual income of CHF 60,000 will have the following situation:

  • Swiss tax at source: calculated on CHF 60,000 by the Ticino employer and paid in Switzerland. The exact rate depends on the canton and income level; it is approximately 8–15% plus AVS, AI and AD contributions.
  • New Agreement allowance: EUR 10,000, approximately CHF 10,800, of Swiss income is not subject to additional Italian taxation.
  • Italian tax return: the cross-border worker declares CHF 60,000 in Italy, applies the Swiss tax credit, and Italian IRPEF is calculated on the amount above the EUR 10,000 allowance and reduced by the Swiss tax credit.

The result is a lower overall tax burden than under the pre-2024 regime.

Double taxation and refunds: the role of the 1976 Convention

The Italy-Switzerland Convention of 9 March 1976 prevents double taxation by providing that employment income is taxable in the country where the work is performed, Switzerland, rather than in Italy. Italy recognises Swiss tax paid and deducts it from IRPEF.

The New Agreement of 2024 introduced refund mechanisms. If a cross-border worker has a low income and Swiss tax exceeds the calculated Italian tax, the Italian Revenue Agency may issue a refund.

AVS, LPP and supplementary pension provision

Living in Brienno and working in Ticino means contributing to the Swiss, not the Italian, social security system. AVS, the old-age and survivors insurance, is mandatory for cross-border workers paid in Switzerland. The employee contribution is 5.3%. This provides a Swiss old-age pension from age 65 for men or 64 for women.

LPP, the occupational pension fund and second pillar, requires an employer contribution of 7–18% according to age group from age 25 onwards. It builds capital for a pension or a lump sum at retirement.

Third pillar 3a is optional but advantageous for cross-border workers. It allows annual contributions with a Swiss tax deduction.

Italian INPS: cross-border workers are excluded from the Italian system, except for unemployment and short-time work. In those cases, they may continue to contribute to INPS at a reduced rate if the employer has arranged it, which is a rare contractual exception.

The result is that a cross-border worker resident in Brienno builds a Swiss pension rather than an Italian one. This is an advantage because Swiss pensions are generally higher, but also a disadvantage because Italian pension rights are not accumulated, apart from limited exceptions.

Key points

Practical checklist before moving to Brienno

Before moving, a future cross-border worker must start a series of administrative steps requiring coordination between Italy and Switzerland.

1. G permit: application and issue The G permit is not requested by the worker alone. It is coordinated between the Swiss employer, which sends the request to SEM or the cantonal migration authority; SEM and the Italian police headquarters, which verify actual residence in Brienno and the legitimacy of the employment relationship; the Municipality of Brienno and the Police Headquarters of Como, which confirm Italian residence; and the Swiss consulate, which coordinates the procedure. The process requires coordination between Swiss and Italian authorities. The permit is issued for 15 years and can be renewed.

2. Residence in Brienno

Registration with the Municipality of Brienno and enrolment with AIRE, the Register of Italians Residing Abroad, through the Italian consulate responsible for the Ticino area. Required documents include a rental or ownership contract, the original Swiss employment contract and a passport.

3. Health insurance (LAMal) G-permit cross-border workers can choose between Swiss LAMal, compulsory health insurance with adult deductibles from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500, and continued Italian insurance (CMI). The choice must be communicated at the beginning of the employment relationship. LAMal provides cover in Switzerland and partly in the EU; CMI covers Italy but requires coordination with Switzerland. Consult our LAMal/CMI calculator to assess which option is convenient for your income and family situation.

4. Bank account and salary payment The Swiss employer requires an IBAN to pay the salary. Options include opening an account with a Swiss bank, which is convenient for avoiding fees on CHF transfers, or keeping an Italian account and receiving international transfers, which may cost more but can be practical for an existing customer.

5. Registration with the Italian Revenue Agency Although an employed cross-border worker does not contribute to INPS as an ordinary worker, the worker must register with the Italian Revenue Agency to declare Swiss employment income. This procedure prevents improper collection of contributions.

AVS, LPP and pension planning

Once employed, the employer automatically registers the worker with the cantonal AVS fund in Ticino and with the company LPP fund, or an external fund depending on the employer. The worker receives communications from the fund administrators showing contributions and accumulated capital.

AVS should not be underestimated. It provides access to a Swiss old-age pension calculated on accumulated contributions. After 10–20 years of cross-border work, a worker accumulates significant credits.

Third pillar 3a is worth considering with a Swiss bank. Annual contributions are deductible for Swiss tax purposes and build a supplementary pension fund. Consult our pension planning tool to simulate your scenario.

Dual residence and practical aspects

Living in Brienno and working in Ticino involves practical management of dual residence: a work address with the Ticino employer in Lugano, Mendrisio or elsewhere; a registered residence address in Italy; employment income taxed in Switzerland and reported in Italy through Form 730; and healthcare through Swiss LAMal, if selected, covering treatment in Switzerland and partly in the European Union, including Italy.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the difference between a new and an old cross-border worker in 2024?
New cross-border workers, hired from 17 July 2023 onwards, have an EUR 10,000 allowance on Swiss employment income, exempt from Italian taxation. Old cross-border workers remain taxed only in Switzerland (Art. 9 of the 2020 Agreement). Both benefit from tax withheld in Switzerland, with an Italian tax credit for new cross-border workers.
Do I have to register with INPS if I work in Ticino from Brienno?
As an employed cross-border worker, you do not contribute to INPS as an ordinary worker. You must register with the Italian Revenue Agency to declare Swiss employment income. Access to unemployment and INPS short-time work depends on the company contract and bilateral CH-IT agreements.
LAMal or Italian insurance: which should I choose?
G-permit cross-border workers can choose Swiss LAMal, compulsory health insurance with adult deductibles from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500, or continued Italian insurance (CMI). The choice must be communicated at the beginning of employment. LAMal provides cover in Switzerland and partly in the EU, while CMI mainly covers Italy.
How does AVS work if I work 20 years in Ticino?
You must contribute to Swiss AVS at 5.3% as an employee. After 20 years of contributions, you accumulate credits towards a Swiss old-age pension calculated on the accumulated contributions. This income may be supplemented by credits from the Italian system under the 1976 Convention.
Is the G permit free or does it have costs?
The G permit is free for the worker and has no issue fee. The Swiss employer starts the procedure with SEM and coordinates with the Italian authorities. The permit is valid for 15 years and can be renewed.
