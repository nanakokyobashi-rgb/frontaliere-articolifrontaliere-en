Living in Inverigo, working in Ticino: border guide (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 1 September 2026

Distance, taxation, G permit, LAMal and costs: everything you need before moving to Inverigo working in Ticino as a border worker.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Inverigo (Como) a few km from the Ticino border: direct connection to the crossings
  • Tax at source withheld only in Switzerland; refunds by Italian declaration 730
  • G Permit, LAMal with option right, Swiss AVS/LPP with INPS integration

Key facts

  • What: Living in Inverigo (Lombardy) and working in Ticino with G border permit
  • Where: Inverigo, province of Como — Canton of Ticino, Switzerland
  • Tax Agreement: New Frontier Agreement, in force from 1 January 2024 (signed 23 December 2020)
  • Tax at source: Withheld by the Swiss employer, tax credit in Italian declaration
  • Deductibles: Old frontier workers (pre 17/7/2023): exemption €7,500 transitional regime 2024–2033; new frontier workers: €10,000

Inverigo, a municipality in the province of Como in Lombardy, is strategically positioned for those who want to live in Italy while maintaining a stable job in Ticino. The proximity to the Swiss border — connected by the Brogeda, Chiasso and Gaggiolo crossings — makes this solution viable for professionals and frontier workers looking for a balance between housing costs and work opportunities across borders.

Border taxation follows a well-established principle: income tax at source is exclusively withheld in Switzerland by the employer. Italy recognises this payment through the tax credit, regulated in an annual declaration (form 730, EC framework).

Operational details

Balanced taxation: how the Italian-Swiss system works

The frontier taxation for those living in Inverigo and working in Ticino is structured on two levels. The gross income received in Switzerland is subject to a withholding tax calculated according to the Swiss federal rates, plus contributions for AVS/AI/IPG (5.3% total for the employee), AD/AC (1.1% up to the cap of CHF 148'200), and LAINF (0.7–1.5% depending on the sector). These payments are mandatory and are managed by the employer.

In Italy, the frontier worker declares the gross Swiss income in the model 730 (quadro CE) and applies the ordinary IRPEF rates (23% up to €28'000, 35% for the second bracket €28'001–50'000, 43% for incomes over €50'000). However, the Italian Revenue Agency applies a tax credit for the Swiss withholdings already paid, balancing the overall taxation and avoiding full double taxation. The result is an effective rate that reflects the weighted average between the Swiss and Italian systems.

For old frontier workers (those who had the status before July 17, 2023), the €7'500 exemption in the transitional regime until 2033 mitigates the transition to the new system. New frontier workers benefit from a €10'000 allowance. These exemption margins further reduce the Italian taxable base.

Cost of living: Inverigo vs. Ticino

Lombardy, and in particular the province of Como, offers a significantly lower cost of living compared to Ticino. Rents, food, services, and utilities are on average 20–30% cheaper in Inverigo compared to urban centers in Ticino such as Lugano, Bellinzona, or Locarno. For a family or a single person looking to optimize their monthly budget, this difference is substantial: a rent of CHF 1'200–1'500 in Ticino corresponds to about €800–1'000 in Inverigo.

Key points

Practical checklist: how to get started

Before moving to Inverigo and starting to work in Ticino as a cross-border commuter, some specific steps are required. Here is the step-by-step procedure.

Step 1: Request for Permit G The residence permit for cross-border commuters (Permit G) is requested to the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) through the Swiss employer or a specialized agency. The SEM processes the application in coordination with the cantonal authorities (Population Office of the Canton Ticino). The permit is linked to the job and has a multi-year validity. Processing times vary, but they usually take 4–8 weeks. Required documents: identification, employment contract, certificate of no criminal record.

Step 2: Registration with AVS and insurance Once employed, the employer automatically registers the cross-border commuter with the competent cantonal AVS office. The cross-border commuter receives an AVS number (13 digits) used throughout their working career in Switzerland. The employer applies family allowances (if applicable according to the Canton), pays contributions for AVS/AI/IPG/AD/AC/LAINF and LPP according to the current rates, and provides the employee with a monthly payslip indicating all deductions.

Step 3: Choosing LAMal coverage Within 3 months of starting employment, the cross-border commuter chooses whether to enroll with a Swiss LAMal insurer (with a deductible from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 for adults) or to maintain Italian coverage with INPS with Swiss integration. The choice affects the cost of the premium, deductibles, and the network of healthcare facilities that can be used. Use the LAMal comparison tools to evaluate the options available in your Canton of work.

Frequently Asked Questions
How long does it take to get the G permit?
The State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) processes G permit applications in coordination with the cantonal authorities of Ticino. On average, the process takes 4–8 weeks from the time the application is submitted through the employer. Times may vary depending on office workload and completeness of documentation. The permit is linked to the workplace and is valid for many years.
How does the tax return work if I live in Inverigo?
Every year fill out form 730 at the Revenue Agency (or via CAF/online). In the EC framework, report the Swiss gross income and withholding taxes already paid. Italy applies the tax credit, balancing the taxation between the two countries. Result: an overall taxation equal to the weighted average between Swiss and Italian rates, without full double taxation. Additional benefits for old frontier workers (€7,500 exemption until 2033) and new frontier workers (€10,000 excess).
Do I need to sign up for LAMal Switzerland or can I keep the INPS?
The border crossing with G permit has the right of choice. Within 3 months of the start of employment, you can opt to: (a) enroll in a Swiss LAMal insurer (with deductibles from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 for adults); (b) stay in the Italian INPS system with integration of Swiss coverage. The choice affects the monthly premiums, deductibles and the usable health network. Compare premiums and hedges before you decide.
What are the travel times from Inverigo to the passes?
Inverigo is about a 15–25 minute drive from the passes of Brogeda, Chiasso and Gaggiolo. From there to the workplace in Ticino (Lugano, Mendrisio, Bellinzona) times vary from 20 to 45 minutes depending on the specific destination and traffic. During peak hours (7–9 a.m., 5-19 p.m.), queues at crossings can significantly lengthen times. Check the actual conditions through the online traffic services before choosing.
Do my Swiss AVS contributions count towards the Italian pension?
Yes. The Italian-Swiss Convention of 9 December 1976 provides for the accreditation of the contribution periods paid in one of the two countries towards the calculation of the total income. Your INPS statement automatically integrates Swiss periods into your pension calculation. Check your statement annually to make sure all periods are recorded correctly at INPS.
