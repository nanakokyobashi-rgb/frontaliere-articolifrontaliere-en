The number of frontier workers in Ticino recorded a slight growth in the second quarter of 2026, while maintaining a decline on an annual basis. According to data published by the Federal Statistical Office (BFS), at the end of the second quarter of 2026 there were about 414,000 border crossers in Switzerland, an increase of 1.3% compared to the same period in 2025.

Distribution by nationality

The majority of Swiss border workers were domiciled in France, with a share of 58.4%. It is followed by Italy with 22.1% and Germany with 16.3%. The growth, however, is not uniform among the different nationalities: in the last year, French border workers have increased by 2.7%, while Italian border workers have decreased by 1.1% and German border workers by 0.2%.

Operational details

Implications for Ticino border workers

The decrease on an annual basis of 0.6% in Ticino, although modest, indicates a weakness compared to the national trend. While in Switzerland the number of border workers grew by 1.3% on an annual basis, the Canton of Ticino recorded a slight decline. This gap could reflect local dynamics linked to the Ticino labour market, where some sectors are reducing commuter recruitment.

Comparison with other Swiss regions

The situation in Ticino is placed in a broader context. In central Switzerland, workers with a G permit decreased by 1% on a quarterly basis and by 1.2% compared to the second quarter of 2025. In Zurich, the contraction on an annual basis was more marked, with a reduction of 1.5%.

These data suggest a general slowdown of the border phenomenon in some areas of central Switzerland, while other regions such as the canton of Ticino show relative stability with slight declines.

# Five-Year Trend

Despite the annual decline, the five-year trend remains positive. Between the second quarter of 2021 and the same period in 2026, the overall number of frontier workers in Switzerland increased from around 350,000 to 414,000, a growth of 18.3%. This indicates that, albeit with recent fluctuations, the medium-term trend remains oriented towards an increase.

Key points

What to do now

Addressing the current situation requires a certain level of awareness. With an annual decline of 0.6% in Ticino, cross-border commuters must evaluate their employment and tax strategies. Here are some concrete steps:

Check your tax situation

Ensure that your tax declaration correctly reflects your period of work in Switzerland. The withholding tax regime, administered by the FTA/ESTV at the federal level and by the cantonal tax administrations, continues to be the main instrument. If you are a cross-border commuter with status prior to July 17, 2023, verify that you benefit from the transitional regime provided by the new agreement.

Check your G permit

The G permit remains the fundamental instrument for operating daily through the border crossings of Brogeda (Chiasso), Gaggiolo (Stabio), Ponte Tresa, and Dirinella (Gandria). Ensure it is always valid and up-to-date, especially if your employment contract undergoes changes.

Monitor the job market

With the 1.1% decline recorded for Italian cross-border commuters, assess whether there are alternatives in growing sectors. The Ticino market continues to present opportunities, albeit with some volatility.
