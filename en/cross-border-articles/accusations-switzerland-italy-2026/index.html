Operational details

Analysis of Possible Consequences

The accusations raised by the editorial could trigger a review of immigration and cross-border work policies between Italy and Switzerland, with a significant impact on Ticino's cross-border workers. In particular, changes could be introduced to the procedures for obtaining the G permit, which is crucial for Italian workers who cross the border every day. Currently, around 65,000 Italian cross-border workers work in Ticino, with about 30,000 in the municipality of Lugano alone. A change in regulations could require a transition period, during which workers should be prepared to present additional documents or meet new conditions. For example, a clause requiring a minimum period of residence in Switzerland before obtaining the G permit could be introduced, similar to what is already provided for other residence permits.

Comparison with Other Regions

Unlike other European regions, Ticino has a particularly high dependence on cross-border work. According to 2023 data, cross-border workers represent about 20% of the Ticino workforce, a significantly higher percentage than other border regions such as the Canton of Geneva (15%) or the Canton of Valais (10%). Diplomatic tensions could therefore have a greater impact here than in other areas. For example, the introduction of quotas for cross-border workers could severely affect municipalities like Mendrisio, where almost 40% of the active population consists of cross-border workers.

Concrete actions for cross-border workers

To address potential challenges related to diplomatic tensions between Italy and Switzerland, cross-border workers should consider the following concrete actions:

1. Monitor developments: It is essential to stay updated on the latest news and developments. For example, follow official statements from the Swiss Federal Department of Justice and Police (DFGP) and the Italian Ministry of the Interior. In particular, keep an eye on changes to the 2002 Free Movement of Persons Agreement (FMPA), which may undergo revisions.

2. Prepare for possible changes: Cross-border workers should be ready for potential changes in immigration and labor policies. This could include reviewing documents, such as the residence permit, and preparing for new procedures. For example, from 2024, Switzerland may introduce new regulations for cross-border workers in the Canton of Ticino, such as an increase in tax withholding for residents in neighboring Italian municipalities like Varese, Como, and Mendrisio.

3. Consult experts: It is advisable to consult experts in labor and immigration law to obtain personalized advice. For example, the legal advisory service of Frontaliere Ticino offers specific support for cross-border workers.
