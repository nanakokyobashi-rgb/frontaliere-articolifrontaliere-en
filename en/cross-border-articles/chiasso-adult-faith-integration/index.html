Chiasso: the faith rediscovered by adults and the changing Ticino (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 1 March 2026

In Chiasso, confirmation is celebrated at 40 years old, a sign of an evolving Ticino where faith becomes a conscious choice. What are the implications for the cross-border community?

Context

TL;DR

  • Adults in Chiasso seek confirmation by choice, not tradition.
  • Chiasso's cross-border workers experience evolving faith dynamics.
  • Don Feliciani highlights a shift from 'faith by inertia' to 'faith by choice'.

Key facts

  • Location: Chiasso, Mendrisiotto, Ticino
  • Trend: Adults in their 20s, 30s, and 40s seeking confirmation
  • Priest: Don Gianfranco Feliciani, retiring next summer
  • Confirmation Candidates: 22 candidates, nearly half are adults
  • Date: Reported by Tio.ch on February 28, 2026
  • Impact: Rediscovery of spirituality with mature awareness
  • Future Plans: Don Feliciani will move to Rancate but remain available to the community

A significant social phenomenon is emerging in the heart of Mendrisiotto, particularly in the city of Chiasso, a crucial crossroads for thousands of cross-border workers who cross the border daily to work in Ticino. Don Gianfranco Feliciani, the long-serving parish priest of Chiasso for 25 years and set to retire next summer, has revealed a significant change in local religious practices: confirmation, a sacrament traditionally associated with adolescence, is now being requested and celebrated by adults in their 20s, 30s, and even 40s. This trend, described by the priest as a shift from a faith 'by inertia' to a 'by choice', reflects a broader transformation in Ticinese society and, by extension, in the cultural context in which cross-border workers find themselves living.

Don Feliciani's observation, having served the Chiasso community with a booming and often provocative voice, highlights that "Christianity does not work as it once did. Today, sacraments are no longer received because 'one must'. But because one wants to. And indeed, I have people being confirmed at 40 years old. By choice." This is not an isolated case: of the 22 candidates for confirmation expected in Chiasso, nearly half fall into this adult age group. This data, reported by Tio.ch on February 28, 2026, suggests a rediscovery of spirituality with mature awareness, in an era that many define as increasingly secular. For cross-border workers, who often seek to integrate into the social fabric of the Canton, understanding these dynamics is crucial. Chiasso, with its nature as a welcoming locality and the circulation of populations from various ethnicities and different migrants, becomes a social laboratory where faith and identity are redefined, influencing the perception and experience of those living and working in this border region.

The parish priest, despite retiring, assured that "I’m not disappearing. I remain a priest. I will take refuge in Rancate. And I will help those in need, I will always be available," emphasizing an unbreakable bond with the community. This spirit of service and adaptation to the new needs of the faithful is an example of how local institutions, including religious ones, are evolving. For cross-border workers, often grappling with the search for a sense of belonging and stable reference points in an environment that can seem foreign, these transformations offer an interesting glimpse into a less traditional Ticino, more inclined towards individual choice, an aspect that can foster new forms of integration and participation in local community life, beyond purely work-related dynamics.

Operational details

This evolution in religious practices in Chiasso is not an isolated phenomenon, but fits into a broader context of social change affecting the entire Canton of Ticino and, more generally, Europe. Don Feliciani indeed emphasized that "our weary Europe at a certain point thought it could set aside even Christian faith. It believed it could do without it. But then what happens? Faith is rediscovered in another way. With even more conviction." This 'change of approach' has significant implications for social cohesion and the sense of community, fundamental aspects also for cross-border workers who experience the Ticinese reality daily.

Impact on Cross-Border Workers and Ticinese Society

  • Cultural Integration: For cross-border workers, understanding these cultural and social nuances can facilitate deeper integration. It is not just about fulfilling work or tax obligations, but about understanding the roots and evolutions of the society that hosts them. The rediscovery of faith as a personal choice, for example, may indicate a greater openness and tolerance towards different spiritual and cultural sensitivities within the community.
  • Social Dynamics in Chiasso: As a border city with a high percentage of residents and commuters from various nations, Chiasso is particularly exposed to these transformations. The presence of "populations from various ethnicities and different migrants" mentioned by Don Feliciani makes the city a microcosm of the challenges and opportunities related to diversity. Cross-border workers, in this sense, actively contribute to this social mosaic, and their participation, even informal, in community life can enrich the social fabric.
  • Volunteering and Associations: If faith becomes a more conscious choice, it is likely that people will be more motivated to actively engage in volunteering initiatives or local associations, not just religious ones. This creates opportunities for cross-border workers to find points of contact and actively contribute to the life of the Canton, beyond mere work presence. Civic and social engagement is a powerful vehicle for integration, often underestimated, which can lead to greater personal satisfaction and a sense of belonging.

The observations of the parish priest of Chiasso are not an economic analysis, but they touch the heart of Ticinese society, revealing a capacity for adaptation and renewal that can be surprising. This context of change, where tradition gives way to personal conviction, offers cross-border workers a unique opportunity to observe and participate in a constantly evolving community, overcoming the stereotypical view of a rigid and unchanging society. It is an invitation to look beyond one's workplace and immerse oneself in the social and cultural dynamics that make Ticino a vibrant and complex environment.
