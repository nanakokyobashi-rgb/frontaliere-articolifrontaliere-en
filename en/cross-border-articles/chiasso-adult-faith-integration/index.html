Chiasso: the faith rediscovered by adults and the changing Ticino (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 1 March 2026

In Chiasso, confirmation is celebrated at 40 years old, a sign of an evolving Ticino where faith becomes a conscious choice. What are the implications for the cross-border community?

Context

TL;DR

  • Adults in Chiasso seek confirmation by choice, not tradition.
  • Chiasso's cross-border workers experience evolving faith dynamics.
  • Don Feliciani highlights a shift from 'faith by inertia' to 'faith by choice'.

Key facts

  • Location: Chiasso, Mendrisiotto, Ticino
  • Trend: Adults in their 20s, 30s, and 40s seeking confirmation
  • Priest: Don Gianfranco Feliciani, retiring next summer
  • Confirmation Candidates: 22 candidates, nearly half are adults
  • Date: Reported by Tio.ch on February 28, 2026
  • Impact: Rediscovery of spirituality with mature awareness
  • Future Plans: Don Feliciani will move to Rancate but remain available to the community

A significant social phenomenon is emerging in the heart of Mendrisiotto, particularly in the city of Chiasso, a crucial crossroads for thousands of cross-border workers who cross the border daily to work in Ticino. Don Gianfranco Feliciani, the long-serving parish priest of Chiasso for 25 years and set to retire next summer, has revealed a significant change in local religious practices: confirmation, a sacrament traditionally associated with adolescence, is now being requested and celebrated by adults in their 20s, 30s, and even 40s. This trend, described by the priest as a shift from a faith 'by inertia' to a 'by choice', reflects a broader transformation in Ticinese society and, by extension, in the cultural context in which cross-border workers find themselves living.

Operational details

This evolution in religious practices in Chiasso is not an isolated phenomenon, but fits into a broader context of social change affecting the entire Canton of Ticino and, more generally, Europe. Don Feliciani indeed emphasized that "our weary Europe at a certain point thought it could set aside even Christian faith. It believed it could do without it. But then what happens? Faith is rediscovered in another way. With even more conviction." This 'change of approach' has significant implications for social cohesion and the sense of community, fundamental aspects also for cross-border workers who experience the Ticinese reality daily.

Key points

For cross-border workers living daily in Ticino, these observations on the social dynamics of Chiasso provide valuable insights well beyond the religious sphere. Understanding the social and cultural fabric of the Canton of Ticino means being able to navigate its peculiarities better, establish deeper relationships, and ultimately live a richer and more rewarding cross-border experience. The rediscovery of faith as a conscious choice, as described by Don Feliciani, is a symptom of a society that values individual motivation, a trait that can resonate in other areas of community and professional life.
