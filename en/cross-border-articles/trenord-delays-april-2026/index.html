Trenord malfunctions: delays and cancellations in Lombardy on April 21

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 22 April 2026

A double technical failure at Albizzate and Milano Certosa caused widespread disruption across the Lombardy rail network on Tuesday, April 21, 2026.

On Tuesday, April 21, 2026, the Lombardy railway network was hit by technical issues that generated widespread repercussions for thousands of travelers, including cross-border workers who use the railway connections to Canton Ticino and surrounding areas daily. The problems were triggered by two distinct technical failures located at critical points of the infrastructure. The first incident occurred in Albizzate, a crucial node on the route connecting Milan with Varese and Porto Ceresio, a vital artery for international commuting. In parallel, a second failure affected the Milan Certosa area, a strategic zone for the operational management of railway traffic and for the movement of trains entering and exiting the Trenord depot.

The dynamics of the disruptions

The combined effect of these two events produced a chain reaction that went beyond the directly affected routes. Although the failures were localized in specific geographical points, the interconnected nature of the regional railway system caused delays and cancellations to spread rapidly across much of the network. Traffic management proved complex precisely due to the involvement of Milan Certosa, a hub that serves as a lung for railway convoys. Consequently, the repercussions were not limited to the convoys in transit through Albizzate but affected numerous regional and suburban connections throughout the Lombardy region. Railway authorities reported widespread delays on various lines from Tuesday morning, with route changes that further complicated commuters' movements. The event once again highlighted the vulnerability of the infrastructure to sudden technical failures, which can compromise the punctuality of entire regional routes in a working morning. Travelers were forced to manage cancellations and unexpected changes, with a direct impact on the regularity of mobility flows to workplaces and offices located both in the Lombard territory and in the border areas with Switzerland.

Operational details

For cross-border workers who cross the border every day to reach their workplace in the Canton of Ticino, episodes of this type represent a significant logistical challenge. Dependence on Lombardy's railway connections, particularly on the Varese-Milan route, means that any interruption or technical fault immediately translates into a complicated management of working hours. On a day like April 21, 2026, commuters faced uncertainties that go beyond the simple delay of the train. The coincidence of a fault in Albizzate and a technical problem in Milan Certosa created an operational block that prevented the normal circulation of trains intended to guarantee the local public transport service.

Operational comparison and mobility scenarios

Compared to normal operating situations, where the commuter can plan their route based on the scheduled times, in the presence of multiple faults, flexibility becomes the only available resource. The commuter often finds themselves needing to evaluate alternatives in real-time, such as using private transport or looking for alternative railway routes that are often saturated. The situation on Tuesday demonstrated how the railway network is an integrated system where the malfunction of a depot, such as that of Milan Certosa, directly influences the ability to dispatch trains even on distant lines such as that of Porto Ceresio. For those who work in Ticino, this means not only delays in arrival but a potential alteration of the entire working day, with repercussions on the balance between private life and professional obligations. It is essential that commuters always keep an eye on official real-time information sources, as the chain of delays generated by faults does not follow a linear progression. The commuter's ability to adapt is severely tested in these circumstances, where the management of railway unforeseen events becomes a constant component of the cross-border work routine, influencing the choice of transport for the following days.

Key points

In the event of rail emergencies like the one that occurred on April 21, 2026, the correct procedure involves constant consultation of official channels to monitor the status of rail traffic and the potential activation of replacement services. Commuters using the Milan-Varese-Porto Ceresio line must be prepared to check mobility apps and information portals to verify, in real time, which trains have been cancelled and which are experiencing significant delays. There is no automatic compensation procedure for individual delays, but timely knowledge of a breakdown allows for the evaluation of alternative options, such as reaching nearby train stations or using private road transport where possible.
