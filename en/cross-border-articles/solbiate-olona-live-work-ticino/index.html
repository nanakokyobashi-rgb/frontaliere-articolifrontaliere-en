The choice to live in Solbiate Olona and work as a cross-border worker in Ticino represents a consolidated economic opportunity, but entails radically changed tax and social security obligations from 1 January 2024 with the entry into force of the New Cross-border Workers Agreement. This agreement, signed on 23 December 2020 and ratified in Italy with Law 83 of 13 June 2023, modifies the structure of taxation and social contributions for those who cross the crossings of Brogeda, Chiasso, Gaggiolo or Ponte Tresa on a daily basis.

Unlike the previous legislation, the withholding tax is now withheld in Switzerland by the employer: there are no longer parallel withholdings from Italy. This mechanism is possible because Italy avoids double taxation through the tax credit, requested in the EC framework of the annual declaration (form 730). For the cross-border worker, it means fewer resources removed from the paycheck every month, but also responsibility in the Italian annual declaration, where the tax credit recovers what has already been paid in Switzerland.

The tax system: one source

The withholding tax is calculated according to the federal and cantonal rates of Ticino. There is no single national percentage: it depends on the canton. The employer applies the withholding tax monthly to the gross salary. The cross-border worker finds it certified in the pay slip and must keep the certificate for the annual Italian declaration. The Double Taxation Convention of 9 March 1976 guarantees that those who work in Switzerland are not taxed twice on the same income.

The New Agreement distinguishes between old and new cross-border workers. The threshold of 10,000 euros is not a general exemption: art. 4 of Law 83/2023 limits it to the income referred to in the art. 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013. This difference is intentionally constructed to protect those already placed, giving time to adapt to the new system.

Operational details

Mandatory social security: AVS, LPP and additional contributions

Those who work in Switzerland as cross-border workers are required to pay AVS/AI/IPG contributions (old age, disability and maternity insurance) at a fixed rate of 5.3% of the gross salary, to be paid by the employee. Alongside this, the Swiss system provides for an AD (unemployment insurance) and AC (loss of earnings compensation) contribution of 1.1% each, with a maximum ceiling of CHF 148'200 of annual salary. Finally, there is the LAINF (accident insurance), paid almost entirely by the employer, while the employee contributes between 0.7% and 1.5%. The overall sum significantly affects the paycheck.

The real distinctive element is the LPP (Legal Pension Plan), the second compulsory pillar starting from 25 years of age. The cross-border worker pays a contribution that varies from 7% to 18% of the gross salary, based on his age. At 25 the contribution is smaller; it grows progressively as we age. It's not optional. This means that a cross-border worker with CHF 4,500 per month will have to pay at least CHF 315 in LPP alone, in addition to CHF 239 in AVS. The Swiss paycheck is much more 'taxed' from a social security point of view than an Italian paycheck, but the result is a considerably more generous Swiss pension income than the Italian INPS.

KVG: health insurance and the choice of cross-border commuter

One frequently confused aspect is health insurance. In Switzerland it is called KVG (Federal Law on Health Insurance) and is mandatory. It's private insurance, not a tax. Cross-border workers belonging to category G (cross-border residence permit) have a special right: they can choose whether to insure themselves with a Swiss KVG fund or remain in the Italian INPS system. The choice must be communicated within the established deadlines and remains binding for at least 12 months.

If you choose the Swiss KVG, you will have to pay a variable monthly premium, typically between CHF 350 and CHF 500 for an adult, with annual deductibles of CHF 300 to CHF 2,500. You will have access to Swiss treatment without administrative problems and certain reimbursement times. If, however, you remain in INPS, you will continue to pay Italian contributions and you will have Italian coverage, but for urgent treatments in Switzerland you will have to manage cross-border reimbursements. Many cross-border commuters choose the Swiss KVG for logistical convenience: you are already in the local system, doctors and hospitals are known, there are no delays in coverage.

The tax credit: how to avoid double taxation

The cross-border worker pays taxes in Switzerland and, theoretically, should also pay taxes in Italy. The system avoids this duplication through the Italian tax credit, governed by the EC framework of the annual declaration (form 730 or Income Models). In the declaration, the cross-border worker declares the Swiss income and the taxes already paid in Switzerland. The Revenue Agency calculates how much you should pay in Italy on that income according to the IRPEF rates (23% up to €28,000, 33% from €28,001 to €50,000, 43% over €50,000), then subtracts what has already been paid in Switzerland. If the difference is negative, the cross-border worker receives a refund; if positive, pay the difference to Italy.

Preparation of the annual return

Every year, in autumn, the cross-border worker must prepare the Italian declaration to recover the tax credit. Follow these steps: collect the annual withholding certificate from the Swiss employer (usually sent by the end of February of the following year); prepares the Italian declaration (form 730 or Income Models); enter the Swiss income in the CE framework; claims the tax credit for Swiss withholdings already paid; send via CAF, accountant or online platform by October 31st. This procedure guarantees the recovery of double taxation.

Frequently Asked Questions
Which income does the €10,000 limit apply to?
Article 9’s transitional regime applies only to workers who meet the Agreement’s definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between 31 December 2018 and 17 July 2023; covered employment income is taxable exclusively in Switzerland. The ordinary regime applies in other cases, with Italian taxation and credit for Swiss tax where applicable. The €10,000 in Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is only the limit for income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law 147/2013, not a general allowance. FTA factsheet · Agreement, Article 9 · Normattiva, Article 4.
Is the source tax payable in both Switzerland and Italy?
No. Tax at source is ONLY withheld in Switzerland by the employer. Italy avoids double taxation by recognizing a tax credit in the EC framework of declaration 730. The border worker requests reimbursement of the difference between what he paid in Switzerland and how much he should pay in Italy according to the IRPEF rates.
What is LAMal and do I have to make sure in Switzerland?
LAMal is the Swiss compulsory health insurance (not a tax). G category border guards can choose between Swiss LAMal or Italian INPS. The choice must be communicated on time and is binding for 12 months. The adult LAMal deductibles range from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 per year.
How much does the AVS and LPP border carrier pay monthly?
The AVS is fixed at 5.3% of the gross salary, mandatory. The LPP varies from 7% to 18% by age group: from 7% to 25 years up to 18% with advancing age. It is not avoidable. Next to these: AD/AC 1.1%, LAINF 0.7–1.5%. The total amount has a significant impact on the paycheck.
When does the New Agreement come into effect and how does it change?
The New Frontier Agreement applied since 1 January 2024 (ratified in Italy by Law 83/2023). The taxation system changes: the source tax passes from a double track (Switzerland + Italy) to a single source in Switzerland, with an Italian tax credit in the EC framework. AVS/LPP contributions remain mandatory; procedures are simplified.
