Arrest in Ticino in international anti-mafia operation

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 21 October 2026

A person was arrested in Ticino on Wednesday.

Context

  • False invoices discovered for over 110 million euros.
  • International money laundering of 68 million via cryptocurrencies.

Arrest in Canton Ticino

In the 12:30 radio newscast of October 21, 2026, the report signed by Alain Melchionda reports the arrest, which took place on Wednesday in Ticino, of a person involved in a large anti‑mafia operation by the Guardia di Finanza of Milan and Monza. The investigation was coordinated by the Milan District Anti‑Mafia Directorate (DDA).

The investigation dismantled the hidden bank of organized crime. The source indicates the involvement of members of the ’ndrangheta, the Camorra and the Fourth Mafia, i.e. the Foggia organized crime. The reported tally includes 30 arrests, over 110 million euros in false invoices and an international money‑laundering scheme of 68 million euros via cryptocurrencies.

For the part carried out in Switzerland, Fedpol conducted several house searches in Canton Ticino at the request of the Milan DDA and on behalf of the Federal Office of the Attorney General. The arrested person was placed in detention for extradition purposes.

Search activities continue in Spain and in several Italian provinces, involving over 350 Guardia di Finanza officers. The report thus describes an action extending beyond Ticino territory, without specifying which provinces are involved or linking the 30 arrests to specific locations. The Ticino municipality concerned and the identity of the person are also not indicated.

Operational details

Cooperation between the authorities

The report distinguishes between the coordination of the investigation and the activities carried out in the Canton of Ticino. The Guardia di Finanza in Milan and Monza conducted the major anti-mafia operation, while the Milan District Anti-Mafia Directorate, referred to as the DDA, coordinated it. For the house searches in the Canton of Ticino, the source identifies Fedpol: the operations were carried out at the request of the Milan DDA and on behalf of the Office of the Attorney General of Switzerland.

The sequence indicated by the source

In the report, the Ticino arrest is included in the same operation that involved multiple territories. In addition to the Canton of Ticino, searches are under way in Spain and in several Italian provinces. The source links the deployment of more than 350 Guardia di Finanza personnel to the activities described, but does not provide a breakdown of personnel by country, province, or individual search.

| Step | Entity or location | Information reported | | Coordination | Milan DDA | Coordinates the operation | | Conduct | Guardia di Finanza in Milan and Monza | Conducts the operation | | Ticino | Fedpol | Several house searches | | Assignment | Office of the Attorney General of Switzerland | Activities in Ticino | | Other locations | Spain and several Italian provinces | Searches under way | | Personnel | Guardia di Finanza | More than 350 personnel |

The cooperation described by the source therefore links the Italian activity to that carried out in the Canton of Ticino, without changing the roles indicated. The Milan DDA appears as the authority that coordinates and requests the operations; Fedpol conducts the house searches in Ticino on behalf of the Office of the Attorney General of Switzerland. The report does not indicate the Italian provinces involved, the Spanish locations, or the Ticino municipality concerned. Nor does it attribute the 30 arrests to individual territories.

Key points

The investigation's figures and what is not indicated

The financial aspect of the story is based on two distinct pieces of data. On the one hand, the investigation led to the discovery of over 110 million euros in false invoices; on the other, the source quantifies the international money laundering through cryptocurrencies at 68 million euros. The report does not provide an overall total and does not clarify whether the two amounts coincide. This article therefore retains two separate figures, referring to two different elements of the investigation.

The definition of shadow bank also remains the one used in the report to describe the organized crime system dismantled by the operation. The source does not explain how this system worked, does not describe the methods used for the false invoices, and does not explain the stages of the money laundering through cryptocurrencies. The stated economic value and the cited financial channel are available, but not a technical reconstruction of the operations.

The phase documented so far

Operationally, searches are still underway in Spain and in several Italian provinces, with over 350 Guardia di Finanza personnel deployed. For Canton Ticino, the documented fact is the arrest of one person during home searches and that person's detention for extradition purposes.

The source does not give the arrested person's name, the Ticino municipality concerned, the individual charges, or the outcome of the extradition proceedings. Nor does it attribute to that person a specific amount among the 110 million in false invoices and the 68 million in money laundering. Nor are subsequent developments after the searches underway described. The available reporting therefore stops at the results of the operation announced in the radio news bulletin of October 21, 2026, without adding local consequences or judicial decisions not reported.
