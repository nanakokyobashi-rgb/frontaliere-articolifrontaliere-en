Operational details

Before and after: what changes

To understand the new development in practical terms, the point of comparison is the service process. The previous form had been exploited to collect data and intentionally circumvent query limits. Following the theft in early September, the online page was deactivated as a precaution. From 1 November 2026, however, searches will return to the intercantonal eAutoindex platform, but with individual searches subject to a fee.

Frequently Asked Questions
Since when will the Ticino license plate list be restored?
The consultation will be reinstated from 1 November 2026 through the eAutoindex intercantonal platform. The change concerns individual searches, which will become paid. The Department of Institutions presents the measure as a technical and financial barrier against any new attempts at automated data collection.
Why do license plate searches become paid?
The decision comes after the large-scale theft of public information about vehicle owners in early September. The previous form had been used to collect license plate numbers, names, surnames and addresses, intentionally circumventing the limits of the queries. The cost of individual searches will have to help create a barrier against new automated collections.
Who had asked not to publish their data will be included?
No. Those who had asked the Circulation Section not to make their data public had not been involved in the theft and will also be excluded from eAutoindex. The transition to the new platform therefore does not change this protection of privacy, which remains valid even after the restoration of the consultation of the license plate list.
Has there been an intrusion into cantonal computer systems?
No. The Canton specifies that there has been no intrusion into the cantonal computer systems. The online page was deactivated as a precautionary measure and the Department of Institutions filed a criminal complaint after the incident. The communication therefore distinguishes the theft of public information from abusive access to cantonal systems.
How to behave with suspicious letters, emails or SMS?
The Department of Institutions invites you to be wary of unexpected letters, emails or SMS asking for urgent payments or personal data, even when they refer to fines, road taxes, tests or tolls. If in doubt, he recommends contacting the authority through its official channels.
