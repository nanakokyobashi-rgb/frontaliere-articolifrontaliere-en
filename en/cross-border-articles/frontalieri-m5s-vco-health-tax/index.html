Frontiersmen, M5S VCO: 'Piedmont and Lombardy divided, so disparities increase' (cross-border guide)

Di Laura Bianchi · Published on 11 August 2026

The 5 Star Movement VCO intervenes on the situation of border workers after the debate at the Firefly Festival in Villadossola.

Context

The Movement focuses in particular on the health tax and the different choices made by the two regions bordering Switzerland. According to official data from 2022, Lombardy applies a health tax of 2.90 euros per file, while Piedmont applies it at 1.90 euros. This disparity creates a climate of uncertainty among border workers who work and live in these regions.

"The health tax is just the least of the problems. There is the question of VAT, car stamps, and driver's licence renewal. All this creates huge confusion and increases expenses for border workers, "says a Bellinzona resident.

Switzerland, by contrast, has a clearer and more predictable tax system. Under the rules in force since 1 January 2020, Swiss border workers are exempt from health and VAT taxes. In addition, they can benefit from a compensation system that reduces the cost differences between Switzerland and Italy.

The disparity between Piedmont and Lombardy is not limited to the health tax. Piedmont, for example, applies a higher car stamp duty than Lombardy. According to the regulations in force since 1 January 2021, Piedmont applies a car stamp duty of 5.5% of the value of the vehicle, while Lombardy applies a tax of 3.5%.

Key points

The 5 Star Movement (M5S) of Varese and Como (VCO) recalled the difficulties related to cross-border traffic, citing the securing of state road 34 of Lake Maggiore, the tunnels on the same artery, the works on state road 337 of the Vigezzo Valley between Re and Ribellasca and the construction site of the bridge over the San Giovanni.

According to the promoters of the M5S VCO, the lack of investment in cross-border roads creates disparities between local communities and increases travel times for freight and passenger transport. “The lack of funds for the maintenance and safety of cross-border roads is a serious issue that directly affects local communities,” says M5S spokesperson VCO.

The securing of state road 34 of Lake Maggiore is a concrete example of how the lack of investment can have negative consequences on cross-border traffic. According to data from ANAS (Azienda Nazionale Autostrade e Suxidiarie), state road 34 of Lake Maggiore is one of the busiest roads in Lombardy and Piedmont, with over 20,000 vehicles per day.

However, the maintenance and safety of this artery has been the subject of criticism and concern from the promoters of the M5S VCO. "The tunnels on State road 34 of Lake Maggiore were built in 1970 and have not undergone significant maintenance since then," says the M5S VCO spokesman.

Frequently Asked Questions
What does the 5 Star VCO Movement say about the health tax?
The Movement focuses in particular on the health tax and the different choices made by the two regions bordering Switzerland.
What are the difficulties related to cross-border traffic?
The M5S VCO recalls the difficulties related to cross-border roads, citing the securing of state road 34 of Lake Maggiore, the tunnels on the same artery, the works on state road 337 of the Vigezzo Valley between Re and Ribellasca and the construction site of the bridge over the San Giovanni.
What are the differences on the car stamp between Piedmont and Lombardy for border workers?
For border crossers, Piedmont applies a car stamp duty equal to 5.5% of the value of the vehicle, while Lombardy applies a tax of 3.5%. These percentages have been in force since 1 January 2021 and create a considerable disparity, especially for those who have to maintain two vehicles, one for each country.
What solutions does the Movement propose for the problems of Italian-Swiss border workers?
The Movement proposes several measures: the reduction of the health tax and VAT in Piedmont, the creation of a compensation system to reduce the cost differences between Switzerland and Italy, the establishment of a single register for border workers, a reform of the car stamp and a system of assistance for those with health problems or disabilities.
Are frontier workers working in Switzerland subject to health taxes or VAT?
Under the rules in force since 1 January 2020, frontier workers working in Switzerland are exempt from health and VAT taxes. They can also benefit from a clearing system that aims to reduce the cost differences between the Swiss and Italian systems, offering greater tax clarity.
