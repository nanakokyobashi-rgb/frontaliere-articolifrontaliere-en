Healthcare professionals and LaMal: Canton Ticino's decisions

Di Laura Bianchi · Published on 11 May 2026

Canton Ticino has made important decisions regarding access to LaMal for cross-border healthcare professionals.

Context

In brief

  • The Canton of Ticino decides on access to LaMal for healthcare professionals.
  • New rules for cross-border workers in the healthcare sector.
  • Practical implications for those residing in Italy and working in Ticino.

Key facts

  • What: Decisions on access to LaMal for healthcare professions.
  • When: By June 30, 2024.
  • Where: Canton of Ticino.
  • Who: Cantonal authorities of Ticino.
  • Amount: Up to CHF 300 for registration procedures.

The Canton of Ticino has recently made important decisions regarding access to mandatory health insurance (LaMal) for healthcare professionals working in the territory. This decision has a significant impact on cross-border workers who reside in Italy and work in the healthcare sector in Switzerland. The new rules aim to clarify the requirements and procedures for accessing LaMal, thus ensuring greater clarity and security for transborder workers.

Implications for Cross-Border Workers

The new provisions of the Canton of Ticino mainly concern healthcare professionals working in Ticino but residing in Italy. These workers will now have to follow specific procedures to access LaMal, which may include the submission of additional documents or proof of a real link with the Swiss territory. The cantonal authorities have emphasized the importance of these measures to ensure a fair and sustainable healthcare system.

Operational Checklist

1. Required Documents:

  • Valid passport or identity card.
  • Work contract in Ticino.
  • Proof of residence in Italy.
  • Previous work certificate (if applicable).

2. Procedures:

  • Submission of the application to the competent office of the municipality of residence in Ticino (e.g., Lugano, Bellinzona, Locarno).
  • Payment of an administrative fee of CHF 100-300.
  • Waiting for confirmation of registration, within 30 working days.

Concrete Examples

  • Case of Lugano: A nurse residing in Varese who works at the Civil Hospital of Lugano will have to submit the application for registration to LaMal by June 30, 2024. The procedure will be managed by the health office of the municipality of Lugano.
  • Case of Bellinzona: A doctor residing in Como who works at the Beata Vergine Hospital of Bellinzona will have to pay an administrative fee of CHF 250 for the registration procedure.

Comparison of Practical Scenarios

  • Scenario A: A nurse who works full-time in Ticino and resides in Italy will have to register with LaMal by June 30, 2024. Health coverage will be guaranteed from July 1, 2024.
  • Scenario B: A doctor who works part-time in Ticino and resides in Italy will have to evaluate whether registration with LaMal is necessary based on the hours worked. If they exceed 50 hours per month, registration will be mandatory.

The new rules could also influence the costs of health insurance for cross-border workers. It is essential that interested workers inform themselves promptly about the new provisions and specific requirements to avoid any coverage problems. The cantonal authorities have promised to provide further details in the coming weeks, but it is already clear that cross-border workers will have to adapt to new procedures to ensure their health coverage.

'The new provisions aim to ensure a fair and sustainable healthcare system for all transborder workers,' said a representative of the cantonal authorities.

Conclusion

The decisions of the Canton of Ticino on access to LaMal for healthcare professionals represent a significant step towards greater clarity and security for cross-border workers. It is essential that interested workers prepare adequately and follow the new procedures to ensure their health coverage without interruptions.

Operational details

Procedures and Requirements

The new rules for cross-border healthcare professionals' access to LaMal, which came into effect on January 1, 2023, include a series of specific requirements. Among these, demonstrating a genuine link to Swiss territory, which can be proven through tax residence, withholding tax payments, or participation in Swiss social assistance programs. Additionally, workers will need to present additional documents attesting to their professional activity in Ticino, such as employment contracts, certificates of employment, and income tax returns.

📊 Operational Checklist:

  • Tax residence in Switzerland (e.g., certificate of domicile)
  • Withholding tax payments (e.g., certificate from the AFC)
  • Participation in Swiss social assistance programs (e.g., accident insurance)
  • Employment contract in Ticino
  • Certificate of employment
  • Swiss income tax return

Comparison with the Previous Situation

Before these new provisions, cross-border healthcare professionals could access LaMal through less stringent procedures. For example, a simple declaration of residence in a border municipality such as Mendrisio or Chiasso was sufficient. The new rules represent a significant change, especially for those who do not have a direct link to Swiss territory. However, the cantonal authorities have assured that these measures are necessary to ensure a fair and sustainable healthcare system.

"These measures are essential to prevent abuse and ensure the sustainability of the Ticino healthcare system," declared the Ticino State Council.

Practical Scenarios

Imagine a cross-border worker who works as a nurse in a Lugano hospital but resides in Varese. According to the new rules, this worker will need to demonstrate a genuine link to Swiss territory to access LaMal. This could include presenting documents that attest to tax residence in Switzerland, such as a certificate of domicile issued by the municipality of residence, or participation in social assistance programs. If the worker fails to demonstrate this link, they may face additional costs for health coverage, amounting to approximately 300-500 CHF per month for equivalent coverage.

Another scenario could involve a doctor working in a Bellinzona hospital but residing in Como. In this case as well, the worker will need to follow the new procedures to access LaMal. What to Do Now

Healthcare professionals who are cross-border workers in Ticino must act urgently to ensure the continuity of their health insurance coverage. The new regulations of the Canton of Ticino, which came into effect on January 1, 2024, require proof of a genuine link to Swiss territory. This link can be demonstrated through various documents, including tax residence, payment of withholding taxes, or participation in social assistance programs. For example, a nurse residing in Chiasso and working in Lugano must submit the Swiss tax return for 2023, which certifies a taxable income of at least CHF 12,908, corresponding to the LaMal coverage minimum.

Step-by-Step Procedures

1. Gather Documents: Start gathering all necessary documents to prove your link to Swiss territory. This may include:

  • Tax residence: Rental contract or property ownership in Ticino.
  • Withholding taxes: Certificate of payment of withholding taxes for 2023.
  • Social assistance: Documents certifying participation in social assistance programs, such as childcare subsidies (CHF 200-300 per month per child).
  • Economic activity: Work contracts or documents certifying stable economic activity in Switzerland.

2. Contact Cantonal Authorities: Contact the cantonal authorities of Ticino for further information on the new procedures and specific requirements for access to LaMal. You can contact the Social Insurance Institute (IAS) in Bellinzona or consult the official website of the Canton of Ticino.

3. Submit the Application: Once you have gathered all the necessary documents, submit your application for access to LaMal following the new procedures established by the cantonal authorities. The application can be submitted online through the official portal or in person at the IAS offices.

4. Monitor Deadlines: Make sure to monitor the deadlines and submit your application in a timely manner to avoid any problems with health coverage. The deadline for submitting applications is set for March 31, 2024.
