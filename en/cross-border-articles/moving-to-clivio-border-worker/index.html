Tax Settings

Border crossers are subject to tax in both Switzerland and Italy. Italy avoids double taxation with the tax credit (EC framework of 730). Switzerland levies the tax at source in Switzerland. The amount of tax varies by income and nationality.

The tax regime for border workers in Ticino is governed by the Income Tax Act (LISR) and the tax conventions between Switzerland and Italy. According to the law, frontier workers are considered to be resident in Switzerland if they stay more than 183 days per year in the country.

Switzerland applies the principle of territoriality, which means that the tax is due only on income produced in Switzerland. However, frontier workers are subject to an additional tax if their income is more than CHF 150,000 per year. The additional tax ranges from 2% to 4% of income above CHF 150,000.

Border workers are considered residents in Italy if they stay more than 183 days a year in the country.
