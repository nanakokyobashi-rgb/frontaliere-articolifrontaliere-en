LAMal vs SSN: the cross-border worker’s right of option

LAMal (Federal Health Insurance Act) is Switzerland’s mandatory health insurance, while the Italian National Health Service (SSN) is Italy’s public healthcare system. If you start working in Switzerland and live in Italy, you have a right of option: you can choose Swiss LAMal or the Italian National Health Service within three months of starting work.

The choice is final for the entire period of uninterrupted cross-border work. Before deciding, identify your regime, whether you are an “old” or “new” cross-border worker, your family composition and where you want to receive care.

The three decisive factors are your tax and insurance regime, your family and where you receive care. Consider them together, because an individual premium, family members and access to care can change the result.

This guide compares the two options through premiums, cost-sharing, territorial coverage and concrete scenarios, so you can decide using verifiable information.

How LAMal works for cross-border workers

The insurance obligation and the cross-border exemption

Anyone who works in Switzerland is subject to the LAMal obligation. If you are domiciled in Italy, you can request an exemption and remain in the Italian system. Apply within three months of starting work to the cantonal authority; in Ticino, the Social Insurance Institute is responsible.

Without a formal application there is no option: you remain subject to the Swiss obligation, retroactive to the start of work. The choice is final for the entire period of uninterrupted cross-border work: changing employer or canton, or changing income or premium, does not reopen it. It reopens only after a period subject to another state’s system or when you become a pension beneficiary.

Monthly LAMal premiums: what it really costs

Residents of Italy do not pay Ticino premiums: they pay the premium each insurer calculates for Italy. The premium depends on three things: the insurer, age class and accident cover. The 14 insurers are Agrisano, Aquilana, Assura, Concordia, CSS, Helsana, KPT, Mutuel, ÖKK, Sanitas, Sodalis, Swica, Visana and Vivao Sympany.

Anyone working at least eight hours per week for the same employer is also legally insured against non-occupational accidents and can choose the premium without accident cover. For residents of an EU state, optional deductibles and choice-based insurance models do not exist.

In 2027, adults pay CHF 306.90 to CHF 509.40 per month without accident cover. The real cost is the premium plus cost-sharing, which for an adult can reach a maximum of CHF 1,000 per year.

How the LAMal deductible works

For residents of Italy there is one ordinary deductible: CHF 300 per year for adults and none for minors. You then pay 10% of costs above the deductible, up to CHF 700 per year for adults and CHF 350 for minors.

Practical example: with CHF 3,000 of medical expenses in Switzerland:

  • You pay: CHF 300 deductible + CHF 270, or 10% of CHF 2,700 = CHF 570.
  • The health insurer covers CHF 2,430.

For an adult, maximum cost-sharing is CHF 1,000 per year and applies to care received in Switzerland. For care in Italy, you pay only any Italian ticket.

LAMal territorial coverage

In Switzerland you have full access to the healthcare system. In Italy, register with the ASL using the certificate issued by the Swiss health insurer and receive services under Italian rules, paying only any ticket.

Private facilities that do not accept the card are not covered. The same rule applies to non-working family members insured with the cross-border worker.

Operational details

How the SSN works for cross-border workers

Registration and contributions

With the option for the Italian National Health Service, you remain registered with the ASL where you live. The cost depends on your regime.

“New” cross-border workers, hired from 17 July 2023 and taxed in Italy as well, pay no specific health contribution: the service is funded through general taxation. “Old” cross-border workers, active between 31 December 2018 and 17 July 2023 and taxed only in Switzerland, who chose the Italian National Health Service, follow the contribution provided by law.

Law 213/2023 (Article 1, paragraphs 237-239) provides for “old” cross-border workers a contribution paid to the region of residence: 3% to 6% of net salary received in Switzerland, progressive according to income and family responsibilities, with a minimum of €30 and a maximum of €200 for each month worked. The contribution also covers dependent family members.

The implementing decree is dated 14 November 2025 and was published in the Official Gazette on 18 December 2025. Lombardy chose 3%, on 2025 income and without retroactive effect. Collection has not been confirmed: for the current status, see the site's page on the health contribution.

What the Italian National Health Service covers

The Italian National Health Service provides universal healthcare in Italy:

  • General practitioner: free visits without limits.
  • Specialist visits: with a referral, paying the regional ticket, from €0 to €36.15 depending on the service and region.
  • Hospital stays: completely free in public hospitals.
  • Medicines: class A free or with a ticket; class C paid by the patient.
  • Emergency department: free for urgent codes, ticket for white codes.
  • Maternity: full coverage for pregnancy and childbirth.
  • Prevention: cancer screening, vaccinations and occupational medicine.

Coverage abroad with the Italian National Health Service

With the European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) you are entitled in Switzerland and other EU/EFTA states to medically necessary care during your stay.

In Switzerland you pay flat-rate cost-sharing of CHF 92 every 30 days of care; for minors it is CHF 33. Planned care, including in Switzerland, requires prior authorisation from the ASL using form S2.

Occupational accidents and occupational diseases remain covered by the Swiss employer’s accident insurance.

Specific family advantages of the Italian National Health Service

If only one parent works, the whole family follows that parent’s choice. If the other parent works or receives a pension in Italy, the children remain insured in Italy. If both parents work in Switzerland, each chooses for themselves and the children follow one system.

With LAMal, a family of two adults and two minors pays, with the same insurer, at least 2 × 279.00 + 2 × 64.20 = CHF 686.40 per month, or CHF 8,236.80 per year. With the most expensive insurer for this family, it reaches CHF 1,257.00 per month (2 × 475.30 + 2 × 153.20).

Key points

Concrete scenarios: what each option really costs

The four scenarios use the premiums and cost-sharing described above. Swiss-franc amounts are not converted into euros: CHF figures remain in CHF and euro figures remain in euros. There is no single threshold for everyone: consider your regime, family and place of care without adding amounts in different currencies.

Scenario 1: single, age 30, “new” cross-border worker

Profile: you live in Italy, work in Switzerland and fall under the new regime.

LAMal option:

  • Monthly premium: CHF 279.00 to CHF 487.20.
  • Annual premium: CHF 3,348 to CHF 5,846.40.
  • With CHF 500 of care in Switzerland, you pay CHF 300 deductible plus 10% of CHF 200, or CHF 320.

Italian National Health Service option:

  • Specific health contribution: none.
  • For care in Italy, you pay any ticket; in Switzerland, you receive necessary care during your stay.

Conclusion: the Italian National Health Service costs less in specific contributions; LAMal buys access to care in both countries.

Scenario 2: couple with two children and one income

Profile: one parent works in Switzerland and the other parent lives with two minors.

LAMal option for everyone with the same insurer:

  • Minimum premium: 2 × CHF 279.00 + 2 × CHF 64.20 = CHF 686.40 per month.
  • Minimum annual premium: CHF 8,236.80.
  • Highest premium with the same insurer: 2 × CHF 475.30 + 2 × CHF 153.20 = CHF 1,257.00 per month.

Italian National Health Service option:

  • The whole family follows the working parent’s choice.
  • Under the new regime there is no specific health contribution; for “old” workers, the legal contribution applies.

Conclusion: for families, the premium gap is widest because every member pays an individual premium.

Scenario 3: over 55 with a chronic condition

Profile: you are over 55 and need ongoing care for a condition.

LAMal option:

  • Adult premium: CHF 279.00 to CHF 487.20 per month.
  • Cost-sharing for care in Switzerland: at most CHF 1,000 per year.
  • In Italy, register with the ASL and pay any ticket.

Italian National Health Service option:

  • In Italy, you may use the ticket exemptions provided for the condition.
  • In Switzerland, you receive necessary care during a stay; planned care requires ASL authorisation.

Conclusion: the deciding factor is where the specialists following you are and which system covers your care pathway.

Scenario 4: “old” cross-border worker resident in Lombardy

Profile: you fall under the transitional regime and chose the Italian National Health Service.

Italian National Health Service contribution:

  • Lombardy chose 3% of net salary.
  • With net pay of €4,000 per month, the contribution is €120 per month.
  • The maximum of €200 per month, or €2,400 per year, is reached at net pay of about €6,700.

Comparison with LAMal:

  • The lowest adult premium in 2026 starts at CHF 3,348 per year.
  • Collection of the contribution has not been confirmed.

Conclusion: compare your regime, family and place of care; do not use a CHF-to-euro conversion to decide. These figures describe the cases in the guide and do not replace checking your personal situation.

When to choose LAMal: the concrete reasons

LAMal may fit your situation in the cases below. This is not an automatic rule: check your regime, family composition and place of care first.

Compare the regime and the care you need, without confusing premiums and contributions from different systems.

1. You mainly receive care in Switzerland

You have full access to the Swiss healthcare system and can see the professionals who support you near your workplace. This can matter when your care pathway is mainly in Switzerland.

2. You want care in both countries

In Switzerland you have full access; in Italy you register with the ASL using the health insurer’s certificate and pay any ticket. Italian coverage therefore follows the rules of the public Italian system.

3. You need planned or specialist care in Switzerland

With the Italian National Health Service, planned care in Switzerland requires prior ASL authorisation. LAMal avoids that step for access to the Swiss system when the care falls within LAMal cover.

4. You want a known maximum cost for care in Switzerland

You know the premium and, for an adult, cost-sharing cannot exceed CHF 1,000 per year. This limit concerns care received in Switzerland; in Italy you pay only any ticket.

When to choose the SSN: the concrete reasons

The Italian National Health Service may fit your situation in the cases below. Here too, distinguish “new” from “old” workers and check your family members’ situation.

1. You assess the cost of your regime first

“New” cross-border workers have no specific contribution; for “old” workers the contribution ranges from €30 to €200 per month. The minimum and maximum do not replace checking the contribution that applies to you.

2. You have a family

With LAMal, each member pays a premium: for two adults and two minors, the starting point is CHF 686.40 per month with the same insurer. Count the family premium for every person, not only for the working parent.

3. You mainly receive care in Italy

You remain registered with the ASL and use the Italian system under its rules, paying any ticket. This is worth examining when your doctors and specialists are in Italy.

4. You have a chronic condition with a ticket exemption

Exemptions provided for the condition can reduce the cost of services in Italy. Check which services are included in the exemption before comparing costs.

Common mistakes to avoid when choosing LAMal vs SSN

1. Believing there is a 7.5% SSN contribution on income

A health contribution of 7.5% of income does not exist: “new” workers have no specific contribution; “old” workers follow the 3% to 6% contribution, with a minimum of €30 and maximum of €200 per month.

2. Failing to count premiums for the whole family

With LAMal, every member pays their own premium. For two adults and two minors, the minimum example is CHF 686.40 per month.

3. Relying on high deductibles or Telmed and HMO models

For residents of Italy, optional deductibles and Telmed and HMO models are not available. The ordinary premium is set by insurer.

4. Missing the three-month deadline

Without a formal application within three months there is no option: the LAMal obligation remains retroactive to the start of work.

5. Confusing LAMal with supplementary insurance

LAMal is basic insurance. Supplementary insurance is optional and covers additional services under its contract.

6. Thinking a new employer lets you change your mind

The choice remains final for the entire period of uninterrupted cross-border work. Changing employer does not reopen it. A new contract alone is not enough, even if the workplace or income changes. Reopening requires one of the specific cases described above, not a personal decision alone.

Frequently asked questions about choosing LAMal vs the Italian National Health Service

Can I switch from the Italian National Health Service to LAMal after the initial choice?

No. The choice is final for the entire period of uninterrupted cross-border work. It reopens only after a period subject to another state’s system or when you become a pension beneficiary; changing employer is not enough. For a new family member, a person insured under LAMal can request an exemption within three months of the event.

Can I switch from LAMal to the Italian National Health Service?

No, the same rule applies: the choice is final. Reopening is possible only in the cases provided for after a period subject to another state’s system or with a pension.

What happens if my spouse works in Italy?

If the other parent works or receives a pension in Italy, the children remain insured in Italy and LAMal applies only to the cross-border worker.

Does LAMal cover care in Italy?

Yes. Register with the ASL using the Swiss health insurer’s certificate and receive services under Italian rules, paying any ticket. Private facilities that do not accept the card are not covered.

Does the Italian National Health Service cover care in Switzerland?

For medically necessary care during a stay, use the European card and pay CHF 92 every 30 days of care. Planned care requires prior ASL authorisation using form S2.

How long do I have to decide?

You have three months from the start of work in Switzerland. Submit the application to the cantonal authority; in Ticino, the Social Insurance Institute is responsible. Without an application, the LAMal obligation remains retroactive.

Can I have both LAMal and private Italian insurance?

Yes, you can take out Italian supplementary insurance. It is separate from basic cover and should be assessed according to the services you need.

How does the choice affect my Italian tax return?

We have not found official confirmation that LAMal premiums are deductible. Ask a CAF or tax adviser to assess your situation.

Quick decision guide

Choose LAMal if:

  • You mainly receive care in Switzerland.
  • You want access to care in both countries.
  • You need planned care in Switzerland.
  • You want a known limit for cost-sharing for care in Switzerland.

Choose the Italian National Health Service if:

  • You fall under a regime with no specific contribution or a contribution compatible with your situation.
  • You have family members who follow your choice.
  • You mainly receive care in Italy.
  • You have a chronic condition with a ticket exemption.

Assess case by case:

  • Where the specialists supporting you are located.
  • How your family and regime are structured.
