LAMal vs SSN: The Cross-Border Worker's Health Insurance Choice

LAMal (Federal Health Insurance Act) is Switzerland's mandatory health insurance system, while SSN (Servizio Sanitario Nazionale) is Italy's national public healthcare service. Cross-border workers starting employment in Switzerland have an option right (diritto di opzione): they can choose between enrolling in Swiss LAMal or maintaining Italian SSN coverage. This choice, which must be communicated within 3 months of starting work, can impact household healthcare spending by thousands of euros per year.

"The option right is the first major decision a cross-border worker faces, often with fragmented information and little time to evaluate. Those who choose without analysing their specific situation risk regretting it for years." — Laura Mantovani, LAMal Insurance Consultant for Cross-Border Workers

According to FOPH (Federal Office of Public Health) data, over 78,000 cross-border workers were employed in Canton Ticino in 2025. Of these, approximately 35% opted for Swiss LAMal, while the remaining 65% maintained Italian SSN coverage (Source: FOPH/BAG, Health Insurance Statistics 2025). The choice depends on individual factors: age, health status, family composition, income, and how frequently medical care is needed.

This article provides a detailed analysis of both systems, with concrete calculations, real scenarios, and a decision framework to help you choose the most advantageous option for your situation.

How LAMal Works for Cross-Border Workers

The Insurance Obligation and the Cross-Border Exemption

In Switzerland, anyone who resides or works is obligated to take out LAMal health insurance. Cross-border workers, as non-resident employees, benefit from a derogation under the Agreement on the Free Movement of Persons (AFMP): they may choose not to join LAMal and remain covered by the healthcare system of their country of residence (Source: Art. 2 OAMal, Health Insurance Ordinance).

The deadline to exercise the option right is 3 months from the start of employment in Switzerland. If no choice is communicated, the worker is automatically enrolled in LAMal. The choice is generally binding: switching from SSN to LAMal is only possible under specific circumstances (change of employer, new employment), while switching from LAMal to SSN requires termination of the Swiss employment relationship.

LAMal premiums for cross-border workers vary significantly based on four main factors:

  • Chosen insurer: the 14 main health insurers in Ticino (CSS, Swica, Helsana, Visana, Concordia, Sanitas, Assura, KPT, Atupri, ÖKK, EGK, Sympany, Agrisano, Aquilana) charge different premiums
  • Annual deductible: from CHF 300 (minimum) to CHF 2,500 (maximum) for adults; from CHF 0 to CHF 600 for children
  • Insurance model: Standard (free choice of doctor), Telmed (telephone consultation before visit), HMO (contracted medical centre), Family Doctor (designated GP)
  • Canton of work: premiums vary by canton; Ticino has higher average premiums than Graubünden

How the LAMal Deductible Works

The deductible is the annual amount you pay out of pocket before the health insurer starts reimbursing expenses. Once the deductible is met, you pay a 10% cost-sharing up to a maximum of CHF 700/year for adults (CHF 350 for children).

Practical example: with a CHF 300 deductible and annual medical expenses of CHF 3,000:

  • You pay: CHF 300 (deductible) + CHF 270 (10% of CHF 2,700) = CHF 570 out of pocket
  • The insurer covers: CHF 2,430

With a CHF 2,500 deductible and the same expenses:

  • You pay: CHF 2,500 (deductible) + CHF 50 (10% of CHF 500) = CHF 2,550 out of pocket
  • The insurer covers: CHF 450

LAMal Territorial Coverage

With LAMal you have full access to the Swiss healthcare system: public hospitals, medical specialists, emergency rooms, medicines on the specialities list. In Italy and the EU/EFTA, you are entitled to urgent and necessary care via the insurance card (equivalent to the EHIC). For planned treatment in Italy, you can request prior authorisation from your health insurer.

How the Italian SSN Works for Cross-Border Workers

Enrolment and Contributions

Cross-border workers who opt for the Italian SSN register with the ASL (Local Health Authority) of their municipality of residence. The health contribution is included in the Italian tax return and calculated as a percentage of total income.

It's not 'free' as many believe — it's paid through the tax system." — Dr Elena Colombo, Tax Consultant Specialising in International Taxation

What the Italian SSN Covers

The SSN offers universal healthcare coverage in Italy:

  • General practitioner (family doctor): unlimited free consultations
  • Specialist visits: with GP referral, paying the regional co-payment (€0 to €36.15 depending on the service and region)
  • Hospital admissions: completely free in public hospitals
  • Medications: Band A drugs (essential) free or with co-payment; Band C drugs paid by the patient
  • Emergency room: free for urgent codes (yellow, red), co-payment for white codes
  • Maternity: comprehensive coverage for pregnancy and childbirth
  • Prevention: cancer screenings, vaccinations, occupational health

Coverage Abroad with the SSN

With the SSN you receive the European Health Insurance Card (EHIC/TEAM), which guarantees:

  • Urgent and necessary care in all EU/EFTA countries, including Switzerland
  • Limitations: only covers services under the host country's public system, not private clinics
  • In Switzerland: the EHIC covers emergencies only. For planned specialist visits, you need prior authorisation from your ASL (S2 form)
  • Planned treatment: possible in the EU with prior authorisation, but not in Switzerland for non-urgent services

Specific SSN Advantages for Families

Dependent family members (spouse, children) are covered by the SSN at no additional cost. This is the most significant advantage over LAMal, where each family member pays an individual premium. For a family with 2 children, the annual saving can exceed CHF 6,000–10,000 compared to LAMal.

When to Choose LAMal: 6 Concrete Reasons

LAMal is the better choice if you identify with at least 3 of these situations:

1. Your Income Exceeds CHF 5,500/month

With the SSN contribution calculated at 7.5% of income, above CHF 5,500/month the SSN costs more than an average LAMal premium. The higher the income, the greater the saving with LAMal. For an income of CHF 8,000/month, savings can exceed CHF 3,000/year.

2. You Work and Seek Care Primarily in Switzerland

If your trusted doctor is in Switzerland, if you need Swiss specialists, or if you spend most of your day in Ticino, LAMal guarantees direct and complete access to the Swiss healthcare system without prior authorisations.

3. You're Single or a Couple Without Children

Without dependent family members, the SSN's economic advantage (free coverage for dependants) doesn't apply. The comparison reduces to a direct premium vs income-based contribution calculation.

4. You Want Short Waiting Times for Specialist Visits

In Switzerland, waiting times for specialist visits average 2–4 weeks, compared to 2–6 months in the Italian public system. For those with urgent or recurring medical needs, this difference is crucial (Source: OECD Health at a Glance 2024, Specialist Waiting Times).

5. You're Under 40 and in Good Health

With a high deductible (CHF 2,500) and an alternative model (HMO or Telmed), LAMal premiums drop below CHF 250/month. If your annual medical expenses are low, the effective cost of LAMal is very contained.

6. You Want Free Choice of Doctor

With the LAMal Standard model, you can consult any doctor or specialist in Switzerland without referrals. This freedom doesn't exist in the SSN, where you always need a GP referral to access specialists.

When to Choose SSN: 6 Concrete Reasons

The Italian SSN is the better choice in these situations:

1. You Have a Family with Children or a Dependent Spouse

This is the decisive factor. With LAMal, each family member pays a separate premium: a family of 4 pays CHF 700–900/month in premiums alone. With the SSN, dependent family members are covered at no additional cost.

2. Your Income is Below CHF 5,000/month

Below this threshold, the SSN contribution (7.5% of income) is lower than a LAMal premium. For example, at CHF 4,000/month, the SSN contribution is approximately €279/month (≈ CHF 300), less than any LAMal premium with a reasonable deductible.

3. You Seek Care Primarily in Italy

If your GP, dentist, children's paediatrician, and regular specialists are all in Italy, you don't need direct access to the Swiss system. The SSN fully covers care on Italian territory.

4. You Have a Chronic Condition with Co-Payment Exemption

The SSN provides co-payment exemptions for chronic conditions (diabetes, hypertension, heart disease, etc.) and for low income. With an exemption, many services become completely free, further reducing the effective cost.

5. You Want Basic Dental Coverage Included

The SSN partially covers dental care (extractions, conservative treatments) through the public or contracted dental service. Basic LAMal does not cover dental care, which requires supplementary insurance (VVG) at additional cost.

6. You Prefer Italian Administrative Simplicity

With the SSN, you don't need to manage invoices, deductibles, reimbursements, or cost-sharing. You visit the doctor, present your health card, and the service is provided. With LAMal, you need to manage the relationship with the health insurer, reimbursements, and deductible tracking.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Choosing

❌ Mistake 1: Thinking the SSN is "Free"

The SSN is not free: the 7.5% health contribution is included in Italian taxes. On an average cross-border worker income, this amounts to €350–500/month. It's "invisible" because you don't receive a separate bill, but it definitely impacts net income.

❌ Mistake 2: Not Considering Total Family Costs

Many people compare only individual costs. But if you have a spouse and two children, you need to quadruple the LAMal premium — while the SSN covers everyone at no additional cost. The difference can exceed CHF 6,000/year.

❌ Mistake 3: Choosing the Wrong Deductible

The CHF 2,500 deductible reduces the premium by 40%, but exposes you to CHF 2,500 in expenses if needed. If you require regular care, the CHF 300 deductible is safer despite the higher premium. Rule of thumb: choose the high deductible only if you have an emergency fund of at least CHF 3,000.

❌ Mistake 4: Missing the 3-Month Deadline

If you don't communicate your choice within 3 months of starting work in Switzerland, you are automatically enrolled in LAMal. The default assignment is to the canton's designated insurer and not necessarily the most affordable one.

❌ Mistake 5: Confusing LAMal with Supplementary Insurance

LAMal is the mandatory basic insurance and offers the same benefits regardless of the health insurer. The differences between insurers concern only the premium and customer service. Supplementary insurance (VVG/LCA) is optional and covers additional services (private room, alternative medicine, dental care).

❌ Mistake 6: Not Considering the CHF/EUR Exchange Rate

The LAMal premium is in CHF, the SSN contribution is calculated in EUR. Fluctuations in the CHF/EUR exchange rate can shift the break-even point by several hundred euros per year. When the CHF is strong, LAMal becomes relatively more expensive for those spending in EUR.

Frequently Asked Questions About LAMal vs SSN

Can I Switch from SSN to LAMal After the Initial Choice?

Switching from SSN to LAMal is only possible when starting a new employment relationship in Switzerland (change of employer or break and resumption). You cannot simply decide to switch mid-year. The new 3-month deadline runs from the start date of the new contract (Source: Art. 2 OAMal; FOPH, Option Right FAQ).

Can I Switch from LAMal to SSN?

Switching from LAMal to SSN is more complex. You must terminate your Swiss employment and, upon restarting with a new employer, you can exercise the option right again within 3 months. Some cantons have stricter interpretations: check with the relevant cantonal authority.

How Does It Work if My Spouse Works in Italy?

If your spouse works in Italy and is enrolled in the SSN, children can be covered by your spouse's SSN even if you choose LAMal. This "mixed model" allows optimisation: LAMal for you (Swiss access) and SSN for the family (free coverage through the spouse).

Does LAMal Cover Care in Italy?

Yes, with limitations. LAMal issues an insurance card equivalent to the EHIC that covers urgent and necessary care in Italy and throughout the EU/EFTA. For planned treatment in Italy, you need prior authorisation from your health insurer. Costs are reimbursed according to the tariffs of the country of treatment.

Does the SSN Cover Care in Switzerland?

Only emergency care via the EHIC card. For planned specialist visits in Switzerland, you need the S2 form issued by your Italian ASL, but it is rarely granted and only for services not available within reasonable timeframes in Italy. In practice, with the SSN, Swiss medical care is almost always at your own expense.

How Much Time Do I Have to Decide?

You have 3 months (90 days) from the start of employment in Switzerland. The deadline is strict: if it passes without communication, you are assigned to LAMal by default. The form must be submitted to the relevant cantonal authority (in Ticino: Istituto delle assicurazioni sociali, IAS).

Can I Have Both LAMal and Italian Private Insurance?

Yes. If you choose LAMal, you can also take out Italian supplementary or top-up insurance to cover dental care, private visits in Italy, or services not included in basic LAMal. Many cross-border workers with LAMal add a supplementary policy costing €30–80/month for dual coverage.

How Does the Choice Affect My Italian Tax Return?

If you choose LAMal, premiums paid are deductible from Italian taxable income as deductible expenses (section RP/E of the 730 or Redditi PF form). If you choose the SSN, the health contribution is already included in IRPEF and does not generate additional deductions.

"The deductibility of LAMal premiums from the Italian tax return is an often-overlooked advantage. For an annual premium of CHF 4,000, the deduction can generate a tax saving of €800–1,200 in IRPEF, reducing the effective cost of LAMal." — Dr Elena Colombo, Tax Consultant Specialising in International Taxation

Quick Decision Guide

Choose LAMal if:

  • ✅ Single or couple without children
  • ✅ Income above CHF 5,500/month
  • ✅ You seek care in Switzerland
  • ✅ You want short waiting times
  • ✅ Young and healthy (high deductible)

Choose SSN if:

  • ✅ Family with children or dependent spouse
  • ✅ Income below CHF 5,000/month
  • ✅ You seek care in Italy
  • ✅ You have chronic condition exemptions
  • ✅ You prefer administrative simplicity

Evaluate on a case-by-case basis if:

  • ⚖️ Income between CHF 5,000 and CHF 6,000/month (grey zone)
  • ⚖️ Chronic condition requiring specialists in both CH and IT
  • ⚖️ Family with only one child (SSN saving is smaller)

Conclusion: A Choice That Deserves Analysis, Not Improvisation

The choice between LAMal and SSN is one of the most important financial decisions for a cross-border worker — it can impact household healthcare spending by CHF 3,000–8,000 per year. There is no universal answer: the optimal choice depends on your income, family composition, health status, and where you prefer to receive care.

Frequently Asked Questions
How does the right of choice work for cross-border commuters working in Switzerland?
Cross-border commuters have the right to choose between registering with the Swiss KVG or maintaining the coverage of the Italian NHS. The choice must be communicated within 3 months of starting work in Switzerland.
How many cross-border commuters work in Switzerland and what percentage does the KVG choose?
According to 2025 data, more than 78,000 cross-border commuters work in the Canton of Ticino alone. About 35% of them choose the Swiss KVG, while 65% maintain the Italian NHS.
What treatment in Italy is covered by the Swiss KVG for cross-border commuters?
The KVG covers urgent and necessary treatment in Italy through the EHIC card (equivalent to the Italian health card). For planned or specialist treatment in Italy, prior authorisation must be obtained from the Swiss health insurance company. Coverage is valid in all EU/EFTA countries.
How are SSN contributions calculated for a cross-border worker who works in Ticino but resides in Lombardy?
In Lombardy, the SSN rate is 7.50% of total income, converted into euros. For example, on an annual income of €50,000, the contribution is €3,750. Payment is made through the Italian tax return.
What happens if a cross-border commuter with KVG has an accident in Italy?
In the event of an accident in Italy, the KVG covers the necessary and urgent care via the EHIC card. The Swiss health insurance company reimburses the costs according to local rates, while any additional costs (e.g. hospitalisation in a private room) remain the responsibility of the insured person.
