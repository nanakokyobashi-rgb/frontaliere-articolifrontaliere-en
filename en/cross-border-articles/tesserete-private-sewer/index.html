Tesserete: private sewer connection, owner pays

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 10 October 2026

Tesserete: the Council of State ruled that the rehabilitation of the private sewer was the owner's responsibility, following the repair paid for by the Municipality of Capriasca.

Context

TL;DR

  • In Tesserete, a wall was leaking foul-smelling liquid.
  • The Municipality of Capriasca paid for the repair.
  • The Council of State assigned the cost to the private party.
  • The owner filed no further appeals and paid.

Key facts

  • Place → Tesserete, Municipality of Capriasca
  • Problem → smelly liquid leakage
  • Cause → breakage of the sewer pipe
  • Dispute → municipal or private sewerage
  • Decision → cost borne by the private individual
  • Outcome → expenses advanced by the Municipality paid by the owner

In Tesserete, a year after a citizen's report, the story of a smelly liquid leaking from a wall has reached its final point. The problem had been dragging on for several months and concerned the wall of the reporter's neighbour. The checks carried out by the Municipality of Capriasca had led to a precise conclusion: at the origin there had been a break in the sewer pipe.

The repair was considered easily solvable. The issue was another: determining who should bear the costs. The owner of the land believed that the Municipality should intervene, because the canalization was municipal. The Municipality instead claimed that the plant was private.

The divergence arose from the history of the conduct. In the past it had been municipal, but several years earlier it had been decommissioned to become private. That service passage did not appear clear. For this reason, the owner had decided to appeal to the Council of State, while the parties continued their legal discussions.

Before the decision arrived, the Municipality of Capriasca still assumed the cost of the repair. The intervention resolved the bad smell, to the satisfaction of the citizen who had reported the problem. The material solution, however, did not close the discussion on who should pay definitively.

The decision of the Council of State

The sentence arrived months later. The Council of State established that the cost of the works fell on the private individual, because the canalization was actually located on his property. The position of the Municipality of Capriasca was therefore considered correct.

The criterion indicated is practical: a canalization is private when it serves only one fund or some contiguous funds; it is public when it serves an entire neighborhood. In the case of Tesserete, the location on the property led to the conduct being considered private.

The owner of the wall ultimately chose not to file a further appeal. He paid the costs that the Municipality had advanced for the cleanup, thus closing both the dispute over the costs and the bad smell affair.

Operational details

The practical value of the judgment lies in the separation between two moments that, in Tesserete, remained distinct: repairing the problem and finally assigning the expense. The Municipality of Capriasca bore the cost while the legal dispute was still open. The subsequent verdict, however, established that the outlay had to fall on the private party. The authority's advance payment therefore did not, on its own, establish who was ultimately responsible for the cost.

The criterion used to distinguish public from private

The decision provides a criterion that can also be understood outside this individual case. When a sewer line serves only one property or several adjoining properties, it is private; when it serves an entire neighborhood, it is public.

The comparison before and after the judgment therefore changes in economic terms, not in the material result: before the decision, the bad smell had already been eliminated; after the decision, it was determined who had to reimburse the expenses paid in advance. The owner chose to close the dispute by paying, without filing a further appeal.

Key points

The Tesserete case makes it possible to reconstruct a precise sequence, useful for understanding how the issue shifted: from the bad smell to the technical cause, from the classification of the sewer line to the decision on costs.

The documented sequence

1. Report. A citizen of Tesserete reports the outflow of a foul-smelling liquid from the wall of the neighboring property. The situation had already been dragging on for several months.

2. Checks. The Municipality of Capriasca investigates the problem and concludes that the cause is a break in the sewer line.

3. Disagreement over ownership. The property owner argues that the sewer line is municipal and that the Municipality must carry out the work. The Municipality considers it private instead. In the background is the passage of a pipeline that was once municipal and was later decommissioned and made private.

4. Appeal and repair. The owner appeals to the Council of State. While the legal dispute continues, the Municipality of Capriasca bears the cost of the repair, and the bad-smell problem is resolved.

5. Judgment and payment. Months later, the decision arrives: the sewer line is located on private property, and the cost falls to the owner. The latter chooses not to appeal further and pays the expenses advanced by the Municipality.

The sequence shows that the solution to the fault and the solution to the dispute can come at different times. In this specific case, the smell was eliminated while the financial issue was still open; the final determination of the costs came only with the judgment. The process described therefore remains composed of municipal checks, discussion about the nature of the sewer line, an appeal and a final decision.
