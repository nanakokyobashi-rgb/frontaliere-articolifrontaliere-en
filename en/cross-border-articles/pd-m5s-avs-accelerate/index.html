Pd, M5S and Avs accelerate initiatives (cross-border guide)

Di Laura Bianchi · Published on 16 June 2026

Italian political parties announce initiatives for July.

Context

In brief

  • Pd, M5S, and Avs accelerate their race towards the general elections.
  • Two public initiatives are announced for July.

Key facts

  • What: Race towards the general elections.
  • When: July.
  • Where: Italy.
  • Who: Pd, M5S, Avs.
  • Amount: Not specified.

The Democratic Party, the Five Star Movement, and the Green and Left Alliance are ramping up preparations for the upcoming Italian general elections. The leaders of the three political forces, Elly Schlein (Pd), Giuseppe Conte (M5S), Nicola Fratoianni, and Angelo Bonelli (Avs), have officially launched this new phase with a selfie posted on social media. The symbolic photo was taken after a lunch at Costanza Hosteria, a restaurant located near Campo de' Fiori, the vibrant heart of the capital.

📊 The next steps Two major public initiatives are expected in July, aiming to consolidate the coalitions' messaging and mobilize their respective voter bases. Although details have not yet been disclosed, it is likely that key topics will include labor, climate, and social justice — central issues in the platforms of these political forces.

💡 Focus on the Italian-Swiss border A crucial topic that the parties might address is cross-border employment, which is particularly relevant for the approximately 76,000 Italian workers employed in Ticino (2022 data). Cross-border workers represent a significant portion of the local economy but are often at the center of debates on minimum wages, taxation, and downward competition. For instance, regulations like the 2020 Italy-Switzerland tax agreement, which changes the taxation methods for cross-border workers, could become a point of discussion.

Operational details

Political Implications

The move by Italian parties appears to be a strategic attempt to strengthen their position ahead of the upcoming national elections. With two public initiatives scheduled for July — one in the North and one in the South — the main progressive forces, including the Democratic Party (Pd), the Five Star Movement (M5S), and the Greens and Left Alliance (Avs), aim to build a united front. Their stated goal is to present a reformist platform capable of attracting a broader electoral base and offering concrete solutions to tackle the country's challenges, from the economic crisis to social inequalities.

Giuseppe Conte, leader of the M5S, has emphasized the urgency of this initiative, stating: > 'Now it's up to us.' According to Conte, the focus will be on changing Italy through proposals that have a direct impact on citizens' daily lives. However, as of now, there are no specific details about what will actually be discussed or proposed during these meetings, leaving many questions unanswered.

Looking Ahead

The July initiatives represent a valuable opportunity for the participating parties to engage with citizens and listen to their concerns. According to internal sources, the Northern meeting is expected to take place in a city symbolic of cross-border work, such as Lugano, where employment and wage dumping could be key topics. In the South, attention is likely to shift toward revitalizing the Mezzogiorno, with a focus on infrastructure and PNRR funds.

📊 Practical Example: In 2022, the median gross salary in Ticino was around 6,500 Swiss francs per month, but many cross-border workers earn less than 4,000 francs, highlighting a problem of wage disparity. Proposals may include measures to encourage collective contracts and ensure greater pay equity.

Key points

What to do

For those interested in following the developments of this political situation, it is crucial to monitor the official channels of the parties involved, such as the Pd, M5S, and Avs, which are intensifying their activities in view of the upcoming elections. For example, the Pd recently announced an event in Lugano on November 15th to discuss the implications of new regulations regarding cross-border workers, which could affect over 70,000 Italian workers in Switzerland, particularly in the Canton of Ticino.

Meanwhile, the Five Star Movement has launched a series of informational meetings in key municipalities such as Chiasso and Mendrisio, with the aim of raising awareness among citizens about the fiscal changes introduced in 2023, such as the agreement on the taxation of cross-border workers, which came into effect on January 1st. This agreement provides that cross-border workers with incomes below 90,000 CHF gross will receive a reduction in their tax burden of up to 30%, but it has raised concerns among workers with higher incomes, who may be subject to higher taxation.

For those who wish to better understand the context and possible implications, it is useful to consult in-depth political analyses and updated informational sources, such as the reports from the official portal of the Canton of Ticino and the documents published by the Italian Ministry of Economy.
