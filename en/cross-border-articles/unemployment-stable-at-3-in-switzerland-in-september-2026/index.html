Unemployment stable at 3% in Switzerland in September 2026

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 6 October 2026

In September, unemployment in Switzerland remains at 3% and in Ticino at 2,9%, with 4'800 unemployed, while job vacancies rise to 44'200.

Context

TL;DR

  • Swiss unemployment at 3% in September.
  • Ticino at 2,9%, with 4'800 unemployed.
  • In Switzerland, 140'500 registered with the URCs.
  • Announced vacancies are 44'200.

Key facts

  • National rate → 3% in September.
  • Ticino → 2,9% in September.
  • Graubünden → 1,1% in September.
  • ILO comparison → 4,9% in the second quarter.
  • Registered with the URCs → 140'500.

Swiss unemployment stood at 3% in September, unchanged from August and July. SECO reported that, at the end of the year's ninth month, 140'500 unemployed people were registered with the regional employment offices (URCs): 1'100 fewer than in August and 7'200 more than a year earlier. Absolute figures are rounded to the nearest hundred for ease of reading. Over twelve months, however, the national rate rose by 0,2 percentage points.

In January 2021, at the most critical point of the pandemic, nearly 170'000 people without work had been recorded, with the rate at 3,7%. For those who live near the border and work in the Canton of Ticino, this comparison provides a general measure of the market in which they operate; section disoccupazione frontalieri should, however, be kept separate from SECO's official figure.

The Ticino figure in the Swiss context

In Ticino, the rate stood at 2,9% in September, unchanged from August and up by 0,3 percentage points year-on-year. From Airolo to Chiasso, there were 4'800 unemployed: 100 more than in the previous month and 400 more than a year earlier. Ticino ranks 11th among the territories most affected by the problem; Geneva and Vaud are experiencing greater difficulties.

The Graubünden recorded a rate of 1,1%, unchanged from the previous month and down by 0,1 percentage points year-on-year. In the canton whose capital is Chur, there were 1'200 unemployed, unchanged. The Graubünden rank fifth among the least affected territories, led by Appenzell Innerrhoden.

The statistic has a precise scope. It counts people actually registered with the URCs and does not include those who have exhausted their entitlement to benefits and live, for example, on savings or welfare. The definition therefore differs from that of the International Labour Organization (ILO), which is based on surveys: in the latest available figure, referring to the second quarter, Swiss unemployment according to the ILO was 4,9%.

Operational details

The differences that matter to those who work across the border

A cross-border worker observing the labor market in Canton Ticino must start from the measured population. People registered with the URC are a different group from people seeking employment: the latter includes, in addition to registered unemployed people, workers attending retraining or further-training courses, participating in employment programs, or earning an interim income. Last month, people seeking employment numbered 227'600, up 0,2% compared with August.

For a cross-border worker, this marks an editorial boundary: the bulletin measures unemployment, not issues such as the G permit, AVS, LPP, LAMal, tax rebates and withholding tax. These elements should not be inferred from the percentages published by SECO.

Age, nationality and duration

The age brackets show three distinct figures: 3,3% among young people aged 15 to 24, 3,2% among 25- to 49-year-olds and 2,6% among over-50s. The breakdown by nationality instead indicates 2,1% for Swiss nationals, unchanged month on month and with +0,1 year on year, and 5,1% for foreign nationals, unchanged month on month and with +0,2 year on year.

Another signal concerns the duration of the job search. The long-term unemployed numbered 25'600, 0,1% more than in August and 25,4% more than twelve months earlier; among them were 10'300 people over 50. The figure therefore adds to the monthly snapshot a measure of the length of registration with the URC.

The number of vacancies announced at placement offices was 44'200, with 500 more than in the previous month and 6'800 more than in the previous year. Anyone who wants to pair the statistic with a search for opportunities can consult the annunci lavoro, keeping overall availability and their own situation separate.

Short-time work also has a different reference period: in June, the latest available figure, it concerned 840 people. In September, by contrast, 3'000 people exhausted their entitlement to unemployment insurance benefits.

Key points

A procedure for reading the next update

When SECO releases a new overview, a cross-border worker can use five simple checks. The goal is not to place on the same footing indicators based on different populations or periods.

Five practical checks

1. Set the period: indicate whether the data concern September or June, as in the case of short-time work, for which the most recent information was available. 2. Identify the indicator: distinguish the percentage rate, the number of people registered with the URCs, people seeking employment, and the ILO survey. 3. Note the comparison: record whether the change is monthly or annual, because the source presents both readings. 4. Check the territory: distinguish the Swiss figure from those for Ticino and the Graubünden before making comparisons. 5. Complete the form: add, when available, age group, nationality, length of registration, job vacancies, and short-time work.

The first check prevents directly comparing data referring to different months. The second reminds us that the number of people registered with the URCs does not coincide with all people seeking employment, while the ILO figure comes from surveys and has a different definition.

The third step serves to avoid confusing a change relative to August with a change over twelve months. The fourth prevents automatically attributing to the Canton of Ticino a figure referring to Switzerland as a whole. Finally, the form must indicate that absolute figures are rounded to the nearest hundred and that some values, such as job vacancies or short-time work, have a specific time frame.

What is the unemployment rate in Switzerland in September 2026?

How many unemployed people are there in Ticino and what is the monthly change?
In Ticino, there were 4,800 unemployed in September 2026, 100 more than in August 2026 and 400 more than in September 2025. The unemployment rate in Ticino is 2.9%, up 0.3 percentage points year-on-year.
How to distinguish SECO data from ILO indicators according to the guide for cross-border commuters?
The guide offers five checks: set the period (September vs. June), identify the indicator (rate, URC members, jobseekers, ILO), record the comparison (monthly or annual), check the region (Switzerland, Ticino, Graubünden), and complete the form with age, nationality, duration, vacancies, and short-time work. This avoids confusing different definitions, such as the SECO (URC members) and the ILO (surveys).
