Crosio della Valle: border crossing between Italy and Ticino (cross-border guide)

Living in Crosio della Valle, working in Ticino: G permit, taxation, crossings and costs. Guide for border workers.

Context

In brief

  • Permesso G allows daily commuting between Crosio della Valle and Ticino without Swiss residence
  • Withholding tax only in Switzerland from January 1, 2024
  • Open borders: Brogeda, Chiasso, Gaggiolo, Ponte Tresa towards Ticino
  • Lower cost of living in Italy, Swiss salary in CHF is advantageous

Key facts

  • What: Permesso G for cross-border workers living in Italy and working in Switzerland
  • When: New Cross-Border Agreement in effect from January 1, 2024
  • Where: Crosio della Valle (Italy) – Canton Ticino (Switzerland)
  • Who: Cross-border workers, Swiss and Italian administrations, employers
  • Tax: Withheld exclusively in Switzerland (tax credit in Italy)
  • Borders: Brogeda, Chiasso, Gaggiolo, Ponte Tresa

Living in Crosio della Valle and working in Ticino is a strategic choice for thousands of cross-border workers. The Permesso G allows daily commuting without the need for permanent residence in Switzerland, while the cross-border tax regime (regulated by the New Cross-Border Agreement effective from January 1, 2024) offers concrete advantages on withholding tax: it is collected exclusively by Switzerland, and Italy avoids double taxation by automatically recognizing the tax credit in the CE section of the 730 declaration.

Permesso G and daily commuting

The Permesso G allows an Italian citizen to work in Ticino without permanently residing in Switzerland. This means you can live in Crosio della Valle and cross the Ticino borders daily (Brogeda, Chiasso, Gaggiolo or Ponte Tresa) to reach your workplace. You are not required to move your residence to Switzerland; you remain a resident in Italy, pay Italian municipal taxes in Crosio della Valle, and benefit from the Swiss cross-border tax structure.

Operational details

Advantages and disadvantages of border choice

Choosing to live in Crosio della Valle and work in Ticino presents concrete pros and cons.

Advantages: The cost of living in Italy (rent, food, services) is typically lower than in Ticino. A Swiss salary in CHF maintains much higher purchasing power than an Italian salary in EUR, allowing you to save significantly. The Swiss social contribution (AVS/AI/IPG at 5.3% paid by the employee, in addition to AD/AC 1.1% and LAINF 0.7-1.5% for the employer) translates into a more robust social security: access the AVS system (first Swiss pillar) and you can join the LPP (second pillar, professional insurance) with rates ranging from 7% to 18% depending on the age group (from 25 years). In addition, you retain the rights of residence and Italian communities: family, social network, local schools remain in Italy.

Disadvantages: Daily commuting involves vehicle wear, fuel costs, and above all loss of time and psychophysical fatigue. Queues at the passes (Brogeda, Chiasso, Gaggiolo, Ponte Tresa) can lengthen the journey by 30–60 minutes on busy days. Remain exposed to Italian legislation regarding municipal taxes, property taxes, and local services. Finally, if you change employer or your contract changes mode, you may lose the right to the G permit.

Key points

How to apply for the G permit and move

To live in Crosio della Valle and work in Ticino, follow these concrete steps:

1. Make sure you have a job contract in Ticino. The G permit is tied to employment: you need a job offer or a contract with a Swiss employer in the canton of Ticino. Without this documentation, your application will be rejected.

2. Gather the required documents. Identity documents (passport or Italian ID card), original employment contract, residence certificates, criminal records and pending charges certificates issued by the competent Italian authorities. Request these from the Municipality of Crosio della Valle and the relevant Police Headquarters. The Swiss employer will be part of the procedure: the communication to the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) of the Swiss Confederation must come from the Swiss authorities.

3. Submit the application through your employer. The Swiss employer will submit the G permit application to the competent Ticino cantonal authority (Division of Economy, Migration Section). You cannot submit the application personally from Italy; it is the employer who manages the Swiss administrative procedure.

4. Wait for approval. Approval times vary from 2 to 6 weeks. Once approved, you will receive the G permit (paper or digital document). The permit is personal and tied to the employer: if you change employers, you will need to apply for a new permit.

5. Registration with the Municipality of Crosio della Valle. Ensure that your residence in Italy is official with the Municipality of Crosio della Valle. The Municipality will have information on your cross-border employment and the importance of correct declaration for tax purposes.

Frequently Asked Questions
Does the G permit allow me to work for a Swiss employer while I live in Italy?
Yes. The G permit authorizes daily commuting: you can live in Crosio della Valle (Italy) and work in Ticino (Switzerland) without having to be a Swiss resident. You must cross the passes (Brogeda, Chiasso, Gaggiolo, Ponte Tresa) daily. The permit is tied to the specific employer and the contract: if you change jobs, you need to apply for a new permit through the new employer.
How does the tax at source work with the New Frontier Agreement from 2024?
From 1 January 2024, the income tax at source on employee income is exclusively withheld from Switzerland (by the employer or the Swiss administration). Italy avoids double taxation by automatically recognizing the tax credit in the EC framework of declaration 730. You will not see additional Italian withholdings on your Swiss paycheck, greatly simplifying tax management.
What social security contributions do I pay in Switzerland as a border worker in Ticino?
As an employee in Ticino, you pay: AVS/AI/IPG 5.3% (at the employee's expense), AD/AC 1.1% with cap CHF 148.200, LAINF 0.7-1.5%. If you join the LPP (professional insurance, second pillar), the rate varies from 7% to 18% depending on the age group (from 25 years onwards). These contributions fund Swiss social security and healthcare, ensuring you have a robust pension and social protection.
Should you choose the Swiss LAMal or keep your registration with the Italian SSN?
It depends on your personal situation. The Swiss LAMal (CHF 300-2,500 per year adult franchise) offers direct access to quality Swiss medical facilities, but the annual premiums are higher than in Italy. If you keep the Italian NHS, the healthcare costs are lower but access to Swiss facilities requires different procedures. Consult a specialist border insurance broker for a personalised assessment.
What are the travel times from the passes at Crosio della Valle to the Ticino work areas?
The passes (Brogeda, Chiasso, Gaggiolo, Ponte Tresa) are just a few kilometres from Crosio della Valle. From Switzerland, the time to Mendrisio is 15–30 minutes (without queues), to Lugano 30–50 minutes, to Bellinzona 60–90 minutes. Queues at crossings can significantly lengthen times during rush hours (7:00 -9:00and 17:00 -19:00). Plan your travels considering these peaks.
