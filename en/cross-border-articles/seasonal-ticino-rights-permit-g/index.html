The new 2024 tax agreement and the implications for seasonal workers

Starting from 1 January 2024, the new tax agreement between Italy and Switzerland, signed on 23 December 2020 and ratified by the Italian Parliament with law 83 of 13 June 2023, comes into full force and transforms the tax framework of Ticino cross-border workers. This agreement redefines how social contributions are calculated and collected, as well as the tax refund mechanism.

For those who carry out seasonal work in Ticino (summer, winter, specific periods of the year), the change is significant and direct. This differentiation applies both to those who work all year round and to those who alternate seasons of activity with periods of inactivity.

The central element of the reform is the withholding tax: it is withheld exclusively by the Swiss employer from the cross-border worker's gross income. To avoid double taxation according to the Italian-Swiss Convention of 9 March 1976, Italy automatically recognizes a tax credit in the EC form of the tax return (730 or Single Form), allowing the cross-border worker to recover the difference between the Swiss tax actually paid and the Italian IRPEF due. The Italian IRPEF follows progressive rates: 23% up to €28,000 per year, 33% between €28,001 and €50,000, 43% over €50,000.

For seasonal cross-border workers, this mechanism considerably simplifies management: the annual declaration in Italy only reports the income for the months actually worked. By reducing the Italian taxable amount, the IRPEF due decreases, frequently generating a refund (refund) in favor of the worker. The transparency of the procedure allows you to better plan the annual cash flow.

Operational details

AVS contributions and management of the second pillar for fragmented employment

The Swiss contribution structure represents a crucial aspect for the seasonal cross-border commuter. Switzerland automatically applies the AVS (Old Age and Survivors Insurance) / AI (Disability Insurance) / IPG (Income Loss Compensation) levies at 5.3% as the employee share, to which is added AD (Unemployment Insurance) / AC (Occupational Accident Insurance) at 1.1%, up to a maximum of CHF 148'200 of annual income. Both are deducted from the pay slip and communicated to the cantonal tax administration (AFC/ESTV at federal level).

For those who work in a fragmented way - only summer, only winter, or specific seasons - the accumulation of contributions still occurs month by month, proportionate to the number of days actually worked. There is no penalty for seasonal employment: each month of work contributes entirely to the individual AVS account, influencing the right and amount of the old-age and disability pension. A six-month work season accumulates 50% of annual contributions; this register remains valid for the entire career.

As regards the second pillar (LPP, occupational pension fund), the contribution varies by age group: from 25 years onwards it fluctuated between 7% and 18% of income. For short seasonal jobs, some employers offer exemptions up to a minimum contract duration; it is advisable to check the conditions on the employment contract.

Health insurance and health rights

The G (border) permit requires compulsory sickness coverage. The cross-border worker can choose between two options: maintain insurance through INPS in Italy (if already registered and paying contributions), or take out a Swiss KVG policy. Many cross-border workers prefer to remain in the Italian system for administrative and family continuity.

If you choose Swiss KVG, the deductibles for adults vary from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 per year, depending on the coverage selected. The choice must be communicated to the employer and the cantonal authorities and remains valid for the entire duration of the employment relationship. The KVG covers hospital, outpatient and pharmaceutical services according to the Swiss federal catalogue.

Seasonal income documentation and tracking

Administrative management requires particular attention. The seasonal work contract in Switzerland follows federal and cantonal rules: guaranteed minimum wage, regular payment of contributions, leave according to law. There are no regulatory exceptions for seasonal workers, although the period of employment is limited. The employer is required to declare the cross-border worker to the cantonal authorities and to the contributory services within specific deadlines, generally before the start of the relationship.

For the annual declaration in Italy, the cross-border worker must precisely document the actual period of employment (start date, end date, total days worked or actual annual gross). The declaration only reports the income of the months with actual employment; the automatic system excludes months of inactivity from the IRPEF calculation.

Key points

Practical procedure: five steps to get started

Step 1 — Request for G permit from the cantonal authorities

Before starting work, the Swiss employer and the cross-border commuter fill out the G permit application at the competent cantonal office. In Ticino, the authority is the Department of Finance and Economy (DFE), in coordination with SEM (Migration State Secretariat). The procedure is simplified and does not require a permanent contract: explicit seasonal contracts are also valid. Typical processing times are 2–3 weeks.

Step 2 — AVS registration and notification to the tax administration

Once the permit has been issued, the cross-border worker obtains an AVS number (if he does not already have one). This number is vital for tracking all Swiss contributions accumulated over the course of your career. The employer immediately communicates the hiring to the Federal Tax Administration (AFC/ESTV) and the Ticino cantonal administration, indicating the start date, expected income and type of contract (seasonal). This flow is automatic and ensures that contributions are recorded correctly.

Step 3 — Checking your first paycheck

In the first pay, the cross-border worker checks the presence of:

  • Swiss withholding tax (visible amount)
  • AVS/AI/IPG withdrawals at 5.3% (must appear)
  • AD/AC withdrawals at 1.1%, if the annualized gross is less than CHF 148'200
  • AVS number clearly indicated
  • Net amount consistent with the gross minus the withholdings indicated

Any discrepancies should be reported to your employer or tax advisor.

Step 4 — Collection of documentation for the Italian declaration

At the end of the working season (or calendar year), the cross-border worker collects:

  • Income certificate from the Swiss employer (official document)
  • Copy of the tax withheld documentation
  • Documentation of valid G permit
  • AVS number
  • Any receipts for contribution payments

Step 5 — Tax return to the Revenue Agency

By May 31st of the following year, the cross-border worker (through CAF, a specialized accountant, or online at the Revenue Agency portal) presents form 730 or Single Model in Italy. In the EC framework of 730 ("Tax credit for income produced abroad") reports:

  • Swiss gross income (of the actual working period)
  • Swiss tax withheld at source
  • AVS number

The Revenue Agency calculates the IRPEF due on the total amount and applies the automatic tax credit. For seasonal cross-border workers with an income of less than €28,000 (23% rate bracket), the result is almost always a refund (refund), credited within 3–6 months to the cross-border worker's Italian bank account.

Frequently Asked Questions
Who can apply for the G permit if you only work seasonally in Ticino?
Anyone residing in Italy and working as an employee in Switzerland, even only for specific seasonal periods, may apply for a G permit. The employer and the border worker must fill out the application at the Department of Finance and Economy (DFE) of Ticino, in coordination with SEM. No permanent employment is required: the permit is valid for temporary and seasonal contracts, as long as the work is actually carried out in Switzerland. The procedure typically takes 2–3 weeks.
How are tax rebates calculated in seasonal work?
The refund is the difference between the Swiss tax already withheld at source and the Italian personal income tax due. In seasonal work, the IRPEF is calculated only on the income of the months actually worked. For example, if the border worker earns €15,000 in 6 months of the season, the Italian personal income tax at 23% (up to €28,000) is lower than the Swiss tax paid, generating a refund. The declaration 730 in Italy in the EC framework activates this tax credit mechanism automatically.
What AVS/AI contributions do I have to pay if I only work a few months a year?
The AVS/AI contributions at 5.3% (employee share) and AD/AC at 1.1% (up to CHF 148'200 gross) accumulate month by month, regardless of the total duration of the annual employment. If you work 3 months, contributions are calculated on those 3 months. These payments flow into your individual AVS account and count in full for the calculation of old age and disability pension: there is no penalty for fragmented employment, as long as it is regularly documented.
Do I have to choose between Italian and Swiss health insurance (LAMal)?
Permit G requires mandatory sickness coverage. If you already have insurance through INPS in Italy and continue to pay contributions, you can keep that coverage. Alternatively, you can opt for LAMal Switzerland, with adult deductibles from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 per year depending on the coverage chosen. The decision must be communicated to the employer and the cantonal authorities.
Who qualifies for the transitional regime in Article 9?
Article 9’s transitional regime applies only to workers who meet the Agreement’s definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between 31 December 2018 and 17 July 2023; covered employment income is taxable exclusively in Switzerland. The ordinary regime applies in other cases, with Italian taxation and credit for Swiss tax where applicable. The €10,000 in Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is only the limit for income referred to in Article 1(175) of Law 147/2013, not a general allowance.
