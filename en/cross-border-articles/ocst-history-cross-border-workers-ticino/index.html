From solidarity to founding

Around the middle of the nineteenth century, in a poor Canton Ticino where agriculture and pastoralism were the main activities, the first small workers' societies based on solidarity and mutual aid were established. The construction of the Gotthard Railway in 1882 changed the canton's economy: agriculture began to decline, while factories, tourism and services appeared in the towns. According to the account presented in January 2021, this turning point was the result of a long social transformation.

The workers' association movement was promoted by Italian immigrants and Ticinese emigrants, who had encountered more advanced social conditions abroad. At the end of the nineteenth century, the first socialist trade unions were established. In 1902, the Chamber of Labour and, in Catholic circles, the Cantonal Circle for Political and Social Studies were formed.

The appeal of Rerum Novarum by Leone XIII, in 1891, had initially left the Catholic world of Ticino indifferent. Thanks to the initiative of don Carlo Roggero, the first Catholic Workers' Leagues spread, particularly in the Locarnese and Bellinzonese areas. In Locarno, in 1906, a permanent secretariat was established; the Bollettino del Bureau Popolare became La Gazzetta del Lavoratore the following year.

The First World War worsened workers' conditions throughout Switzerland. In November 1918, there was a three-day general strike in the main Swiss centers. A group of Christian-social workers was formed in Bellinzona. In 1919, the Federation of Christian-Social Trade Unions was founded; on May 18, OCST was officially established in Ticino. The statutes were adopted on November 16 in Bellinzona. Il Lavoro began in 1920 and ceased in 1927.

Don Luigi Del Pietro became secretary on September 1, 1929, after the bishop of Lugano, Mons. Aurelio Bacciarini, decided to entrust the union to him. Inspired by the social teaching of the Church, he relaunched OCST despite the hostility of socialist trade unions, part of the employers and respectable society. In 1933, he resumed Il Lavoro and opened the Casa del Popola in Lugano. By the end of the 1930s, OCST had matched the Chamber of Labour in membership and later became the most representative trade union in Ticino. Del Pietro's death in 1977 led to internal restructuring.

The link with cross-border work appears in a specific passage: during the years of the economic boom, OCST was at the forefront of protecting foreign workers and significantly increased its membership, also thanks to the unionization of many cross-border workers. For a cross-border worker, this is the key point to retain: their presence becomes part of the history of the union within the growth of employment in Ticino and the services that OCST consolidated.

The economic context described by the source helps put that passage in perspective without separating it from the rest of the labor market. In the 1950s and until the beginning of the 1970s, construction, tourism and services expanded, while major hydroelectric projects and new roads accompanied the Canton of Ticino's entry into modernity. People from Ticino entered mainly the tertiary sector, while immigrants entered the secondary sector. With the abundance of jobs, union activity lost some of its vitality, but contributed to building the welfare state.

Frequently Asked Questions
When was OCST founded and what are its origins?
OCST was officially founded on May 18, 1919, with the adoption of its statutes in Bellinzona on November 16 of the same year. Its roots lie in the small workers' societies based on solidarity and mutual aid that emerged in Canton Ticino around the mid-nineteenth century. The construction of the Gotthard Railway in 1882 marked an economic turning point, leading to the decline of agriculture and the emergence of factories, tourism and services. Workers' associationalism was also promoted by Italian immigrants and Ticinese emigrants.
What was OCST's role in relation to cross-border workers?
During the period of the economic boom, which lasted from the 1950s to the beginning of the 1970s, OCST stood out for being at the forefront of protecting foreign workers. In this context, the organization significantly increased the number of its members, also thanks to the unionization of numerous cross-border workers. This demonstrates how the presence of cross-border workers was integrated into the history of the trade union, contributing to the growth of work in Ticino and the consolidation of the services offered by OCST.
What key events preceded the founding of OCST?

Workers' conditions deteriorated with the First World War, culminating in the general strike of November 1918 in Switzerland. In 1919, the Federation of Christian-Social Trade Unions was founded, preceding the birth of OCST.
