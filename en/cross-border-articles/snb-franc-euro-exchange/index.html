SNB: weak franc, Euro at 0.9423 CHF

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 25 September 2026

The SNB confirms the rate at zero. The euro rises to 0.9423 francs, the dollar to 0.8268: impact on the value for frontier workers.

  • Euro rises to 0.9423 francs

In the aftermath of the monetary policy decisions announced this morning by the Swiss National Bank, the franc is clearly losing ground on the currency markets. The reaction was immediate: the dollar reached its highest level in about a year against the Swiss currency, while the euro returned to rise steadily above the psychological threshold of 0.94 francs.

In current trading, the greenback is trading at 0.8268 francs. The single European currency instead stood at 0.9423 francs. For a cross-border worker thinking about the euro equivalent of a sum expressed in francs, this is the data to follow: the source photographs the exchange rate at a specific moment, without providing an individual amount to convert.

The SNB decision and the exchange rate

As widely expected by analysts, the SNB confirmed the reference rate at zero percent. However, operators' attention quickly shifted to indications relating to the trend of the exchange rate. According to a market observer, the issuing institution is more closely monitoring currency fluctuations in both directions.

The choice to keep the rate unchanged therefore does not close the game on the franc. The movement observed after the announcement concerns the value of the currency on the markets and, for those who work across the border, makes reading the euro-franc price central. The CHF/EUR comparator allows you to follow this relationship without confusing it with the separate dollar data.

The source also links the recent weakening of the franc to the upward pressure on energy prices. An excessive depreciation would risk fueling inflationary pressures coming from abroad; a stronger franc, on the contrary, would have the merit of curbing those pressures.

However, the SNB recognizes that a weaker franc offers an assist to the Swiss economy, facilitating the operations and international competitiveness of export-oriented companies. The future of the exchange remains uncertain. For this reason, Schlegel reiterated the institute's willingness to intervene promptly on the foreign exchange market, should circumstances require it.

Operational details

For the cross-border worker, the useful reading starts from the distinction between the value in francs and the equivalent value in euros. If a sum remains denominated in CHF, the observed movement does not in itself change the number of francs. If, however, the sum is converted into EUR, the weakening of the Swiss currency tends to reduce the euro equivalent for the same amount and price. It is a conditional scenario, not a personal measure that can be obtained from the text: the source indicates neither salary nor amount, nor a variation applied to a pay slip.

The first distinction avoids a frequent confusion: the movement on the markets does not, in itself, equate to a cut in salary expressed in francs. The effect emerges when the equivalent value is measured in euros. For those who incur expenses in the single currency, the point of attention is therefore the moment of conversion and not just the nominal level of income.

The second distinction concerns the horizon. The SNB is observing the shift in both directions and has left open the possibility to intervene if necessary. There is no prediction of the next movement. For the cross-border worker, it means that the price described by the source is a market reference, not a valid guarantee for future conversions. A cross-border worker should therefore avoid transforming a single survey into a promise on the evolution of its value.

Then there is the macroeconomic framework. The weak franc helps export-oriented companies, while excessive weakness can increase inflationary pressures from abroad, especially together with the push of energy prices. The two consequences coexist: what supports the competitiveness of companies does not necessarily coincide with what protects the euro value of those who collect francs.

The perimeter must be kept separate from other topics. The text does not deal with G permit, AVS, LPP, KVG, refunds, withholding tax or double taxation. It is therefore not possible to use this exchange rate update to deduce changes to those matters. For an orderly analysis, it is best to start from the currency relationship and add other elements only when a source indicates them.

Key points

Whoever needs to transform the news into a check can follow a simple procedure, built on the available data. The source does not indicate deadlines, documents or personal thresholds: the action therefore concerns the reading of the quotation and the calculation of the countervalue, not an administrative practice.

A four-step verification

1. Set the reference. Note the quote used and its direction. The source distinguishes between euro-franc and dollar-franc: they should not overlap. For those who think in euros, the first reference is the single currency.

2. Identify the starting amount. Write separately the sum expressed in francs and the currency in which you want to read the equivalent value. Without an individual amount, the source numbers remain market quotes, not a personal estimate.

3. Check the direction of movement. A franc that loses ground against the euro must be read differently from a franc that strengthens. The SNB is monitoring fluctuations in both directions; therefore a detection should not be transformed into an automatic prediction. The correct step is to compare the new data with that previously observed, without attributing to it a certainty that the source does not offer.

4. Separate the change from other items. The equivalent value in euros depends on the quotation applied to the amount in francs. The news does not describe changes to a salary, a contract or other elements of the cross-border worker's position.
