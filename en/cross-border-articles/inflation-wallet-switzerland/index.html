Key facts

Author: Elio del Biaggio Date: Sep 20, 2026 Update: 19:01

In Switzerland, inflation, price stability, and purchasing power are regularly discussed through figures that, on paper, appear reassuring for citizens and for those who cross the border daily to work in the Canton of Ticino. However, there is a fundamental question that we should have the courage to ask ourselves: whether we are truly measuring everything that heavily impacts family budgets. Between the official inflation reported by statistical indices and the one actually experienced every day at home and on the roads, the gap can prove to be particularly significant. The central core of the issue does not lie in questioning the correctness of the statistics themselves, but rather in understanding what these indicators actually measure and, above all, which fundamental items are deliberately excluded from the reference baskets. When we witness substantial price increases that directly hit primary sectors such as energy, fuels, foodstuffs, housing-related costs, transportation, health insurance premiums, and a wide range of essential services, the cost of daily life grows in a tangible and unmistakable way.

Operational details

It is therefore insufficient and misleading to state that overall inflation is contained when, at the same time, a family is forced to allocate an increasingly large share of its income to maintaining the exact same standard of living as before. A purely average index may be impeccable and correct from a strictly statistical point of view, but at the same time prove entirely inadequate and unrepresentative of the actual daily experience lived by a household. Not all consumers purchase the exact same goods, not all face the same structure of fixed and variable costs, and not all suffer with the same intensity from the repercussions of price increases recorded in different economic sectors. For this reason, simply speaking generically about inflation without analyzing the complexity of overall economic pressure can become a misleading exercise that fails to capture the financial reality of taxpayers.

A statistical snapshot that does not match reality

The concrete risk associated with this approach is the construction of a representation of reality that appears artificially reassuring, while the management of everyday household budgets tells a completely different story characterized by greater financial difficulties. An official inflation rate maintained at low levels can easily turn into an untruthful figure when evaluated against the overall perception of the cost of living, especially where all the expense items that heavily impact the economic stability of families are not adequately included and weighted. Addressing this issue requires an approach based on realism and, above all, the utmost intellectual honesty, avoiding sugarcoating an economic situation in which real costs constantly exceed the official estimates published by federal statistical agencies.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the difference between official inflation and the cost of living?
There is a significant gap between the official inflation reported by the statistical indices and that experienced concretely every day by families and frontier workers. The main reason lies in what the indicators actually measure and in the fundamental items that are excluded from the reference baskets, such as the constant increase in energy, fuels, foodstuffs, housing, transport and sick pay premiums.
How do energy and fuels affect daily spending?
The price paid at the pump for fuels does not depend only on oil on global markets, but is largely determined by taxation and significant ancillary charges. Similarly, the cost of energy is not limited to bills or refuelling, but penetrates far into the price of any good or service produced, transported and marketed.
How can frontier workers monitor the impact of surcharges?
