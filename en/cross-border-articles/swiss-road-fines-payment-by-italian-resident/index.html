Frequently Asked Questions
When did the New Frontier Agreement between Switzerland and Italy come into force?
The New Frontier Agreement was signed on 23 December 2020 and entered into force on 1 January 2024. This agreement introduces significant changes for Italian residents working in Switzerland, also affecting the management of road fines.
How can an Italian border worker pay a traffic fine received in Switzerland?
The Italian border worker can pay the Swiss road fines online, via the website of the Swiss Tax Office, by entering the fine reference number and the tax code. Alternatively, the payment can be made at the local office of the Tax Office, choosing between methods such as credit card or bank transfer.
What is the deadline for paying Swiss road fines and what happens if you don't comply?
Swiss road fines must be paid within 30 days of the date of receipt, as specified in Art. 14 of RTS 531.1. If payment is not made within this period, an additional penalty of CHF 100 will be incurred. For example, a fine of CHF 60 not paid on time may cost CHF 160.
Can you provide examples of the amounts of the Swiss road fines and their equivalent in Euros?
Examples of fines include CHF 20 for parking (approx. €18.50), with a term of 30 days, or CHF 50 for speeding (approx. €45.50), with a term of 14 days. A fine of CHF 100 per parking space (approx. €91.00) has a term of 21 days. Amounts and terms vary based on the violation.
Where can I find more information about the Swiss rules on road fines for border crossers?
For more information on Swiss road fines, border workers can consult the website of the Swiss Tax Office or contact their local office. Further details may be requested from the Italian Ministry of Foreign Affairs or the Italian Consulate in Lugano, for specific clarifications.
