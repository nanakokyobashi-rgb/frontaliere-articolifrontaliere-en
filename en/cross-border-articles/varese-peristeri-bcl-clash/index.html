The challenge after Murcia

Three official matches, three victories: this is the calling card with which Peristeri Athens arrives in Varese for the second day of the Basketball Champions League. The Greek team brings a clear path between championship and cup to Masnago, while Itelyum seeks their first European success after the defeat in Murcia.

The most obvious name is Ty Nichols. The American, leader of the Athenians, ended the championship match against Iraklis played on Saturday evening with 33 points. His record in the three outings played so far is 20 points against Patras, 21 against Bamberg and 33 against Iraklis: a start that explains why the red and white defense will have to follow him carefully.

Peristeri won in their home country on their debut against Patras and then against Iraklis. In the cup, however, they beat the Germans Bamberg at home the previous week. That match was a see-saw: the Hellenic team accumulated an advantage, immediately made a comeback and overtook, then finished ahead at 93-87.

An already heavy passage

The match in Masnago, scheduled for Tuesday 13 October at 8pm, has an immediate impact on coach Kastritis' team. A success against Peristeri would break the ice in the cup, move the table and allow Varese to reach the match against Bamberg after a victory.

The group regulations leave little room for errors: there are four teams and the last one is destined for elimination after six games. Defending the field factor therefore serves to avoid unpleasant surprises. Everyone, including Scola, said they wanted to continue the European path as much as possible. The setting should also be adequate: the aim is for around 2,500 spectators, with a good presence of visiting fans.

The game's key issues

Itelyum will have to be better not only than the one seen in Murcia, but also than the version that beat Trieste on Saturday in a nail-biter. The hours after the league game left doubts about the bench decisions: in the first two quarters, substitutions were constant and made with the stopwatch in hand, even at the expense of players who were finding their rhythm.

The second issue concerns the big men. Attempts to make the final pass from the middle of the paint produced turnovers, while defensive switches often moved the big men away from the basket, leaving the paint exposed. This approach can become a strength when the mechanisms work and the team rediscovers its best form. Until then, however, the lack of alternatives remains risky, especially in Europe, where the average level is high and physical play is often more tolerated than in the LBA.

Saturday's game also offered positive signs. Della Valle's return made the offense more dangerous and, with McDowell still absent, helped shore up the backcourt. In the third quarter, Varese showed progress at both ends of the court, while some players took steps forward.
