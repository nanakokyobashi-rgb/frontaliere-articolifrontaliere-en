Castiglione Olona & Ticino: Guide for Border Workers (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 22 September 2026

Travel times, taxation, cost of living and transfer advice: everything border crossers need to know before choosing Castiglione Olona as an Italian base.

Context

In a nutshell

  • Distance: ~50 km from Castiglione Olona to the Brogeda border
  • Average travel time: 45-70 minutes by car (variable traffic on A9/A2)
  • Taxation: Swiss source tax (5-13%) + Italian tax credit
  • Cost of living: Ticino +30-40% vs Northern Italy, but average salaries +50%
  • Key advantage: Italian tax exemption up to €10,000 (new cross-border commuters)

Key facts

Border distance: 50 km Travel time: 45-70 min Tax exemption: €10,000 (new cross-border commuters) AVS rate: 5.3% Cost of living Ticino vs IT: +30-40% Cross-border agreement: In force since January 1, 2024

Castiglione Olona, with its historic center and efficient services, increasingly attracts cross-border commuters working in Ticino. Located approximately 50 km from the Brogeda border crossing, it represents a compromise between Italian quality of life and Swiss job opportunities. Travel times by car vary between 45 and 70 minutes, depending on traffic on the A9 and A2, while rail connections via Varese take about 90 minutes. Its strategic position near the A8/A9 motorway makes it preferable to more distant municipalities like Induno Olona or Ponte Tresa.

Taxation: how the source tax works

Cross-border commuters are subject to source tax in Switzerland (rates between 5% and 13% depending on canton and income), while in Italy they benefit from a tax credit that avoids double taxation. The new cross-border agreement that came into force on January 1, 2024 introduced a €10,000 exemption for new cross-border commuters (compared to €7,500 for those already active before July 17, 2023). Mandatory Swiss social contributions: AVS/AI/IPG (5.3%), AD/AC (1.1%), LAINF (0.7-1.5%) and LPP (7-18%).

Operational details

Advantages and Disadvantages of Transferring

Benefits:

  • Tax savings: Up to €10,000 tax-free in Italy for new frontier workers
  • Higher salaries: Average +50% compared to Northern Italy
  • Efficient services: Ticino health and transport accessible with G permit

Disadvantages:

  • Stressful commuting: Queues in Brogeda/Gaggiolo at rush hour
  • High car costs: CH insurance + maintenance for mountain routes
  • Mandatory LAMal: Swiss Health Insurance (CHF 300-2.500/anno)

Recommended areas in Castiglione Olona

Residential areas near the motorway exit (e.g. Via XXV Aprile) reduce access times to the A9. For families, the Villa Toledo area offers schools and parks. Avoid secondary roads to Vedano Olona if you depend on the car: in case of snow, the priority of evacuation is for the main arteries.

Practical scenarios: what if…

...become a teleworker? The new agreement allows up to 90 days/year of working from home without tax impact. In addition, you risk losing the Italian franchise. ...exceed €50,000 per year? The Italian personal income tax rate is charged at 43% on the surplus part, but the tax credit mitigates the burden. ...I have an accident in Switzerland? The LAINF covers the costs, but it is worth supplementing with private insurance for non-occupational accidents.

Key points

Concrete Actions for Relocation

Step 1: Obtain Permit G

Apply online via the Canton Ticino portal within 8 days of employment. Required documents: employment contract, passport, proof of Italian residence. Cost: CHF 100 (annual renewal).

Step 2: Choose Health Insurance

Cross-border workers have the right to choose: within 3 months, select between:

  • Swiss LAMal (cost CHF 300–2,500/year depending on deductible)
  • Italian SSN (with E106 certificate, but note limitations for treatment in Switzerland)

Step 3: Optimize Taxation

Submit the 'Cross-border Workers' Declaration' to the Italian Revenue Agency by April 30 annually. Retain AVS contribution receipts for tax credits. For incomes over €50,000, evaluate the benefits of a deductible third-pillar 3a insurance policy in Switzerland.

Frequently Asked Questions
Can I keep Italian health insurance as a frontier?
Yes, with the E106 certificate, but it only covers basic care in Switzerland. For full access to Ticino hospitals (e.g. EoC), Swiss LAMal is recommended. Check waiting times for specialists in Italy vs. CH.
What happens if I lose my job in Ticino?
You are entitled to Swiss unemployment benefit (up to 2 years) if you have worked for at least 12 months. The INPS recognizes AVS contributions for the Italian pension. Report the unemployment status to the URC Ticino immediately.
Can I buy a house in Ticino with residence in Castiglione Olona?
Yes, frontier workers can buy property in Switzerland as second owners. Beware, however, of property tax (0.3-0.5% of value) and cantonal restrictions for non-residents. Evaluate the cost of the loan in CHF vs EUR.
