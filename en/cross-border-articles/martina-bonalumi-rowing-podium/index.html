Martina Bonalumi: double international podium finish in the eight

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 3 October 2026

The coxswain of Dumenza wins bronze at the FISU World University Championships in Canada and silver at the Under-23 European Championships in Poland.

Two international podium finishes within just a few weeks have placed Martina Bonalumi's name at the center of Dumenza's pride. The young coxswain of the Italian women's eight first won the bronze medal at the FISU World University Championships held in Ontario, Canada, and then a prestigious silver medal at the Under-23 European Championships in Kruszwica, Poland.

The journey recounted by the Dumenza community therefore unfolds across two closely spaced events and two notable placings. After her podium performance in Canada, Bonalumi guided the Italian boat in the European category final. On board with her were Alessandra Grasselli, Sara Lisi, Vittoria Piller, Isabella Mihalescu, Neve Aroldi, Lucrezia Monaci, Matilde Orsetti and Giorgia Sciattella.

The Italian crew put in a highly solid and consistent performance. The final ended with Italy in second place, behind Romania and ahead of all the other crews competing. The Italian Rowing Federation highlighted the extraordinary cohesion demonstrated by the Italian eight during the international competition.

On behalf of the municipal administration and the citizens, Mayor Corrado Nazario Moro sent Bonalumi a congratulatory message. The mayor linked the two podium finishes not only to the sporting result, but also to the human value of the performance and the young coxswain's ability to take on the responsibilities of the role.

“Behind these results,” commented the mayor of Dumenza, Corrado Nazario Moro, “are talent, sacrifice, consistency and an extraordinary ability to work as a team.”

In the mayor's message, Martina Bonalumi's result thus also becomes a source of shared pride. The medal won in Poland, together with the bronze earned in Ontario, is presented as the fruit of collective work in which the coxswain had the task of guiding the Italian eight. The double sequence fills the entire Dumenza community with pride and makes the young fellow citizen's international journey visible.

Operational details

The meaning of a collective result

The double podium finish is presented by the mayor of Dumenza as a meeting point between individual performance and collective work. Martina Bonalumi is the face of the news, but the European result belongs to a crew: her role acquires meaning together with the eight athletes of the Italian boat. Federazione Italiana Canottaggio in fact highlighted the cohesion of the eight, not an isolated result.

The coxswain as a point of balance

In the account of the race, the coxswain is not an accessory presence. The source attributes a series of precise responsibilities to this role: reading how the competition is unfolding, guiding the boat, giving instructions at decisive moments, and turning nine people into a single crew. These are different tasks, but they are linked by the same goal: holding the collective effort together.

This function explains why the recognition given to Martina Bonalumi concerns both her qualities and her rapport with her teammates. The ability to work as a team, emphasized by the mayor, does not erase individual talent. It places it within a performance in which each member contributes to the final result.

The second element indicated by Moro is its educational value. The success of the young local woman is seen as a message addressed to younger people: talent alone is not enough. Sacrifice, consistency, and a willingness to work together are also needed. The medal thus becomes the visible part of a journey built on perseverance and collaboration.

For the Dumenza community, the significance of the result is also measured by the opportunity to share the joy of the two podium finishes. The mayor emphasized that every time Martina steps onto a podium, she also carries with her a piece of the town. The local dimension does not replace the international one, but accompanies it: the performance remains that of the Italian eight, while the pride is shared by the entire community.

Read this way, the story is not merely the chronicle of two medals. It is the story of a sporting role that requires leadership and listening, of a crew that must move as one, and of a result that the town interprets as an example of collective work.

The medals are also different, because the first event ended with bronze and the second with silver.

The second step is to attribute the result correctly. Martina Bonalumi is the coxswain of the Italian women's eight, while the European medal came at the end of a race contested by the entire Italian boat. To understand the placing, it is therefore not enough to focus on the name of the young woman from Dumenza: it is necessary to consider the team performance, the solidity shown during the final and the cohesion highlighted by the Italian Rowing Federation.

The third step concerns the local significance. The mayor's message links the sporting result to four elements: talent, sacrifice, perseverance and the ability to work as a team. These are the same elements that make it possible to understand the coxswain's role beyond the simple classification result. Guiding the boat means reading the race, giving instructions when necessary and keeping nine people together within a single crew.

Following these three steps avoids two partial interpretations. The first would be to consider only the individual recognition, forgetting the teammates in the eight. The second would be to stop at the placing, without grasping the educational value indicated by the mayor. Instead, the source offers a more complete perspective: two closely spaced podium finishes, a team performance and a message aimed at younger people.
