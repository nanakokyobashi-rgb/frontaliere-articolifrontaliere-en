Ristorni, Switzerland threatens Lombardy: withdraw the tax (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 2 July 2026

The Varese senator of the Democratic Party, Alessandro Alfieri, calls on the Lombardy Region to withdraw the health tax to avoid the blocking of rebates by Switzerland.

Context

In brief

  • Switzerland blocks rebates to border municipalities in Lombardy.
  • Senator Alessandro Alfieri asks the Lombardy Region to withdraw the health tax.
  • The tax represents profiles of unconstitutionality and violates the Italian-Swiss tax agreement.
  • The border municipalities of the provinces of Varese and Como will be the most affected.
  • What: Block of rebates by Switzerland.
  • When: After the introduction of the health tax.
  • Where: Border municipalities of Lombardy.
  • Who: Lombardy Region and Canton Ticino.
  • Amount: Not specified. The Varese senator of the Democratic Party, Alessandro Alfieri, has called on the Lombardy Region to immediately withdraw the health tax, after the news of the blocking of rebates by Switzerland to the Italian border municipalities. The decision of the Canton of Ticino was taken precisely because of the arrival of this tax. "Unfortunately, sorry to say, but we were right," Alfieri said in a statement. He had repeatedly denounced that the health tax represented profiles of unconstitutionality and was against the legislation provided for in the agreement on the new taxation between Italy and Switzerland.

Impact on Border Municipalities

The block of rebates will mainly affect the border municipalities of the provinces of Varese and Como, which are already facing the absurd cut in rebates made by the government, which has decided to remove 39 million owed to them to give them to the regions. "In the face of the evidence, which we had repeatedly denounced as the Democratic Party together with the cross-border workers' unions, Giorgetti and Fontana should stop: withdraw the health tax and return the rebates to the border municipalities," Alfieri concludes.

Operational details

Practical Analysis

The decision of the Canton of Ticino to block rebates to border municipalities in Lombardy has significant implications for cross-border workers and local administrations. The health tax, introduced by the Lombardy Region, is seen as a mistake that violates the Italian-Swiss tax agreement. This agreement provides that part of the taxes paid by cross-border commuters will be returned to the border municipalities, but with the introduction of the new tax, the Canton of Ticino has decided to block these rebates. Before the introduction of the health tax, the border municipalities of Lombardy received a share of the taxes paid by cross-border workers. This sum was crucial for the budget of these municipalities, which are often largely dependent on cross-border workers for their economy. With the blocking of rebates, these municipalities will find themselves in a difficult financial situation, with fewer resources available for essential services such as health, education and infrastructure.

Possible scenarios If the Lombardy Region does not withdraw the health tax, border municipalities could face cuts in public services and an increase in local taxes to compensate for the loss of rebates. On the other hand, if the tax is withdrawn, municipalities could see an improvement in their finances and greater economic stability. However, this scenario depends on the willingness of the Lombardy Region to listen to the requests of Senator Alfieri and the cross-border workers' unions.
