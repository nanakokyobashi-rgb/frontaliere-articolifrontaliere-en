Living in Gerenzano and working in Ticino: a guide for border workers (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 2 September 2026

Distance, crossings, G permit, taxes, AVS and LAMal: everything you need to know before moving to Gerenzano from Ticino or other border areas.

Context

In brief

  • Gerenzano is just a few km from the Ticino border, ideal for commuters to Lugano and Valle di Blenio
  • Permit G (cross-border worker) requires stable employment in Ticino and continuous residence in Italy
  • Withholding tax deducted in Switzerland; possible refunds through Italian declaration (quadro CE of the 730)
  • New cross-border agreement: effective from January 1, 2024, with a €10,000 allowance for new cross-border workers

Key facts

  • What: Cross-border life between Gerenzano (Italy, province of Varese) and Ticino (Switzerland)
  • When: The new cross-border agreement is effective from January 1, 2024
  • Where: Brogeda, Gaggiolo, Chiasso crossings for the Gerenzano–Ticino connection
  • Who: Permit G reserved for Italian residents who work stably in Switzerland
  • Amount: Annual €10,000 allowance for new cross-border workers (partial tax exemption)

Gerenzano, a municipality in the province of Varese less than 50 km from the Ticino border, is an increasingly popular choice for those who decide to work in Switzerland while remaining domiciled in Italy. The proximity to major cross-border crossings and the lower cost of living compared to the Swiss territory make this area attractive for cross-border workers seeking a balance between Swiss salary and Italian quality of life.

Anyone who decides to live in Gerenzano and work in Ticino must first understand the regime of Permit G (cross-border worker). This document, issued by the Swiss authorities, is exclusively reserved for Italian citizens who reside permanently in Italy and carry out dependent or independent work in Switzerland. Permit G is not automatic: it requires a work contract signed with a Ticino employer, verifiable work stability, and a fixed residence in the Italian municipality of residence.

Operational details

The Swiss contribution system and social security

For those working in Ticino, even if they live in Gerenzano, the payment of social security contributions follows Swiss rules. The AVS (Old Age and Survivors' Insurance) is mandatory from the first day of work: the contribution for the employee is 5.3% of the gross salary. In addition to the AVS, it is mandatory to join the disability insurance (AI) and unemployment insurance (IPG), both included in the same rate of 5.3%. Furthermore, for accident insurance (LAINF), the contribution varies between 0.7% and 1.5% depending on the professional risk.

Many cross-border commuters are not aware that the second pillar (LPP, i.e., the Swiss supplementary pension scheme) is also mandatory for them. From the age of 25, the Swiss employer is required to contribute to the pension fund. This contribution, which varies between 7% and 18% of the salary depending on the age group and the company pension plan, represents a significant added value: at the time of retirement or transfer, the cross-border commuter will have access to a pension or accumulated capital that they would not have received in Italy.

Key points

Pre-move checklist

Before deciding to move to Gerenzano to work in Ticino, it is advisable to verify some concrete elements. First, consult the Ticino employer about the availability of administrative support: many large Ticino companies have established procedures for hiring cross-border workers and can facilitate the G permit application with the SEM.

Second, acquire the exact banking and tax coordinates. The cross-border worker must open a bank account that can receive salaries paid in CHF, but many Ticino accounts have minimum balance requirements or fees. In parallel, it is useful to verify in advance how to manage the Italian tax return: consult a chartered accountant or a tax consultant specialized in cross-border workers to understand the CE framework of the 730 and the tax return payment deadlines.

Third: choose the health insurance category. If you choose LAMal, contact a Ticino health insurance company directly (e.g., contact the canton or specialized intermediaries) to receive a personalized offer. If you remain in the Italian CMI, notify the insurer of any changes of residence and verify integrated cover between Italy and Switzerland.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the difference between permit G and permit B if I live in Gerenzano?
The G (border) permit is reserved for those who reside permanently in Italy (Gerenzano) and work every day in Switzerland. Permit B, on the other hand, requires the transfer of residence to Switzerland. With the G permit you remain domiciled in Italy, you pay taxes in part in Italy (with a Swiss refund); with the B permit you transfer your residence to Ticino and pay Swiss taxes in full. The choice depends on your long-term project and family needs.
Why is the source tax withheld only in Switzerland and not in Italy?
The Double Taxation Convention between Italy and Switzerland (9 December 1976) attributes the right to tax labour income to the State where the work is carried out. Since you work in Ticino (Switzerland), the tax is withheld there by the employer. Italy, to avoid double taxation, allows you to recover through the tax credit (EC framework of 730): declare the Swiss income and request the refund of the difference between the Swiss tax paid and the theoretical Italian tax.
With the €10,000 deductible (new 2024 agreement), how much do I actually save?
The €10,000 deductible is a partial exemption from Italian regional and municipal taxes, not national tax. If you earn €50,000 gross per year in Switzerland, you will only declare €40,000 in Italy. The savings depend on the Ticino regional rate of the municipality of Gerenzano (about 1–2% gross). Use the [calcolatore di stipendio](nav:calculator) for a custom estimate based on your specific income.
If I choose LAMal Ticinese, will I continue to access Italian health services?
Yes, but in different ways. With LAMal Switzerland, you are covered by Ticino hospitals and have access to general practitioners registered in Switzerland. For treatment in Italy, you will have to resort to privatists or reimburse me. It remains advisable to maintain complementary Italian insurance to protect situations that require care in the country of residence. Carefully evaluate the [quadro LAMal](nav:health) before making your final choice.
What documents do I need to apply for a G permit if I live in Gerenzano?
The Ticino employer must submit the request to the migration office (SEM). You will need to provide: valid Italian passport/ID card, signed employment contract, Gerenzano's certificate of residence, criminal record certificate. Timeframe: Approximately 4–8 weeks from completion of documentation. Always check with your employer or a specialist consultant for the updated list.
