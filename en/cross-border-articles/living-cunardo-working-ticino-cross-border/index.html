The cross-border worker's choice: Italy and Switzerland

Living in Cunardo — on the Italian side of the Province of Varese — and commuting daily to work in Canton Ticino is a well-established solution for those who wish to combine Swiss salaries with Italian property costs. On January 1, 2024, the New Cross-Border Workers Agreement entered into force, signed on December 23, 2020 and ratified by Italy through Law 83 of June 13, 2023. This agreement established new rules on taxation, social security and the rights of cross-border workers with a G permit (residents in Italy, employed in Switzerland).

The convention to eliminate double taxation between Italy and Switzerland, signed on March 9, 1976, remains the cornerstone of coordination: Switzerland taxes employment income, while Italy recognizes it through a tax credit on the income tax return. No double taxation — only coordination between the two systems.

Withholding tax: where it is withheld

One of the key advantages of the cross-border worker regime is that withholding tax on employment income is withheld in Switzerland. Your Ticino employer remits it directly to the Swiss tax authorities. The Italian Revenue Agency does not directly withhold anything from your payslip.

Crucial point: you must still complete the Italian Form 730 every year, in particular Section CE, to declare foreign employment income and obtain a tax credit for the portion already paid in Switzerland. This is the mechanism that prevents double taxation.

Tax allowance: old vs new cross-border workers

The New Cross-Border Workers Agreement distinguishes between two categories:

  • Legacy cross-border workers continue to be taxed only in Switzerland (Article 9 of the 2020 Agreement).
  • The transitional regime under Article 9 of the Agreement applies only to those who meet the treaty definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between December 31, 2018 and July 17, 2023; covered employment income is taxed exclusively in Switzerland. In other cases, the ordinary regime applies, with Italian taxation and a credit for Swiss tax. The 10.000 euro amount under Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is merely the limit for income referred to by Article 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013, not a general allowance. AFC fact sheet · Agreement, Article 9 · Normattiva, Article 4.

The allowance is employment income that is not taxable in Italy — a protection that the new agreement calibrated differently to recognize the acquired rights of those who were already working before it entered into force. If you earn less than the allowance, there is no Italian tax. If you earn more, the excess income is subject to Italian IRPEF (23% up to €28'000, 33% between €28'001–50'000, 43% above €50'000).

Operational details

Social security: AVS, LPP and coordination with INPS

As a cross-border worker resident in Cunardo and employed in Ticino, you are subject to Swiss social security contributions. AVS (old age and survivors insurance) and AI (disability insurance) involve a deduction of 5.3% of the gross salary to be paid by the employee. This is the basis of Swiss first pillar pension provision.

In addition to the AVS/AI, you pay contributions for unemployment insurance (AD) and insurance against accidents at work (LAINF), with rates respectively of 1.1% (capped at CHF 148'200 of annual salary) and 0.7–1.5% depending on the sector of activity.

The second Swiss pillar, the LPP (pension fund), has a variable rate between 7% and 18% depending on the age group — starting from the 25th year of life. This is the component that accumulates the capital for the Swiss supplementary pension income.

Coordination with INPS: You must register at the competent INPS Center (province of Varese) as a cross-border worker. The Italy-Switzerland social security agreement recognizes the contributions paid to the AVS in your Italian pension curriculum, guaranteeing continuity of coverage even in the event of a future transfer or change of status.

Health insurance: KVG and right of option

As a cross-border commuter you are covered by the Swiss compulsory health insurance (KVG - Health Insurance Act). The cross-border regime (Group G) allows you a right of option: you can choose to insure with a Swiss fund or maintain coverage with an Italian mutual institution, as long as the benefit is equivalent.

Deductibles for adults range from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 per year, with monthly premiums varying according to the insurer and the level of coverage. If you opt for the Italian solution, check that the institution also covers services in the Canton of Ticino — your place of work — to avoid gaps in cross-border coverage.

If the Swiss tax is higher than the Italian one, you will receive a refund; otherwise, the difference is compensated.

Tax refunds: when and how to request them

If in the initial years of your career as a cross-border worker the Swiss tax withheld actually exceeds the Italian tax due, you are entitled to request a rebate (refund). This frequently happens when income is lower than initial estimates or when the Ticino cantonal rates are particularly favourable. The request is formalized in declaration 730, credit section.

Key points

Step by step: how to get started

If you are about to move to Cunardo and start an employment contract in Ticino, follow these operational steps:

Step 1: Municipal Registration and Permit G

Once you have established your residence in Cunardo, register with the municipal registry. Simultaneously, contact the Single Window for Immigration (SUI) of the Province of Varese or the competent office for issuing the Permit G (cross-border resident). This document is mandatory to work in Switzerland as an Italian resident. The permit is usually granted with validity equal to the period of the employment contract (1–5 years, renewable).

You will need the employment certificate from your Ticino employer, as well as registration of your Italian residence.

Step 2: Registration with INPS and the Tax Agency

Notify the INPS Center in Varese (or through the website inps.it) of your new status as a cross-border worker employed in Switzerland. Provide the details of your Swiss employment contract and your Permit G number.

Also register with the Tax Agency (Varese branch) as an Italian resident with foreign employment income from Switzerland. You will receive an updated tax identification number if you do not already have one.

Step 3: Choice of Health Insurance (LAMal)

Within 3 months of starting employment, you must be covered. Contact a Swiss insurance company directly (e.g., Helsana, Sympany, CSS, Visana — the main ones) or ask your employer if they have an agreement with an insurance fund. If you choose to maintain Italian coverage, verify that your mutual fund also covers services provided in the Canton of Ticino.

Step 5: Preparation of Annual Tax Documentation

Every year by June 30, you must file your Italian 730 tax return. Collect from your Swiss employer the CUD certificate (Unified Certification of Withholdings), the AVS contribution statement, and LAMal information. In the return, complete section CE (foreign employment income) and section RM (foreign tax credits). This coordination avoids double taxation.

  • By June 30: file the 730 return for the tax credit on your share of Swiss tax
  • 90 days before your Permit G expires: initiate the renewal process with the SUI
  • Within 3 months of starting employment: LAMal registration completed

Living in Cunardo and working in Ticino makes sense if:

Frequently Asked Questions
If I work in Ticino and live in Cunardo, where do I pay taxes?
The income tax at source is withheld by your Swiss employer and paid to the Swiss tax authorities. The Italian Revenue Agency DOES NOT withhold taxes directly on the paycheck. However, every year you fill out the Italian form 730 (CE framework) to declare foreign income and obtain a tax credit on the portion already paid in Switzerland. The Convention between Italy and Switzerland signed on 9 March 1976 coordinates the two systems: no double taxation, only coordination. If the Swiss tax is higher than the Italian IRPEF, you receive a refund; otherwise the difference is compensated on the deductible.
What social security contributions are withheld from the paycheck?
As a border worker Group G: AVS/AI 5.3% of gross, unemployment (AD) and disaster (AC) insurance 1.1% (capped CHF 148'200 per annum), accidents at work (LAINF) 0.7-1.5%, LPP (second pillar) 7–18% according to age group from 25th year. The total amount varies between 14% and 27% of the monthly gross. At the same time, you must register with the Italian INPS for the recognition of contributions in your social security history, guaranteeing continuity in the event of a future transfer.
Can I insure myself in Italy for the disease or do I have to choose a Swiss fund?
You have the right of option (Group G): you can choose between Swiss LAMal and equivalent Italian coverage. If you opt for Italy, check that the mutual fund also covers the services provided in the Canton of Ticino, where you work. Many border crossers choose LAMal because it offers standardized performance, retired portability and a consolidated network in Ticino. Adult LAMal deductibles range from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500 per year, with variable premiums.

How do I avoid double taxation between Italy and Switzerland?
The Double Taxation Convention (9 March 1976) automatically coordinates the system: Switzerland taxes labour income (source tax), Italy recognises it and applies tax credit in declaration 730 (EC framework). If the Swiss tax withheld is higher than the Italian personal income tax due on the excess deductible, you receive a refund; otherwise compensation. Fill out the 730 carefully every year: no double taxation, only harmonization.
