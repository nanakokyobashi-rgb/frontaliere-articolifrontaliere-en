Unemployment benefits: EU aligns Swiss workers with residents

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 15 May 2026

New EU rules for unemployment benefits: the country of work pays, not the country of residence. What changes for Ticino-Italy cross-border workers.

Context

In brief

  • EU equates cross-border workers and residents for unemployment benefits
  • The country of employment pays the allowance, not the country of residence
  • Switzerland is not obliged to follow the new rules

Key facts

  • What: Revision of Regulation (EC) No 883/2004
  • When: Agreement reached on April 22, 2026, expected to enter into force in July 2026
  • Where: European Union
  • Who: Council of the EU and European Parliament
  • Amount: Switzerland reimbursements 2025: 283 million CHF to neighboring countries, 600 million CHF received

The agreement reached in Brussels overturns decades of practice: in the future, it will be the country where one works — and not where one lives — that will pay the allowance to those who lose their job. Switzerland observes, but is not obliged to follow. A community worker who becomes unemployed in Switzerland could soon count on the same protections as a Swiss citizen. The principle of equal treatment between cross-border workers and residents has received a first formal green light: on April 22, 2026, the delegations of the Council of the EU and the European Parliament reached a provisional agreement within the so-called "trilogue", and on April 29, the Coreper — the Committee of Permanent Representatives of the governments of the EU — approved the text. The norm at the center of the debate is the revision of Regulation (EC) No 883/2004, which coordinates national social security systems.

What changes for cross-border workers

The change of principle: from the place of residence to the place of work. Today, the cross-border worker who loses their job in Switzerland receives the unemployment allowance from their country of residence: if they live in Como, it is the Italian INPS that pays, while Berna reimburses the foreign state the benefits paid, up to a maximum of five months. With the new architecture based on the principle "lex loci laboris" — the law of the place of work — the mechanism is reversed: it will be the country in which the cross-border worker last worked that will bear the cost of the allowance. To access the benefit provided by the country of employment, the worker must have paid contributions for at least 22 consecutive weeks in that country.

Concrete examples

A nurse residing in Varese and employed in Mendrisio, a programmer from Como who works in Lugano: if they lose their job, today they turn to the INPS or the corresponding Italian service. Tomorrow, with the reform adopted, they would register with a Swiss Regional Employment Office (URC) and be equated to a Swiss unemployed person, accessing the benefits provided for by the Law on unemployment insurance (LADI): up to 260, 400 or 520 daily allowances, within a two-year time frame. If the worker chooses to return to Italy to look for work in their own country, they can "export" the Swiss subsidy for a maximum of six months, extendable at the discretion of the granting authority. If, on the other hand, they remain available for the Swiss labor market and maintain contact with the URC, no time limit applies.

Switzerland's position

The game, however, is far from over. The final approval by the plenary session of the European Parliament is expected for July 2026, followed by the final green light from the national governments. Only after these steps will the regulation formally enter into force in the EU. But for Switzerland there is no automatism. Regulation 883/2004 is transposed into Swiss law through the Agreement on the free movement of persons (Annex II): any changes require the explicit consent of the Confederation through the Switzerland-EU Joint Committee ALC. "Any amendment to the regulation does not apply directly in Switzerland," clarifies the SECO, which adds that it does not want to anticipate assessments "until a final decision has been issued."

Operational details

Practical implications for cross-border workers

The new EU regulation introduces a significant change for cross-border workers employed in Switzerland. Currently, a cross-border worker residing in Italy and employed in Switzerland receives unemployment benefits from their country of residence, such as INPS. With the reform, however, it will be the country of employment that provides the subsidy, provided that the worker has paid contributions for at least 22 consecutive weeks.

Concrete steps for cross-border workers

If the reform is implemented in Switzerland, cross-border workers will need to follow some steps to access the new benefits. Here’s a practical guide:

1. Check the contributions paid: Ensure you have paid contributions for at least 22 consecutive weeks in the country of work. 2. Register with the URC: If you lose your job, register with the Swiss Regional Employment Center (URC) to access the benefits provided by the Unemployment Insurance Act (LADI). 3. Maintain contact with the URC: If you decide to remain available for the Swiss job market, keep in touch with the URC to avoid time limits on benefits. 4. Export the subsidy: If you decide to return to Italy to look for work, you can export the Swiss subsidy for a maximum of six months, extendable at the discretion of the granting authority.
