Unemployment: rate at 3.0%, stable per month but increasing annually (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 7 September 2026

The unemployment rate in Switzerland is stable at 3.0% on a monthly basis, but is up 0.2 points on a yearly basis.

Context

In brief

  • The unemployment rate in Switzerland is stable at 3.0% on a monthly basis.
  • An increase of 0.2 points on an annual basis.
  • In Ticino, the rate is at 2.9%.
  • Young people and those over 50 are the most affected groups.

Key facts

  • What: Stable unemployment rate.
  • When: August 2024.
  • Where: Switzerland.
  • Who: State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (Seco).
  • Amount: 3.0%.

The unemployment rate in Switzerland remained stable at 3.0% on a monthly basis, but increased by 0.2 points on an annual basis. According to data released today by the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (Seco), at the end of August the number of unemployed registered with the regional employment centers (URC) rose to 141,500, an increase of 2,300 compared to July and 9,400 compared to the previous year. The seasonally adjusted rate is at 3.1%, stable monthly.

In Ticino, the unemployment rate stood at 2.9% in August, an increase of 0.1 points compared to July and 0.3 points on an annual basis. In Graubünden, the rate remained stable at 1.1%, with an increase of 0.1 points on an annual basis. In absolute terms, in Ticino there are 4,800 unemployed, an increase of 200 compared to July and 500 compared to the previous year. In Graubünden, the figure is 1,200 unemployed, stable compared to July and an increase of 100 compared to the previous year.

Ticino is in eleventh place among the cantons most affected by the problem, with Geneva in first place with a rate of 5.2%. Graubünden is in fifth place in the ranking of the least affected, with Appenzell Innerrhoden in first place with a rate of 0.8%.

Operational details

Implications for frontier workers

Unemployment statistics have significant implications for frontier workers working in Switzerland and residing in Italy. An increase in unemployment can affect the labour market and employment opportunities for frontier workers. It is important for frontier workers to be aware of labour market trends and employment policies in Switzerland in order to be able to make informed decisions regarding their career and employment situation.

Comparison with the previous situation

Compared to the previous situation, the unemployment rate has remained stable on a monthly basis, but is increasing on an annual basis. This indicates that although the labour market is relatively stable in the short term, there are signs of a slight worsening in the long term. Border workers should closely monitor these trends and consider how they might affect their job prospects.

Possible scenarios

If unemployment continues to rise, frontier workers could face increased competition for available jobs. This may make it harder to find a job or keep your job. On the other hand, if the labour market remains stable or improves, frontier workers may have greater opportunities to find work or advance in their careers.

Concrete actions for border workers

For frontier workers who want to stay informed and prepared for labour market trends, it is important to follow some concrete actions. First of all, it is crucial to regularly monitor unemployment data and employment policies published by the State Secretariat for the Economy (Seco). This can provide valuable insights into available job opportunities and labour market trends.

Step-by-step procedure

1. Consult the data of the Seco: Visit the website of the Secretariat of State for the Economy (Seco) to access the latest data on unemployment and employment policies. 2. Contact Regional Placement Offices (ROCs): Border workers can contact ROCs for specific information on available job opportunities and support services offered. 3. Improve skills: Consider participating in training or refresher courses to improve your skills and qualifications. 4. Stay up-to-date: Subscribe to newsletters or follow reliable sources to stay up-to-date on labor market trends and employment opportunities.
