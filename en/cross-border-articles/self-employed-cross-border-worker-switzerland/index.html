Engaging in self-employed activity in Switzerland as a person resident in Italy, that is, as a so-called cross-border worker, requires a precise understanding of the current regulatory framework. Since January 1, 2024, the new agreement on cross-border workers has been in force, ratified in Italy by Law 83 of June 13, 2023. This legislation defines how income is taxed. It is essential to distinguish between the status of an employee and that of a self-employed worker. While withholding tax is the rule for employees, tax management for self-employed workers requires particular attention to the Federal Tax Administration (AFC). Withholding tax is withheld in Switzerland, while Italy prevents double taxation through the tax credit mechanism in section CE of Form 730. The authorities responsible for setting tax rates are the cantonal tax administrations and the AFC at the federal level. It is important to note that bodies such as UFAS or BFS do not set these rates, as they are responsible respectively for social security and statistical data collection. The double taxation convention, signed on March 9, 1976, remains a fundamental pillar for regulating income flows between the two countries. Switzerland, not being a member of the EU or EEA, applies specific rules that every self-employed worker must incorporate into their financial plan. For the purposes of Article 9 of the Agreement, “old cross-border workers” are persons who met the tax requirements and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais on July 17, 2023, or during the period from December 31, 2018, to July 17, 2023. For employment income covered by Article 9, taxation remains exclusively at source in Switzerland; for other workers, the ordinary regime applies, with taxation in Italy, a credit for Swiss tax and Swiss withholding at 80% of the ordinary rates. The 10.000 euro referred to in Article 4 of Law 83/2023 is merely the income threshold cited in Article 1, paragraph 175, of Law 147/2013 and not a general tax-free allowance for new cross-border workers. Social security regulations

Social security is an essential component for those who are self-employed. Self-employed workers must pay AVS/AI/IPG contributions, which differ from the 5.3% rates applicable to employees. Proper registration with the competent bodies ensures the necessary insurance coverage, including contributions for unemployment and disability insurance, managed in accordance with federal directives. Social security planning must also take into account LAMal, compulsory health insurance, for which cross-border workers have the right of option, with deductibles ranging from 300 to 2.500 CHF for adults.

Frequently Asked Questions
How is double taxation avoided for a self-employed person?
The self-employed person avoids double taxation by using the tax credit. The taxes paid in Switzerland on the tax at source are deducted from the IRPEF due in Italy, correctly filling in the EC framework of the model 730, in accordance with the agreements in force between the two countries.
What are the social security contributions required for self-employment?
The self-employed worker must pay the AVS/AI/IPG contributions. Although the percentage for employees is 5.3%, for the self-employed the rates may vary. You must register with the competent AVS compensation fund in the Canton of Ticino to regularize your social security position.
What changes for 'old' and 'new' frontiersmen with the new agreement?
For Article 9 of the Agreement, “old frontier workers” are people who met the tax requirements and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais on 17 July 2023 or during the period from 31 December 2018 through 17 July 2023. Article 9 keeps covered employment income taxable exclusively at source in Switzerland; other workers follow the ordinary regime, with ordinary taxation in Italy, a credit for Swiss tax and Swiss withholding at 80% of the ordinary rates. The €10,000 figure in Article 4 of Italian Law No. 83/2023 is only the income limit referred to in Article 1(175) of Law No. 147/2013; it is not a general allowance for new frontier workers. Sources: ESTV factsheet (Italian) · Agreement, Art. 9 · Law No. 83/2023, Art. 4.
