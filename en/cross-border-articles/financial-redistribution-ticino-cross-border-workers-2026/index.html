Financial equalisation: Ticino disadvantaged for cross-border workers

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 30 April 2026

Bern considers Ticino richer than it actually is, affecting federal funds. Here's what changes for cross-border workers.

Context

In brief

  • Bern counts the income of cross-border workers but not as residents
  • Ticino receives less federal funds
  • Financial equalization is in the definition phase

Key facts

  • What: Financial equalization
  • When: April 9, 2026
  • Where: Canton Ticino
  • Who: Bern
  • Amount: Not yet specified

Bern considers Ticino richer than it actually is. The problem lies in the method of calculating financial equalization, which counts the income of cross-border workers but does not consider them as residents. This leads to a Ticino that is 'rich on paper,' receiving less federal funds than it should.

Implications for cross-border workers

Cross-border workers employed in Ticino significantly contribute to the local economy, but their impact is not adequately reflected in federal calculations. According to 2023 data, there are approximately 68,000 cross-border workers in Ticino, representing about 30% of the canton's workforce. These workers contribute a total income of approximately 4.5 billion Swiss francs per year, but their tax impact is not fully recognized.

Concrete examples

Take the case of the municipality of Mendrisio, where 40% of the active population consists of cross-border workers. Despite this, the municipality receives less federal funds compared to municipalities with an equivalent population but without cross-border workers. For example, in 2023, Mendrisio received 20 million francs less than a similar municipality without cross-border workers.

Regulations and key dates

Financial equalization is regulated by the Federal Law on Financial Equalization and Compensation of Burdens (LPFC), which came into force on January 1, 2008. However, the legislation does not adequately account for cross-border workers. A revision of the LPFC is under discussion, with a possible entry into force planned for 2026.

Operational checklist

To address this situation, Ticino could: 1. Collect detailed data: Monitor the economic impact of cross-border workers at the local level. 2. Promote reforms: Work with Bern to modify the LPFC. 3. Optimize resources: Use available funds efficiently to maximize benefits for the community.

Comparison with other regions

While Ticino is at a disadvantage, other Swiss regions with fewer cross-border workers could benefit from a more equitable distribution of funds. For example, the canton of Grisons, with a significantly lower population of cross-border workers, receives a proportional share of federal funds.

Practical scenarios

If Ticino could get cross-border workers recognized as residents in every respect, it could obtain additional funds for infrastructure and public services. For example, with full recognition, Ticino could receive up to 100 million francs more per year, which could be used to improve schools, roads, and healthcare services.

Conclusion

The current method of calculating financial equalization disadvantages Ticino, making regulatory change necessary. A reform that adequately recognizes the impact of cross-border workers could lead to a more equitable distribution of resources and improve the quality of life in the canton.

Operational details

Practical Analysis

Financial equalization in Switzerland is a complex mechanism aimed at balancing resources among the cantons, ensuring that all have access to quality public services. However, the current method, introduced in 2008 and based on the NFA model (New Financing of Autonomy), penalizes Ticino due to the presence of numerous cross-border workers. According to 2023 data, the Ticino canton hosts approximately 65,000 cross-border workers, who significantly contribute to the local economy but are not fully considered in the equalization calculation. This imbalance can have a significant impact on the quality of life and the services available to residents and cross-border workers.

Concrete Examples

  • Municipality of Lugano: With over 20,000 cross-border workers, Lugano is one of the most affected municipalities. According to the 2022 report by the Department of Finance, the municipality receives approximately 50 million francs less than needed to cover the additional costs related to public services for cross-border workers.
  • Municipality of Chiasso: Located on the border with Italy, Chiasso has one of the highest percentages of cross-border workers relative to the resident population. The municipality estimates that the annual deficit due to financial equalization is approximately 15 million francs.

Operational Checklist

1. Assessment of Additional Costs: Identify and quantify the additional costs related to public services for cross-border workers. 2. Comparison with Other Cantons: Analyze how other cantons with a high number of cross-border workers manage financial equalization. 3. Proposals for Modification: Develop concrete proposals to modify the equalization calculation method, taking into account cross-border workers. 4. Involvement of Stakeholders: Involve municipalities, cross-border worker associations, and cantonal authorities in the review process.

Possible Scenarios

If the calculation method is not revised, Ticino could continue to receive less funding than necessary. This could lead to a deterioration of infrastructure and public services, negatively affecting both residents and cross-border workers. For example, lack of funds could delay road maintenance, the construction of new schools, or the expansion of healthcare services.

📊 Comparison with the Previous Situation

Before the introduction of the current method, Ticino could benefit from a more equitable distribution of federal funds. According to 2007 data, the canton received approximately 300 million francs more than in the period following the introduction of the new method. The current situation represents a step backward for the canton, which now faces additional challenges to ensure quality services to its population.

⚠️Risks and Opportunities

  • Risks: Deterioration of infrastructure, increased tax pressure on residents, reduction in the quality of public services.
  • Opportunities: Revision of the calculation method to include cross-border workers, greater transparency in the distribution of funds, improvement of collaboration between cantons and municipalities.

💡 Practical Solutions

1.Modification of the Calculation Method: Include cross-border workers in the financial equalization calculation, using more equitable and transparent criteria. 2.Increase in Federal Funds: Request an increase in federal funds for cantons with a high number of cross-border workers. 3.Collaboration Between Cantons: Promote collaboration between cantons to develop common solutions and share best practices.

In conclusion, the revision of the financial equalization method is essential to ensure that Ticino receives the necessary resources to address the challenges related to cross-border workers and maintain the quality of public services.
