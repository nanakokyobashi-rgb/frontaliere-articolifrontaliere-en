Travelling accident: LAINF protection for border workers (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 22 August 2026

What does the LAINF cover in the event of an accident on the way home from work at the border? Practical guide for border workers Ticino-Italy.

Context

In a nutshell

  • LAINF covers accidents on the way home from work at the Ticino-Italy border
  • Difference between ordinary route and work trip: coverage managed differently
  • Timely reporting: shared responsibility between employer and border workers
  • SUVA manages claims and compensation

Key facts

  • What: LAINF accident insurance for commute
  • Where: Ticino-Italy border (crossings: Brogeda, Chiasso, Gaggiolo)
  • Who: SUVA (Swiss accident insurance manager)
  • Responsible body: Employer and border guards for reporting
  • Type of accident: During the ordinary work-home journey or travel
  • Protection: Reimbursement of medical expenses and benefits in case of disability

For a worker who resides in Italy and works in Switzerland, the on-the-go accident represents a specific event during the journey between home and the workplace. This protection is guaranteed by the LAINF (Accident Insurance Act), managed by SUVA, the institution that administers accident insurance in the Swiss employment system.

The LAINF covers injuries that occur during the direct journey from home to the workplace and back. However, the concept of “direct” is crucial: the journey must be the ordinary and reasonable route. If the border crossing makes significant deviations from the usual route (stopping to shop, visiting a friend), the coverage could be compromised.

Operational details

Scenario: Injury on the way

Let's imagine two concrete scenarios. In the first, the border crosser suffers a road accident along the ordinary route (for example, at the Brogeda or Chiasso crossing during rush hours). In the second scenario, the accident occurs during an unexpected business trip.

In both cases, the LAINF intervenes, but the claim procedure may differ slightly. In the first case, the border worker communicates the accident to the employer, provides details of the place (time, witnesses, data of the other vehicle if involved), and SUVA initiates the investigation based on the employer's report. In the second case (transfer), the employer must document that the transfer was explicitly ordered or authorised, and that the border worker was carrying out a work task at the time of the accident.

Documents and Claim Procedure

The claim at SUVA requires specific documentation. The border agent must provide:

  • Accident report with exact date, time, place
  • Names and contact details of witnesses (if available)
  • Medical report documenting the accident
  • Certification of the employer on the nature of the route
  • Copy of employment contract or employment certificate

Key points

Step-by-step procedure after an accident

Step 1: Immediate reporting Report the accident to your employer as quickly as possible. If you are able to move, speak directly with your manager or HR department. If you are unable to, call your employer from the accident site or have a third party contact them on your behalf.

Step 2: Medical assistance Go to the nearest emergency room if necessary, or contact your personal physician. Ensure that the medical report clearly states the date, time, and circumstances of the accident (commute home-work). Keep all prescriptions and original medical documents.

Step 3: Documentation collection Document the accident site (take photos if possible), collect the names and contact information of witnesses, and note weather or traffic conditions. If other vehicles are involved, obtain the other driver's insurance information.

Step 4: Formal report to SUVA Your employer submits the official report to SUVA within the required timeframe. You correspond directly with SUVA by providing your insurance number (found in your LAINF insurance certificate) and attaching medical and circumstantial documentation.

Step 5: Follow-up and monitoring Maintain contact with SUVA during the investigation. Respond promptly to any requests for clarification or additional documentation. Delays in responding could hinder the evaluation process.

Contacts and resources

SUVA has local offices and dedicated phone numbers for claims. Contact the competent branch based on your canton of work: Ticino has an accessible SUVA office. You can also consult SUVA's online portal to monitor the status of your claim in real time.

Frequently Asked Questions
What insurance covers the accident while commuting to work at the border?
The LAINF (Accident Insurance Act), managed by SUVA, covers accidents that occur during the direct route between home and the workplace. For the Ticino-Italy border crossing, this protection is essential because the route crosses the border. SUVA provides full reimbursement of medical expenses and monetary benefits in the event of temporary or permanent disability.
What is the difference between an ordinary commute and a work trip according to the LAINF?
In the ordinary journey, the border worker moves from his home to his usual place of work. The LAINF automatically covers this route. In the case of travel (travel for work reasons to a different location), the coverage remains valid, but the employer must document that the travel was explicitly ordered for professional reasons and not a personal deviation.
How long does the border worker have to report the injury?
The border worker must report the accident to the employer as soon as possible, ideally within the first hours of the incident. The employer is then responsible for forwarding the complaint to SUVA on time. A timely report greatly facilitates the investigation and recognition of performance.
Does SUVA reimburse all medical expenses resulting from the accident?
Yes, SUVA covers the full medical expenses resulting from the recognized accident (hospitalization, treatment, rehabilitation, prescribed medications). In addition, if the accident causes incapacity for work, SUVA pays a daily allowance (usually a percentage of salary). In case of permanent disability, coverage includes monthly annuities or one-time allowances.
If the accident occurs at the border crossing, what insurance is involved?
If the accident occurs during the ordinary home-work journey, even at the border crossing (Brogeda, Chiasso, Gaggiolo), the Swiss LAINF intervenes since the worker is covered by Swiss insurance during working hours and the relative journey. SUVA handles the claim following the standard procedure without particular complications related to the accident site.
