The analysis of the weather conditions in Ticino and the rest of Switzerland shows how the change in weather conditions directly affects the lives of border workers. The massive heatwave, which has reached record levels in some regions, is about to be replaced by milder temperatures, but the differences between the north and south of the country remain significant. The expected drop in temperatures in the north of the Alps, between 18 and 21°C, is a relief for commuters and outdoor activities. However, the prolongation of the intense heat in the south, until midweek, keeps the focus high on risks of heat stroke, dehydration and public health problems. Key points

To better cope with this weather situation, border workers in Ticino must adopt some simple but effective strategies. First of all, check the official weather forecasts, available on the MeteoSwitzerland website, on a daily basis to check for temperature changes and weather alerts. Second, plan your commute to and from work to avoid the hottest hours, preferably in the morning or late evening. A useful checklist includes: - Check traffic conditions at major crossings such as Chiasso and Gaggiolo. - Make sure you have water and sunscreen with you. - Respect for cooling breaks during outdoor activities. - Adapt clothing with lightweight and breathable garments. If you use car sharing or have a car, check the status of the vehicle and the operation of the air conditioner. Please note that in the event of thunderstorms, it may be necessary to change travel plans or postpone some activities. For outdoor workers, it is essential to respect breaks and hydrate frequently, avoiding prolonged exposure to the sun. Health authorities recommend eating light meals and dressing in appropriate clothing.
