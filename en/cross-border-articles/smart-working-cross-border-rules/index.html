Smart Working for Cross-Border Workers: What's Changing in 2026

In recent years, the topic of smart working for cross-border workers has taken center stage in the economic discussions between Italy and Switzerland. On 9 February 2026 the protocol amending the 2020 agreement on the taxation of cross-border workers entered into force (ratified in Italy by Law No. 217 of 29 December 2025): the home-office rules applied provisionally since 1 January 2024 are now permanent.

Under the protocol, cross-border workers can work up to 25% of their working time from their home in Italy without any change to the applicable tax rules or to their cross-border worker status. The 40% often quoted applied only to the transitional arrangement from 1 February to 31 December 2023, for contracts that provided for it: since 2024 the threshold is 25%. Working from home beyond 25% changes the cross-border worker status under the agreement, with tax consequences to be checked case by case.

The new rules apply to workers holding a G permit, who daily cross borders like Brogeda, Gaggiolo, and Ponte Tresa but seek greater workplace flexibility. For instance, a cross-border worker with 220 working days per year can work from home for about 55 days (25%) without altering their tax regime. Beyond this threshold, Italian taxation will apply, which typically involves higher tax rates compared to Switzerland.

'This is a significant shift for cross-border workers,' commented a representative of the Federal Finance Office (UFF), emphasizing how the agreement aims to balance fiscal competitiveness with the flexibility needs of modern workers.

Operational details

Practical and Tax Implications: What You Need to Know

For cross-border workers opting for smart working, it's essential to adhere to a few key rules. The 25% tax threshold is calculated annually, so keeping a detailed record of workdays spent in Italy and Switzerland is crucial. This helps prevent discrepancies during potential tax audits by Italian or Swiss authorities.

Neither the protocol nor the 2020 agreement requires the employer to report individual telework days to the Italian tax authorities: the automatic exchange of information under art. 7 of the agreement runs between tax authorities, from the canton to the Agenzia delle Entrate, and covers personal details, gross salary, social contributions and withholding tax. In Ticino, however, if employment ends during the year and the worker asks, the employer must certify the telework days already worked that year (art. 5a of the Ticino withholding tax regulation). A reliable tracking system, shared with the employer, helps avoid bureaucratic disputes. If the 25% tax threshold is exceeded, the tax treatment must be reviewed; social security contributions follow the separate framework-agreement threshold instead: they move to the Italian system from 50% telework, or already from 25% if the employer has not requested an A1 certificate.

A practical example clarifies the situation: Andrea, a resident of Varese, works for a company in Lugano. If Andrea works from home one day a week (about 20% of working time), he stays below the 25% tax threshold, about 55 days out of 220; two days a week (40%) would exceed it. Additionally, it's advisable to check the impact on INPS social security contributions and on taxation in Italy.

Practical Tips for Cross-Border Workers in Smart Working

💡 To avoid tax issues, it's essential to use a system to track workdays. This could include digital tools provided by your employer or a simple personal logbook. Additionally, always check your net salary to assess the impact of any tax changes.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the limit of smart working annually for Swiss-Italy frontier workers in 2026?
In 2026, cross-border workers can work up to 25% of their working time from home in Italy without changes to the tax regime, under the protocol in force since 9 February 2026 and applicable since 1 January 2024. For 220 working days, this is about 55 days.
How does it affect the overcoming of the limit of 25% of smart working on health benefits for frontier workers Switzerland-Italy?
Health and social insurance coverage does not depend on the 25% tax threshold but on EU/EFTA social security rules: with an A1 certificate requested by the employer under the framework agreement on cross-border telework, you remain insured in Switzerland up to 49.9% telework; without an A1 the threshold is 25%. Beyond that, insurance moves to Italy: check your situation with the AHV compensation office and your health insurer.
What documents should I keep to demonstrate smart working days in case of tax control?
Keep a detailed log with dates, hours worked and place of conduct, as well as any emails or business communications that entitle you to work remotely. In case of over 25%, controls can be more stringent.
