Hockey, Ambrì conquer the BCF Arena: Fribourg beaten 2-1

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 4 October 2026

After two defeats, Ambrì rediscover their solidity and win 2-1 in Fribourg. Goals by Müller and Schnarr prove decisive in an evening of great defensive sacrifice.

After two consecutive home defeats, Ambrì were looking for crucial points in the den of the Swiss champions. At BCF Arena, the response came with a 2-1 victory, built on solidity, sacrifice and efficiency—in other words, the elements Tapola wanted to rediscover. The blue-and-whites thus conquered Friborgo's rink with full merit.

A response after the home defeats

The lineup also presented some difficulties. Inti Pestoni returned to the lineup in place of the suspended Borradori, alongside Joly and Kodytek. In addition, Tapola had to deal with Tim Heed's absence, taking the ice with just six regular defensemen.

Despite the depleted defense, Ambrì started strongly, immediately putting the Swiss champions under pressure. Pestoni also looked dangerous, but was unable to beat Galley, preferred to Waeber. A penalty to Grassi in the offensive zone temporarily halted the Leventine side's momentum.

Friborgo's subsequent power play was neutralized by the blue-and-white penalty kill, though not without a few scares. Before the game, that special-teams situation was the worst in the League, with just 66,6% success.

At 12', Ambrì took the lead. Streule's mistake paved the way for Miles Müller, who made no mistake. Shortly afterward, Schmid hit a glaring post, but the visitors continued to create the best chances. The first period ended with Ambrì ahead and having rediscovered themselves after the last two outings. Müller and Schnarr's goals then decided an evening that ended with the blue-and-whites' 2-1 victory.

Operational details

The tactical key to victory

Compactness, transitions and management of key moments

Ambrì's 2-1 win can be understood through a game plan that remained recognizable over the three periods. The team started strongly, maintained a compact and aggressive approach in the middle period and preserved the same identity at the start of the third period. The willingness to break forward accompanied the defensive phase without turning it into a passive approach.

The first element concerns precisely the ability to stick to Tapola's plan when Friborgo increased the pressure. The Swiss champions grew stronger in the middle period, but Wüthrich was ready on shots from Sörensen and Bertschy. At the same time, the blue-and-whites did not give up on the counterattack: Järventie found himself all alone in front of Galley with the puck for the second goal on his stick, but was unable to convert.

The second element is the management of the penalty kill. Grassi's penalty in the offensive zone had given Friborgo the opportunity to exploit the numerical advantage. The blue-and-white unit nevertheless neutralized the power play, despite a few moments of concern. The 66,6% figure makes this passage even more significant in the story of the game, because the special-teams situation entered the match as the worst in the League.

The third element is the resistance in the third period. Friborgo increased the pace, driven above all by Sörensen's line, but Ambrì responded with short shifts, great intensity and maximum focus. When the defense and Wüthrich were not enough, the post intervened once again: Nemeth's shot, shortly before the halfway point of the period, made the home cage tremble. The performance therefore stemmed from the balance between chances created, pressure absorbed and the ability to remain faithful to the game plan.
