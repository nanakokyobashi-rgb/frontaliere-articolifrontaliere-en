Yes to the G Permit for Cross-Border Apprentices in Ticino

March 9, 2024, marks a pivotal breakthrough for the Canton of Ticino and its work permit system, representing an important step towards greater flexibility and attractiveness for young people seeking training and employment in Switzerland. The decision by the National Council to extend the G permit to apprentices crossing the border, for the entire duration of their traineeship, opens new opportunities and addresses the needs of an evolving labor market that is strongly linked to practical training and cross-border mobility.

Until now, regulations imposed significant restrictions. In particular, young people from Italy undertaking apprenticeships in Ticino—often in areas like Ponte Tresa, Gaggiolo, Chiasso, and Lugano—faced limitations in obtaining the G permit. The previous regulation, introduced in 2015 through the Switzerland-Italy agreement, allowed the permit to be issued only for full-time jobs and for periods up to 12 months, renewable but with restrictions that hindered long-term training activities. The new regulation, approved via a parliamentary motion, removes these limitations, enabling apprentices to hold the permit for the entire duration of their traineeship, which can extend up to three years, without interruptions or complex renewals.

A concrete example: an 18-year-old Italian resident of Ponte Tresa starts an apprenticeship in the mechanical sector with a company in Gaggiolo. Under the previous rules, they could only obtain the G permit for 12 months, risking frequent renewals or interruptions. Now, thanks to the new measure, they can be issued a permit valid for the entire training period, easing their integration and professional development.

📊 Recent data show approximately 3,500 cross-border apprentices are active in Ticino, with 65% coming from Italy—mainly from Varese, Como, and Lecco regions. The possibility of obtaining a G permit for the entire traineeship duration offers an additional incentive for these young individuals, also supporting local companies’ employment stability, which benefits from a qualified and stable workforce.

With this decision, the aim is to make the Swiss training system more competitive compared to European systems like Germany or France, where mobility and permit continuity are key factors of attractiveness. The current regulation also includes an operational checklist for companies and young people interested:

  • Verify the traineeship duration and compliance with G permit requirements.
  • Submit the application via the SECO online portal, attaching training documentation and proof of residence.
  • Communicate promptly any changes or renewals to avoid suspensions.
  • Monitor deadlines and stay updated on potential regulatory changes.

By comparing practical scenarios before and after the measure's implementation, it becomes clear that Ticino companies will be able to plan apprentices’ training paths more securely and without interruptions, fostering local labor market stability. This new regulation represents a significant step forward for vocational training and cross-border mobility in Ticino, reinforcing the image of a welcoming, dynamic canton ready to adapt to future challenges.

Operational details

Yes to the G Permit for Cross-Border Apprentices in Ticino

Practically speaking, the G permit for cross-border apprentices is a fundamental solution for young people aiming to combine vocational training with work in Ticino. This authorization allows residence and work in the canton for the entire traineeship duration, without time limits tied to temporary contracts or unstable work conditions. The regulation applies to those starting apprenticeships with certified companies in the canton, involving key sectors such as commerce, industry, crafts, and services.

For example, a young person from Chiasso or Mendrisio beginning an apprenticeship at a medium-sized company based in one of these towns can benefit from the G permit without time restrictions, provided the traineeship lasts up to three years, as per Swiss law. Applications are submitted to the Ticino Department of Finance and Economy (DFE) offices, with documentation including proof of enrollment in a recognized training institute, the training contract, and proof of temporary residence in Switzerland.

Operational checklist: - Enrollment in a recognized Swiss training institute - Valid training contract with a certified company - Identity documents and proof of temporary residence - Any health insurance attestations

The permit issuance process is generally quick, with waiting times around 30 days. A notable innovation is the automatic renewal of the permit, which occurs without requiring a new application, provided the training continues uninterrupted. This measure helps ensure greater stability for young workers and facilitates their integration into the territory, positively impacting the local socio-economic fabric.

Comparing practical scenarios, a 19-year-old apprentice starting a traineeship in the commerce sector in Bellinzona can easily obtain and retain the G permit until the end of their training, even with subsequent renewals. Conversely, without this authorization, the young person might have to leave after their temporary contract ends, risking interruption of their training and losing opportunities in the local job market.

Regulatory references: the G permit is governed by the Swiss Ordinance of October 4, 2006, with updates in 2023, which specify a minimum monthly income of CHF 1,200 as a basis for support, up to a maximum of CHF 2,500 depending on market conditions and employment needs. These parameters ensure young apprentices have adequate financial backing during their traineeship, supporting their professional and personal development sustainably.

Key points

Yes to the G Permit for Cross-Border Apprentices in Ticino

For cross-border apprentices from Italy, especially from the Trentino-Alto Adige municipalities like Bolzano, Merano, or Brunico, obtaining the G permit is a significant breakthrough, easing access to the Ticino job market and improving living and integration conditions. This measure, officially introduced through the regulatory revision of January 1, 2022, allows young people aged 15 to 25, enrolled in recognized training institutes, to work in Ticino with greater simplicity and security.

To qualify for the G permit, apprentices must meet key requirements, including enrollment in an official training institution such as the Bellinzona Technical School or Lugano Vocational Training Center, and a valid traineeship contract with at least 20 hours per week. The application must be submitted at least 30 days before the traineeship begins via the official portal of the Ticino Department of Territory, supported by documents such as the training contract, a copy of identity documents, and proof of residence in Italy.

Concrete example: a young person from Bolzano enrolled in an IT technician course who plans a 6-month traineeship in Lugano can apply for the G permit at a cost of about 150 Swiss francs. The permit can be renewed up to 12 months, with a total limit of 24 months if the training continues.

📊 Advantages of the G permit:

  • Legal and regular work
  • Access to health services via basic insurance
  • Eligibility for tax benefits, such as travel or training expense deductions
  • Up to 12 months of stay, renewable, with possible extension if studies are prolonged

💡 Operational checklist:

  • Verify required documentation (contract, enrollment certificate)
  • Use the permit calculator on Frontaliere Ticino’s website to estimate costs and requirements
  • Submit the application at least one month before the traineeship starts
  • Wait for approval, typically in 2-3 weeks

⚠️ Remember that the G permit allows working and staying in Ticino only during the traineeship and training activity. Afterward, renewals or other permit types, like the B permit for longer-term work or residence, should be considered.

Scenario comparison: a trainee moving to Lugano with the G permit can save around 20% on living costs compared to a residence permit, plus benefit from simplified administrative procedures compared to more complex permits like the B. The regulation aims to facilitate the integration of young Italians into the Ticino economy, helping to meet the demand for qualified workers in vocational training sectors.
