Children from border families can enjoy many opportunities to grow and develop in a multilingual and multicultural environment. Bilingual schools in Switzerland and Italy offer an ideal environment for learning to speak and write in two or more languages. This approach can help children develop greater cultural and linguistic awareness, which can be useful in an increasingly globalized society.

In the Canton of Ticino, for example, there are many bilingual schools that offer a curriculum that includes Italian and German. According to the Ministry of Education, Worship and Sport of the Canton of Ticino, in 2020/21 there were 27 bilingual schools offering an education in Italian and German. These schools are often located in border areas, where children can easily interact with their German-speaking peers.

The project involves the participation of children of different nationalities, with the aim of improving language and cultural skills. In 2020, the project involved 150 children of different nationalities, with an investment of CHF 150,000.

The project aims to improve the linguistic and cultural skills of children, with a focus on Italian and French. In 2019, the project involved 100 children, with an investment of CHF 80,000.

Raising Italian-German-French border bilingual children

Raising a child in a frontier family can offer numerous practical advantages on the future labour market. Greater labour mobility and cultural understanding can help children develop as individuals and become more competitive in the labour market in the future.

In the Canton of Ticino, where one of the most active borders between Italy and Switzerland is located, children from border families can benefit from greater accessibility to educational and cultural services. For example, the school "Liceo Classico S. Anna" from Bellinzona, which offers courses in Italian, German and French, has seen a 15% increase in enrolment over the last 5 years, thanks to the increased presence of border families in the area.

According to the regulations of the Swiss Federal Council of 2008, children from border families are entitled to receive bilingual education, i.e. in two official languages of Switzerland (German, French or Italian). This means that children can receive school training in a language other than their mother tongue, helping to develop broader language skills.
