Living in Albese with Cassano and working in Ticino (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 19 August 2026 · Updated on 25 September 2026

Albese with Cassano is a convenient choice for Ticino border crossers: cost of living, G permit, 2024 taxes and border crossings illustrated in detail.

Context

In Brief

  • Albese con Cassano is a municipality in the province of Como, Intelvi Valley, Ticino border
  • New frontier agreement in force from January 1, 2024
  • Source tax withheld only in Switzerland; tax credit in Italy
  • G permit required; €10,000 exemption (new frontiersmen)

Key Facts

  • What: Declare dependent income in Switzerland as a frontier resident in Como
  • When: New agreement from January 1, 2024
  • Where: Ticino-Como border (Brogeda, Chiasso, Gaggiolo, Ponte Tresa passes)
  • Who: Frontiersmen with G permit, employees in Ticino, residents in Italy
  • Amount: €10,000 annual exemption (new frontiersmen); €7,500 exemption (old frontiersmen until 2033)

Albese con Cassano, a small municipality in the province of Como in Lombardy, attracts an increasing number of frontiersmen who choose to live a few kilometers from the Ticino border due to cost of living and proximity to work. The decision to move from one Italian region to a border location is not only a geographical issue: it has significant implications in terms of taxation, social security, and acquired rights on the transfrontier labor front.

With the entry into force of the new frontier agreement on January 1, 2024 (signed on December 23, 2020 and ratified in Italy with Law 83 of June 13, 2023), the terms of the Italian-Swiss Convention of March 9, 1976 have been modified, which directly affect the calculation of taxes on income from work and the social security treatment of those living in Como and working in Switzerland.

Why choose the Como-Ticino border

The province of Como, particularly the municipalities of the Intelvi Valley such as Albese con Cassano, represents a balanced solution for those who want to work in the Swiss cantons (especially Ticino) while reducing housing costs. The Italian-Swiss economic dualism offers frontiersmen a logistical advantage: the short daily commute is compensated by a higher purchasing power in the Comasco Valley compared to Ticinese municipalities of similar size. Rents, food and services remain generally less expensive in the province of Como, even if they are nearby the border.

The main passes for those residing in Albese are Brogeda (for the Intelvi Valley), Chiasso (via A2), Gaggiolo (via SP San Fedele-Cassano) and Ponte Tresa (western side). Each pass has variable access conditions and waiting times depending on the time, season and traffic events; the choice depends on the workplace in Ticino (Lugano, Bellinzona, Locarno, Mendrisio).

The frontier worker permit (G permit)

Frontiersmen who work in Ticino and reside in Albese con Cassano need a G permit, issued by the Italian authorities for the right to perform a subordinate activity in Switzerland. The G permit is subject to the signing of an employment contract with a Ticinese employer and represents the fundamental document for crossing the border daily. It is a personal document that cannot be transferred, issued by the Questura or Prefecture of Como, and has multi-year validity (usually 5-10 years) provided the employment relationship in Switzerland is maintained. Operational details

Cross-Border Taxation in the 2024-2033 Regime

The new cross-border agreement modifies the tax regime for individuals living in Italy and working in Switzerland. Income tax withheld by the Swiss employer is based solely on the federal, cantonal, and municipal tax rates of Ticino. Italy does not apply a parallel source tax: the risk of double taxation is managed through the mechanism of tax credit in the model 730, section CE.

This means that as a cross-border resident in Como, you receive a net Swiss salary (with Swiss taxes deducted) and subsequently declare the gross income in Italy, benefiting from the Swiss tax credit to offset the Italian tax on the same taxable base.

Transitional Regime: Old and New Cross-Border Residents

The new agreement distinguishes between two categories:

Old Cross-Border Residents (residing in Italy before July 17, 2023): benefit from an exemption of €7,500 per year on the Italian portion of income, valid from 2024 to 2033. This means that the income subject to Italian taxation is reduced by €7,500.

New Cross-Border Residents (residing in Italy from July 17, 2023): are entitled to a €10,000 annual exemption, valid for the entire period they maintain their cross-border status.

Both regimes imply that the gross income declared to the Swiss employer remains unchanged; the tax effect only applies on the Italian side of the declaration.

Tax Rates in Switzerland

In Ticino, the source tax is determined by federal, cantonal, and municipal tariffs. As a dependent cross-border worker, you will automatically pay:

  • AVS/AI/IPG (old-age, disability, unemployment): 5.3% of gross salary at the expense of the dependent
  • Accident insurance (AD/AC): 1.1% up to CHF 148,200 per year (maximum)
  • Malpractice insurance (LAMal): mandatory if you choose the Swiss system, with adult co-payments CHF 300–2,500
  • LPP (complementary pension 2nd pillar): 7–18% depending on age (from 25 years)

All these amounts are deducted from the salary; the federal/cantonal source tax applies to the net pensionable income.

Income Tax Declaration in Italy

Each cross-border worker must complete the model 730 at the Italian Revenue Agency by October 31 of each year, indicating in section CE the income produced in Switzerland and the Swiss tax credit. The amount certified by the Swiss employer (source tax certificate in Switzerland) serves to offset the Italian tax, avoiding double taxation. Key points

Practical Guide: Five Steps to Become a Frontalier

Step 1: Employment Contract and Permit G

The first step is to sign an employment contract with an employer in Ticino. The employer will provide the necessary documentation to apply for the frontalier residence permit (Permit G) at the Questura of Como or the competent territorial prefecture, depending on the province of residence. The permit authorizes work in Switzerland as a resident frontalier in Italy and must be requested before the start of the employment relationship. On average, the permit G application process takes 30–60 days.

Step 2: INPS Registration and Previdential Communications

Although the main contribution payments are made in Switzerland (through AVS), it is necessary to inform the INPS that you are a frontalier with foreign work. This avoids double contributions and allows the recognition of Swiss contributory periods for Italian pensions (if requested in the future). Contact your CAF reference at Como to deposit the frontaliership communication with Italian authorities.

Step 3: Choice and Registration of Health Insurance

If you reside in Italy but work in Switzerland, you can opt for:

  • Swiss health insurance (LAMal): universal, with CHF 300–2,500 adult deductibles and variable monthly premiums per canton/insurer
  • Italian coverage (INPS/SSN): possible if you maintain Italian system registration

The choice must be communicated to the Swiss employer and competent authorities within the prescribed terms (usually within 30 days of starting work). It remains valid for a minimum period, usually 12 months.

Step 4: Annual Tax Declaration

By October 31 of each year, the frontalier must submit the model 730 to the Revenue Agency (through CAF or autonomously online). Essential documents:

  • Certified gross income from the Swiss employer (source tax certificate)
  • Withholding tax paid in Switzerland
  • Applicable exemption (€7,500 for old frontaliers, €10,000 for new ones)
  • Swiss credit for compensation

The CE section of the declaration must report the detail of income produced in Switzerland and the corresponding credit.

Step 5: Monthly Net Salary Simulation and Monitoring

Before signing the contract and after each salary change, use the pay slip calculator (nav:calculator) on the Frontaliere Ticino website to view the impact of Swiss withholding tax, AVS/LPP/LAINF contributions, and Italian deductibles on your monthly net salary. This tool is essential for budget planning, previdential savings, and annual checks.

Monitoring Deadlines and Useful Contacts

Practical tip: add the following deadlines to the calendar:

  • October 31: deadline for submitting the 730 declaration to the Revenue Agency
  • Start of year: verify the update of the exemption in the model 730
  • June 30: deadline for renewing the Permit G (if imminent expiry)
  • Annually: review LAMal coverage (comparison of Swiss premiums vs maintaining INPS coverage)

Do not tackle the transfer without fiscal and previdential preparation. The pay slip calculator (nav:calculator) is your first ally for quantifying the expected net; also verify the declarative deadlines with the CAF at Como to avoid fines and Revenue Agency checks.

Frequently Asked Questions
Do I have to pay taxes in Italy if I work in Switzerland and live in Albese with Cassano?
Yes, you must declare your income to the Revenue Agency using form 730, EC framework. The tax at source is withheld by the Swiss employer according to the Ticino rates; Italy applies a separate rate on income, but grants a tax credit for compensation. With the new agreement (from 1 January 2024), if you are a new frontier worker, you are entitled to a deductible of €10,000 per annum on the Italian tax base.
Is the G permit enough to live in Como and work in Lugano?
Yes, the G permit entitles you to work in Switzerland. It is a document issued by the Italian authorities (Police Headquarters/Prefecture of Como) and is recognised at the Swiss borders. An additional Swiss permit is not required for the border worker residing in Italy with a valid Italian G permit.
Where to pay AVS and LPP contributions as Ticino border workers?
AVS (old age, disability), IPG (unemployment) and LPP (supplementary pension) contributions are paid in Switzerland, directly from your employer as withholding on the paycheck (5.3% employee for AVS/AI/IPG). You do not have to pay contributions to INPS for work in Switzerland, but you remain registered with INPS for social security coordination between the two systems.
Can I keep Italian health insurance instead of Swiss health insurance?
Yes, the border crossing with G permit has the right of option. You can keep your Italian coverage (SSN/INPS) or choose a Swiss LAMal. The choice must be declared to the employer and the competent bodies (INPS in Italy, Lombardy Region, CAF), and remains valid for a minimum period of 12 months.
When does the new border agreement come into force and what are the benefits?
The new border agreement is in force from 1 January 2024. If you were a border worker before 17 July 2023, you are entitled to an exemption of €7,500 per year on Italian income (transitional regime 2024–2033). If you have acquired frontier status after 17 July 2023, the deductible is €10,000. Both reduce the income subject to Italian taxation.
