Record haul in Colverde: over 100 kg of marijuana seized (cross-border guide)

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 17 April 2026

The provincial police of Como have seized 105 kg of marijuana found in abandoned sacks near the Ticino border. The operation was launched following an environmental check.

Context

In brief

  • 105 kg of marijuana seized in Colverde, Como
  • Random discovery during environmental control
  • Drugs hidden in bags wrapped in plastic film

Key Facts

  • Substance: Marijuana (dried plant and leaves)
  • Quantity: >105 kg
  • Location: Colverde, Gironico, Como
  • Authority: Provincial Police of Como, Judicial Authority
  • Reason: Environmental control against waste abandonment
  • President of the Province: Fiorenzo Bongiasca
  • Border: Near Chiasso and Mendrisio, Canton Ticino
  • Date: Day before publication

A quantity of drugs equal to over a quintal was seized in Colverde, in the locality of Gironico, after an ordinary environmental surveillance activity led to the discovery of black bags abandoned in the green. The agents of the provincial police of Como, who intervened to check a pile of illegal waste, noticed that some bags were wrapped in several layers of plastic film, a detail that aroused suspicion.

After a thorough check, marijuana was found inside the containers both in plant and in dried leaves. The material was subjected to a narcotest that confirmed the presence of THC. The total quantity, weighed and seized, exceeded 105 kg, a figure that made the operation of great importance for the judicial authorities. The competent prosecutor's office was immediately informed of the incident.

The president of the Province of Como, Fiorenzo Bongiasca, underlined how this intervention confirms "how decisive is a constant presence on the territory and the daily surveillance work carried out by our Provincial Police". According to Bongiasca, a control born from environmental needs has led to a result of great impact in the fight against illegal phenomena, demonstrating that the protection of the territory is a fundamental tool for intercepting and countering criminal activities.

An operation born from environmental control

The activity that led to the seizure did not arise from a specific report on the presence of drugs, but from a routine monitoring against the illegal abandonment of waste. The bags, apparently containing normal waste, instead hid a load of drugs of considerable size. The police highlighted how the care in the packaging (several layers of film) was a key element to trigger suspicion and proceed with more in-depth checks.

The implications for the Ticino border

The town of Colverde is located a few kilometers from the border with the Canton of Ticino, in particular from the area of Chiasso and Mendrisio. Although the operation was conducted by the Italian authorities, the proximity to Switzerland raises questions about the possible final destination of the drugs. According to investigative sources, loads of this size could be destined for Italian domestic or cross-border markets, with direct consequences also for the Ticino territory.

Reactions of Ticino institutions

At the moment, there are no official communications from the Ticino authorities regarding this seizure. However, the news comes at a time when the issue of border security is particularly felt, also in light of the recent anti-drug operations carried out in other transit areas between Italy and Switzerland, such as the Brogeda crossing.

Key points

What should cross-border workers and Ticino residents do following the discovery of this drug seizure near the border? Authorities are urging collaboration by reporting suspicious situations, but what are the concrete steps to take to act effectively? Here's a step-by-step guide to help contribute to territorial security without exposing yourself to unnecessary risks.

Step 1: Recognize a suspicious situation

Not all piles of abandoned bags in green areas are necessarily dangerous, but certain elements can raise suspicions:

  • Excessive packaging: bags wrapped in multiple layers of plastic film, adhesive tape, or other materials;
  • Unusual location: piles in isolated areas, far from busy roads or public spaces;
  • Distinctive odor: presence of a strong, persistent smell typical of narcotic substances;
  • Other clues: traces of recent passage, abandoned objects near the bags, vehicles parked nearby.

Step 2: Do not intervene directly

It is crucial not to touch the bags or suspicious objects. Even if they appear to contain only waste, they could conceal dangerous substances or other illegal materials. Additionally, tampering with the scene could compromise any investigations by the authorities.

Step 3: Report to the competent authorities

If you are in Ticino, you can contact:

  • Ticino cantonal police: dial 117;
  • Border Guard: for reports related to border crossings or frontier zones;
  • Municipal police of your place of residence;
  • Carabinieri or State Police (if in Italian territory): dial 112.

Step 4: Provide precise information

When making a report, it is helpful to provide as much detail as possible:

  • Exact location of the pile of bags (address, landmarks, GPS coordinates if available);
  • Detailed description of the bags and their apparent contents;
  • Any traces of recent passage (vehicles, people, unusual noises);
  • Time of the report and any subsequent updates.

Step 5: Wait for authorities to intervene

After reporting, authorities will assess the situation and act promptly. In case of immediate danger (e.g., presence of toxic or explosive substances), it is advisable to leave the area and wait for officers to arrive.

Step 6: Collaborate with investigations

If you witness a suspicious event, you may be asked to provide statements to the authorities. In such cases, it is important to remain calm and answer investigators' questions accurately. Authorities guarantee the confidentiality of the information provided.

What NOT to do

  • Do not open the bags: even if it seems like an environmental check, handling the bags can be dangerous;
  • Do not remove or move suspicious objects;
  • Do not share unverified information on social media or discussion groups;
  • Do not take personal initiatives to investigate the situation.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the risks for a cross-border worker if they unknowingly transport drugs across the border?
In Switzerland, drug trafficking is a criminal offense, even if unintentional. Penalties can reach up to 20 years in prison, depending on the quantity and circumstances. In Italy, the offense is serious and carries criminal sanctions. It is crucial to avoid any contact with suspicious substances or objects and to report the situation immediately to the authorities.
How can I tell the difference between a normal pile of waste and a suspicious one?
Suspicious bags often have excessive packaging (multiple layers of film, adhesive tape), are located in isolated areas, and may emit strong odors. If you notice these signs, do not touch the bags and report them immediately to the authorities. Never open or move suspicious objects.
Which Ticino-Como border crossings are under increased scrutiny following this operation?
Following the seizure in Colverde, the crossings at **Brogeda** (Chiasso) and **Gaggiolo** (Stabio) are at higher risk of extraordinary checks. The **Ponte Chiasso** bridge may also receive greater attention from authorities.
Could I be called as a witness after making a report?
Yes, if your report leads to an investigation, you may be contacted by authorities to provide a statement. Authorities guarantee confidentiality and protection of personal data. It is important to respond accurately, without adding unverified details.
Are there official channels for reporting suspicious situations in Ticino?
Yes. In addition to emergency numbers **117** (cantonal police) and **112** (Carabinieri/State Police), Ticino has online reporting services via official police apps or municipal websites. For cross-border workers, it is advisable to use communication channels dedicated to border crossings.
