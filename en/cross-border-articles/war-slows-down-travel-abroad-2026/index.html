War halts foreign travel: a third of Swiss cancel plans (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 5 May 2026

According to TCS, a third of Swiss don't feel safe for holidays outside their borders. Spain and Portugal are the preferred destinations.

Context

In brief

  • One third of Swiss people refrain from traveling abroad due to insecurity
  • Political instability and wars influence travel habits
  • Spain and Portugal are the most appreciated destinations
  • Ticino cross-border workers prefer nearby destinations like Italy and France

Key facts

  • What: Feeling of insecurity for traveling abroad
  • When: 2026 Barometer conducted in February/March
  • Where: Switzerland
  • Who: Swiss Touring Club (TCS)
  • Amount: 33% of Swiss people consider foreign vacations insecure

The war is deterring travel abroad. According to the TCS, one third of Swiss people do not feel safe leaving the country for vacations. The sense of security among Swiss people when traveling abroad is worsening. According to the tenth barometer of the Touring Club (TCS), one third consider vacations outside national borders as rather insecure, compared to 19% a year ago. This is a rate that had not been reached since the pandemic. According to the survey results, published today, political instability and wars are the main factors influencing travel habits (66%). These are followed by terrorist threats (44%), crime (34%), and health risks (33%).

Implications for cross-border workers

The desire to travel remains high. The percentage of those who never do it has decreased, from 19% in 2025 to 9% this year. About 10% travel more often than before, 40% less, while for 38% the frequency has remained unchanged. 'This demonstrates a stabilization of the desire to travel, after the recovery effect observed in the first years following Covid,' explains the TCS in a note. European destinations are gaining popularity, with Spain and Portugal particularly appreciated, while North America has lost some of its appeal.

Compared to previous editions, there has also been a change in the choice of transportation. 51% of respondents indicated their own car as the preferred means, a rate that two years ago was still at 67%. The private car has therefore now fallen in preference to the same level as airplane (51%) and train (50%). The 2026 barometer was conducted in the months of February and March. The survey involved 1004 citizens, from the three main linguistic regions of the country, as well as 810 TCS members.

Checklist for Ticino cross-border workers

For Ticino cross-border workers, who often work in Switzerland but reside in Italy, the choice of vacations is also influenced by practical factors such as proximity and costs. Here is an operational checklist to plan safe and economical trips: 1. Check entry requirements: Verify the necessary documents for the destination country, especially if outside the EU. 2. Compare costs: Use price comparators for flights, trains, and accommodations. 3. Health insurance: Check the insurance coverage, especially for countries with expensive healthcare systems. 4. Local transportation: Inform yourself about the public and private transportation available at the destination. 5. Safety: Consult the safety advice of the Federal Department of Foreign Affairs (FDFA).

Comparison of Practical Scenarios

For a cross-border worker from Chiasso who works in Lugano, a trip to Spain might cost around 1,500 CHF for two weeks, including flight, accommodation, and local transport. In comparison, a holiday in Italy, such as in Milan or Turin, could cost around 800 CHF for the same period, thanks to the proximity and lower transport costs. Additionally, for cross-border workers residing in Italy, traveling to Switzerland for the weekend is a popular option, with contained costs and ease of access.

Regulations and Incentives

As of January 1, 2024, Switzerland has introduced new regulations for cross-border workers, which include tax incentives for those residing in Italy and working in Switzerland. For example, cross-border workers can benefit from a 20% tax deduction on travel costs between their residence and workplace, up to a maximum of 3,000 CHF per year. This incentive can be particularly useful for those who decide to travel during weekends or holidays, combining work and leisure.

Conclusion

Despite the perceived insecurity, the desire to travel remains strong among Swiss and Ticino cross-border workers. The choice of destinations and means of transport is influenced by factors such as safety, costs, and proximity. With careful planning and the use of tax incentives, it is possible to enjoy safe and economical holidays, both within and outside the country.

Key points

What to do if you want to travel abroad

If you decide to travel abroad despite security concerns, it's important to take some precautions. First, it's advisable to inform yourself about the political and security situation of the chosen destination. You can consult the websites of the Federal Department of Foreign Affairs (FDFA) or the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of your country to obtain up-to-date information.

Steps to follow

1. Inform yourself about the political and security situation of the chosen destination.

  • For example, for Ticino cross-border workers traveling to Italy, it's useful to monitor the red or orange zones indicated by the Italian Department of Civil Protection.
  • According to recent data, 30% of Swiss people canceled international trips in 2023, with a significant drop in destinations like Ukraine, Lebanon, and Turkey.

2. Consult the websites of the FDFA or the Ministry of Foreign Affairs for up-to-date information.

  • The FDFA provides real-time updates on any risks and travel advice.
  • For residents of Lugano, the Swiss Consulate in Milan is a reference point for assistance and information.

3. Evaluate the available transport options and choose the one you consider safest.

  • For example, cross-border workers traveling between Chiasso and Milan can opt for direct trains like the Gotthard Panorama Express, which offers a safe and scenic route.
  • Costs vary: a round-trip train ticket costs approximately 50 CHF, while by car, you add the costs of fuel and highway tolls.

4. Make sure you have travel insurance that covers any medical emergencies or other unforeseen events.

  • Standard travel insurance covers up to 100,000 CHF for medical expenses, but it's important to check the specific conditions.
  • For cross-border workers, some cross-border health insurances, such as LAMal, offer coverage abroad, but with limitations.

5. Prepare an emergency kit with medications, important documents, and useful contacts.

  • An emergency kit should include headache medication, antihistamines, bandages, and disinfectants.
  • It's useful to have a digital copy of documents, such as the passport and ID card, saved on a cloud or a USB device.

Operational checklist

  • 📊 Documents: Passport, ID card, health insurance, driver's license.
  • 📊 Health: Essential medications, first aid kit, travel insurance.
  • 📊 Contacts: Local emergency numbers, Swiss embassy or consulate, family and friends contacts.
  • 📊 Transport: Booked tickets, route map, information on schedules and stops.

Comparisons between practical scenarios

  • Train: Safe, convenient for medium distances, but with fixed schedules.

  • Cross-border health insurance vs. travel insurance:
