On 1 January 2024, the New Cross-border Workers Agreement came into force, signed on 23 December 2020 and ratified in Italy with Law 83 of 13 June 2023. For the cross-border worker who checks his Swiss pay slip in the Canton of Ticino in 2026, this sequence is the starting point: the reference is not an agreement that came into force in 2026, but a regulation that is already operational.

The tax principle is clearly defined: the cross-border worker's income is withheld only in Switzerland. Italy avoids double taxation through the tax credit to be indicated in the EC framework of 730. It is therefore not a double withholding tax on the same income. The Italy-Switzerland Convention on double taxation was signed on 9 March 1976; Switzerland is not a member of the EU or EEA.

To read the payslip as a single person, it is best to separate the tax part from the social items. Withholding tax rates, including federal and cantonal ones, are established by federal and cantonal laws. At the federal level, administration is the responsibility of AFC/ESTV; at the cantonal level, the cantonal tax administrations intervene. UFAS and BFS do not set these rates: the first concerns social security, AVS/AI, while the second collects statistical data.

The dates that separate the regimes

The Italian picture changes from 2026. For the Italian IRPEF three bands are indicated: 23% up to €28,000, 33% from €28,001 to €50,000, 43% over €50,000. For incomes 2024–2025 the reference remains 23%, 35%, 43%. The modification is referred to in Law 199/2025, article 1, paragraph 3.

Finally, the G permit, the AVS and the LPP enter into the pay slip control; for health coverage the correct term is KVG, i.e. health insurance, not "health tax". The new 2026 tax agreement must therefore be placed in the actual chronology: the New Cross-border Agreement operates from 1 January 2024.

Five checks on the payslip

2. Isolate the tax. Search for the withholding tax on earned income and check the principle applied: for cross-border workers it is only in Switzerland. Frequently Asked Questions
Is income withholding tax due in both countries?
For the cross-border commuter's earned income, the deduction takes place only in Switzerland. Italy avoids double taxation by means of the tax credit to be carried over to the EC framework of 730. The Italy-Switzerland Double Taxation Convention was signed on 9 March 1976. This criterion must be kept distinct from the individual contribution items of the payslip.
What is the difference between old and new cross-border commuters?
Old cross-border commuters are those who were already cross-border commuters before 17 July 2023 and fall under the 2024–2033 transitional regime, with an exemption of €7,500. For new cross-border commuters, the deductible indicated is €10,000. The distinction must be checked before reading the net of the 2026 Swiss payslip and before comparing the Italian tax situation.
What percentages are included in Swiss contributions?
The source indicates AHV/IV/EO at 5.3% for the employee, ALV/HC at 1.1% with a cap of CHF 148,200, UVG at 0.7–1.5% and BVG at 7–18% for age group from the age of 25. They are different items and must be read separately from withholding tax and the KVG.
What does a cross-border commuter need to know about the KVG?
KVG indicates health insurance. G cross-border commuters have the right of choice. For adults, the deductibles indicated range from CHF 300 to CHF 2,500. This health parameter should not be confused with the contribution rates of the payslip, such as AHV/IV/EO, ALV/AC, UVG or BVG.
