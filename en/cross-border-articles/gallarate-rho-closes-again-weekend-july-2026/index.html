Context

In short - Closure of the Gallarate-Milan line on the weekend of 11-13 July 2026 - Technological upgrading and quadrupling of the line - New stop in Nerviano and Parabiago adjustment planned - What : Closure of the railway line for upgrading works - When: From the evening of Saturday 11 to dawn of Monday 13 July 2026 - Where: Gallarate-Milan line, Rho-Parabiago section - Who: RFI (Italian Railway Network) - Amount: Approximately 640 million euros (also with PNRR funds) The Gallarate-Milan railway line will suffer another interruption on the weekend between Saturday 11 and Monday 13 July 2026. Specifically, the trains are scheduled to stop from 22:40 on Saturday 11 July until 05:10 on Monday 13 July 2026. This closure, lasting a total of about 30 hours, is necessary to continue the technological and infrastructural upgrading of the railway section. The main objective of these works is the quadrupling of the line between the Rho and Parabiago stations, a segment about 8 kilometres long. At the same time, significant works will be carried out for the adaptation of the Parabiago station and for the insertion of a new stop in Nerviano. Frequently Asked Questions
When does the Gallarate-Milano line close?
The railway line Gallarate-Milano will be closed for works from 22:40 on Saturday 11 July at 05:10 on Monday 13 July 2026. This period of interruption is due to technological and infrastructural development.
What are the main works planned?
The works include the quadrupling of the line between Rho and Parabiago (about 8 km), the insertion of a new stop in Nerviano and the adjustment of Parabiago station. It is also planned to create a connecting wheel with the FNM Saronno-Malpensa line.
Will the Malpensa service be guaranteed?
Yes, the railway service to Malpensa airport and from there to Milano Centrale will remain active during the closing period of the Gallarate-Milano line. However, access to the Gallarate station may vary.
What is the investment amount for these jobs?
The total value of the investment amounted to approximately 640 million euros. A part of these funds comes from the National Resumption and Resilience Plan (PNRR).
What are the implications for frontier workers?
Frontiers using this line may suffer delays due to increased road traffic on alternative routes to border crossings. It is advisable to start more in advance and monitor official communications to plan trips.
