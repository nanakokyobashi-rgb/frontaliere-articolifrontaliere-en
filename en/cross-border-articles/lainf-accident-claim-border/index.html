Accident in progress: LAINF complaint for border workers (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 3 September 2026

LAINF covers injuries on the commute to the border. Here's what's different about a trip and how to file a report.

Context

In brief

  • LAINF covers accidents on the way to work up to the Swiss border
  • Report to SUVA if the accident occurs in Switzerland
  • Crucial difference: regular commute ≠ extraordinary business trip

Key facts

  • What: Accident insurance coverage (LAINF) for cross-border commuters at the border
  • Managing entity: SUVA (Switzerland) if the accident occurs in Swiss territory
  • Alternative coverage: INPS (Italy) if the accident occurs beyond the border
  • Difference: Regular commute always covered; business trip: depends on circumstances
  • Where: Brogeda, Chiasso, Gaggiolo crossings; A2 routes, regional roads

A cross-border commuter who suffers an accident on the way from home to work is entitled to Swiss LAINF coverage. But what happens if the accident occurs at the Brogeda, Chiasso crossing, or beyond the border? And how to differentiate a claim for a regular commute from one for a business trip? The distinction is crucial as it determines which regulations apply and which entity manages the compensation.

When LAINF coverage applies

The LAINF (Federal Law on Accident Insurance) in Switzerland automatically covers employed workers. For a cross-border commuter with a G permit who resides in Canton Ticino and works in Italy, the protection depends on where the accident occurs.

If the accident happens during the commute before crossing the Swiss border — for example, on the road from Chiasso to the company in Lombardy — Swiss LAINF coverage applies, as the worker is still under the jurisdiction of the Swiss insurance system.

Conversely, if the accident occurs in Italian territory, Italian regulations apply, and compensation falls under the jurisdiction of the INPS (National Institute for Social Security).

Regular commute vs. business trip: the crucial difference

A fundamental aspect for the claim concerns the nature of the trip. A regular commute is the ordinary and repetitive route that the cross-border commuter takes daily from their Ticino home to their usual workplace. A business trip, on the other hand, is an extraordinary journey to a different destination from the usual workplace — a construction site, a branch office, a meeting with a distant client.

This distinction is not formal: it changes the insurance coverage. A regular commute is always covered by LAINF; a business trip to a secondary location might fall into a different category, depending on the specific circumstances.

For the cross-border commuter, it is essential to accurately document the type of trip at the time of the claim, attaching testimonies, photos of the location, times, and statements about the intended destination.

Operational details

How the Italian-Swiss Convention Regulates Coverage

The Convention between Italy and Switzerland, in force since March 9, 1976, governs the treatment of cross-border workers in terms of social security and insurance. In the event of an accident at the border, it is this bilateral norm that decides which system covers the incident.

If a cross-border worker with a Permesso G suffers an accident between their Swiss residence and the border crossing — for example, on the A2 in Swiss territory — LAINF responds directly through SUVA. If, however, the accident occurs after Chiasso or Brogeda, even if the worker was still on their way to the company in Italy, it is the Italian work insurance (managed by INPS) that intervenes.

This geographical distinction is sometimes delicate: if the accident occurs exactly at the border or in an ambiguous border area, it is crucial to obtain the report from the Swiss or Italian road police, which precisely attests to the exact location and thus the jurisdiction.

What Changes for the Ticino Cross-Border Worker

For those working with a Permesso G, the advantage is having LAINF coverage that extends to the border, ensuring protection during the daily 30-60 minute commute. This is particularly relevant because the journey to Italy involves specific risks: delays at border crossings, concentrated traffic, morning queues, overtaking maneuvers in critical areas.

If the accident occurs in Swiss territory, the cross-border worker does not need to contact the Italian company or INPS directly: the report goes to SUVA, which follows the entire procedure according to Swiss law.

If it occurs already in Italy, the protocol changes completely. Here, INPS comes into play, and the cross-border worker must coordinate with the Italian employer to start the procedure with the transalpine social security institute. The times, forms, daily allowance amounts, and level of coverage can differ significantly from the Swiss system.

Scenario: When the Border is the Limit

Consider a cross-border worker reaching the Brogeda border crossing during rush hour. An accident during this journey will be covered by SUVA, as it occurs in Switzerland and falls within the "home-work" journey under LAINF.

If, however, the same cross-border worker, once they have crossed the border and are already in Italian territory on the provincial road to the company, suffers an accident, the responsibility shifts to the Italian INPS. Here, the compensation will follow Italian criteria and amounts, not Swiss. The Swiss daily allowance and the Italian one can differ significantly, as can the management times of the procedure and the medical checks required.

For this reason, keeping documents that attest to the exact location (witnesses, photos, medical certificates with location, road police report) is essential to protect one's rights and avoid disputes over jurisdiction.

Key points

SUVA reporting procedure: essential steps

If the accident occurred in Swiss territory during the commute to work, the cross-border worker must initiate a reporting procedure with SUVA. The report does not need to be submitted personally by the worker: usually, the employer or the treating physician will transmit it.

Step 1: Immediate notification of the employer. The cross-border worker immediately informs the company of the accident, specifying the time, location (Brogeda, Chiasso, provincial road, etc.), dynamics of the event, and names of any witnesses.

Step 2: Medical care and documentation. If necessary, the cross-border worker seeks medical care (emergency room, clinic, hospital) and carefully keeps all medical certificates, radiological reports, prescriptions, and treatment documentation.

Step 3: Filling out and submitting the SUVA form. The employer (or the treating physician) fills out the official SUVA reporting form and submits it to the insurer within the deadlines set by Swiss law.

Step 4: Investigation and communication of the outcome. SUVA initiates the investigation, requests any clarifications, and communicates the outcome of the coverage, the amounts of daily allowances, and the details of the continuation of the treatment to the cross-border worker.

Essential documentation to expedite the process

To avoid delays and disputes, the cross-border worker must compile a complete file:

  • Written statement of the event (date, exact time, geographical location, dynamics, causes)
  • Initial medical certificate (first treatment in hospital or clinic with stamp and date)
  • Photographs of the accident site (if possible, taken within the first few hours)
  • Names and contacts of witnesses present
  • Copy of the G Permit and employment contract
  • Statement of usual destination and regular route
  • Swiss road police report (if the accident was reported to the authorities)

This documentation is crucial to prove to SUVA that it was a regular commute to work and not an extraordinary trip or deviation from the route.

When to consult a specialist

If the accident is serious or involves prolonged absences from work, or if SUVA partially denies coverage or requests complex clarifications, the cross-border worker should consult a specialist in labor law or social security law. In Canton Ticino, cross-border worker associations and unions offer free or low-cost consultations.

In addition, if the accident involves the border — for example, if the exact location of the accident is unclear or if the cross-border worker crossed the border shortly before the incident — it is useful to obtain the Swiss road police report, which certifies the location and clarifies the jurisdiction in an irrefutable manner.

Frequently Asked Questions
What does the LAINF cover for a border worker who injures himself at the border?
The LAINF covers accidents that occur during the commute, up to the Swiss border crossing. If the accident occurs in Swiss territory (for example, on the A2 before Chiasso or Brogeda), SUVA coverage is triggered. If it happens over the border, in Italy, the responsibility passes to the Italian INPS. It is crucial to document the exact location of the accident to determine jurisdiction.
What is the difference between commuting and commuting for LAINF purposes?
A home-work journey is the ordinary and daily route from your home in Ticino to your usual place of work; it is always covered by the LAINF. A trip is an extraordinary move to a different destination (construction site, branch, distant customer); coverage depends on the circumstances and the contract. For the complaint, the border worker must clearly specify the intended destination.
Who files the complaint with SUVA: the worker or the employer?
Normally, it is the employer or the attending physician who files the complaint with SUVA, not the border worker directly. The border worker must immediately inform the employer of the accident and provide all details and medical documentation. The employer then entrusts SUVA with the practice following Swiss procedures.
What documentation is needed for a LAINF accident report at the border?
Statement of the incident (place, time, dynamics), initial medical certificate, photos of the place (if available), names of witnesses, copy of Permit G, employment contract and statement of intended destination. If the accident is at the border, it is useful to acquire the report of the Swiss Road Traffic Police to attest to the exact place and jurisdiction.
What happens if the accident happens across the border, in Italy?
If the accident occurs in Italian territory, the coverage passes to the Italian INPS, not to SUVA. The border worker must coordinate with the Italian employer to start the procedure at INPS. Timeframes, allowance amounts and coverage criteria may differ from the Swiss system. It is advisable to consult a specialist in cross-border law to protect your rights.
