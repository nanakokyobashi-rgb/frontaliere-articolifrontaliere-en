The tax framework for border crossers

Under current legislation, earned income in Switzerland is subject to a taxation mechanism involving both states, exceeding the previous regime based on Swiss taxation alone. The frontier worker must declare the income in Italy, where the Revenue Agency applies IRPEF rates (23% up to 28,000 euros, 33% between 28,001 and 50,000 euros, 43% over 50,000 euros). To avoid double taxation, the taxpayer can use the tax credit by completing the EC framework of Model 730, deducting what has already been paid in Switzerland. The transitional regime of Article 9 of the Agreement applies only to those who meet the conventional definition and worked in Graubünden, Ticino or Valais between 31 December 2018 and 17 July 2023; the covered employment income is taxed exclusively in Switzerland. In other cases, the ordinary regime applies, with Italian taxation and credit for Swiss tax. The 10,000 euros of Article 4 L. 83/2023 are only the limit for income referred to in Article 1, paragraph 175, L. 147/2013, not a general deductible. Management of social contributions

The gross salary is subject to social security deductions managed according to Swiss law. The AVS/AI/IPG share to be paid by the employee is set at 5.3%. To this is added the contribution for unemployment insurance (AD/AC) equal to 1.1%, calculated on a maximum of CHF 148,200 per year. As regards occupational pension provision (LPP), deductions vary between 7% and 18% based on the age group, with contribution obligations starting from the age of 25.

Health and KVG insurance

A crucial aspect for cross-border workers is the right to opt for health insurance. G cross-border commuters can choose between Swiss healthcare coverage (LAMal) or maintaining the Italian healthcare system. In case of membership of the KVG, the deductibles for adults vary between 300 and 2,500 CHF. It is essential to note that tax rates and contributions are established by federal or cantonal laws and administered by the Federal Tax Administration (AFC/ESTV), not being the responsibility of the UFAS or the BFS. The double taxation agreement between Italy and Switzerland, signed on 9 March 1976, remains the fundamental legal pillar for relations between the two states, considering that Switzerland is not a member of the EU or the EEA.

What are the IRPEF rates applied to frontier workers?
Border workers are subject to Italian personal income tax with progressive rates: 23% up to 28,000 euros, 33% for the share between 28,001 and 50,000 euros, and 43% for the part exceeding 50,000 euros.

What are the mandatory social contributions in Switzerland?
The employee pays 5.3% for AVS/AI/IPG and 1.1% for unemployment insurance (AD/AC) up to a ceiling of CHF 148,200. The LPP varies between 7% and 18% depending on age.
What is the right of option for LAMal?
The right of option allows G border workers to choose whether to be insured against diseases according to the Swiss system (LAMal), with deductibles between 300 and 2,500 CHF, or to maintain the coverage of the Italian health system.
