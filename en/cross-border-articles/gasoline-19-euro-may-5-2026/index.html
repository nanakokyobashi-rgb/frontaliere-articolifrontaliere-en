Petrol exceeds 1.9 euros per litre: first time in two years (cross-border guide)

Di Valerie Linc · Published on 5 May 2026

Petrol price hits 1.926 euros per litre in self-service mode, while diesel slightly decreases.

Context

In brief

  • Petrol at 1.926 euros/litre self-service
  • Diesel at 2.044 euros/litre self-service
  • 6 cent increase for petrol

Key facts

  • What: Average fuel prices
  • When: 5 May 2026
  • Where: National road and motorway network
  • Who: Ministry of Enterprises and Made in Italy
  • Amount: 1.926 euros/litre for self-service petrol

The price of petrol continues to rise. Today, 5 May 2026, for the first time in two years, it exceeds the 1.9 euro per litre mark. Diesel slightly decreases, remaining just over four cents above two euros, while LPG is at 82 cents per litre. The Ministry of Enterprises and Made in Italy reports that, based on the latest data collected by the Mimit Fuel Prices Observatory, today the average price of fuels in 'self-service' mode along the national road network is 1.926 euros/l for petrol and 2.044 euros/l for diesel. On the motorway network, however, the average self-service price is 1.981 euros/l for petrol and 2.114 euros/l for diesel.

Details by sales method and brand

According to the usual survey by Staffetta Quotidiana, IP has increased the recommended prices of petrol by 6 cents per litre and reduced those of diesel by four. For Q8, we record a one-cent decrease on petrol, and for Tamoil, a one-cent decrease on diesel. Operational details

Analysis of Practical Implications

The increase in gasoline prices has a significant impact on cross-border workers who daily cross the border between Italy and Switzerland. With gasoline prices exceeding 1.9 euros per liter, transportation costs rise, affecting family budgets. For cross-border workers who use their cars to commute to work in Switzerland, fuel costs represent a significant expense. Additionally, fuel costs also affect freight transport, with potential repercussions on consumer goods prices.

Comparison with the Previous Situation

Before the increase, the average price of gasoline was below 1.9 euros per liter. With the current rise, cross-border workers must account for an additional cost for fuel refills. For example, for a 50-liter refill, the additional cost is approximately 8.60 euros on highways and 8.52 euros on average roads. This increase can significantly impact the monthly budgets of cross-border worker families, especially those with multiple vehicles or long daily commutes.

Future Scenarios

If the trend of rising prices continues, cross-border workers may be forced to reconsider their travel habits. Some might opt for alternative transportation methods, such as carpooling or public transport, to reduce transportation costs. Others might consider purchasing more fuel-efficient vehicles. Furthermore, the rise in fuel prices could push cross-border workers to request salary increases or additional benefits to offset the higher transportation costs.

For cross-border workers who use their car to commute to Switzerland, it's important to closely monitor fuel prices and look for any available offers or discounts.
