On 29 November, the population will vote on the VAT increase chosen by Parliament to finance the 13th AVS annuity. The consultation will come a few days before the first payments, according to the source.

On Tuesday, after the position expressed by the Federal Council the previous week, Left, Center and Evangelical Party defended the solution. In a statement, the alliance argues that the stability of the AVS and the security of pensions require solid funding of the 13th annuity. The same alliance warns that a no would endanger the AVS.

What the alliance supports

To finance the 13th AVS annuity, supported in a popular vote in 2024, Parliament opted for an increase in VAT of 0.4 points. The increase excludes basic necessities, to protect modest incomes.

The coalition wants to respond to the arguments of PLR and UDC, who oppose the project. Léonore Porchet (Verdi/VD) used the case of a middle-income person to compare the two effects:

MEP Léonore Porchet (Greens/DV) defended the measure by observing that, for a person with an average income, the annual increase in VAT would be about 85 francs, while the additional income would reach about 2,000francs per year at the time of retirement: "It's worth it".

Operational details

The figure to be read with more caution is the comparison between 85 and 2,000francs. The first figure is described as extra VAT per year for a middle-income person; the second as an additional annual annuity at retirement age. They are therefore two different moments: an annual disbursement and a benefit placed in the retirement phase. The source does not present them as a custom calculation for each border crossing.

For a frontier worker who wants to apply the figure to his situation, the text does not indicate the precise income to be used, the composition of the household, the consumption basket or the date of application of the increase. It does not even list the basic necessities excluded from the measure. For this reason, the 85 francs cannot be treated as an identical figure for each person: they are the estimate cited by Porchet for an average income.
