Unitas turns 80: The association's outlook and future

Di Marco Ferrari · Published on 29 April 2026

The Association for the Blind and Visually Impaired of Italian-speaking Switzerland celebrates 80 years, facing financial challenges and new inclusive projects in the region.

Context

In brief

  • Unitas celebrates 80 years of activity in the Ticino region.
  • President Fabio Casgnola will step down on May 23.
  • An inclusive concert with the OSI has been announced for late August.
  • Financial resources remain a priority challenge.

Key facts

  • What: Eightieth anniversary of Unitas.
  • When: Celebrations underway, change of presidency on May 23.
  • Where: Historic headquarters on Via Nassa, Lugano.
  • Who: Association for the blind and visually impaired of Italian-speaking Switzerland.
  • Founder: Tarcisio Bisi (1946).

The Association for the blind and visually impaired of Italian-speaking Switzerland, known as Unitas, has officially inaugurated the celebrations for its eightieth anniversary. The event, presented during a press conference held on Via Nassa in Lugano, retraced the history of the organization, founded in 1946 on that very same street by Tarcisio Bisi. The anniversary represents not only a chronological milestone but also a moment of reflection on the current needs of the community it serves. During the meeting, a clear desire to look toward the future emerged, while maintaining a firm commitment to supporting the people the association assists daily. The outgoing president, Fabio Casgnola, took the opportunity to outline future prospects, confirming that the appointment of the new presidency will take place on May 23, marking the end of his three-year term. The association emphasized that the keyword for this special year is 'future', understood as the continuous evolution of services and activities needed to meet the members' needs in a constantly changing social and economic context. The celebrations will involve the active participation of local partners, aiming to strengthen the presence of Unitas throughout the Canton of Ticino. Despite the festivities, the atmosphere is pragmatic: the conference highlighted critical challenges, most notably the issue of financial contributions, which currently are not sufficient to cover all operational and assistance requirements. Awareness of these difficulties drives the association to seek new forms of collaboration and to raise public awareness regarding the importance of continuous support. The history of the association, which has its roots in the post-war period, continues to be a fundamental reference point for Italian-speaking Switzerland, working in synergy with local institutions to ensure support and social inclusion. Operational continuity remains the primary goal, despite the leadership change scheduled for late spring.

Operational details

Prospects and Challenges of the Inclusive System

The eightieth anniversary of Unitas raises important questions about the dynamics of assistance in Ticino. For citizens and families who interact with social services and support, the stability of entities like Unitas is crucial. The criticality related to financial contributions, explicitly mentioned by President Casgnola, reflects a tension common to many public utility entities in the canton. When funding does not cover expenses, there is a risk of contracting the services provided, with a direct impact on the quality of life of those who benefit from the services. The analysis of the current situation suggests that the future of inclusion in Ticino will depend on the ability to diversify revenues and maintain constant dialogue with public entities. Compared to the past, needs have become more complex: it is no longer just about basic assistance, but about cultural and social integration, as demonstrated by the initiative with the Orchestra della Svizzera italiana (OSI). This type of activity is not only recreational but serves as a model for active inclusion, allowing members to experience music from within the orchestra. For anyone operating or residing in the territory, supporting these realities passes through knowledge of their activities and the structural difficulties they face. The comparison with previous years highlights greater operational pressure, aggravated by rising costs and a need for technological innovation that requires constant investment. The association, while looking ahead, does not forget its history but must necessarily adapt the funding model. Organizations operating in the social sector often have to balance revenues between public funds, private donations, and self-financing activities. The challenge for the coming years will be to maintain the excellence of the services offered while ensuring long-term economic sustainability. The transparency communicated during the press conference is a signal of wanting to openly address the issue of resources, inviting citizens and institutions to a broader reflection on the value of the work done by Unitas for the social cohesion of Ticino.

Key points

Participate in Events and Support the Association

For those who wish to support Unitas' activities or take part in the initiatives planned for its 80th anniversary, it is essential to follow the official updates. The program includes high-profile events designed to raise awareness about the association beyond its usual circle. The inclusive concert at the end of August, organized in collaboration with OSI, offers a unique opportunity for 120 members to experience an orchestral performance first-hand. Additionally, the open house day scheduled for mid-October, coinciding with White Cane Day, will be a key moment to raise public awareness about issues related to visual disabilities. For residents and cross-border workers interested in understanding how social dynamics influence daily life in Ticino, consulting local guides is always an excellent first step.

You can stay informed about presidential appointments and financial developments by following the association's official channels. Regarding economic and tax aspects, those who wish to assess their contribution or better understand the tax implications of donations or volunteer activities can use the salary calculator to get a clear overview of their financial situation. Financial planning remains a cornerstone for anyone looking to support public interest causes in Switzerland. The procedure to become a member or supporter is straightforward and can be accessed directly through Unitas' official contacts. It is advisable to periodically check the association’s website for deadlines related to autumn events and for any program changes.

Active participation is not limited to donations but extends to attending events, which are the true driving force of visibility for the association. Let us remember that, in a context where financial resources are insufficient, every form of support—from volunteering to attending charitable events—becomes crucial to ensuring the continuity of services. We encourage all interested parties to mark the dates in May and October on their calendars, as these are pivotal moments for the future of the organization and its historic mission of supporting blind and visually impaired individuals in Italian-speaking Switzerland.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the main event scheduled for the 80th anniversary of Unitas?
The association has announced several initiatives. Among these, a highlight is an inclusive concert with the OSI scheduled for the end of August, which will allow 120 members to sit among the musicians. Additionally, an open house day is planned for mid-October to mark White Cane Day.
Are there any changes in the association's leadership?
Yes. President Fabio Casgnola has announced that he will be stepping down after three years in the role. The appointment of the new association presidency is set for Saturday, May 23.
What is the association's main current challenge?
The main challenge highlighted during the press conference concerns financial resources. The association has noted that the financial contributions currently available are not sufficient to cover all the needs and planned activities.
