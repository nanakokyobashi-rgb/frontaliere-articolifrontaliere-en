The sequence of disputes

In Varese province, approximately 31 thousand workers are involved; in Como province, 32 thousand. In recent years, for cross-border workers from the Varese and Como areas, the disputes have concerned taxes, rights and cross-border work rules. These issues begin with family finances and extend to relations between Italy and Switzerland and the budgets of municipalities in the border area.

At the center of the dispute is the so-called “health tax”: this contribution is предусмотрed for old cross-border workers and is strongly opposed by trade unions. The dispute has involved meetings and public initiatives, accompanied by announcements of legal challenges. The issue then became intertwined with the long-standing matter of tax rebates, that is, tax resources linked to cross-border workers, which represent an important source of revenue for many municipalities in the border area.

For Varese, the issue has had particular significance. Reinstating the 4% threshold in the ratio between cross-border workers and the population left the provincial capital without direct access to the funds. According to the source, this loss of access amounts to approximately 4 million euros per year, following the two previous years in which the provincial capital had received those sums.

The matter also concerns rights. The new tax regime introduced a distinction between “old” and “new” workers, adding complexity to an already complex system. In addition, there are the new unemployment benefit provided for by Law 83/2023 and teleworking, now among the central issues in discussions about the rules governing cross-border work. The matter therefore unfolds on several fronts: taxation, tax rebates, welfare, mobility and equal treatment.

The tax issue and the limits of the framework

Dates and regimes not to be confused

The source refers to a “new tax regime” and distinguishes between “old” and “new” workers. The verified reference for this matter specifies that the New Cross-Border Workers Agreement was signed on December 23, 2020, entered into force on January 1, 2024, and that the Italian ratification is Law 83 of June 13, 2023. The same reference clarifies the distinction mentioned by the source. Old cross-border workers remain taxed only in Switzerland (Art. 9 of the 2020 Agreement). For new cross-border workers, the allowance is €10'000. On employment income, withholding tax is deducted in Switzerland for cross-border workers; Italy avoids double taxation through the tax credit in section CE of form 730. The Italy-Switzerland Convention for the Avoidance of Double Taxation was signed on March 9, 1976, while Switzerland is not a member of the EU or EEA.

These elements help interpret the tax section without attributing the same withholding to both countries.
