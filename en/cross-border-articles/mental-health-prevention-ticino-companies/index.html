Mental health: DSS brings prevention into companies

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 5 October 2026

DSS and Forum GSA Ticino promote mental health at work. Data: 32,3% of young people in Ticino feel drained, 61% of apprentices are suffering.

In Bellinzona, the Department of Health and Social Affairs (DSS) is strengthening its commitment to promoting mental health through a collaboration with Forum GSA Ticino, which is active in workplace health management. The initiative brings awareness-raising, information and training activities to Ticino companies, with the aim of fostering work environments focused on well-being and offering tools to those who work there.

If a cross-border worker works for one of the companies concerned, the relevant context is therefore the workplace. The source does not distinguish between residents and cross-border workers and does not present a measure linked to place of residence: it describes an offering aimed at Ticino companies, with training sessions open to all interested organizations. For those living across the border, the page on vivere in Svizzera remains a separate in-depth resource; this article concerns prevention in the workplace.

Work as a place of well-being

«Mental health is a fundamental component of people’s well-being and health», reads the DSS note.

The Department recalls that, in addition to individual behaviors and choices, life contexts also contribute to well-being. The workplace is one of the places where working people spend a significant part of their lives: it should enable them to feel well, develop skills, find meaning in what they do, feel supported and cultivate positive relationships.

The data cited in the communication paint a complex picture. Promozione Salute Svizzera’s regular survey on work-related stress indicates that about one third of the working population in Switzerland is emotionally exhausted and has a critical level in the Job Stress Index, that is, an unfavorable ratio between workloads and available resources. Among working people aged 25-34, 32,3% in Ticino report feeling emotionally drained, compared with 24% of the Swiss average.

The WorkMed survey, conducted in 2024, adds the apprenticeship dimension: 61% of apprentices in Switzerland experience mental health problems in a broad sense, and between 25% and 30% would need more active support. In Ticino, almost 1 apprentice in 3 reports anxiety disorders, while 86,9% would never have discussed them with the school or training company. The source interprets this latter figure as a sign of the difficulty of accessing available support.

Key points

How to proceed

Four practical steps

The press release makes it possible to identify a course of action without adding details that were not provided. For those who work across the border, the prerequisite remains a Ticino-based company: the source does not describe an individual registration linked to the place of residence.

1. Define the company's interest. The offer is aimed at all interested Ticino companies. A cross-border worker can therefore start with their own organization, without turning the news into a personal procedure that the source does not describe.

2. Match the proposal to the role. Executives, human resources managers and interested employees turn to training on mental health in companies. Apprenticeship trainers, on the other hand, find the Apprentice program, designed for those who support young people during their apprenticeship.

3. Choose the event. A breakfast meeting and an afternoon workshop are planned for October 16. Both are part of the day dedicated to mental health proposed by Promozione Salute Svizzera and Forum GSA Ticino.

4. Complete the registration. The two events are free and open to all interested companies, but require advance registration. This is the operational requirement explicitly stated by the source.

The sequence separates access from content: first the company signs up, then the recipient follows the proposal that matches their role. In the case of apprenticeships, the material is designed for people who support apprentices and aims to provide concrete knowledge and tools.

The collaboration ultimately aims to foster conditions that contribute to young people's mental health and the organization's well-being. To complete your guidance on working in Ticino, use the calcolatore stipendio.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the objective of the initiative between DSS and Forum GSA Ticino?
The initiative aims to strengthen mental health prevention within Ticino companies. The collaboration aims to promote wellness-oriented work environments, offering concrete tools for awareness, information and training to managers, collaborators and trainees. The project was created to combat work-related stress, which in Switzerland involves costs estimated at around 6.5 billion francs per year, promoting an environment where people can feel supported and valued.
What are the main data on job discomfort in Ticino?
The data highlight a critical situation: among active Ticino workers aged between 25 and 34, 32.3% declare themselves emotionally drained, exceeding the Swiss average of 24%. As for apprentices, the WorkMed 2024 survey indicates that 61% experience psychic problems and almost 1 in 3 in Ticino report anxiety disorders. In addition, 86.9% of Ticino apprentices have never spoken about it with their company or school, highlighting a barrier in access to support.
How can workers participate in prevention activities?
The offer is structured for companies. Interested companies can register for training days, such as the one scheduled for 16 October, which includes a breakfast meeting and afternoon workshops. The proposals are divided by target: 'Mental health in companies' is aimed at managers and collaborators, while 'Apprentice' is dedicated to internship trainers. The workers concerned must verify their company's adherence to the initiative, as there are no individual assistance procedures directly from the Department.
