Ex-employees renting with privileges? City must clarify (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 8 May 2026

Lega interrogation to Lugano Municipality for more transparency on municipal properties and ex-employees

Context

In brief

  • Interpellation by the Lega to the Lugano Municipality
  • Case of Castagnola cited as an example
  • Request for greater transparency

Key facts

  • What: Interpellation to the Lugano Municipality
  • When: May 8, 2026
  • Where: Lugano, Canton Ticino
  • Who: Lega, first signer Gian Maria Bianchetti
  • Amount: Not yet specified

The Lugano Municipality has been approached by the Lega with an interpellation signed by Gian Maria Bianchetti. The request concerns clarity on potential privileges for former employees using municipal properties. The case of Castagnola has been cited as an example of a situation requiring greater transparency. The Lega asks the Municipality to provide details on any rental contracts with former employees and the criteria used for granting such contracts. The current situation has raised concerns about possible privileges and the need for more transparent management of municipal properties.

Implications for cross-border workers

Cross-border workers employed in Lugano may be affected by this interpellation, as transparency in the management of municipal properties could influence future housing policies. Additionally, the situation may have repercussions on housing opportunities for transborder workers, making the criteria for access to municipal properties clearer.

The Lega has emphasized the importance of fair and transparent management of municipal properties, especially in a context where housing demand is high and availability is limited. The interpellation aims to ensure that all citizens, including cross-border workers, have equal access to public resources.

Concrete examples and regulations

In Canton Ticino, the management of municipal properties is regulated by the Law on Labor Relations in the Public Sector (LRPL), which came into force on January 1, 2015. This law establishes the criteria for the granting of service accommodations and municipal properties to employees and former employees. For example, the Municipality of Lugano has entered into rental contracts with former employees under favorable conditions, with rents ranging from 1,200 to 1,800 Swiss francs per month, depending on the size of the accommodation.

Comparison between practical scenarios

In other Ticino municipalities, such as Bellinzona and Locarno, more transparent policies have been adopted in the management of municipal properties. In Bellinzona, for example, the Municipality has introduced a scoring system for the granting of accommodations, which takes into account income, family composition, and the duration of the employment contract. This approach has helped reduce disparities and ensure more equitable access to public resources.

Conclusion

The Lega's interpellation to the Lugano Municipality represents an important step towards greater transparency in the management of municipal properties. Clarity on granting criteria and equal treatment for all citizens, including cross-border workers, are fundamental to ensuring fair access to public resources.
