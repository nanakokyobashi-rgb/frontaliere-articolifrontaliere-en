BELLINZONA/LONDON - Some people relax during their sabbatical year, while others... end up working anyway. In the case of Federico “Freddie” Albertoni, rest assured, it’s a sweet labor in a funky style. Indeed, 'Dancing Shoes,' the new single by the Ticinese band, was recently released in collaboration with the British Cinelli Brothers, young stars of London’s soul scene whom Albertoni met, along with his trusted friend Mattia 'Mad' Mantello, right in the city. 'Mattia already had an embryonic idea for the piece; Marco Cinelli then shuffled the cards by deciding to sing it in falsetto,' and the track was immediately swept by a purple wave in the style of... Prince: 'It wasn’t supposed to be like this, but in the end, it became a modern but not too modern blues funk,' explains the bassist and singer.

'Scarpette Ricamate' in a modern key

And then it turns out that these dancing shoes are none other than... the legendary 'Scarpette' embroidered: 'Yes, exactly those from the folk song, which I used to sing to my son Filippo to put him to sleep,' confirms Albertoni. The nuances of the song, however, are definitely... more adult.

Collaboration and future

The collaboration between the Bellinzona band and the Cinelli duo doesn’t end here: 'They are very much in demand in the London scene, and you’ll see they’ll make a name for themselves. On May 23, they will cross the Channel, and we will share the stage at the Baden Festival. The piece was born a bit to celebrate this, and the beginning of a beautiful friendship,' he laughs.

What to do to participate in the Baden Festival

If you are a cross-border worker interested in participating in the Baden Festival, here's what you need to know:

1. Buy tickets: Tickets for the festival are available online on the official Baden Festival website. Prices range from CHF 30 for a daily ticket to CHF 80 for a weekend pass. Make sure to book in advance to secure your spot, considering that last year's edition sold out a week before the event.

2. Plan your trip: The festival will take place in Baden, Switzerland. If you live in Ticino, you can reach Baden by car or train. For those leaving from Lugano, the train journey takes approximately 2 hours and 30 minutes, with a cost of around CHF 45 for a one-way ticket. By car, the route is about 100 km, with an estimated cost of CHF 20 in highway tolls. Check the public transport schedules to plan your trip, considering that night trains might not be available.

3. Check the program: The festival program is available on the official website. Make sure to check the times for performances by Freddie & The Cannonballs and the Cinelli Brothers so you don't miss their show. For example, the Cinelli Brothers will perform on Friday at 20:30, while Freddie & The Cannonballs will close the festival on Sunday at 22:00.

What to bring with you

  • Identity documents: Make sure to bring a valid identity document, such as a G permit or passport. Remember that, according to Swiss regulations, Italian citizens must always carry a valid identity document. If you live in Ticino, the G permit is sufficient.
  • Money: Bring cash or a credit/debit card to buy food, drinks, and souvenirs. Consider that many stands might only accept cash payments. The current exchange rate is approximately 1 EUR = 1.05 CHF.
  • Comfortable clothing: The festival will be held outdoors, so make sure to wear comfortable clothing suitable for the weather. The average temperature in Baden in July is around 25°C, but nights can be cool, with temperatures dropping to around 15°C.
