S50 breakdown in Busto Arsizio: passengers stranded for 3 hours

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 2 October 2026

On 29 September, an S50 Malpensa-Bellinzona train remained stopped for more than three hours 200 meters from the station. Europa Verde asks Rfi and Trenord for explanations.

On 29 September, Europa Verde in the province of Varese raised the issue of the reliability of the cross-border service to Malpensa, following a breakdown of a train travelling from Malpensa to Bellinzona. The political movement’s statement asks for explanations about what happened and the consequences of the train’s stoppage for rail traffic.

Reconstruction of the incident

According to Europa Verde’s reconstruction, the train reportedly stopped about 200 metres from Busto Arsizio FS station. However, according to testimonies cited by the movement, the short distance from the platform did not result in authorization to evacuate the train and escort passengers safely to the station.

According to the statement, passengers reportedly remained inside the train for more than three hours. The source specifies that the opening photo of the original article is a file photo and does not concern this incident: a point that Europa Verde distinguishes from the reconstruction of the breakdown.

The questions are addressed to the operators of the infrastructure and rail service, identified respectively as Rfi and Trenord. Europa Verde asks why passengers were not authorized to get off and why, according to the reconstruction contained in the statement, three hours were needed to arrange for the train to be towed to Busto Arsizio station.

The movement links the case to the debate on the reliability of the cross-border connection, also discussed in Switzerland. In the statement, it argues that:

«Switzerland is in fact beginning to consider the service operated in Italy as not up to standard and is loudly calling for the shuttle bus service to Malpensa to be reactivated, as it is considered more reliable and more punctual».

This position forms part of Europa Verde’s criticism of the quality of the rail service and the management of disruptions affecting commuters in the area. Finally, the movement asks what measures will be taken to prevent travellers from being left on the train for hours without answers.

Operational details

What emerges for those crossing the border

For the cross-border commuter who uses the S50, the episode described by Europa Verde should be viewed on two levels: the continuity of the cross-border connection and the practical management of passengers when the train stops. The source provides no schedules, frequencies, normal journey times or alternative routes; it is therefore not possible to turn this case into a general measure of the line. The practical fact documented is another: a train bound for Bellinzona reportedly stopped near a station and, according to the movement's account, the passengers reportedly remained on board for over three hours.

Two different questions

The first issue concerns safety and evacuation. Europa Verde reports testimonies about the failure to authorize passengers to get off, not a formally reported decision in the source with reasons from the operators. The second concerns the recovery of the train: the note asks why three hours were needed to organize the towing to Busto Arsizio. Separating the two levels avoids reducing everything to a simple delay: on the one hand, there is what was reportedly communicated by the operations room; on the other, the time attributed to organizing the intervention.

Frequently Asked Questions
What happened to the S50 train on 29 September?
A convoy of line S50, coming from Malpensa airport and heading to Bellinzona, suffered a forced stop due to a technical failure on 29 September. The train stopped about 200 metres from Busto Arsizio FS station. Despite the proximity to the dock, passengers were stuck inside the carriages for over three hours, according to the reconstruction released by the Green Europe political movement in the province of Varese.
What explanations were asked of the railway managers?
Green Europe asked specific questions of RFI, the infrastructure manager, and Trenord, the service manager. The requests for clarification concern the lack of authorization for the evacuation of travelers to the station, despite the short distance of only 200 meters. In addition, it is asked why it took more than three hours to organise the towing of the failed train to the Busto Arsizio dock, a time deemed excessive for the management of discomfort.
What is Switzerland's position on the shuttle bus service?
According to what is reported in the Green Europe note, Switzerland is considering the railway service operated on Italian territory as not meeting the necessary standards. For this reason, the movement reports that the reactivation of the shuttle bus service to Malpensa is being loudly requested. This on-road alternative is considered more reliable and punctual than the current cross-border rail connection, often marked by significant failures and delays.
