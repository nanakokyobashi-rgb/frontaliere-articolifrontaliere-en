Developments and prospects

In the Canton of Ticino, the minimum wage is CHF 20.00/hour for regular hours and CHF 20.50/hour for night or holiday hours. This means that a worker who works 42 hours per week, with a regular schedule, will receive CHF 840/hour and CHF 864/hour if they work nights or holidays. For the month of June 2026, a worker working 42 hours per week will receive CHF 3640/month. It is important to note that these minimum wages are only applicable to workers working on behalf of an employer, and not to self-employed workers or employees of a company.

Cantonal minimum wages were introduced following the approval of cantonal initiatives. Some cantons have already implemented minimum wages, while others are still under discussion. The City of Lucerne has already approved a minimum wage from 1 January 2026.

Switzerland, in particular, is known for its stable and competitive economy, but the issue of minimum wages has been debated in some cantons. In Ticino, for example, the minimum wage has been the subject of discussion in recent years. In 2022, the State Council of the Canton of Ticino approved an increase in the minimum wage, which was set at CHF 4,600 per month for adults and CHF3,600 for young people under the age of 20.

The legislation in force provides that employers must pay at least the minimum wage to all employees working in the Canton. This also includes seasonal workers and temporary workers. However, it is important to note that the minimum wage is not applicable to employees who work part-time or on a fixed-term basis.

Key points

If you want to know what your pockets will look like after the minimum wage increases, you might consider using another tool to calculate your income. In Ticino, Law no. 151 of 2 April 2019 establishes that minimum wages must be updated annually based on inflation. According to the decree of 24 February 2022, the minimum hourly amounts for 2023 were set at 4.82 Swiss francs for workers with less than 2 years' experience and 5.33 Swiss francs for workers with more than 2 years' experience.

To get a better idea of how these changes might affect your income, let's consider a concrete example. Suppose you are a worker from Bellinzona with 5 years' experience, working 40 hours a week with an hourly wage of 6 Swiss francs. According to the law, the minimum hourly wage should be 5.33 Swiss francs. In this case, your monthly net income would be about 2,640 Swiss francs, considering an employment of 90% of the gross salary.

However, it is important to note that this calculation is only an approximation and that your actual income may vary based on factors such as taxes, transportation charges, and other tax deductions.
