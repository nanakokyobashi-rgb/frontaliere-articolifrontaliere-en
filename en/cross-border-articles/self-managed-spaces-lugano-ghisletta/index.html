Self-management spaces in Lugano: Ghisletta, 'The topic is not on the table' (cross-border guide)

Di Valerie Linc · Published on 4 May 2026

Work begins on the redevelopment of Lugano's former slaughterhouse, while discussions continue on the future use of the former Viganello sewing school for independent culture.

Context

In brief

  • Work begins on the former Lugano Slaughterhouse
  • Ghisletta: 'No specific discussions planned for self-management'
  • Future former Viganello sewing school could host independent culture

Key facts

  • What: Redevelopment of the former Lugano Slaughterhouse
  • When: Work began this morning
  • Where: Lugano
  • Who: Raoul Ghisletta, head of real estate for the City of Lugano
  • Duration: Work estimated to last between a month and a half and two months

A new chapter has begun for the former Lugano Slaughterhouse. Work on the redevelopment of the area began this morning, marking the definitive end of a symbolic place for Ticino self-management, which still seems 'homeless', as explained to Ticinonews by Raoul Ghisletta, head of real estate for the City of Lugano. 'On this topic, we have no specific discussions planned.'

The situation is different regarding independent culture. 'In this sense, there are discussions, particularly concerning the future former Viganello sewing school, which will move to Chiasso in 2028: if an agreement is reached with the Canton, since the building is canton-owned, it could be used for so-called independent culture.'

Implications for cross-border workers

The redevelopment of the former Lugano Slaughterhouse and the possible allocation of the future former Viganello sewing school to independent culture could have a significant impact on the community of cross-border workers living and working in Ticino. These spaces could offer new cultural and social opportunities, improving the quality of life and integration of cross-border workers into the local community.

Operational details

Procedure for the Redevelopment of the Former Slaughterhouse

The overall duration of the operation started this morning is estimated between a month and a half and two months, after which the work will move inside the building to free the walls and allow architects to verify the state of the art of the building. 'It is a protected monument, so it must be maintained and restored,' Ghisletta points out, recalling that 'for this reason it is important to be able to ascertain the exact conditions of the building.'

Subsequently, the matter will return to the municipal council. 'A credit will have to be voted on to design the interior spaces and one for the construction.' In terms of timing, 'the first step could arrive next year, while the work could start in two or three years.'

Practical Implications for Cross-Border Workers

Cross-border workers interested in participating in the cultural and social life of the Canton of Ticino should closely monitor the development of these projects. The redevelopment of the former slaughterhouse and the possible allocation of the future former sewing school of Viganello to independent culture could offer new opportunities for artistic and cultural expression.

Furthermore, cross-border workers may be invited to participate in public consultations or to express their opinions through official channels. It is important that cross-border workers regularly inform themselves about the developments of these projects and actively participate in the discussions, so as to contribute to creating spaces that meet their needs and expectations.

Key points

Concrete actions for cross-border workers

For cross-border workers who want to actively participate in the cultural and social life of the Canton of Ticino, it's important to follow some concrete steps. First of all, it's essential to stay updated on the developments of the former slaughterhouse and the future former sewing school in Viganello. This can be done by regularly consulting the official websites of the Municipality of Lugano and the Canton of Ticino.

Furthermore, cross-border workers can participate in public consultations and express their opinions through official channels. This can help ensure that cultural spaces meet their needs and expectations. Finally, cross-border workers can join discussion groups and social networks dedicated to culture and self-management, so as to stay informed and involved.

Useful tools for cross-border workers

Cross-border workers can use various tools and resources available online to stay updated and actively participate in the cultural life of the Canton of Ticino. For example, they can consult the official websites of the Municipality of Lugano and the Canton of Ticino for updated information on ongoing projects and participation opportunities.

Additionally, they can subscribe to newsletters and discussion groups dedicated to culture and self-management, so as to receive news and updates directly in their email inbox. These tools can help cross-border workers stay informed and involved, contributing to creating a richer and more diverse cultural environment.
