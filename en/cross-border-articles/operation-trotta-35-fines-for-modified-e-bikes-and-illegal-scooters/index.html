Operation TROTTA: 35 fines for modified e-bikes and illegal scooters

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 9 October 2026

In eight days of TROTTA checks in Ticino, 35 citations, 9 non-compliant vehicles (power 1'200-1'600 W, speed up to 48 km/h) and 15 judicial proceedings.

On September 30, the TROTTA summer operation concluded in Ticino, a campaign of checks on soft mobility organized by the cantonal police together with the municipal police forces. The checks covered e-bikes and electric scooters, with the aim of verifying the vehicles’ compliance and users’ adherence to the rules.

The patrols worked for eight days at 11 strategic checkpoints distributed across Mendrisiotto, Luganese, Locarnese and Bellinzonese. The areas mentioned cover the main population centers in the region affected by the operation. Alongside enforcement, officers handed out informational material and provided practical advice to people stopped in high-traffic areas.

The tally of violations

The checks concluded with 35 violations. In 15 cases, the seriousness of the infractions led to further legal proceedings: 13 reports were forwarded to the Juvenile Magistrate’s Office and two formal complaints to the Public Prosecutor’s Office.

For the technical checks, a special roller test bench was used directly on site. The measurements revealed irregularities in speed and construction characteristics in nine vehicles. Five vehicles had been tampered with by their drivers to increase performance; four others did not comply with the traffic requirements in force in Switzerland.

Among the non-compliant vehicles, e-bikes with power outputs between 1'200 and 1'600 watts were identified, exceeding the legal limit of 1'000 watts. The highest speed measured was 48 km/h. In this case, the driver was reported to the Public Prosecutor’s Office.

Focus on minors

A specific section concerned age and driving requirements. Violations were found in 13 cases: 11 drivers were under 14, and two, aged between 14 and 16, were riding without a Category M licence. All were reported to the Juvenile Magistrate’s Office, including one boy riding a non-compliant vehicle.

The picture compiled by the cantonal police thus shows checks on compliance with the rules and technical inspections: the campaign checked speeds, vehicle construction, drivers’ ages and the required licences.

Operational details

For a cross-border worker who uses an e-bike or an electric scooter in Canton Ticino, the news has a practical effect only under one condition: that they are the one riding that vehicle. The source does not say how many cross-border workers were stopped and does not link the reports to commuters or home-to-work routes. The connection should therefore not be extended beyond this use case.

Age, licence and behaviour

The rule recalled by the cantonal police can be read as follows:

The distinction avoids confusing ownership of the vehicle with the right to ride it. A cross-border worker who has turned 16 does not need to present a driving document for this requirement, but the source also specifies that compliance concerns speed and construction characteristics. Therefore, age does not automatically make an e-bike or electric scooter compliant. Tampering to increase performance and non-compliant vehicles are two different situations, both of which were covered by the checks.

For everyday travel, the cantonal police emphasizes lights that are always on, reflectors and high-visibility clothing. A helmet is strongly recommended even when the law does not expressly require it. These are safety instructions for stopped users, not a new tax or employment procedure for people crossing the border.

Key points

For those who use one of these vehicles in Ticino, the practical check can be carried out before setting out. The operation ended on 30 September, but the guidance issued by the cantonal police sets out a simple sequence: driver's age, licence when required, technical compliance and safety.

A four-step check

1. Check the driver's age. Anyone under 14 may not ride a light moped; between 14 and 16, an M licence is required; from the age of 16, no driving document is required.

2. Keep the vehicle compliant. The source reports that patrols checked speed and construction characteristics using a roller test bench. A modification to increase performance can therefore become an infraction.

3. Prepare for safe riding. The lights must remain on, reflectors and high-visibility clothing must be used; a helmet is recommended even when it is not expressly required by law.

4. Consider what can happen during a check. Officers provided informational material and practical advice, but the campaign's results also show that judicial proceedings were initiated. When violations related to age or licensing emerged, the young people were reported to the Juvenile Magistrate's Office. In the case of the speed of 48 km/h, a report was filed with the Public Prosecutor's Office.

The scope for cross-border workers

This checklist concerns anyone who actually uses an e-bike or an electric scooter in Ticino. The source does not link the operation to work permits, taxes or contributions and provides no basis for extending the check to other aspects of the employment relationship. For the cross-border reader, the practical takeaway is therefore to keep the two aspects separate: check the vehicle and driving requirements when using these vehicles, without attributing to the campaign rules that are not stated.
