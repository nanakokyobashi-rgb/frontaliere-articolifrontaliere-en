At the 109th assembly of the Chamber of Commerce of the Canton of Ticino, held today in Bellinzona, the message was clear: Ticino must find its compass again and the economy cannot be called upon to pay the bill. Andrea Gehri, president of Cc-Ti, has indicated four priorities: restoring finances, encouraging economic growth, training young people ready for future challenges and promoting a vision of a country capable of uniting everyone, from right to left.

Criticism of politics

Gehri did not name names, but criticized the political management. In his opinion, politics addresses the ordinary without governing the extraordinary.

«Politics is managing the ordinary, but not the extraordinary».

In the president's message there is no mea culpa aimed at businesses. Companies, according to Gehri, work very quietly, they do not ask for subsidies and instead ask for framework conditions that allow them to prosper. The contested point is the withdrawal from the economy for decisions which, in its reading, do not fall within the economic competence.

The examples cited include the thirteenth AVS initiative and two initiatives relating to health insurance premiums. Gehri observed that in these cases money is spent without knowing where to get it first. It did not close the door to an intervention on revenues, if included in the symmetry of sacrifices. The proposal is to sit at the same table with politics, social partners and civil society: the Cc-Ti, he said, is available.

Administration at the center

Director Luca Albertoni has broadened the picture. Doing business is more difficult not only in Ticino, but also in the context of the German crisis and American tariffs. Hence Cc-Ti's support for the Stop the increase in cantonal employees initiative, with the Chamber on the promoting committee.

Albertoni assured that heavy cuts to services are not needed. He calls for bringing the administration back to normal proportions, so that the state is effective for the citizens. Some tasks, he added, could pass to the private sector. But he said he doesn't believe there will be a bloodbath in the end: that's not the aim of the initiative.
