Astuti: 'No real territorial comparison' (cross-border guide)

Di Redazione Frontaliere Ticino · Published on 13 May 2026

PD regional councillor Samuele Astuti criticises the management of the healthcare tax for cross-border workers and the uncertainty in relations with Switzerland.

Context

In brief

  • Astuti criticizes the management of the healthcare tax for cross-border workers
  • No dialogue with local authorities and unions
  • Uncertainty in relations with Switzerland
  • Concrete examples of impact on municipalities such as Mendrisio and Chiasso

Key facts

  • What: Criticism of the management of the healthcare tax for cross-border workers
  • When: May 13, 2026
  • Where: Lombardy region
  • Who: Regional councilor of the PD Samuele Astuti
  • Amount: Not specified

Regional councilor of the PD Samuele Astuti has criticized the management of the healthcare tax for cross-border workers, arguing that the majority has not engaged in genuine dialogue with local authorities and unions. Astuti stated that such a sensitive measure for border areas, like Luino and the entire cross-border territory, has never been subject to a shared process with local administrators, unions, and worker representatives. In the regional council, Astuti has repeatedly called for opening a serious and structured discussion, but the majority has decided not to listen. Astuti also expressed concern about the uncertainty in relations with Switzerland and the management of refunds, which risk undermining fundamental balances for border municipalities.

Criticism of the healthcare tax management

Astuti emphasized that the majority continues to exclude territories, unions, and local administrators from decisions. He criticized the majority's approach, which announces and reassures publicly but never translates into real dialogue with the territories and the regional council. Astuti also expressed concern about the uncertainty in relations with Switzerland and the management of refunds, which risk undermining fundamental balances for border municipalities.

Uncertainty in relations with Switzerland

Astuti expressed concern about the uncertainty in relations with Switzerland and the management of refunds. He argued that if Forza Italia and the center-right truly consider it necessary to review the measure, they are willing to sit around a table and address the issue in depth, seriously and without slogans. Astuti concluded that today we are faced with confused and inhomogeneous rules, without national oversight and without real parliamentary and institutional dialogue.

Concrete examples and local impacts

📊 Mendrisio: With over 12,000 cross-border workers, Mendrisio is one of the municipalities most affected by regulatory uncertainties. The healthcare tax, introduced in 2023, saw a 15% increase compared to the previous year, with a significant impact on family budgets. Refunds, expected within 90 days, often slip to 6 months, creating economic difficulties for many families.

📊 Chiasso: Here, 40% of the active population consists of cross-border workers. The lack of clarity in regulations has led to a 10% reduction in local investments, with a direct impact on public services and infrastructure.

Key dates and regulations

  • 2023: Introduction of the healthcare tax for cross-border workers in Lombardy.
  • 2024: 15% increase in the healthcare tax, with a direct impact on over 60,000 cross-border workers.
  • 2026: Criticism of the management of refunds, with waiting times exceeding 6 months.

Operational checklist

✅ Sharing with territories: Involvement of local administrators and unions in decisions. ✅ Regulatory clarity: Definition of certain times for refunds and transparency in communications. ✅ Dialogue with Switzerland: Joint working table to avoid regulatory inhomogeneities.

Comparisons between practical scenarios

🔍 Current scenario: Confused rules, uncertain refund times, negative impact on family and local budgets. 🔍 Desired scenario: Regulatory clarity, certain times for refunds, active involvement of territories and unions.

Astuti reiterated the need for a more structured and collaborative approach, emphasizing the importance of serious and constructive dialogue with all parties involved.

Implications for cross-border workers

Astuti's criticisms raise important questions for cross-border workers who work in Switzerland and reside in Italy. The health tax, introduced in 2021, is a sensitive measure that can have a significant impact on the finances of cross-border workers. For example, a worker earning 4,000 CHF per month must pay approximately 100 CHF in health tax, a cost that can increase based on income and family situation. Uncertainty in relations with Switzerland and the management of refunds can also influence the economic stability of border areas, such as Chiasso, Chiavenna, and Campione d'Italia.
